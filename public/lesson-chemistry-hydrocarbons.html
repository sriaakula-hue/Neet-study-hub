Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons

Key concepts

  • Acyclic saturated alkanes have formula CnH₂n+₂. Acyclic alkenes with one double bond have CnH₂n; acyclic alkynes with one triple bond have CnH₂n−₂.
  • Addition reactions are characteristic of many unsaturated hydrocarbons. Alkanes can undergo radical substitution.
  • Benzene has a delocalised aromatic pi system and commonly undergoes electrophilic substitution.

Worked connection

Acyclic C₃H₈ matches an alkane formula. C₃H₆ can be an alkene or a cycloalkane, so formula alone does not establish structure.

Watch out

The peroxide effect for addition of hydrogen halides to alkenes is characteristic of HBr, not a universal rule for HCl and HI.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Ethene contains how many pi bonds?

One

Its C=C double bond has one sigma bond and one pi bond.

2. Ethyne contains how many pi bonds between its carbons?

Two

The triple bond consists of one sigma and two pi bonds.

3. Hydrogenation of an alkene to an alkane is an:

Addition reaction

Hydrogen adds across the double bond under suitable catalytic conditions.

4. Acyclic compounds with one C≡C bond have general formula:

CnH₂n−₂

Relative to an alkane, one triple bond removes four hydrogen atoms.

5. In ordinary Markovnikov HBr addition to propene without peroxides, the major product is:

2-bromopropane

The usual ionic addition places Br on the more substituted carbon in this example.

6. Ethane’s carbon–carbon bond is:

A sigma bond

A single C–C bond is a sigma bond.

7. The simplest six-carbon aromatic ring is:

Benzene

Benzene has a cyclic conjugated six-pi-electron system; the other options are not equivalent aromatic structures.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. An acyclic alkane with three carbon atoms has formula:

  1. C₃H₄
  2. C₃H₆
  3. C₃H₈
  4. C₃H₁₀
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. C₃H₈

CnH₂n+₂ gives C₃H₈.

2. Benzene commonly undergoes:

  1. Electrophilic substitution
  2. Only nucleophilic addition
  3. Only polymerisation
  4. No reactions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electrophilic substitution

Substitution retains aromatic stabilisation.

3. The usual peroxide effect in hydrogen-halide addition is observed with:

  1. HCl
  2. HBr
  3. HI
  4. HF equally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. HBr

The radical-chain route is effective for HBr under suitable conditions.

4. Ethene contains how many pi bonds?

  1. One
  2. Zero
  3. Two
  4. Three
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One

Its C=C double bond has one sigma bond and one pi bond.

5. Ethyne contains how many pi bonds between its carbons?

  1. Two
  2. One
  3. Zero
  4. Three
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two

The triple bond consists of one sigma and two pi bonds.

6. Hydrogenation of an alkene to an alkane is an:

  1. Addition reaction
  2. Elimination reaction
  3. Only isotope exchange
  4. A nuclear reaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Addition reaction

Hydrogen adds across the double bond under suitable catalytic conditions.

7. Acyclic compounds with one C≡C bond have general formula:

  1. CnH₂n−₂
  2. CnH₂n+₂
  3. CnH₂n
  4. CnHn
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CnH₂n−₂

Relative to an alkane, one triple bond removes four hydrogen atoms.

8. In ordinary Markovnikov HBr addition to propene without peroxides, the major product is:

  1. 2-bromopropane
  2. 1-bromopropane
  3. Benzene
  4. Ethyne
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2-bromopropane

The usual ionic addition places Br on the more substituted carbon in this example.

9. Ethane’s carbon–carbon bond is:

  1. A sigma bond
  2. Only a pi bond
  3. Two pi bonds
  4. A hydrogen bond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A sigma bond

A single C–C bond is a sigma bond.

10. The simplest six-carbon aromatic ring is:

  1. Benzene
  2. Hexane
  3. Cyclohexane
  4. Hexene
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Benzene

Benzene has a cyclic conjugated six-pi-electron system; the other options are not equivalent aromatic structures.

11. Acyclic saturated hydrocarbons belong to the:

  1. Alkane series
  2. Alkyne series
  3. Aromatic series only
  4. Alcohol series
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Alkane series

Alkanes have only carbon-carbon single bonds and no rings in the stated case.

12. The general formula of an acyclic monoalkene is:

  1. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  2. CₙH₂ₙ₋₆
  3. CₙHₙ
  4. CₙH₂ₙ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CₙH₂ₙ

One double bond reduces hydrogen count by two relative to an acyclic alkane.

13. The general formula of an acyclic monoalkyne is:

  1. CₙH₂ₙ
  2. CₙH₂ₙ₊₄
  3. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
  4. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

One triple bond corresponds to two degrees of unsaturation.

14. Hydrogenation of an alkene with a suitable metal catalyst generally forms an:

  1. Amide
  2. Alkane
  3. Alcohol always
  4. Carboxylic acid always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Alkane

Adding hydrogen across the double bond saturates the carbon-carbon bond.

15. Bromine solution is decolourised by an ordinary alkene because of:

  1. Addition across its double bond
  2. Nuclear decay
  3. Only solvent evaporation
  4. Formation of a noble gas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Addition across its double bond

The pi bond reacts with bromine, consuming its characteristic colour.

16. Complete combustion of a pure hydrocarbon produces:

  1. Only CO
  2. Only carbon and hydrogen gases
  3. Only nitrogen dioxide
  4. CO₂ and H₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CO₂ and H₂O

Sufficient oxygen oxidises carbon to carbon dioxide and hydrogen to water.

17. Chlorination of methane under ultraviolet light proceeds mainly by:

  1. Only ionic precipitation
  2. Condensation polymerisation
  3. Free-radical substitution
  4. Electrophilic aromatic substitution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Free-radical substitution

Light initiates chlorine radicals and a chain substitution mechanism.

18. Initiation in alkane chlorination involves:

  1. Loss of methane's nucleus
  2. Homolysis of Cl–Cl
  3. Heterolysis of every C–H bond
  4. Formation of CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Homolysis of Cl–Cl

Absorbed light creates two chlorine radicals from a chlorine molecule.

19. Ethane's staggered conformation is generally more stable than eclipsed because it has lower:

  1. Torsional strain
  2. Atomic mass
  3. Number of protons
  4. Carbon valency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Torsional strain

Staggering reduces eclipsing interactions between adjacent bonds.

20. Markovnikov addition of HBr to propene without peroxide gives mainly:

  1. 1-Bromopropane
  2. Benzene
  3. Propanal
  4. 2-Bromopropane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2-Bromopropane

The usual ionic pathway favours the more stable secondary carbocation intermediate.

21. The peroxide effect is classically important for radical addition of:

  1. HI
  2. HF
  3. HBr
  4. HCl
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. HBr

The radical-chain energetics support anti-Markovnikov HBr addition under suitable peroxide conditions.

22. HBr addition to propene in peroxide conditions gives mainly:

  1. Propanone
  2. 1-Bromopropane
  3. 2-Bromopropane
  4. Propanoic acid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1-Bromopropane

The radical pathway favours the more stable radical and places bromine at the terminal carbon.

23. Terminal alkynes are more acidic than alkanes because their conjugate base has:

  1. An sp carbon with greater s character
  2. No carbon electrons
  3. Only sp³ orbitals
  4. A positive charge always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An sp carbon with greater s character

Greater s character stabilises electron density on the acetylide carbon.

24. A terminal alkyne can react with a sufficiently strong base to form an:

  1. Alkane directly
  2. Aromatic nucleus always
  3. Ammonium salt only
  4. Acetylide ion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acetylide ion

Removal of the terminal proton yields the acetylide carbanion.

25. Ozonolysis of an alkene followed by reductive workup is used to identify:

  1. Only neutron counts
  2. Only aromaticity
  3. The original double-bond position
  4. Only carbon isotopes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The original double-bond position

Cleavage into carbonyl products reveals which carbons formed the original double bond.

26. Benzene has a planar ring with how many delocalised pi electrons?

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 4
  4. 8
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 6

Six p orbitals contribute six pi electrons to the aromatic system.

27. The elementary Hückel rule for a planar conjugated monocycle is:

  1. 4n+2 pi electrons
  2. 4n pi electrons always
  3. Any odd electron count
  4. Zero pi electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4n+2 pi electrons

The closed-shell aromatic pattern is 4n+2 under the stated geometric and conjugation conditions.

28. Benzene commonly reacts by substitution rather than ordinary addition because substitution preserves:

  1. Only molecular mass
  2. Only hydrogen count
  3. A saturated ring
  4. Aromatic stabilisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Aromatic stabilisation

Addition would disrupt its stabilised delocalised pi system.

29. Nitration of benzene uses an electrophile commonly represented as:

  1. NH₂⁻
  2. N₂
  3. NO₂⁺
  4. NO₃⁻
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. NO₂⁺

The nitrating mixture generates nitronium ion as the attacking electrophile.

30. An alkyl group on benzene usually directs electrophilic substitution towards:

  1. No positions
  2. Ortho and para positions
  3. Only meta position
  4. Only replacement of carbon nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ortho and para positions

Electron donation from an alkyl substituent commonly activates ortho and para sites.
