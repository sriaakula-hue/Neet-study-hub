Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Anthers produce pollen; ovules contain female gametophytes. Pollination transfers pollen to a stigma, whereas fertilisation involves gamete fusion.
  • In typical angiosperm double fertilisation, one male gamete fuses with the egg and the other fuses with the central cell containing two polar nuclei.
  • The resulting zygote is diploid and primary endosperm nucleus is usually triploid. Embryo and endosperm have distinct functions.

Connect the ideas

With haploid gametes n, egg fusion gives 2n; fusion with two polar nuclei gives 3n.

Common mistake

Pollination does not itself guarantee fertilisation. Pollen must germinate and the pollen tube must reach the embryo sac.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The pollen grain represents the male:

Gametophyte

A pollen grain is the reduced male gametophyte developing from a microspore.

2. Microsporogenesis involves formation of microspores by:

Meiosis

Diploid microspore mother cells undergo meiosis to form haploid microspores.

3. A typical mature Polygonum-type embryo sac is:

Seven-celled and eight-nucleate

The central cell contains two polar nuclei, so nucleus count exceeds cell count by one.

4. Autogamy involves pollen transfer within the:

Same flower

Pollination occurs between anther and stigma of the same flower.

5. Geitonogamy occurs between different flowers on the:

Same plant

It involves a pollinating transfer between flowers but is genetically similar to self-pollination.

6. Xenogamy involves pollen transfer between:

Different plants of the same species

Cross-pollination between different plants can introduce genetically different pollen.

7. The seed coat generally develops from the ovule’s:

Integuments

The integuments form the protective seed-coat layers as the ovule matures into a seed.

1. Typical angiosperm endosperm formed by triple fusion is:

  1. Haploid
  2. Diploid
  3. Triploid
  4. Always tetraploid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Triploid

One male gamete and two polar nuclei usually contribute three haploid chromosome sets.

2. Pollination is the transfer of pollen to the:

  1. Root
  2. Stigma
  3. Embryo
  4. Endosperm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Stigma

Pollen is transferred from anther to stigma.

3. Fusion of a male gamete with the egg forms the:

  1. Zygote
  2. Seed coat
  3. Pollen grain
  4. Fruit wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zygote

Syngamy produces the diploid zygote.

4. The pollen grain represents the male:

  1. Gametophyte
  2. Sporophyte plant
  3. Endosperm
  4. Seed coat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gametophyte

A pollen grain is the reduced male gametophyte developing from a microspore.

5. Microsporogenesis involves formation of microspores by:

  1. Meiosis
  2. Fertilisation
  3. Only ordinary mitosis of a gamete
  4. Seed germination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meiosis

Diploid microspore mother cells undergo meiosis to form haploid microspores.

6. A typical mature Polygonum-type embryo sac is:

  1. Seven-celled and eight-nucleate
  2. Eight-celled and seven-nucleate
  3. Four-celled and four-nucleate
  4. One-celled and diploid only

Answer: A. Seven-celled and eight-nucleate

The central cell contains two polar nuclei, so nucleus count exceeds cell count by one.

7. Autogamy involves pollen transfer within the:

  1. Same flower

8. Geitonogamy occurs between different flowers on the:

  1. Same plant
  2. Different species

Answer: A. Same plant

It involves a pollinating transfer between flowers but is genetically similar to self-pollination.

9. Xenogamy involves pollen transfer between:

10. The seed coat generally develops from the ovule’s:
