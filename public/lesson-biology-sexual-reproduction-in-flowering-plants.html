Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Anthers produce pollen; ovules contain female gametophytes. Pollination transfers pollen to a stigma, whereas fertilisation involves gamete fusion.
  • In typical angiosperm double fertilisation, one male gamete fuses with the egg and the other fuses with the central cell containing two polar nuclei.
  • The resulting zygote is diploid and primary endosperm nucleus is usually triploid. Embryo and endosperm have distinct functions.

Connect the ideas

With haploid gametes n, egg fusion gives 2n; fusion with two polar nuclei gives 3n.

Common mistake

Pollination does not itself guarantee fertilisation. Pollen must germinate and the pollen tube must reach the embryo sac.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Typical angiosperm endosperm formed by triple fusion is:

  1. Haploid
  2. Diploid
  3. Triploid
  4. Always tetraploid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Triploid

One male gamete and two polar nuclei usually contribute three haploid chromosome sets.

2. Pollination is the transfer of pollen to the:

  1. Root
  2. Stigma
  3. Embryo
  4. Endosperm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Stigma

Pollen is transferred from anther to stigma.

3. Fusion of a male gamete with the egg forms the:

  1. Zygote
  2. Seed coat
  3. Pollen grain
  4. Fruit wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zygote

Syngamy produces the diploid zygote.

4. The pollen grain represents the male:

  1. Gametophyte
  2. Sporophyte plant
  3. Endosperm
  4. Seed coat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gametophyte

A pollen grain is the reduced male gametophyte developing from a microspore.

5. Microsporogenesis involves formation of microspores by:

  1. Meiosis
  2. Fertilisation
  3. Only ordinary mitosis of a gamete
  4. Seed germination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meiosis

Diploid microspore mother cells undergo meiosis to form haploid microspores.

6. A typical mature Polygonum-type embryo sac is:

  1. Seven-celled and eight-nucleate
  2. Eight-celled and seven-nucleate
  3. Four-celled and four-nucleate
  4. One-celled and diploid only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Seven-celled and eight-nucleate

The central cell contains two polar nuclei, so nucleus count exceeds cell count by one.

7. Autogamy involves pollen transfer within the:

  1. Same flower
  2. Different species always
  3. Different plant only
  4. Root system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Same flower

Pollination occurs between anther and stigma of the same flower.

8. Geitonogamy occurs between different flowers on the:

  1. Same plant
  2. Different species
  3. Different genera always
  4. Different populations only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Same plant

It involves a pollinating transfer between flowers but is genetically similar to self-pollination.

9. Xenogamy involves pollen transfer between:

  1. Different plants of the same species
  2. Only parts of one flower
  3. Only one plant’s roots
  4. Unrelated kingdoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Different plants of the same species

Cross-pollination between different plants can introduce genetically different pollen.

10. The seed coat generally develops from the ovule’s:

  1. Integuments
  2. Embryo
  3. Endosperm
  4. Pollen tube
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Integuments

The integuments form the protective seed-coat layers as the ovule matures into a seed.

11. A typical anther commonly contains how many microsporangia?

  1. 8
  2. 4
  3. 1
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 4

The standard bilobed anther has four pollen-sac compartments.

12. The nutritive layer surrounding developing pollen is the:

  1. Tapetum
  2. Epidermis alone
  3. Endodermis
  4. Pericycle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Tapetum

Tapetal cells supply materials supporting pollen development.

13. Microspore mother cells produce microspores through:

  1. Only mitosis
  2. Only budding
  3. Only fertilisation
  4. Meiosis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Meiosis

Reduction division generates haploid spores.

14. A typical microspore tetrad contains:

  1. Eight triploid cells
  2. One tetraploid cell
  3. Four haploid cells
  4. Two diploid cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Four haploid cells

One meiotic mother cell commonly yields four microspores.

15. The resistant outer pollen wall is called the:

  1. Endocarp
  2. Exine
  3. Intine
  4. Tonoplast
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Exine

Exine contains the durable polymer sporopollenin.

16. The inner pollen wall is called the:

  1. Intine
  2. Exine
  3. Testa
  4. Pericarp
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intine

The inner wall commonly contains cellulose and pectin.

17. A germ pore is a region where pollen exine has reduced or absent:

  1. Only DNA
  2. Only ribosomes
  3. Only water
  4. Sporopollenin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sporopollenin

This region permits pollen-tube emergence.

18. The pollen vegetative cell mainly supports:

  1. Only seed-coat formation
  2. Only fruit-wall division
  3. Pollen-tube development
  4. Only endosperm meiosis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Pollen-tube development

Its cytoplasm provides the growing tube's functional machinery.

19. The pollen generative cell commonly gives rise to:

  1. Eight synergids
  2. Two male gametes
  3. Four eggs
  4. One endosperm nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two male gametes

Mitosis produces the two sperm cells used in double fertilisation.

20. The ovule is attached to the placenta by a:

  1. Funicle
  2. Filament
  3. Style
  4. Petiole
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Funicle

The funicle is the stalk linking ovule and placenta.

21. The small opening in ovule integuments is the:

  1. Chalaza
  2. Hilum
  3. Nucellar ridge
  4. Micropyle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Micropyle

It can serve as the pollen-tube entry route.

22. The ovule region opposite the micropyle is the:

  1. Filament
  2. Petiole
  3. Chalaza
  4. Stigma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Chalaza

The chalazal end joins the supporting ovule tissues.

23. The tissue surrounding the embryo sac inside an ovule is mainly the:

  1. Tapetum
  2. Nucellus
  3. Exine
  4. Endocarp
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nucellus

The megasporangial tissue surrounds the female gametophyte.

24. A typical Polygonum-type mature embryo sac has:

  1. Seven cells and eight nuclei
  2. Eight cells and seven nuclei
  3. Four cells and four nuclei
  4. Two cells and two nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Seven cells and eight nuclei

The central cell contains two polar nuclei before fusion.

25. At the micropylar end, the egg apparatus includes an egg and:

  1. Three antipodals
  2. Two pollen grains
  3. Four megaspores
  4. Two synergids
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two synergids

Synergids flank and assist the egg region.

26. The filiform apparatus is associated with:

  1. Pollen exine
  2. Embryonic cotyledons
  3. Synergids
  4. Antipodals only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Synergids

It helps guide and support pollen-tube entry.

27. The three cells at the chalazal end are typically:

  1. Cotyledons
  2. Antipodals
  3. Synergids
  4. Male gametes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Antipodals

They occupy the opposite end from the egg apparatus.

28. Pollen transfer within the same flower is:

  1. Autogamy
  2. Xenogamy
  3. Geitonogamy exclusively
  4. Apomixis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Autogamy

Self-pollination occurs between that flower's anther and stigma.

29. Pollen transfer between flowers on the same plant is:

  1. Xenogamy
  2. Autogamy in the same flower
  3. Parthenocarpy
  4. Geitonogamy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Geitonogamy

The transfer resembles crossing functionally but remains genetically selfing.

30. Fusion of a male gamete with the two polar nuclei is commonly termed:

  1. Meiosis
  2. Budding
  3. Triple fusion
  4. Syngamy alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Triple fusion

Three haploid nuclear contributions ordinarily form a triploid primary endosperm nucleus.
