Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry

Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry

Key concepts

  • Carbon forms four bonds in many organic compounds. Common bonding descriptions include sp³, sp² and sp hybridisation.
  • A nucleophile donates an electron pair; an electrophile accepts an electron pair. Bond cleavage can be homolytic or heterolytic.
  • Inductive effects act through sigma bonds; resonance describes delocalisation represented by contributing structures.

Worked connection

In methane, carbon is described as sp³ with tetrahedral geometry; in ethene each carbon is sp²; in ethyne each is sp.

Watch out

Resonance contributors are not separate molecules rapidly interconverting. The actual structure is delocalised.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. An electrophile is an electron-pair:

Acceptor

Electrophiles accept an electron pair during bond formation; they may be charged or neutral.

2. Carbon hybridisation in ethene is commonly described as:

sp²

Each carbon has three sigma-bond directions and a remaining p orbital for the pi bond.

3. Carbon hybridisation in ethyne is commonly described as:

sp

Two sigma directions give linear sp geometry; two remaining p orbitals support the two pi bonds.

4. Heterolytic bond cleavage assigns both bonding electrons to:

One fragment

One fragment gains the pair, commonly creating an anion and a cation.

5. The inductive effect is transmitted mainly through:

Sigma bonds

Bond polarisation is transmitted through the sigma framework and typically weakens with distance.

6. Resonance contributors describe:

One delocalised structure

Contributors are representations; the actual electronic structure is the resonance hybrid.

7. Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different:

Connectivity

Changing atom connectivity creates constitutional or structural isomers.
