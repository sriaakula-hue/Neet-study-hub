p-Block Elements: Trends

p-Block Elements: Trends

Key concepts

  • The p block spans groups 13–18. A typical p-block valence configuration is ns²np¹ to ns²np⁶; helium is an exception with 1s².
  • The first element of a group can differ from heavier members because of its small size and electronic structure.
  • The inert-pair effect can favour lower oxidation states in heavier p-block elements; metallic character generally increases down a group.

Worked connection

For heavier group-13 elements, the +1 oxidation state can become relatively more stable than it is for lighter members.

Watch out

Group trends do not make all compounds equivalent. Distinguish general periodic patterns from specific compound chemistry.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A typical group-13 valence configuration is:

ns²np¹

Group 13 commonly has three valence electrons with one in the p subshell.

2. A typical group-16 valence configuration is:

ns²np⁴

The outer configuration has six valence electrons, including four p electrons.

3. A common reason the first element of a p-block group differs from heavier members is its:

Small size and distinctive electronic structure

Small size, high electronegativity and orbital differences affect bonding patterns.

4. Down a p-block group, metallic character generally:

Increases

Larger atoms usually lose electrons more readily, though detailed trends and exceptions must be considered.

5. The usual valence configuration of group 18 excluding helium is:

ns²np⁶

A filled valence s and p shell contributes to relative chemical inertness.

6. In heavier group-14 elements, the inert-pair effect can favour:

+2 relative to +4

Reduced involvement of the ns² pair makes the lower +2 state relatively more stable in heavier members.

7. Boron is usually classified as a:

Metalloid

Its properties lie between conventional metallic and nonmetallic categories.

  1. Valence ns² electrons
  2. All neutrons
  3. Only core protons
  4. Every p electron

Answer: A. Valence ns² electrons

The valence s-electron pair can remain less involved in bonding.
