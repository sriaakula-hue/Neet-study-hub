p-Block Elements: Trends

p-Block Elements: Trends

Key concepts

  • The p block spans groups 13–18. A typical p-block valence configuration is ns²np¹ to ns²np⁶; helium is an exception with 1s².
  • The first element of a group can differ from heavier members because of its small size and electronic structure.
  • The inert-pair effect can favour lower oxidation states in heavier p-block elements; metallic character generally increases down a group.

Worked connection

For heavier group-13 elements, the +1 oxidation state can become relatively more stable than it is for lighter members.

Watch out

Group trends do not make all compounds equivalent. Distinguish general periodic patterns from specific compound chemistry.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

1. A typical group-13 valence configuration is:

ns²np¹

Group 13 commonly has three valence electrons with one in the p subshell.

2. A typical group-16 valence configuration is:

ns²np⁴

The outer configuration has six valence electrons, including four p electrons.

Small size and distinctive electronic structure

Increases

5. The usual valence configuration of group 18 excluding helium is:

ns²np⁶

6. In heavier group-14 elements, the inert-pair effect can favour:

+2 relative to +4

Reduced involvement of the ns² pair makes the lower +2 state relatively more stable in heavier members.

7. Boron is usually classified as a:

Metalloid

Its properties lie between conventional metallic and nonmetallic categories.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A typical group-17 valence configuration is:

  1. ns²np¹
  2. ns²np³
  3. ns²np⁵
  4. ns²np⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. ns²np⁵

Halogens normally have seven valence electrons.

2. The inert-pair effect concerns the reduced participation of:

  1. Valence ns² electrons
  2. All neutrons
  3. Only core protons
  4. Every p electron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Valence ns² electrons

The valence s-electron pair can remain less involved in bonding.

3. Helium has the electron configuration:

  1. 1s²
  2. 1s²2s²
  3. 2p⁶
  4. 1s¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1s²

Helium has two electrons in its 1s orbital.

4. A typical group-13 valence configuration is:

  1. ns²np¹
  2. ns²np²
  3. ns²np⁵
  4. ns²np⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ns²np¹

Group 13 commonly has three valence electrons with one in the p subshell.

5. A typical group-16 valence configuration is:

  1. ns²np⁴
  2. ns²np¹
  3. ns²np²
  4. ns²np⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ns²np⁴

The outer configuration has six valence electrons, including four p electrons.

6. A common reason the first element of a p-block group differs from heavier members is its:

  1. Small size and distinctive electronic structure
  2. Lack of a nucleus
  3. Lack of valence electrons
  4. Identical atomic number to all members
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Small size and distinctive electronic structure

Small size, high electronegativity and orbital differences affect bonding patterns.

7. Down a p-block group, metallic character generally:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases always
  3. Stays exactly identical
  4. Is impossible
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increases

Larger atoms usually lose electrons more readily, though detailed trends and exceptions must be considered.

8. The usual valence configuration of group 18 excluding helium is:

  1. ns²np⁶
  2. ns¹
  3. ns²np³
  4. ns²np⁵
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ns²np⁶

A filled valence s and p shell contributes to relative chemical inertness.

9. In heavier group-14 elements, the inert-pair effect can favour:

  1. +2 relative to +4
  2. −4 only
  3. +8 always
  4. No oxidation state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +2 relative to +4

Reduced involvement of the ns² pair makes the lower +2 state relatively more stable in heavier members.

10. Boron is usually classified as a:

  1. Metalloid
  2. Noble gas
  3. Alkali metal
  4. Halogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Metalloid

Its properties lie between conventional metallic and nonmetallic categories.

11. The differentiating electron of a p-block element enters a:

  1. p subshell
  2. d subshell
  3. f subshell
  4. Nuclear shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. p subshell

The p-block classification is based on filling a valence p subshell.

12. Group 13 elements commonly have valence configuration:

  1. ns²np²
  2. ns²np⁵
  3. ns¹
  4. ns²np¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. ns²np¹

Three valence electrons give the typical group-13 configuration.

13. Group 14 elements commonly have valence configuration:

  1. ns²np⁴
  2. ns²np⁶
  3. ns²np²
  4. ns²np¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. ns²np²

Group 14 has four valence electrons in the elementary outer-shell pattern.

14. Group 15 elements commonly have valence configuration:

  1. ns²np⁶
  2. ns²np³
  3. ns²np²
  4. ns²np⁴
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. ns²np³

Five valence electrons correspond to two s and three p electrons.

15. Group 16 elements commonly have valence configuration:

  1. ns²np⁴
  2. ns²np³
  3. ns²np⁵
  4. ns¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ns²np⁴

The chalcogen family commonly has six outer-shell electrons.

16. Helium differs from the usual noble-gas valence pattern because it has:

  1. 2s²2p⁶
  2. 1s¹
  3. 2p⁶
  4. 1s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1s²

The first shell contains only an s subshell and is complete with two electrons.

17. The inert-pair effect refers to reduced participation of:

  1. Inner neutrons
  2. The entire nucleus
  3. The outer ns² pair in bonding
  4. All p electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The outer ns² pair in bonding

In heavier p-block elements, the s pair may remain nonbonding, favouring lower oxidation states.

18. Which oxidation state becomes relatively more stable for lead compared with carbon?

  1. +6
  2. +2
  3. +7
  4. −5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. +2

The inert-pair effect favours Pb(II) relative to Pb(IV).

19. For thallium, the inert-pair effect favours the stability of:

  1. +1 over +3
  2. +5 over +1
  3. −3 over +1
  4. +7 over +3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +1 over +3

Its ns² pair is less available for bonding, supporting the lower oxidation state.

20. Strong catenation in carbon is associated with:

  1. An empty first shell
  2. Very weak covalent bonds
  3. Absence of orbital overlap
  4. Strong C–C bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Strong C–C bonds

Stable bonds between carbon atoms allow chains and rings.

21. Among these halogens, the strongest elemental oxidising agent under usual aqueous conditions is:

  1. Br₂
  2. I₂
  3. F₂
  4. Cl₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. F₂

Fluorine has the greatest standard tendency to be reduced among these halogens.

22. Down the halogen group, electronegativity generally:

  1. Becomes negative
  2. Decreases
  3. Increases without exception
  4. Stays identical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Decreases

Greater size and shielding reduce attraction for shared electrons.

23. Chlorine's electron-gain enthalpy is more negative than fluorine's mainly because:

  1. Fluorine's compact 2p shell has greater electron repulsion
  2. Chlorine has fewer protons
  3. Fluorine is metallic
  4. Chlorine has no p orbitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fluorine's compact 2p shell has greater electron repulsion

Adding an electron to compact fluorine experiences stronger repulsion than in chlorine's larger shell.

24. Hydrogen bonding makes water's boiling point unusually high compared with:

  1. NaCl
  2. MgO
  3. Diamond
  4. H₂S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. H₂S

Extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding raises water's boiling point relative to similar molecular hydrides.

25. A characteristic oxidation state available to oxygen in most oxides is:

  1. −4
  2. +4
  3. −2
  4. +7
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. −2

Oxygen commonly gains or shares electrons corresponding to oxidation state −2, with important exceptions.

26. The oxidation state of oxygen in H₂O₂ is:

  1. +2
  2. −1
  3. −2
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. −1

Peroxides contain an O–O bond and assign −1 to each oxygen.

27. In OF₂, oxygen has oxidation state:

  1. +2
  2. −2
  3. −1
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +2

Fluorine is −1, so neutral OF₂ requires oxygen to be +2.

28. Which first-row p-block element cannot expand its usual valence-shell octet?

  1. Phosphorus
  2. Sulfur
  3. Chlorine
  4. Nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nitrogen

Second-period atoms are represented by an octet-limited valence shell in elementary Lewis structures.

29. Across a period, many p-block oxides tend to become more:

  1. Basic without exception
  2. Isotopically identical
  3. Acidic
  4. Metallic without exception
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Acidic

Increasing nonmetallic character commonly corresponds to more acidic oxides.

30. Which is a familiar amphoteric oxide?

  1. SO₃
  2. Al₂O₃
  3. Na₂O
  4. CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Al₂O₃

Aluminium oxide can react with both acids and bases.
