Current Electricity

Current and resistance

Current is the rate of flow of charge: I = Q/t for a steady current. A potential difference can drive current through a conductor.

V = IR   ·   P = VI

Ohm’s law applies to an ohmic conductor with its physical conditions, including temperature, constant. Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).

Worked example

A 6 Ω resistor across 12 V carries 2 A. It dissipates 24 W.

Connections

Resistances add in series. In parallel, reciprocals add: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A charge of 18 C passes in 6 s. Average current is:

3 A

Current is charge per time: I = Q/t = 18/6 = 3 A.

2. A 4 Ω resistor carries 3 A. Voltage is:

12 V

For an ohmic resistor, V = IR = 3 × 4 = 12 V.

3. Two 6 Ω resistors in parallel have equivalent resistance:

3 Ω

For equal parallel resistors, equivalent resistance is half of either resistor: 6/2 = 3 Ω.

4. Kirchhoff’s junction rule expresses conservation of:

Charge

No steady charge accumulation at a junction means total entering current equals total leaving current.

5. Kirchhoff’s loop rule uses the algebraic sum of potential changes around a closed loop equal to:

Zero

A complete loop returns to the same potential; the rule expresses energy balance with consistent signs.

6. A 2 Ω resistor carries 4 A. Dissipated power is:

32 W

P = I²R = 4² × 2 = 32 W.

7. If an ohmic wire’s length doubles at fixed cross-section and temperature, resistance:

Doubles

R = ρL/A, so doubling L at fixed ρ and A doubles resistance.

1. A 6 Ω resistor is connected across 12 V. The current is:

  1. 0.5 A
  2. 2 A
  3. 6 A
  4. 72 A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 A

Ohm’s law gives I = V/R = 12/6 = 2 A.

2. Two resistors, 2 Ω and 3 Ω, are connected in series. Their equivalent resistance is:

  1. 1.2 Ω
  2. 1 Ω
  3. 6 Ω
  4. 5 Ω
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5 Ω

In series, resistances add: R = 2 + 3 = 5 Ω.

  1. 6 W
  2. 14 W
