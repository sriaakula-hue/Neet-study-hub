Motion in a Straight Line

Motion in a straight line

Displacement is the change in position; distance is the total path length. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Average speed is total distance divided by elapsed time.

v = u + at   ·   s = ut + ½at²

These equations apply when acceleration is constant. Here u is initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration, t time and s displacement.

Worked example

A body starts from rest with constant acceleration 2 m/s² for 3 s. Its final velocity is 6 m/s and displacement is 9 m.

Watch out

A round trip can have zero displacement and nonzero distance. Average velocity is then zero.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A body moves at constant velocity 5 m/s for 8 s. Its displacement is:

40 m

For constant velocity, displacement is vt = 5 × 8 = 40 m. No acceleration term is required.

2. Velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration is:

2 m/s²

Acceleration is change in velocity divided by time: (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s².

3. A body slows uniformly from 12 m/s to rest in 3 s. Its displacement is:

18 m

With constant acceleration, average velocity is (u + v)/2 = 6 m/s; displacement is 6 × 3 = 18 m.

4. The area under a velocity–time graph represents:

Displacement

Integrating velocity over time gives displacement. Negative velocity regions contribute negative signed area.

5. A position–time graph with constant slope describes:

Constant velocity

The slope is velocity. A constant slope therefore means constant velocity, including its sign.

6. A walker travels 30 m east and then 10 m west. Distance and displacement magnitude are:

40 m and 20 m

Distance adds path lengths, 30 + 10 = 40 m. Net displacement is 30 − 10 = 20 m east.

7. Which quantity can be negative in one-dimensional motion?

Signed velocity

Velocity is signed relative to the chosen positive direction. Distance, positive elapsed time and mass are nonnegative.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A body starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 3 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 3 m
  2. 6 m
  3. 9 m
  4. 18 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 9 m

s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 m.

2. A student returns to the starting point after a walk. Their average velocity for the trip is:

  1. Always equal to speed
  2. Zero
  3. Equal to distance/time
  4. Impossible to determine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Zero

Net displacement is zero. Average velocity = displacement/time = 0.

3. The slope of a velocity–time graph represents:

  1. Displacement
  2. Acceleration
  3. Mass
  4. Force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Acceleration

The slope is change in velocity divided by change in time.

4. A body moves at constant velocity 5 m/s for 8 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 40 m
  2. 13 m
  3. 1.6 m
  4. 80 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 40 m

For constant velocity, displacement is vt = 5 × 8 = 40 m. No acceleration term is required.

5. Velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration is:

  1. 2 m/s²
  2. 3 m/s²
  3. 8 m/s²
  4. 12 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 m/s²

Acceleration is change in velocity divided by time: (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s².

6. A body slows uniformly from 12 m/s to rest in 3 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 18 m
  2. 36 m
  3. 4 m
  4. 9 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 18 m

With constant acceleration, average velocity is (u + v)/2 = 6 m/s; displacement is 6 × 3 = 18 m.

7. The area under a velocity–time graph represents:

  1. Displacement
  2. Acceleration
  3. Mass
  4. Power
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Displacement

Integrating velocity over time gives displacement. Negative velocity regions contribute negative signed area.

8. A position–time graph with constant slope describes:

  1. Constant velocity
  2. Constant nonzero acceleration
  3. Zero displacement always
  4. Changing mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Constant velocity

The slope is velocity. A constant slope therefore means constant velocity, including its sign.

9. A walker travels 30 m east and then 10 m west. Distance and displacement magnitude are:

  1. 40 m and 20 m
  2. 20 m and 40 m
  3. 40 m and 40 m
  4. 20 m and 20 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 40 m and 20 m

Distance adds path lengths, 30 + 10 = 40 m. Net displacement is 30 − 10 = 20 m east.

10. Which quantity can be negative in one-dimensional motion?

  1. Signed velocity
  2. Distance travelled
  3. Elapsed time
  4. Mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Signed velocity

Velocity is signed relative to the chosen positive direction. Distance, positive elapsed time and mass are nonnegative.

11. A car covers equal distances at 20 m/s and 30 m/s. Its average speed is:

  1. 25 m/s
  2. 10 m/s
  3. 50 m/s
  4. 24 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 24 m/s

For equal distances, average speed is 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂)=1200/50=24 m/s.

12. A car travels for equal time intervals at 20 m/s and 30 m/s. Its average speed is:

  1. 60 m/s
  2. 12 m/s
  3. 25 m/s
  4. 24 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 25 m/s

Equal time intervals give average speed (20+30)/2=25 m/s.

13. A particle has position x=3t²+2t in metres, with t in seconds. Its velocity at t=2 s is:

  1. 8 m/s
  2. 14 m/s
  3. 16 m/s
  4. 12 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 14 m/s

Velocity is dx/dt=6t+2; at 2 s it is 14 m/s.

14. For x=4t³ metres with t in seconds, acceleration at t=1 s is:

  1. 24 m/s²
  2. 12 m/s²
  3. 4 m/s²
  4. 8 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 24 m/s²

Differentiate twice: v=12t² and a=24t; at 1 s, a=24 m/s².

15. A particle starts with velocity 4 m/s and acceleration −2 m/s². When does it first come to rest?

  1. 4 s
  2. 1 s
  3. 8 s
  4. 2 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2 s

Set v=u+at=4−2t=0, giving t=2 s.

16. A body starts from rest with constant acceleration 3 m/s². Distance covered during the fourth second is:

  1. 13.5 m
  2. 6 m
  3. 10.5 m
  4. 24 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10.5 m

s(4)−s(3)=½×3×(16−9)=10.5 m.

17. A stone is dropped from rest through 45 m. Neglect air resistance and use g=10 m/s². Fall time is:

  1. 1.5 s
  2. 3 s
  3. 4.5 s
  4. 9 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3 s

h=½gt², so t=√(2×45/10)=3 s.

18. A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m/s. With g=10 m/s² and no drag, maximum height above launch is:

  1. 20 m
  2. 40 m
  3. 10 m
  4. 200 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20 m

At the highest point v=0. Height is u²/(2g)=400/20=20 m.

19. A ball thrown vertically upwards at 30 m/s returns to its launch height. Use g=10 m/s² and neglect drag. Total time is:

  1. 3 s
  2. 9 s
  3. 1.5 s
  4. 6 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 6 s

Time of flight back to the same height is 2u/g=60/10=6 s.

20. Two vehicles move along the same straight road in the same direction at 18 m/s and 12 m/s. Relative speed is:

  1. 15 m/s
  2. 216 m/s
  3. 6 m/s
  4. 30 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6 m/s

For motion in the same direction, relative speed is the magnitude of the difference: 18−12=6 m/s.

21. Two cyclists approach one another along a straight road at 5 m/s and 7 m/s. Their separation decreases at:

  1. 6 m/s
  2. 12 m/s
  3. 2 m/s
  4. 35 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 12 m/s

Approaching speeds add, giving 5+7=12 m/s.

22. A 120 m train passes a stationary pole in 8 s at constant speed. Train speed is:

  1. 15 m/s
  2. 8 m/s
  3. 960 m/s
  4. 120 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 15 m/s

The train moves one train length: speed=120/8=15 m/s.

23. A 100 m train passes completely over a 200 m bridge at 20 m/s. Time taken is:

  1. 10 s
  2. 5 s
  3. 20 s
  4. 15 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 15 s

The front must travel train length plus bridge length: (100+200)/20=15 s.

24. A velocity–time graph is a horizontal line at −3 m/s for 4 s. Displacement during that interval is:

  1. −3 m
  2. 0 m
  3. −12 m
  4. 12 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. −12 m

Signed area under the graph is (−3)×4=−12 m.

25. A particle moves at +2 m/s for 3 s, then −2 m/s for 3 s. Its average speed over 6 s is:

  1. 1 m/s
  2. 2 m/s
  3. 0 m/s
  4. 4 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 m/s

Total distance is 6+6=12 m, so average speed is 12/6=2 m/s despite zero displacement.

26. A body moving at 10 m/s brakes with constant acceleration −5 m/s². Stopping distance is:

  1. 10 m
  2. 20 m
  3. 5 m
  4. 50 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 10 m

Using v²=u²+2as with v=0 gives 0=100−10s, so s=10 m.

27. For constant acceleration, doubling the initial speed while keeping the same braking acceleration magnitude makes stopping distance:

  1. Twice as large
  2. Half as large
  3. Unchanged
  4. Four times as large
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Four times as large

Stopping distance is u²/(2|a|), so doubling u multiplies distance by four.

28. At the highest point of a vertical throw, neglecting drag, which statement is correct?

  1. Acceleration is upward
  2. Gravity disappears
  3. Velocity is zero but acceleration is downward
  4. Velocity and acceleration are both zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Velocity is zero but acceleration is downward

Instantaneous velocity vanishes at the turning point, but gravity still causes downward acceleration g.

29. A position–time curve becomes progressively steeper in the positive direction. This indicates:

  1. Negative displacement necessarily
  2. Increasing positive velocity
  3. Constant positive velocity
  4. Zero acceleration necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Increasing positive velocity

The slope dx/dt is velocity. Increasing positive slope means positive velocity is increasing.

30. For one-dimensional motion, a negative acceleration necessarily means:

  1. Acceleration points in the negative chosen direction
  2. The body is slowing down
  3. Velocity is negative
  4. Distance travelled is negative
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acceleration points in the negative chosen direction

The sign gives direction relative to the chosen axis. A body with negative velocity and negative acceleration speeds up.
