Motion in a Straight Line

Motion in a straight line

Displacement is the change in position; distance is the total path length. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Average speed is total distance divided by elapsed time.

v = u + at   ·   s = ut + ½at²

These equations apply when acceleration is constant. Here u is initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration, t time and s displacement.

Worked example

A body starts from rest with constant acceleration 2 m/s² for 3 s. Its final velocity is 6 m/s and displacement is 9 m.

Watch out

A round trip can have zero displacement and nonzero distance. Average velocity is then zero.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A body moves at constant velocity 5 m/s for 8 s. Its displacement is:

40 m

For constant velocity, displacement is vt = 5 × 8 = 40 m. No acceleration term is required.

2. Velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration is:

2 m/s²

Acceleration is change in velocity divided by time: (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s².

3. A body slows uniformly from 12 m/s to rest in 3 s. Its displacement is:

18 m

With constant acceleration, average velocity is (u + v)/2 = 6 m/s; displacement is 6 × 3 = 18 m.

4. The area under a velocity–time graph represents:

Displacement

Integrating velocity over time gives displacement. Negative velocity regions contribute negative signed area.

5. A position–time graph with constant slope describes:

Constant velocity

The slope is velocity. A constant slope therefore means constant velocity, including its sign.

6. A walker travels 30 m east and then 10 m west. Distance and displacement magnitude are:

40 m and 20 m

Distance adds path lengths, 30 + 10 = 40 m. Net displacement is 30 − 10 = 20 m east.

7. Which quantity can be negative in one-dimensional motion?

Signed velocity

Velocity is signed relative to the chosen positive direction. Distance, positive elapsed time and mass are nonnegative.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A body starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 3 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 3 m
  2. 6 m
  3. 9 m
  4. 18 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 9 m

s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 m.

2. A student returns to the starting point after a walk. Their average velocity for the trip is:

  1. Always equal to speed
  2. Zero
  3. Equal to distance/time
  4. Impossible to determine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Zero

Net displacement is zero. Average velocity = displacement/time = 0.

3. The slope of a velocity–time graph represents:

  1. Displacement
  2. Acceleration
  3. Mass
  4. Force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Acceleration

The slope is change in velocity divided by change in time.

4. A body moves at constant velocity 5 m/s for 8 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 40 m
  2. 13 m
  3. 1.6 m
  4. 80 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 40 m

For constant velocity, displacement is vt = 5 × 8 = 40 m. No acceleration term is required.

5. Velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration is:

  1. 2 m/s²
  2. 3 m/s²
  3. 8 m/s²
  4. 12 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 m/s²

Acceleration is change in velocity divided by time: (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s².

6. A body slows uniformly from 12 m/s to rest in 3 s. Its displacement is:

  1. 18 m
  2. 36 m
  3. 4 m
  4. 9 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 18 m

With constant acceleration, average velocity is (u + v)/2 = 6 m/s; displacement is 6 × 3 = 18 m.

7. The area under a velocity–time graph represents:

  1. Displacement
  2. Acceleration
  3. Mass
  4. Power
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Displacement

Integrating velocity over time gives displacement. Negative velocity regions contribute negative signed area.

8. A position–time graph with constant slope describes:

  1. Constant velocity
  2. Constant nonzero acceleration
  3. Zero displacement always
  4. Changing mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Constant velocity

The slope is velocity. A constant slope therefore means constant velocity, including its sign.

9. A walker travels 30 m east and then 10 m west. Distance and displacement magnitude are:

  1. 40 m and 20 m
  2. 20 m and 40 m
  3. 40 m and 40 m
  4. 20 m and 20 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 40 m and 20 m

Distance adds path lengths, 30 + 10 = 40 m. Net displacement is 30 − 10 = 20 m east.

10. Which quantity can be negative in one-dimensional motion?

  1. Signed velocity
  2. Distance travelled
  3. Elapsed time
  4. Mass

Answer: A. Signed velocity

Velocity is signed relative to the chosen positive direction. Distance, positive elapsed time and mass are nonnegative.
