Electrochemistry foundations

Electrochemistry foundations

Core idea

Oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode. In a galvanic cell, a spontaneous redox reaction supplies electrical energy. Electrons flow through the external circuit from anode to cathode.

Worked connection

Using standard reduction potentials, E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. Values +0.34 V and −0.76 V yield E°cell = +1.10 V.

Watch out

The anode is negative in a galvanic cell but positive in an electrolytic cell. Define electrodes by reaction, not by a universal sign.

Redox connection

Oxidation is electron loss and reduction is electron gain. An oxidising agent is reduced; a reducing agent is oxidised. Oxidation numbers help track electron transfer but are formal bookkeeping quantities.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. An oxidising agent undergoes:

Reduction

It accepts electrons from another species and is therefore reduced.

2. A reducing agent undergoes:

Oxidation

It donates electrons to another species and is therefore oxidised.

3. For a galvanic cell, ΔG is related to cell emf by:

ΔG = −nFE

Positive galvanic emf gives negative Gibbs energy change for the spontaneous cell reaction.

4. A salt bridge chiefly helps maintain:

Electrical neutrality by ion migration

Ions migrate to offset charge accumulation, while electrons pass through the external metallic circuit.

5. In an electrolytic cell, the anode is normally:

Positive

The power source removes electrons from the positive anode, where oxidation occurs; electrode signs differ from a galvanic cell.

6. Using F ≈ 96,500 C/mol, transferring 1 mol of electrons carries charge:

96,500 C

Faraday’s constant is the charge per mole of electrons.

7. The Nernst equation describes the dependence of emf on:

Reaction quotient and temperature

Concentration or activity changes enter through the reaction quotient; emf need not equal its standard value.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Oxidation occurs at the:

  1. Cathode
  2. Anode
  3. Salt bridge only
  4. Voltmeter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Anode

The anode is defined as the electrode where oxidation occurs.

2. In a galvanic cell, external electrons flow from:

  1. Cathode to anode
  2. Anode to cathode
  3. Salt bridge to cathode
  4. Solution to voltmeter only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Anode to cathode

Oxidation releases electrons at the anode; reduction consumes them at the cathode.

3. Standard reduction potentials are +0.34 V (cathode) and −0.76 V (anode). E°cell is:

  1. −1.10 V
  2. −0.42 V
  3. +0.42 V
  4. +1.10 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +1.10 V

E°cell = 0.34 − (−0.76) = +1.10 V.

4. An oxidising agent undergoes:

  1. Reduction
  2. Oxidation
  3. No electron change ever
  4. Only evaporation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reduction

It accepts electrons from another species and is therefore reduced.

5. A reducing agent undergoes:

  1. Oxidation
  2. Reduction
  3. No electron change ever
  4. Only sublimation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Oxidation

It donates electrons to another species and is therefore oxidised.

6. For a galvanic cell, ΔG is related to cell emf by:

  1. ΔG = −nFE
  2. ΔG = +nFE always
  3. ΔG = E/nF
  4. ΔG = nF/E
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ΔG = −nFE

Positive galvanic emf gives negative Gibbs energy change for the spontaneous cell reaction.

7. A salt bridge chiefly helps maintain:

  1. Electrical neutrality by ion migration
  2. Electron flow through the salt directly
  3. A constant zero voltage
  4. A vacuum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electrical neutrality by ion migration

Ions migrate to offset charge accumulation, while electrons pass through the external metallic circuit.

8. In an electrolytic cell, the anode is normally:

  1. Positive
  2. Negative
  3. Uncharged always
  4. The site of reduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Positive

The power source removes electrons from the positive anode, where oxidation occurs; electrode signs differ from a galvanic cell.

9. Using F ≈ 96,500 C/mol, transferring 1 mol of electrons carries charge:

  1. 96,500 C
  2. 965 C
  3. 9,650 C
  4. 193,000 C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 96,500 C

Faraday’s constant is the charge per mole of electrons.

10. The Nernst equation describes the dependence of emf on:

  1. Reaction quotient and temperature
  2. Only electrode colour
  3. Only container shape
  4. Only atmospheric pressure in every cell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reaction quotient and temperature

Concentration or activity changes enter through the reaction quotient; emf need not equal its standard value.

11. A resistor of 20 Ω has conductance of what magnitude?

  1. 0.05 S
  2. 0.025 S
  3. 0.075 S
  4. 0.1 S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.05 S

Conductance is the reciprocal of resistance. Answer: 0.05 S.

12. A cell has cathode reduction potential +0.2 V and anode reduction potential −0.2 V. Standard emf is:

  1. 0.2 V
  2. 0.6 V
  3. 0.8 V
  4. 0.4 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.4 V

E°_cell=E°_cathode−E°_anode; both electrode values are reduction potentials. Answer: 0.4 V.

13. Electrolysis passes 19300 C through Ag⁺ solution. Use F=96500 C/mol and M_Ag=108 g/mol, with 100% efficiency. Deposited silver mass is:

  1. 32.4 g
  2. 43.2 g
  3. 21.6 g
  4. 10.8 g
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 21.6 g

For Ag⁺+e⁻→Ag, one electron per ion gives m=MQ/F. Answer: 21.6 g.

14. An electrochemical reaction transfers 2 mol electrons per mole of reaction and has E°=0.2 V. Use F=96500 C/mol. Magnitude of ΔG° is:

  1. 77200 J/mol
  2. 38600 J/mol
  3. 19300 J/mol
  4. 57900 J/mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 38600 J/mol

ΔG°=−nFE°. A positive emf gives negative free-energy change; the question asks its magnitude. Answer: 38600 J/mol.

15. A cell at 298 K has n=1 and Q=10^2. Use 0.059 V for the Nernst base-10 factor. E°−E is:

  1. 0.118 V
  2. 0.059 V
  3. 0.177 V
  4. 0.236 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.118 V

Nernst: E=E°−(0.059/n)log₁₀Q, with dimensionless Q. Answer: 0.118 V.
