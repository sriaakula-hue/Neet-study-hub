Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

Chemical bonding basics

An ionic bond is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. A covalent bond involves shared electron pairs.

Examples

Sodium chloride forms an ionic crystal lattice. The H₂ molecule contains a covalent bond formed by a shared pair of electrons.

Polarity

Electronegativity differences influence bond polarity. Molecular polarity also depends on shape: CO₂ has polar bonds but is a nonpolar molecule because its bond dipoles cancel in its linear geometry.

Watch out

“Polar bonds” and “polar molecule” are different descriptions.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The molecular shape of NH₃ is:

Trigonal pyramidal

Three bonding pairs and one lone pair around nitrogen produce a trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape.

2. The molecular shape of H₂O is:

Bent

Oxygen has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs; the molecular geometry is bent.

3. A sigma bond commonly arises from orbital overlap:

Along the internuclear axis

Sigma overlap has cylindrical symmetry around the bond axis; pi overlap is lateral.

4. A carbon–carbon double bond contains:

One sigma and one pi bond

The first overlap gives a sigma bond and the additional lateral overlap gives a pi bond.

5. Using molecular orbital theory, O₂ is paramagnetic because it has:

Unpaired electrons

Two electrons occupy separate degenerate antibonding pi orbitals in the ground-state description.

6. Molecular-orbital bond order is calculated as:

(Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2

Electrons in bonding orbitals stabilise while antibonding electrons oppose bonding; each pair contributes one net bond.

7. Strong conventional hydrogen bonding requires H bonded to a strongly electronegative atom such as:

N, O or F

A strongly polar N–H, O–H or F–H bond can interact with a suitable lone-pair acceptor.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Which best describes a covalent bond?

  1. Shared electron pair(s)
  2. Attraction between nuclei only
  3. Transfer of neutrons
  4. A gravitational interaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shared electron pair(s)

A covalent bond involves electron pairs shared between bonded atoms.

2. Why is CO₂ a nonpolar molecule despite having polar bonds?

  1. It has no electrons
  2. Its linear geometry makes the bond dipoles cancel
  3. Carbon is a metal
  4. All covalent bonds are nonpolar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Its linear geometry makes the bond dipoles cancel

The two C–O bond dipoles are equal and opposite in linear CO₂, giving zero net dipole.

3. An ionic bond is primarily:

  1. Electron-pair sharing
  2. Electrostatic attraction between opposite ions
  3. Neutron sharing
  4. Gravity between atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Electrostatic attraction between opposite ions

Oppositely charged ions attract electrostatically.

4. The molecular shape of NH₃ is:

  1. Trigonal pyramidal
  2. Trigonal planar
  3. Linear
  4. Tetrahedral with no lone pair
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Trigonal pyramidal

Three bonding pairs and one lone pair around nitrogen produce a trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape.

5. The molecular shape of H₂O is:

  1. Bent
  2. Linear
  3. Trigonal planar
  4. Square planar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bent

Oxygen has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs; the molecular geometry is bent.

6. A sigma bond commonly arises from orbital overlap:

  1. Along the internuclear axis
  2. Only sideways above and below the axis
  3. Only between nuclei directly
  4. Without electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Along the internuclear axis

Sigma overlap has cylindrical symmetry around the bond axis; pi overlap is lateral.

7. A carbon–carbon double bond contains:

  1. One sigma and one pi bond
  2. Two sigma bonds
  3. Two pi bonds only
  4. One ionic bond only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One sigma and one pi bond

The first overlap gives a sigma bond and the additional lateral overlap gives a pi bond.

8. Using molecular orbital theory, O₂ is paramagnetic because it has:

  1. Unpaired electrons
  2. No electrons
  3. Only paired electrons
  4. A net positive charge necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unpaired electrons

Two electrons occupy separate degenerate antibonding pi orbitals in the ground-state description.

9. Molecular-orbital bond order is calculated as:

  1. (Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2
  2. Bonding + antibonding electrons
  3. Antibonding electrons only
  4. Always the number of atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. (Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2

Electrons in bonding orbitals stabilise while antibonding electrons oppose bonding; each pair contributes one net bond.

10. Strong conventional hydrogen bonding requires H bonded to a strongly electronegative atom such as:

  1. N, O or F
  2. Na, K or Ca
  3. He, Ne or Ar
  4. Only carbon in methane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. N, O or F

A strongly polar N–H, O–H or F–H bond can interact with a suitable lone-pair acceptor.

11. The usual molecular shape of methane, CH₄, is:

  1. Square planar
  2. Trigonal planar
  3. Linear
  4. Tetrahedral
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Tetrahedral

Four bonding electron domains around carbon and no lone pairs give tetrahedral geometry with ideal angles near 109.5°.

12. BF₃ is commonly described as having which geometry around boron?

  1. Tetrahedral
  2. Bent
  3. Trigonal planar
  4. Trigonal pyramidal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Trigonal planar

Three bonding domains and no lone pair on boron give trigonal planar geometry, about 120°.

13. An isolated gaseous BeCl₂ molecule has which usual shape?

  1. Trigonal pyramidal
  2. Linear
  3. Bent
  4. Tetrahedral
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Linear

The isolated molecule has two bonding domains around Be and no lone pairs in the elementary VSEPR model, giving 180° geometry. Solid-state structure differs.

14. In a water molecule, the number of lone pairs on the central oxygen in its standard Lewis structure is:

  1. Two
  2. One
  3. Zero
  4. Three
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two

Oxygen has six valence electrons. Two O–H bonds leave four nonbonding electrons, or two lone pairs.

15. In NH₃, the nitrogen has how many bonding pairs and lone pairs in the usual Lewis structure?

  1. Four bonding pairs and no lone pair
  2. Two bonding pairs and two lone pairs
  3. One bonding pair and three lone pairs
  4. Three bonding pairs and one lone pair
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Three bonding pairs and one lone pair

Nitrogen's five valence electrons support three N–H bonds and one nonbonding pair.

16. The usual hybridisation assigned to carbon in methane is:

  1. sp
  2. dsp²
  3. sp³
  4. sp²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. sp³

Four equivalent sigma bonds in the elementary bonding model are described using four sp³ hybrid orbitals.

17. Each carbon in ethene is usually assigned which hybridisation?

  1. sp³d
  2. sp²
  3. sp³
  4. sp
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. sp²

Each carbon has three sigma-bond directions and one unhybridised p orbital used for the pi bond, corresponding to sp².

18. Each carbon in ethyne is usually assigned which hybridisation?

  1. sp
  2. sp²
  3. sp³
  4. sp³d²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. sp

Each carbon has two sigma-bond directions and two unhybridised p orbitals forming two pi bonds, giving sp hybridisation.

19. A carbon–carbon triple bond contains:

  1. Three sigma bonds
  2. Two sigma and one pi bond
  3. Three pi bonds
  4. One sigma and two pi bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. One sigma and two pi bonds

One head-on overlap gives the sigma bond, while two sideways overlaps give the two pi bonds.

20. The total number of sigma and pi bonds in ethene, C₂H₄, is:

  1. Six sigma and zero pi
  2. Three sigma and three pi
  3. Five sigma and one pi
  4. Four sigma and two pi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Five sigma and one pi

There are four C–H sigma bonds and one C–C sigma bond; the double bond also contributes one pi bond.

21. The total number of sigma and pi bonds in ethyne, C₂H₂, is:

  1. Four sigma and one pi
  2. Three sigma and two pi
  3. Two sigma and three pi
  4. Five sigma and zero pi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Three sigma and two pi

Two C–H sigma bonds plus one C–C sigma bond give three sigma bonds. The triple bond supplies two pi bonds.

22. In VSEPR theory, the usual ordering of repulsions is:

  1. Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair
  2. Bond pair–bond pair > lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair
  3. All repulsions are identical
  4. Lone pairs cause no repulsion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair

Lone pairs occupy more angular space near the central atom than bonding pairs, giving stronger repulsions in the elementary model.

23. For comparable carbon–carbon bonds, the usual bond-length ordering is:

  1. Triple > double > single
  2. Double > single > triple
  3. All lengths are equal
  4. Single > double > triple
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Single > double > triple

Greater bond order generally shortens the bond, so a single C–C bond is longer than a double bond, which is longer than a triple bond.

24. In molecular-orbital theory, the bond order of H₂ is:

  1. 2
  2. 0.5
  3. 1
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1

Two electrons occupy the bonding sigma orbital and none the antibonding orbital: (2−0)/2=1.

25. In the elementary molecular-orbital model, He₂ has bond order:

  1. 0.5
  2. 0
  3. 1
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0

Its four electrons fill two bonding and two antibonding positions, so (2−2)/2=0; the model predicts no ordinary stable covalent He₂ molecule.

26. The molecular-orbital bond order of N₂ is:

  1. 3
  2. 2
  3. 1
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3

The ground-state molecular-orbital electron count gives eight bonding and two antibonding valence electrons: (8−2)/2=3.

27. Removing one electron from an antibonding orbital of O₂ to form O₂⁺ changes its bond order from 2 to:

  1. 1.5
  2. 1
  3. 3
  4. 2.5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2.5

Reducing the antibonding electron count by one increases (N_b−N_a)/2 by one half.

28. Which molecule is polar under its usual molecular geometry?

  1. BF₃
  2. CH₄
  3. NH₃
  4. CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. NH₃

Trigonal pyramidal NH₃ has a nonzero net dipole. Symmetric CO₂, BF₃ and CH₄ have cancelling bond dipoles.

29. The formal charge on nitrogen in the usual NH₄⁺ Lewis structure is:

  1. +2
  2. +1
  3. −1
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. +1

Formal charge=valence electrons−nonbonding electrons−half bonding electrons=5−0−4=+1.

30. Resonance contributors of a molecule differ principally in:

  1. Electron placement while nuclear positions remain fixed
  2. Positions of the nuclei
  3. Total atom count
  4. Overall molecular charge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electron placement while nuclear positions remain fixed

Resonance contributors represent different allowed electron arrangements for the same nuclear framework. The real molecule is a resonance hybrid, not rapidly switching structures.
