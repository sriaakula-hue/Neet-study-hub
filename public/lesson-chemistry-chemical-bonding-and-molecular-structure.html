Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

Chemical bonding basics

An ionic bond is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. A covalent bond involves shared electron pairs.

Examples

Sodium chloride forms an ionic crystal lattice. The H₂ molecule contains a covalent bond formed by a shared pair of electrons.

Polarity

Electronegativity differences influence bond polarity. Molecular polarity also depends on shape: CO₂ has polar bonds but is a nonpolar molecule because its bond dipoles cancel in its linear geometry.

Watch out

“Polar bonds” and “polar molecule” are different descriptions.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The molecular shape of NH₃ is:

Trigonal pyramidal

Three bonding pairs and one lone pair around nitrogen produce a trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape.

2. The molecular shape of H₂O is:

Bent

Oxygen has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs; the molecular geometry is bent.

3. A sigma bond commonly arises from orbital overlap:

Along the internuclear axis

Sigma overlap has cylindrical symmetry around the bond axis; pi overlap is lateral.

4. A carbon–carbon double bond contains:

One sigma and one pi bond

The first overlap gives a sigma bond and the additional lateral overlap gives a pi bond.

5. Using molecular orbital theory, O₂ is paramagnetic because it has:

Unpaired electrons

Two electrons occupy separate degenerate antibonding pi orbitals in the ground-state description.

6. Molecular-orbital bond order is calculated as:

(Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2

Electrons in bonding orbitals stabilise while antibonding electrons oppose bonding; each pair contributes one net bond.

7. Strong conventional hydrogen bonding requires H bonded to a strongly electronegative atom such as:

N, O or F

A strongly polar N–H, O–H or F–H bond can interact with a suitable lone-pair acceptor.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Which best describes a covalent bond?

  1. Shared electron pair(s)
  2. Attraction between nuclei only
  3. Transfer of neutrons
  4. A gravitational interaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shared electron pair(s)

A covalent bond involves electron pairs shared between bonded atoms.

2. Why is CO₂ a nonpolar molecule despite having polar bonds?

  1. It has no electrons
  2. Its linear geometry makes the bond dipoles cancel
  3. Carbon is a metal
  4. All covalent bonds are nonpolar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Its linear geometry makes the bond dipoles cancel

The two C–O bond dipoles are equal and opposite in linear CO₂, giving zero net dipole.

3. An ionic bond is primarily:

  1. Electron-pair sharing
  2. Electrostatic attraction between opposite ions
  3. Neutron sharing
  4. Gravity between atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Electrostatic attraction between opposite ions

Oppositely charged ions attract electrostatically.

4. The molecular shape of NH₃ is:

  1. Trigonal pyramidal
  2. Trigonal planar
  3. Linear
  4. Tetrahedral with no lone pair
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Trigonal pyramidal

Three bonding pairs and one lone pair around nitrogen produce a trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape.

5. The molecular shape of H₂O is:

  1. Bent
  2. Linear
  3. Trigonal planar
  4. Square planar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bent

Oxygen has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs; the molecular geometry is bent.

6. A sigma bond commonly arises from orbital overlap:

  1. Along the internuclear axis
  2. Only sideways above and below the axis
  3. Only between nuclei directly
  4. Without electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Along the internuclear axis

Sigma overlap has cylindrical symmetry around the bond axis; pi overlap is lateral.

7. A carbon–carbon double bond contains:

  1. One sigma and one pi bond
  2. Two sigma bonds
  3. Two pi bonds only
  4. One ionic bond only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One sigma and one pi bond

The first overlap gives a sigma bond and the additional lateral overlap gives a pi bond.

8. Using molecular orbital theory, O₂ is paramagnetic because it has:

  1. Unpaired electrons
  2. No electrons
  3. Only paired electrons
  4. A net positive charge necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unpaired electrons

Two electrons occupy separate degenerate antibonding pi orbitals in the ground-state description.

9. Molecular-orbital bond order is calculated as:

  1. (Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2
  2. Bonding + antibonding electrons
  3. Antibonding electrons only
  4. Always the number of atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. (Bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2

Electrons in bonding orbitals stabilise while antibonding electrons oppose bonding; each pair contributes one net bond.

10. Strong conventional hydrogen bonding requires H bonded to a strongly electronegative atom such as:

  1. N, O or F
  2. Na, K or Ca
  3. He, Ne or Ar
  4. Only carbon in methane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. N, O or F

A strongly polar N–H, O–H or F–H bond can interact with a suitable lone-pair acceptor.
