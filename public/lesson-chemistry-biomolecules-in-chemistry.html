Biomolecules in Chemistry

Biomolecules in Chemistry

Key concepts

  • Carbohydrates include monosaccharides and larger saccharides. Glucose is an aldohexose; fructose is a ketohexose.
  • Proteins are amino-acid polymers joined by peptide bonds. Denaturation disrupts higher-order structure and often function without necessarily breaking peptide bonds.
  • Nucleic acids contain nucleotides. Vitamins differ in solubility; vitamins B and C are water-soluble, while A, D, E and K are fat-soluble.

Worked connection

Sucrose is non-reducing because both anomeric centres are involved in its glycosidic bond; glucose is a reducing sugar.

Watch out

Denaturation is not the same as complete hydrolysis into amino acids.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Fructose is an open-chain:

Ketohexose

It contains six carbon atoms and a ketone group in the open-chain form.

2. Sucrose is non-reducing because its glycosidic linkage involves:

Both anomeric centres

Neither anomeric centre remains available to form the usual reducing open-chain carbonyl.

3. A peptide bond links the carboxyl group of one amino acid to another’s:

Amino group

The amide linkage forms between a carboxyl and amino group, with water loss in the simple condensation description.

4. A nucleotide contains a base, sugar and:

Phosphate

A nucleoside has base and sugar; adding phosphate makes a nucleotide.

5. DNA typically contains which sugar?

Deoxyribose

DNA uses 2-deoxyribose, while RNA typically uses ribose.

6. Which set contains only fat-soluble vitamins?

A, D, E, K

The conventional fat-soluble group is A, D, E and K.

7. The primary structure of a protein describes its:

Amino-acid sequence

Primary structure specifies residue order and covalent connectivity; higher structures describe folding and assembly.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Glucose is classified as an:

  1. Aldohexose
  2. Ketopentose
  3. Amino acid
  4. Nucleotide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Aldohexose

It has six carbon atoms and an aldehyde in its open-chain form.

2. Which vitamin is water-soluble?

  1. A
  2. D
  3. E
  4. C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. C

Vitamin C belongs to the water-soluble group.

3. Protein denaturation primarily disrupts:

  1. Higher-order structure
  2. Every peptide bond necessarily
  3. All nuclei
  4. Only isotope ratios
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Higher-order structure

Secondary and tertiary structures may be disrupted while primary structure remains intact.

4. Fructose is an open-chain:

  1. Ketohexose
  2. Aldopentose
  3. Amino acid
  4. Polypeptide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ketohexose

It contains six carbon atoms and a ketone group in the open-chain form.

5. Sucrose is non-reducing because its glycosidic linkage involves:

  1. Both anomeric centres
  2. No oxygen
  3. Only one carbon atom overall
  4. Only peptide bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both anomeric centres

Neither anomeric centre remains available to form the usual reducing open-chain carbonyl.

6. A peptide bond links the carboxyl group of one amino acid to another’s:

  1. Amino group
  2. Methyl group necessarily
  3. Phosphate only
  4. Sulphate only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Amino group

The amide linkage forms between a carboxyl and amino group, with water loss in the simple condensation description.

7. A nucleotide contains a base, sugar and:

  1. Phosphate
  2. Only fatty acid
  3. Only glycerol
  4. Only chloride
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Phosphate

A nucleoside has base and sugar; adding phosphate makes a nucleotide.

8. DNA typically contains which sugar?

  1. Deoxyribose
  2. Ribose
  3. Glucose
  4. Fructose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Deoxyribose

DNA uses 2-deoxyribose, while RNA typically uses ribose.

9. Which set contains only fat-soluble vitamins?

  1. A, D, E, K
  2. B, C, D, E
  3. B, C only
  4. A, B, C, K
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A, D, E, K

The conventional fat-soluble group is A, D, E and K.

10. The primary structure of a protein describes its:

  1. Amino-acid sequence
  2. Only 3D folding
  3. Only subunit count
  4. Only solubility
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Amino-acid sequence

Primary structure specifies residue order and covalent connectivity; higher structures describe folding and assembly.

11. A carbohydrate containing an aldehyde group is classified as an:

  1. Ketose
  2. Amide
  3. Steroid
  4. Aldose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Aldose

Aldoses have an aldehyde-type carbonyl in their open-chain representation.

12. A carbohydrate containing a ketone group is classified as a:

  1. Peptide
  2. Nucleotide
  3. Ketose
  4. Aldose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ketose

Ketoses contain a ketone-type carbonyl in their open-chain form.

13. Glucose in its open-chain form is an:

  1. Disaccharide
  2. Aldohexose
  3. Ketohexose
  4. Aldopentose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Aldohexose

It has six carbons and an aldehyde group in the open-chain representation.

14. Fructose in its open-chain form is a:

  1. Ketohexose
  2. Aldohexose
  3. Aldopentose
  4. Polypeptide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ketohexose

Fructose is a six-carbon ketose.

15. Ribose is a sugar with how many carbons?

  1. 6
  2. 3
  3. 12
  4. 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5

Ribose is a pentose present in RNA nucleotides.

16. Sucrose hydrolysis yields:

  1. Glucose and galactose
  2. Only ribose
  3. Glucose and fructose
  4. Two glucose units
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Glucose and fructose

Sucrose contains glucose and fructose joined through their anomeric centres.

17. Lactose hydrolysis yields:

  1. Only ribose
  2. Glucose and galactose
  3. Glucose and fructose
  4. Two fructose units
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Glucose and galactose

Milk sugar lactose contains galactose and glucose.

18. Maltose hydrolysis yields:

  1. Two glucose molecules
  2. Glucose and fructose
  3. Glucose and galactose
  4. Two amino acids
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two glucose molecules

Maltose is a glucose disaccharide.

19. Sucrose is nonreducing because its glycosidic bond involves:

  1. No oxygen atoms
  2. Only free aldehydes
  3. Only a phosphate group
  4. Both anomeric carbons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both anomeric carbons

Neither monosaccharide has a free anomeric centre available for the usual reducing behaviour.

20. The linkage joining monosaccharide units is generally a:

  1. Metallic bond
  2. Nuclear bond
  3. Glycosidic linkage
  4. Peptide linkage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Glycosidic linkage

A glycosidic bond links the sugar-derived units through an anomeric centre.

21. Amino acids in proteins are joined by:

  1. Only ionic crystals
  2. Peptide bonds
  3. Glycosidic bonds
  4. Phosphodiester bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Peptide bonds

The amide linkage forms between amino and carboxyl groups.

22. A zwitterionic amino acid bears:

  1. Both positive and negative groups
  2. Only a positive group
  3. Only a negative group
  4. No ionic group ever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both positive and negative groups

An ammonium group and carboxylate can coexist in the same molecule.

23. Protein primary structure describes its:

  1. Only overall folded shape
  2. Only subunit association
  3. Only colour
  4. Amino-acid sequence
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Amino-acid sequence

The ordered sequence of residues defines primary structure.

24. Protein denaturation typically disrupts folding without necessarily breaking:

  1. All ionic interactions
  2. All hydrophobic interactions
  3. The peptide backbone
  4. All hydrogen bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The peptide backbone

Higher-order structure changes while primary sequence often remains intact.

25. An essential amino acid is one that must be supplied adequately because the body:

  1. Always destroys it immediately
  2. Cannot synthesise enough of it
  3. Never uses it
  4. Contains none in proteins
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cannot synthesise enough of it

Dietary supply is required when endogenous synthesis is insufficient.

26. The sugar in DNA is:

  1. 2-Deoxyribose
  2. Ribose
  3. Fructose
  4. Sucrose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2-Deoxyribose

DNA lacks the ribose 2′ hydroxyl found in RNA.

27. The base found in DNA instead of RNA's uracil is:

  1. Adenine
  2. Guanine
  3. Cytosine
  4. Thymine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Thymine

Thymine is the standard DNA pyrimidine corresponding to uracil in RNA.

28. A nucleoside contains a nitrogenous base and a:

  1. Peptide
  2. Lipid tail only
  3. Sugar
  4. Phosphate only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sugar

Adding phosphate to a nucleoside gives a nucleotide.

29. A nucleotide differs from a nucleoside by containing:

  1. A second protein chain
  2. A phosphate group
  3. An extra amino acid
  4. Only an extra carbon nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A phosphate group

Base plus sugar plus phosphate constitutes a nucleotide.

30. Vitamin C is commonly known as:

  1. Ascorbic acid
  2. Retinol
  3. Calciferol
  4. Tocopherol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ascorbic acid

Ascorbic acid is the familiar chemical name associated with vitamin C.
