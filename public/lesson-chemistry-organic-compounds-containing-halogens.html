Organic Compounds Containing Halogens

Organic Compounds Containing Halogens

Key concepts

  • Haloalkanes contain a halogen bonded to an alkyl carbon; haloarenes contain a halogen attached to an aromatic ring.
  • SN2 substitution is concerted and typically involves backside attack with inversion at a reacting stereocentre. SN1 proceeds through a carbocation intermediate.
  • Elimination competes with substitution depending on substrate, reagent, solvent and temperature.

Worked connection

For a simple SN2 reaction, rate = k[substrate][nucleophile]. Steric hindrance around the reacting carbon can reduce the rate.

Watch out

No single substrate rule predicts every reaction. Solvent and nucleophile strength must also be considered.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A typical SN2 rate law is:

  1. k[substrate] only
  2. k[nucleophile] only
  3. k[substrate][nucleophile]
  4. Always zero order
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. k[substrate][nucleophile]

Both reactants participate in the rate-determining concerted event.

2. SN1 substitution involves a:

  1. Carbocation intermediate
  2. Free neutron intermediate
  3. No intermediate ever
  4. Peptide intermediate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carbocation intermediate

The leaving group departs to form a carbocation before nucleophile attack.

3. SN2 attack at a stereocentre typically gives:

  1. Inversion
  2. Only retention
  3. Only racemisation
  4. No configuration effect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inversion

Backside attack leads to inversion at the reacting carbon.

4. Chlorobenzene is a:

  1. Haloarene
  2. Haloalkane with only sp³ carbon
  3. Carboxylic acid
  4. Primary amine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Haloarene

Chlorine is attached directly to an aromatic ring carbon.

5. In bromoethane, the carbon bonded to bromine is commonly:

  1. sp³ hybridised
  2. sp hybridised
  3. sp² aromatic
  4. A metal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. sp³ hybridised

The saturated carbon has tetrahedral sigma bonding.

6. Increasing steric hindrance at the reacting carbon generally slows:

  1. SN2 substitution
  2. Every SN1 equally
  3. Every reaction to zero
  4. Nuclear fission
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. SN2 substitution

Backside nucleophilic approach is harder when surrounding groups obstruct the site.

7. A simple SN1 rate law commonly depends directly on:

  1. Substrate concentration
  2. Both substrate and nucleophile concentrations equally
  3. Only nucleophile concentration
  4. Only product concentration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Substrate concentration

Ionisation of substrate to form the carbocation is the rate-determining step in the usual model.

8. Elimination from a suitable haloalkane can form an:

  1. Alkene
  2. Alkane necessarily
  3. Amide necessarily
  4. Noble gas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Alkene

Removal of a leaving group and adjacent hydrogen can create a carbon–carbon double bond.

9. Compared with alkyl chlorides, chlorobenzene resists ordinary nucleophilic substitution partly because of:

  1. Resonance-related C–Cl bond character and aryl structure
  2. Absence of chlorine
  3. No electrons
  4. A lower atomic number of chlorine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Resonance-related C–Cl bond character and aryl structure

Aryl bonding and reaction geometry differ from simple alkyl SN1/SN2 cases.

10. An SN1 pathway at a chiral centre can lead to:

  1. Loss of stereochemical purity
  2. Only perfect inversion in every case
  3. No carbocation
  4. Mandatory complete retention
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Loss of stereochemical purity

A planar carbocation permits attack from more than one face; actual outcomes may show unequal face access.

11. A haloalkane contains a halogen bonded to:

  1. Only an sp carbon
  2. Only a metal centre
  3. Only a nitrogen atom
  4. An sp³ carbon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. An sp³ carbon

The halogen replaces hydrogen at a saturated carbon in the usual haloalkane definition.

12. In a haloarene, the halogen is directly bonded to:

  1. Only hydrogen
  2. Only oxygen
  3. An aromatic ring carbon
  4. Only a side-chain sp³ carbon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An aromatic ring carbon

Direct attachment to the aromatic ring distinguishes a haloarene.

13. An SN2 reaction occurs through:

  1. Only proton exchange
  2. A concerted backside attack
  3. A free carbocation in two separate steps
  4. Only radical initiation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A concerted backside attack

Bond formation and leaving-group departure occur in one concerted transition state.

14. An SN2 displacement at a stereogenic centre commonly gives:

  1. Inversion of configuration
  2. Only complete retention
  3. No stereochemical consequence ever
  4. A new nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inversion of configuration

Backside approach reverses the arrangement at the reacting carbon.

15. The usual rate law for a simple SN2 reaction is:

  1. Rate=k only
  2. Rate=k[substrate]² always
  3. Rate=k[nucleophile] only
  4. Rate=k[substrate][nucleophile]
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rate=k[substrate][nucleophile]

Both reactants participate in the elementary rate-determining step.

16. The usual rate law for a simple SN1 reaction is:

  1. Rate=k[nucleophile]²
  2. Rate=k only
  3. Rate=k[substrate]
  4. Rate=k[substrate][nucleophile]
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rate=k[substrate]

Rate-limiting ionisation involves the substrate before nucleophile capture.

17. SN1 reactions are commonly favoured by substrates forming:

  1. Only stable neutrons
  2. Stable carbocations
  3. Only methyl anions
  4. No charged intermediate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Stable carbocations

Carbocation stability lowers the barrier for the ionisation step.

18. For simple alkyl halides in SN2 reactions, steric hindrance is least for:

  1. Methyl halides
  2. Tertiary halides
  3. Highly branched secondary halides
  4. Neopentyl halides
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methyl halides

Backside access is easiest at the least crowded carbon centre.

19. Alcoholic KOH acting on a suitable haloalkane commonly favours:

  1. Only oxidation to an acid
  2. Only nitration
  3. Formation of an ester directly
  4. Elimination to an alkene
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Elimination to an alkene

Basic alcoholic conditions support removal of hydrogen halide.

20. Aqueous hydroxide reacting by substitution with a suitable haloalkane commonly forms an:

  1. Aldehyde always
  2. Amide always
  3. Alcohol
  4. Alkyne always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alcohol

Hydroxide replaces the leaving halide group in a substitution pathway.

21. In the Wurtz reaction, alkyl halides are treated with sodium in:

  1. Concentrated nitric acid
  2. Dry ether
  3. Aqueous acid
  4. Wet steam
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Dry ether

Dry ether supports sodium-mediated coupling while avoiding water reaction.

22. The Wurtz reaction of methyl halide can produce:

  1. Ethane
  2. Methanol
  3. Ethene only
  4. Ethanal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ethane

Coupling two methyl groups forms a carbon-carbon single bond.

23. In the Finkelstein reaction, sodium iodide is commonly used in:

  1. Water only
  2. Molten iron
  3. Liquid ammonia only
  4. Acetone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acetone

Acetone favours halide exchange and precipitation of sodium chloride or bromide.

24. The Swarts reaction is associated with preparation of:

  1. Only amides
  2. Only nitriles
  3. Alkyl fluorides
  4. Only alcohols
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alkyl fluorides

Appropriate metal fluorides replace chloride or bromide with fluoride.

25. An alkyl halide reacting with KCN commonly gives a:

  1. Amine directly
  2. Nitrile
  3. Nitroalkane
  4. Alcohol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nitrile

Carbon-end attack by cyanide forms an R–C≡N bond.

26. Compared with chloroalkanes, chlorobenzene resists ordinary nucleophilic substitution partly because its C–Cl bond has:

  1. Partial double-bond character
  2. No covalent character
  3. A free carbocation always
  4. Only hydrogen bonding
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Partial double-bond character

Resonance interaction and sp² carbon strengthen the aryl carbon-halogen bond.

27. Halogens on benzene are generally:

  1. Strongly activating and meta directing
  2. Always activating and ortho only
  3. Neither inductive nor resonance active
  4. Deactivating but ortho/para directing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Deactivating but ortho/para directing

Their electron withdrawal deactivates while lone-pair resonance influences orientation.

28. For comparable alkyl substrates, a common leaving-group order is:

  1. All exactly equal
  2. Cl⁻ > I⁻ > F⁻ > Br⁻
  3. I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻
  4. F⁻ > Cl⁻ > Br⁻ > I⁻
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻

Larger halides commonly leave more readily and carbon-iodine bonds are easier to break.

29. A tertiary haloalkane is one whose halogen-bearing carbon is attached to:

  1. One other carbon
  2. Three other carbons
  3. Three hydrogens
  4. No other carbon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Three other carbons

Primary, secondary and tertiary classification counts carbon neighbours of the reacting carbon.

30. Elimination forming the more substituted alkene is commonly described by:

  1. Zaitsev's rule
  2. Hund's rule
  3. Avogadro's law
  4. Pauli's principle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zaitsev's rule

In many elimination reactions the more substituted alkene is favoured, with important condition-dependent exceptions.
