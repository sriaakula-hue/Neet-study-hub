Laws of Motion

Newton’s laws of motion

The first law describes inertia: an object remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it.

Fnet = ma

For constant mass, net force equals mass times acceleration. Force and acceleration are vectors. The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

Worked example

A 3 kg object under a net force of 12 N accelerates at 4 m/s².

Third law

Interaction forces occur in equal and opposite pairs on different bodies. They do not cancel on a single body’s free-body diagram.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A 4 kg body has acceleration 3 m/s². Net force is:

12 N

Newton’s second law for constant mass gives net force ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N.

2. A net force of 15 N acts for 2 s. Its impulse magnitude is:

30 N s

For constant force, impulse is FΔt = 15 × 2 = 30 N s, equal to momentum change.

3. In an isolated system with zero net external force, total linear momentum is:

Conserved

Internal interaction forces cancel in the total momentum balance. Zero external force implies constant total momentum, which need not be zero.

4. Static friction on a body before slipping:

Adjusts up to a limiting value

Static friction responds to the required tangential force until its maximum μsN is reached.

5. A surface has μs = 0.4 and normal force 50 N. Maximum static friction is:

20 N

The maximum static-friction magnitude is μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N.

6. A passenger leans forward when a vehicle suddenly stops mainly because of:

Inertia

The body tends to maintain its previous motion while the vehicle is slowed by external forces.

7. A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at 6 m/s. Required centripetal force is:

18 N

The inward net force is mv²/r = 2 × 36/4 = 18 N. Centripetal force describes a net-force requirement, not a new force type.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A net force of 12 N acts on a 3 kg body. Its acceleration is:

  1. 4 m/s²
  2. 9 m/s²
  3. 15 m/s²
  4. 36 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 m/s²

For constant mass, a = F/m = 12/3 = 4 m/s².

2. Newton’s third-law force pair acts on:

  1. The same body
  2. Different bodies
  3. Only stationary bodies
  4. Only identical masses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Different bodies

The interaction forces act on the two different interacting bodies.

3. Momentum of a 2 kg body moving at 5 m/s is:

  1. 2.5 kg m/s
  2. 7 kg m/s
  3. 10 kg m/s
  4. 25 kg m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10 kg m/s

p = mv = 2 × 5 = 10 kg m/s.

4. A 4 kg body has acceleration 3 m/s². Net force is:

  1. 24 N
  2. 12 N
  3. 7 N
  4. 1.33 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 12 N

Newton’s second law for constant mass gives net force ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N.

5. A net force of 15 N acts for 2 s. Its impulse magnitude is:

  1. 60 N s
  2. 30 N s
  3. 7.5 N s
  4. 17 N s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30 N s

For constant force, impulse is FΔt = 15 × 2 = 30 N s, equal to momentum change.

6. In an isolated system with zero net external force, total linear momentum is:

  1. Conserved
  2. Always zero
  3. Always increasing
  4. Equal to kinetic energy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conserved

Internal interaction forces cancel in the total momentum balance. Zero external force implies constant total momentum, which need not be zero.

7. Static friction on a body before slipping:

  1. Adjusts up to a limiting value
  2. Always equals μsN
  3. Is always zero
  4. Always exceeds μsN
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Adjusts up to a limiting value

Static friction responds to the required tangential force until its maximum μsN is reached.

8. A surface has μs = 0.4 and normal force 50 N. Maximum static friction is:

  1. 0.008 N
  2. 20 N
  3. 50 N
  4. 125 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 20 N

The maximum static-friction magnitude is μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N.

9. A passenger leans forward when a vehicle suddenly stops mainly because of:

  1. Inertia
  2. A new forward gravitational force
  3. Zero mass
  4. Loss of momentum conservation in all systems
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inertia

The body tends to maintain its previous motion while the vehicle is slowed by external forces.

10. A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at 6 m/s. Required centripetal force is:

  1. 72 N
  2. 18 N
  3. 3 N
  4. 12 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 18 N

The inward net force is mv²/r = 2 × 36/4 = 18 N. Centripetal force describes a net-force requirement, not a new force type.

11. A 5 kg block lies on a horizontal surface with μs=0.5. Use g=10 m/s². A horizontal force of 10 N is applied and the block remains at rest. Friction magnitude is:

  1. 25 N
  2. 50 N
  3. 0 N
  4. 10 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 10 N

The maximum static friction is 0.5×50=25 N, but static friction supplies only the 10 N needed for equilibrium.

12. A sliding 5 kg block is on a horizontal surface with μk=0.2. Use g=10 m/s². Kinetic friction magnitude is:

  1. 2 N
  2. 25 N
  3. 10 N
  4. 50 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10 N

With no other vertical forces, N=mg=50 N. Kinetic friction is μkN=0.2×50=10 N.

13. A 2 kg block on a frictionless horizontal surface is pulled by 10 N horizontally. Acceleration is:

  1. 10 m/s²
  2. 5 m/s²
  3. 20 m/s²
  4. 0.2 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5 m/s²

Net horizontal force is 10 N, so a=F/m=10/2=5 m/s².

14. Two horizontal forces, 18 N rightward and 6 N leftward, act on a 3 kg block. Its acceleration is:

  1. 4 m/s² rightward
  2. 8 m/s² rightward
  3. 4 m/s² leftward
  4. 6 m/s² rightward
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 m/s² rightward

Choose right positive. Net force=18−6=12 N and acceleration=12/3=4 m/s² rightward.

15. A 60 kg person stands on a scale in a lift accelerating upwards at 2 m/s². Use g=10 m/s². The scale's normal force is:

  1. 600 N
  2. 480 N
  3. 120 N
  4. 720 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 720 N

N−mg=ma, giving N=m(g+a)=60×12=720 N.

16. A 60 kg person stands on a scale in a lift accelerating downwards at 2 m/s². Use g=10 m/s². The scale's normal force is:

  1. 600 N
  2. 120 N
  3. 480 N
  4. 720 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 480 N

With upward positive, N−mg=−ma; N=m(g−a)=60×8=480 N.

17. A lift and its passenger undergo ideal free fall. The passenger's apparent weight is:

  1. Equal to mass in kilograms
  2. Zero
  3. Equal to mg
  4. Twice mg
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Zero

Both accelerate at g downward. No support force is required, so the normal reaction and apparent weight vanish.

18. A 4 kg block rests on a smooth plane inclined at 30° to horizontal. Use g=10 m/s². The downslope gravitational component is:

  1. 20 N
  2. 40 N
  3. 20√3 N
  4. 10 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20 N

The component parallel to the plane is mg sin30°=4×10×½=20 N.

19. A block slides down a smooth plane inclined at 30°. Use g=10 m/s². Its acceleration down the plane is:

  1. 10 m/s²
  2. 5√3 m/s²
  3. 0 m/s²
  4. 5 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5 m/s²

On a frictionless incline, a=g sinθ=10×½=5 m/s², independent of mass.

20. A 4 kg block rests on a plane inclined at 30° with no other normal force. Use g=10 m/s². The normal reaction is:

  1. 40 N
  2. 10√3 N
  3. 20√3 N
  4. 20 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20√3 N

Normal balance gives N=mg cos30°=40×√3/2=20√3 N.

21. Masses 3 kg and 2 kg hang over an ideal massless frictionless pulley with a light string. Use g=10 m/s². Acceleration magnitude is:

  1. 1 m/s²
  2. 2 m/s²
  3. 10 m/s²
  4. 5 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 m/s²

For an ideal Atwood machine, a=(m₁−m₂)g/(m₁+m₂)=(3−2)×10/5=2 m/s².

22. For an ideal Atwood machine with masses 3 kg and 2 kg and g=10 m/s², string tension is:

  1. 24 N
  2. 30 N
  3. 20 N
  4. 50 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 24 N

Acceleration is 2 m/s². For the ascending 2 kg mass, T−20=2×2, so T=24 N.

23. A 2 kg and a 3 kg block touch on a smooth horizontal table. A 20 N force pushes the 2 kg block towards the 3 kg block. Contact force on the 3 kg block is:

  1. 20 N
  2. 8 N
  3. 4 N
  4. 12 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 12 N

The common acceleration is 20/(2+3)=4 m/s². The contact force accelerating the 3 kg block is 3×4=12 N.

24. A 0.2 kg ball moving at +10 m/s rebounds with velocity −10 m/s. Change in momentum is:

  1. 0 kg·m/s
  2. −2 kg·m/s
  3. −4 kg·m/s
  4. +4 kg·m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. −4 kg·m/s

Δp=m(v−u)=0.2×(−10−10)=−4 kg·m/s. The sign indicates the chosen negative direction.

25. A constant net force of 8 N changes momentum by 24 kg·m/s. Duration of the force is:

  1. 16 s
  2. 3 s
  3. 192 s
  4. 0.33 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3 s

Impulse FΔt=Δp, so Δt=24/8=3 s.

26. A force–time graph is a triangle with base 4 s and peak force 10 N, all positive. Impulse is:

  1. 20 N·s
  2. 40 N·s
  3. 10 N·s
  4. 2.5 N·s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20 N·s

Impulse equals area under the force–time graph: ½×4×10=20 N·s.

27. A car travels around a level circular road. The force providing horizontal centripetal acceleration is ordinarily:

  1. Weight alone
  2. Normal reaction alone
  3. A separate outward force
  4. Static friction from the road
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Static friction from the road

Weight and normal reaction balance vertically. Tyre-road static friction provides the inward horizontal force when tyres do not slide.

28. If speed doubles at fixed mass and circular-path radius, the required centripetal force becomes:

  1. Half as large
  2. Unchanged
  3. Four times as large
  4. Twice as large
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Four times as large

F=mv²/r; doubling v multiplies v² and hence required inward force by four.

29. A frictionless banked circular road has r=20 m and tanθ=0.5. Use g=10 m/s². Suitable speed is:

  1. 100 m/s
  2. 10 m/s
  3. 20 m/s
  4. 5 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 m/s

Frictionless banking requires tanθ=v²/(rg). Thus v=√(20×10×0.5)=10 m/s.

30. Inertia of a body is quantitatively measured by its:

  1. Mass
  2. Speed
  3. Acceleration
  4. Applied force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mass

Mass measures resistance to changes in velocity. More mass requires more net force for a given acceleration.
