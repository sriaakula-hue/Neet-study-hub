Laws of Motion

Newton’s laws of motion

The first law describes inertia: an object remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it.

Fnet = ma

For constant mass, net force equals mass times acceleration. Force and acceleration are vectors. The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

Worked example

A 3 kg object under a net force of 12 N accelerates at 4 m/s².

Third law

Interaction forces occur in equal and opposite pairs on different bodies. They do not cancel on a single body’s free-body diagram.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A 4 kg body has acceleration 3 m/s². Net force is:

12 N

Newton’s second law for constant mass gives net force ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N.

2. A net force of 15 N acts for 2 s. Its impulse magnitude is:

30 N s

For constant force, impulse is FΔt = 15 × 2 = 30 N s, equal to momentum change.

3. In an isolated system with zero net external force, total linear momentum is:

Conserved

Internal interaction forces cancel in the total momentum balance. Zero external force implies constant total momentum, which need not be zero.

4. Static friction on a body before slipping:

Adjusts up to a limiting value

Static friction responds to the required tangential force until its maximum μsN is reached.

5. A surface has μs = 0.4 and normal force 50 N. Maximum static friction is:

20 N

The maximum static-friction magnitude is μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N.

6. A passenger leans forward when a vehicle suddenly stops mainly because of:

Inertia

The body tends to maintain its previous motion while the vehicle is slowed by external forces.

7. A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at 6 m/s. Required centripetal force is:

18 N

The inward net force is mv²/r = 2 × 36/4 = 18 N. Centripetal force describes a net-force requirement, not a new force type.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A net force of 12 N acts on a 3 kg body. Its acceleration is:

  1. 4 m/s²
  2. 9 m/s²
  3. 15 m/s²
  4. 36 m/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 m/s²

For constant mass, a = F/m = 12/3 = 4 m/s².

2. Newton’s third-law force pair acts on:

  1. The same body
  2. Different bodies
  3. Only stationary bodies
  4. Only identical masses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Different bodies

The interaction forces act on the two different interacting bodies.

3. Momentum of a 2 kg body moving at 5 m/s is:

  1. 2.5 kg m/s
  2. 7 kg m/s
  3. 10 kg m/s
  4. 25 kg m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10 kg m/s

p = mv = 2 × 5 = 10 kg m/s.

4. A 4 kg body has acceleration 3 m/s². Net force is:

  1. 24 N
  2. 12 N
  3. 7 N
  4. 1.33 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 12 N

Newton’s second law for constant mass gives net force ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N.

5. A net force of 15 N acts for 2 s. Its impulse magnitude is:

  1. 60 N s
  2. 30 N s
  3. 7.5 N s
  4. 17 N s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30 N s

For constant force, impulse is FΔt = 15 × 2 = 30 N s, equal to momentum change.

6. In an isolated system with zero net external force, total linear momentum is:

  1. Conserved
  2. Always zero
  3. Always increasing
  4. Equal to kinetic energy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conserved

Internal interaction forces cancel in the total momentum balance. Zero external force implies constant total momentum, which need not be zero.

7. Static friction on a body before slipping:

  1. Adjusts up to a limiting value
  2. Always equals μsN
  3. Is always zero
  4. Always exceeds μsN
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Adjusts up to a limiting value

Static friction responds to the required tangential force until its maximum μsN is reached.

8. A surface has μs = 0.4 and normal force 50 N. Maximum static friction is:

  2. 20 N
  3. 50 N
  4. 125 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 20 N

The maximum static-friction magnitude is μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N.

9. A passenger leans forward when a vehicle suddenly stops mainly because of:

  1. Inertia
  2. A new forward gravitational force
  3. Zero mass
  4. Loss of momentum conservation in all systems

Answer: A. Inertia

The body tends to maintain its previous motion while the vehicle is slowed by external forces.

10. A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at 6 m/s. Required centripetal force is:

  1. 72 N
  2. 18 N

  4. 12 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 18 N

The inward net force is mv²/r = 2 × 36/4 = 18 N. Centripetal force describes a net-force requirement, not a new force type.
