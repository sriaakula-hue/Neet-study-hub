Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

Key concepts

  • The magnetic force magnitude on a moving charge is |q|vB sin θ. Its direction depends on the vector cross product and charge sign.
  • A wire carrying current I in a uniform field experiences force ILB sin θ for a straight segment of length L.
  • A magnetic force alone does no work on a point charge because it is perpendicular to velocity. A current loop behaves as a magnetic dipole.

Worked example

A charge moving parallel to the field has zero magnetic force. With perpendicular motion, the magnitude is |q|vB.

Watch out

A magnetic field can change velocity direction without changing speed when no other forces do work.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. For a positive charge, magnetic-force direction is given by:

v × B

The Lorentz magnetic force is q(v × B); negative q reverses this direction.

2. For circular motion with velocity perpendicular to uniform B, radius is:

mv/(|q|B)

Equating |q|vB to mv²/r gives r = mv/(|q|B).

3. Outside a long straight wire, B varies with perpendicular distance r as:

1/r

For an ideal long wire, B = μ₀I/(2πr).

4. Two long parallel wires carrying currents in the same direction:

Attract

The magnetic interaction is attractive for same-direction currents and repulsive for opposite directions.

5. An ideal long solenoid’s field inside is approximately:

μ₀nI

n is turns per unit length; neglecting edge effects gives B ≈ μ₀nI.

6. To convert a galvanometer into an ammeter, add a:

Low-resistance shunt in parallel

The parallel shunt carries most current, protecting the galvanometer and lowering effective resistance.

7. To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, add:

High resistance in series

Series resistance limits current and gives the desired voltage range.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A charge moving parallel to a magnetic field experiences magnetic force:

  1. qvB
  2. 2qvB
  3. Zero
  4. qB/v
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zero

The magnetic force is qvB sin θ. For motion parallel to the field, θ = 0° and sin θ = 0, so the force is zero.

2. Work done by the magnetic force alone on a point charge is:

  1. Always positive
  2. Always negative
  3. Zero
  4. Always qVB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zero

The force is perpendicular to instantaneous displacement.

3. A perpendicular 2 A current in 0.5 m of wire in a 3 T field has force:

  1. 1 N
  2. 3 N
  3. 6 N
  4. 12 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3 N

F = ILB = 2 × 0.5 × 3 = 3 N.

4. For a positive charge, magnetic-force direction is given by:

  1. v × B
  2. B × v
  3. v + B
  4. Always B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. v × B

The Lorentz magnetic force is q(v × B); negative q reverses this direction.

5. For circular motion with velocity perpendicular to uniform B, radius is:

  1. mv/(|q|B)
  2. |q|B/(mv)
  3. mB/v
  4. v/(mB)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. mv/(|q|B)

Equating |q|vB to mv²/r gives r = mv/(|q|B).

6. Outside a long straight wire, B varies with perpendicular distance r as:

  1. 1/r
  2. r
  3. 1/r²
  4. r²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1/r

For an ideal long wire, B = μ₀I/(2πr).

7. Two long parallel wires carrying currents in the same direction:

  1. Attract
  2. Repel
  3. Always have zero force
  4. Have gravitational force only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Attract

The magnetic interaction is attractive for same-direction currents and repulsive for opposite directions.

8. An ideal long solenoid’s field inside is approximately:

  1. μ₀nI
  2. μ₀I/(2πr) everywhere
  3. Zero
  4. μ₀n/I
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. μ₀nI

n is turns per unit length; neglecting edge effects gives B ≈ μ₀nI.

9. To convert a galvanometer into an ammeter, add a:

  1. Low-resistance shunt in parallel
  2. High resistance in series only
  3. Capacitor only
  4. Transformer only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Low-resistance shunt in parallel

The parallel shunt carries most current, protecting the galvanometer and lowering effective resistance.

10. To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, add:

  1. High resistance in series
  2. Low resistance in parallel
  3. No resistance ever
  4. A permanent short circuit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. High resistance in series

Series resistance limits current and gives the desired voltage range.

11. A charge of 2 C moves at 3 m/s perpendicular to a uniform 0.2 T magnetic field. Force magnitude is:

  1. 0.6 N
  2. 1.8 N
  3. 2.4 N
  4. 1.2 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1.2 N

Magnetic force magnitude is |q|vB sinθ; perpendicular motion gives sinθ=1. Answer: 1.2 N.

  3. 0.4 N

13. A circular N-turn coil has N=20, current 2 A and area 0.03 m². Dipole moment magnitude is:

  2. 1.2 A·m²
  3. 0.6 A·m²
