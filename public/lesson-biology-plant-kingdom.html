Plant Kingdom

Plant Kingdom

EXPANDED REVISION · LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Compare plant groups using vascular tissue, reproductive structures and dominant life-cycle stages.

1. Follow the reproductive cycle

A haploid gametophyte produces gametes by mitosis. Fertilisation forms a diploid zygote, which develops into a sporophyte. The sporophyte produces haploid spores through meiosis. Spores then develop into gametophytes. This alternation separates gamete production from spore production.

2. Nonvascular and seedless groups

Bryophytes lack true vascular tissue. Their gametophyte is dominant and the attached sporophyte depends on it to varying degrees. Water is typically needed for motile male gametes to reach the egg. Pteridophytes, such as ferns, have vascular tissue and a dominant sporophyte but do not produce seeds.

3. The seed-plant distinction

Gymnosperms produce seeds without enclosure in an ovary. Angiosperms produce flowers, with ovules enclosed in ovaries that usually develop into fruits. Both groups have reduced gametophytes. A seed is a reproductive structure containing an embryo; it is not simply another name for a spore.

4. Algal comparisons

Green algae commonly contain chlorophyll a and b and store starch. Brown algae typically contain fucoxanthin, with laminarin and mannitol as storage products. Red algae contain pigments including phycoerythrin and store floridean starch. Use combinations of traits rather than colour alone.

5. Heterospory and double fertilisation

Heterospory means production of two spore types: microspores and megaspores. In typical angiosperms, double fertilisation produces a diploid zygote and usually triploid primary endosperm nucleus. One product develops into the embryo; the other supports endosperm development.

Worked decision and pitfalls

A plant with vascular tissue but no seeds could be a pteridophyte. If it has seeds, ask whether its ovules are enclosed in an ovary. Pine and mango both have seeds, but pine is a gymnosperm and mango an angiosperm. Do not confuse a visible dominant sporophyte with the absence of a gametophyte.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Which group includes vascular, seedless plants such as ferns?

  1. Bryophytes
  2. Pteridophytes
  3. Gymnosperms
  4. Angiosperms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Pteridophytes

Pteridophytes have vascular tissues but reproduce by spores.

2. Seeds not enclosed in an ovary are characteristic of:

  1. Gymnosperms
  2. Angiosperms
  3. Algae
  4. Bryophytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gymnosperms

Gymnosperm seeds are not enclosed in a fruit derived from an ovary.

3. The dominant generation of a typical bryophyte is the:

  1. Sporophyte
  2. Gametophyte
  3. Zygote alone
  4. Seed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gametophyte

The gametophyte is the conspicuous, dominant bryophyte generation.

4. A sporophyte is generally:

  1. Haploid
  2. Diploid
  3. Without chromosomes
  4. Always a seed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Diploid

It develops from the diploid zygote.

5. In a typical alternation of generations, spores are produced through:

  1. Meiosis in the sporophyte
  2. Fertilisation in the leaf
  3. Mitosis of every gamete
  4. No division
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meiosis in the sporophyte

Meiosis reduces chromosome number to form haploid spores.

6. A haploid gametophyte normally produces gametes by:

  1. Mitosis
  2. Meiosis reducing haploid to zero
  3. Fertilisation
  4. Only budding of a seed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mitosis

The gametophyte is already haploid, so gamete formation uses mitosis.

7. Heterospory means production of:

  1. Two kinds of spores
  2. Only two flowers
  3. Two identical seeds
  4. Two kingdoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two kinds of spores

Microspores and megaspores differ in size and developmental role.

8. A characteristic pigment of brown algae is:

  1. Fucoxanthin
  2. Haemoglobin
  3. Melanin only
  4. Insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fucoxanthin

Fucoxanthin contributes to the brown appearance.

9. Floridean starch is associated with:

  1. Red algae
  2. Brown algae only
  3. Ferns only
  4. Pine pollen only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Red algae

Red algae characteristically store floridean starch.

10. In typical angiosperm double fertilisation, syngamy forms the:

  1. Diploid zygote
  2. Triploid zygote
  3. Haploid seed coat
  4. Pollen tube
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Diploid zygote

Fusion of two haploid gametes gives the diploid zygote.

11. Green-algal chloroplast pigments characteristically include:

  1. Only chlorophyll c
  2. Only bacteriochlorophyll
  3. Only haemoglobin
  4. Chlorophyll a and b
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Chlorophyll a and b

These pigments are typical of green algae and land plants.

12. Brown algae owe their characteristic colour largely to:

  1. Haemoglobin
  2. Melanin only
  3. Fucoxanthin
  4. Phycoerythrin alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Fucoxanthin

Fucoxanthin masks the underlying chlorophyll colour.

13. Red algae characteristically contain the accessory pigment:

  1. Carotene alone
  2. Phycoerythrin
  3. Fucoxanthin only
  4. Haemoglobin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Phycoerythrin

The phycobilin contributes to their characteristic appearance.

14. Typical green-algal reserve food is:

  1. Starch
  2. Glycogen exclusively
  3. Urea
  4. Cellulose only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Starch

Starch is commonly stored within green-algal chloroplast systems.

15. Brown algae commonly store:

  1. Only animal glycogen
  2. Only sucrose crystals
  3. Only cellulose
  4. Laminarin and mannitol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Laminarin and mannitol

These are characteristic reserve products in brown algae.

16. Red algae characteristically store:

  1. Only glycogen
  2. Only urea
  3. Floridean starch
  4. Only laminarin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Floridean starch

Floridean starch is the familiar red-algal reserve material.

17. Bryophytes are called amphibians of the plant kingdom because they require water for:

  1. Only spore survival
  2. Sexual fertilisation
  3. Every mature tissue to float
  4. Only photosynthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sexual fertilisation

Motile male gametes need a water film to reach the egg.

18. The dominant independent phase in many bryophytes is the:

  1. Gametophyte
  2. Sporophyte exclusively
  3. Zygote alone
  4. Endosperm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gametophyte

The haploid leafy or thalloid gametophyte is conspicuous and independent.

19. A typical bryophyte sporophyte remains nutritionally connected to the:

  1. Only root xylem
  2. Only endosperm
  3. Only pollen tube
  4. Gametophyte
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Gametophyte

The sporophyte is dependent to varying degrees on its supporting gametophyte.

20. Pteridophytes possess vascular tissues but ordinarily lack:

  1. Leaves in every species
  2. Spores
  3. Seeds
  4. Roots in every species
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Seeds

They reproduce by spores rather than enclosed seeds.

21. The dominant phase in ferns is the:

  1. Zygote only
  2. Sporophyte
  3. Endosperm
  4. Pollen grain
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sporophyte

The familiar fern plant is diploid and vascular.

22. A fern gametophyte is commonly called a:

  1. Prothallus
  2. Protonema
  3. Cone
  4. Fruit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prothallus

The small haploid prothallus bears reproductive structures.

23. Production of two spore types is called:

  1. Homospory
  2. Isogamy
  3. Budding
  4. Heterospory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Heterospory

Microspores and megaspores differ in size and reproductive role.

24. A classic heterosporous pteridophyte example is:

  1. Spirogyra
  2. Funaria
  3. Selaginella
  4. Marchantia
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Selaginella

Selaginella produces microspores and megaspores.

25. Gymnosperm seeds are described as naked because they are not enclosed in a:

  1. Embryonic tissue
  2. Fruit-forming ovary
  3. Seed coat
  4. Megasporangium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fruit-forming ovary

Ovules and seeds are not inside an angiosperm-type ovary.

26. A gymnosperm example is:

  1. Pinus
  2. Mango
  3. Rice
  4. Sunflower
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pinus

Pine belongs to the coniferous gymnosperm group.

27. Angiosperms characteristically form seeds within:

  1. Only exposed cones
  2. Only anther walls
  3. Only rhizoids
  4. Fruits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fruits

After development, the ovary contributes to the fruit enclosing seeds.

28. Double fertilisation is a characteristic reproductive process of:

  1. All fungi
  2. All bryophytes
  3. Angiosperms
  4. All algae
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Angiosperms

One sperm forms the zygote while the other contributes to endosperm.

29. In a haplontic life cycle, the main multicellular phase is:

  1. Tetraploid always
  2. Haploid
  3. Diploid
  4. Triploid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Haploid

The zygote is typically the main diploid stage before meiosis.

30. In a diplontic life cycle, the main multicellular phase is:

  1. Diploid
  2. Haploid
  3. Triploid
  4. Without chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Diploid

The haploid stage is restricted largely to gametes.
