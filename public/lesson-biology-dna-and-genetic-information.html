DNA and genetic information

DNA and genetic information

Core idea

DNA is composed of nucleotide units containing a sugar, phosphate and nitrogenous base. In typical double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with thymine, and guanine pairs with cytosine. The strands are antiparallel.

Worked connection

If double-stranded DNA contains 20% adenine, it contains 20% thymine and 30% each of guanine and cytosine.

Watch out

RNA usually contains ribose and uracil instead of deoxyribose and thymine. DNA replication is semiconservative: each daughter duplex has one parental and one newly synthesized strand.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The two strands of a typical DNA duplex run:

Antiparallel

One strand runs 5′ to 3′ while the complementary strand runs 3′ to 5′.

2. DNA polymerases ordinarily extend a new strand in the:

5′ to 3′ direction

Nucleotides are added to the growing strand’s 3′ hydroxyl end.

3. Transcription synthesises RNA using a:

DNA template

RNA polymerase reads the appropriate DNA template strand.

4. Translation converts information in mRNA into a:

Polypeptide

Ribosomes interpret codons while transfer RNAs deliver amino acids.

5. A codon usually contains how many nucleotides?

Three

The genetic code is read in triplets; there are 64 possible triplet codons.

6. The usual initiation codon AUG also codes for:

Methionine

AUG specifies methionine and commonly initiates translation; initiation context also matters.

7. A stop codon ordinarily specifies:

Termination rather than an amino acid

Release factors recognise stop codons and terminate the polypeptide in the usual code.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. In typical double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with:

  1. Uracil
  2. Guanine
  3. Thymine
  4. Cytosine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Thymine

A pairs with T; G pairs with C.

2. Double-stranded DNA contains 20% adenine. Its guanine percentage is:

  1. 20%
  2. 30%
  3. 40%
  4. 60%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30%

T = 20%; G + C = 60%; G = C = 30%.

3. Semiconservative DNA replication produces duplexes with:

  1. Two old strands
  2. Two new strands only
  3. One old and one new strand
  4. No parental material
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. One old and one new strand

Each daughter duplex retains one parental strand paired with a new strand.

4. The two strands of a typical DNA duplex run:

  1. Antiparallel
  2. Always parallel
  3. In unrelated rings
  4. Without direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Antiparallel

One strand runs 5′ to 3′ while the complementary strand runs 3′ to 5′.

5. DNA polymerases ordinarily extend a new strand in the:

  1. 5′ to 3′ direction
  2. 3′ to 5′ direction
  3. Either direction equally without constraint
  4. No direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 5′ to 3′ direction

Nucleotides are added to the growing strand’s 3′ hydroxyl end.

6. Transcription synthesises RNA using a:

  1. DNA template
  2. Protein template only
  3. Lipid template
  4. Glucose template
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. DNA template

RNA polymerase reads the appropriate DNA template strand.

7. Translation converts information in mRNA into a:

  1. Polypeptide
  2. DNA duplex
  3. Lipid bilayer directly
  4. Starch chain
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Polypeptide

Ribosomes interpret codons while transfer RNAs deliver amino acids.

8. A codon usually contains how many nucleotides?

  1. Three
  2. One
  3. Two
  4. Four
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Three

The genetic code is read in triplets; there are 64 possible triplet codons.

9. The usual initiation codon AUG also codes for:

  1. Methionine
  2. Glycine
  3. Tryptophan
  4. Proline
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methionine

AUG specifies methionine and commonly initiates translation; initiation context also matters.

10. A stop codon ordinarily specifies:

  1. Termination rather than an amino acid
  2. A new phosphate
  3. Only methionine
  4. Only a sugar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Termination rather than an amino acid

Release factors recognise stop codons and terminate the polypeptide in the usual code.

11. A phosphodiester linkage joins nucleotides between:

  1. Two lipid tails
  2. The 3′ hydroxyl and 5′ phosphate positions
  3. Two nitrogenous bases directly
  4. Two proteins
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The 3′ hydroxyl and 5′ phosphate positions

The covalent sugar-phosphate backbone has 3′–5′ connectivity.

12. The two strands of ordinary double-helical DNA run:

  1. Antiparallel
  2. Parallel in the same direction
  3. Without polarity
  4. Only circularly with no direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Antiparallel

One strand runs 5′→3′ opposite the other's 3′→5′ orientation.

13. Adenine pairs with thymine using how many hydrogen bonds?

  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2

Standard Watson–Crick A–T pairing has two hydrogen bonds.

14. Guanine pairs with cytosine using how many hydrogen bonds?

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 3
  4. 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3

Standard Watson–Crick G–C pairing has three hydrogen bonds.

15. In a DNA sample with 30% adenine, thymine percentage is:

  1. 60%
  2. 30%
  3. 20%
  4. 40%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30%

For standard double-stranded DNA, A and T percentages are equal.

16. In double-stranded DNA with 30% adenine, guanine percentage is:

  1. 20%
  2. 30%
  3. 40%
  4. 10%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20%

A+T=60%; the remaining 40% splits equally between G and C.

17. DNA replication is described as semiconservative because each daughter duplex has:

  1. Two entirely parental strands
  2. Two entirely new strands only
  3. No parental material
  4. One parental and one new strand
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. One parental and one new strand

Each old strand templates a newly synthesised partner.

18. DNA polymerase ordinarily extends a strand in the direction:

  1. Both equally without conditions
  2. Neither direction
  3. 5′→3′
  4. 3′→5′
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 5′→3′

New nucleotides attach to the existing 3′ hydroxyl.

19. The leading strand is synthesised mainly:

  1. Without nucleotides
  2. Continuously towards the replication fork
  3. As short fragments only
  4. Without a template
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Continuously towards the replication fork

Template orientation permits extension along fork movement.

20. Short lagging-strand DNA pieces are called:

  1. Okazaki fragments
  2. Introns
  3. Codons
  4. Exons only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Okazaki fragments

Discontinuous synthesis produces fragments later joined together.

21. The enzyme joining appropriate DNA fragments is:

  1. Amylase
  2. Pepsin
  3. ATP synthase
  4. DNA ligase
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. DNA ligase

Ligase seals backbone breaks after fragment processing.

22. A primer is needed because common replicative DNA polymerases require a pre-existing:

  1. Neutron chain
  2. Lipid layer
  3. 3′ hydroxyl end
  4. 5′ protein end
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3′ hydroxyl end

Polymerases extend rather than initiate an entirely unprimed strand.

23. Transcription produces RNA using a:

  1. Cellulose template
  2. DNA template
  3. Protein template
  4. Lipid template
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. DNA template

RNA polymerase copies sequence information from the template strand.

24. The RNA sequence generally resembles the DNA coding strand except that:

  1. U replaces T
  2. G replaces C always
  3. A replaces every G
  4. All bases are reversed chemically
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. U replaces T

The coding strand matches RNA polarity and base order with this substitution.

25. A codon consists of:

  1. Two amino acids
  2. Four proteins
  3. One chromosome
  4. Three RNA bases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Three RNA bases

Triplet base sequences specify amino acids or translation signals.

26. The usual translation initiation codon is:

  1. UAG
  2. UGA
  3. AUG
  4. UAA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. AUG

AUG commonly begins translation and encodes methionine.

27. Which is a standard stop codon?

  1. UUU
  2. UAA
  3. AUG
  4. UGG
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. UAA

UAA, UAG and UGA normally specify translation termination.

28. An anticodon is found on:

  1. tRNA
  2. DNA polymerase
  3. A histone only
  4. A lipid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. tRNA

The tRNA triplet recognises a complementary mRNA codon.

29. In the lac operon, an inducer promotes transcription primarily by:

  1. Destroying RNA polymerase
  2. Deleting all structural genes
  3. Removing all ribosomes
  4. Reducing repressor binding to the operator
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Reducing repressor binding to the operator

Inducer interaction relieves repression under suitable regulatory conditions.

30. Eukaryotic pre-mRNA splicing removes:

  1. All phosphate groups
  2. All codons
  3. Introns
  4. All exons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Introns

Splicing joins retained exons into a processed transcript.
