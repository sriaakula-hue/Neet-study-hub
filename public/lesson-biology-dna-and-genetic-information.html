DNA and genetic information

Core idea

DNA is composed of nucleotide units containing a sugar, phosphate and nitrogenous base. In typical double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with thymine, and guanine pairs with cytosine. The strands are antiparallel.

Worked connection

If double-stranded DNA contains 20% adenine, it contains 20% thymine and 30% each of guanine and cytosine.

Watch out

RNA usually contains ribose and uracil instead of deoxyribose and thymine. DNA replication is semiconservative: each daughter duplex has one parental and one newly synthesized strand.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The two strands of a typical DNA duplex run:

Antiparallel

One strand runs 5′ to 3′ while the complementary strand runs 3′ to 5′.

2. DNA polymerases ordinarily extend a new strand in the:

5′ to 3′ direction

Nucleotides are added to the growing strand’s 3′ hydroxyl end.

3. Transcription synthesises RNA using a:

DNA template

RNA polymerase reads the appropriate DNA template strand.

4. Translation converts information in mRNA into a:

Polypeptide

Ribosomes interpret codons while transfer RNAs deliver amino acids.

5. A codon usually contains how many nucleotides?

Three

The genetic code is read in triplets; there are 64 possible triplet codons.

6. The usual initiation codon AUG also codes for:

Methionine

AUG specifies methionine and commonly initiates translation; initiation context also matters.

7. A stop codon ordinarily specifies:

Termination rather than an amino acid

Release factors recognise stop codons and terminate the polypeptide in the usual code.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. In typical double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with:

  1. Uracil
  2. Guanine
  3. Thymine
  4. Cytosine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Thymine

A pairs with T; G pairs with C.

2. Double-stranded DNA contains 20% adenine. Its guanine percentage is:

  1. 20%
  2. 30%
  3. 40%
  4. 60%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 30%

T = 20%; G + C = 60%; G = C = 30%.

3. Semiconservative DNA replication produces duplexes with:

  1. Two old strands
  2. Two new strands only
  3. One old and one new strand
  4. No parental material
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. One old and one new strand

Each daughter duplex retains one parental strand paired with a new strand.

4. The two strands of a typical DNA duplex run:

  1. Antiparallel
  2. Always parallel
  3. In unrelated rings
  4. Without direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Antiparallel

One strand runs 5′ to 3′ while the complementary strand runs 3′ to 5′.

5. DNA polymerases ordinarily extend a new strand in the:

  1. 5′ to 3′ direction
  2. 3′ to 5′ direction
  3. Either direction equally without constraint
  4. No direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 5′ to 3′ direction

Nucleotides are added to the growing strand’s 3′ hydroxyl end.

6. Transcription synthesises RNA using a:

  1. DNA template
  2. Protein template only
  3. Lipid template
  4. Glucose template
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. DNA template

RNA polymerase reads the appropriate DNA template strand.

7. Translation converts information in mRNA into a:

  1. Polypeptide
  2. DNA duplex
  3. Lipid bilayer directly
  4. Starch chain
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Polypeptide

Ribosomes interpret codons while transfer RNAs deliver amino acids.

8. A codon usually contains how many nucleotides?

  1. Three
  2. One
  3. Two
  4. Four
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Three

The genetic code is read in triplets; there are 64 possible triplet codons.

9. The usual initiation codon AUG also codes for:

  1. Methionine
  2. Glycine
  3. Tryptophan
  4. Proline
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methionine

AUG specifies methionine and commonly initiates translation; initiation context also matters.

10. A stop codon ordinarily specifies:

  1. Termination rather than an amino acid
  2. A new phosphate
  3. Only methionine
  4. Only a sugar
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Termination rather than an amino acid

Release factors recognise stop codons and terminate the polypeptide in the usual code.
