Animal Kingdom

Animal Kingdom

Key concepts

  • Animal classification uses symmetry, germ layers, body cavity, segmentation and the presence of a notochord.
  • Cnidarians possess cnidocytes. Arthropods have jointed appendages. Echinoderms have a water vascular system.
  • Chordates possess a notochord at least during development, along with a dorsal hollow nerve cord and other defining traits.

Connect the ideas

An earthworm is an annelid, a cockroach is an arthropod and a starfish is an echinoderm.

Common mistake

Adult echinoderms generally have radial symmetry, while their larvae are bilaterally symmetrical.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Jointed appendages are characteristic of:

  1. Arthropoda
  2. Porifera
  3. Cnidaria
  4. Platyhelminthes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Arthropoda

Arthropods possess jointed appendages and an exoskeleton.

2. A water vascular system is characteristic of:

  1. Annelida
  2. Mollusca
  3. Echinodermata
  4. Nematoda
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Echinodermata

Echinoderms use a water vascular system with tube feet.

3. Cnidocytes are characteristic of:

  1. Chordata
  2. Cnidaria
  3. Arthropoda
  4. Annelida
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cnidaria

Cnidocytes are the specialised stinging cells of cnidarians.

4. Poriferans characteristically have a:

  1. Canal system
  2. Vertebral column
  3. Jointed exoskeleton
  4. True coelom with organs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Canal system

Water enters pores and passes through a canal system, supporting feeding, respiration and waste removal.

5. Flatworms are generally:

  1. Acoelomate
  2. Pseudocoelomate
  3. True coelomate
  4. Radially symmetric only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acoelomate

Platyhelminthes lack a body cavity between the body wall and digestive tract.

6. Roundworms are typically:

  1. Pseudocoelomate
  2. Acoelomate
  3. Vertebrates
  4. Diploblastic cnidarians
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pseudocoelomate

Their body cavity is not completely lined by mesoderm, so it is a pseudocoelom.

7. Annelids show characteristic:

  1. Metameric segmentation
  2. Jointed appendages
  3. Notochord in all adults
  4. Water vascular systems
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Metameric segmentation

Repeated body segments with corresponding organisation are characteristic of annelids.

8. Adult echinoderms and their larvae commonly differ because adults are radial while larvae are:

  1. Bilateral
  2. Always asymmetrical
  3. Without cells
  4. Only segmented
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bilateral

This life-stage contrast is a frequent classification checkpoint.

9. A dorsal hollow nerve cord is a characteristic chordate feature, unlike the typical:

  1. Ventral solid nerve cord of many nonchordates
  2. Plant xylem
  3. Fungal hypha
  4. Animal exoskeleton
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ventral solid nerve cord of many nonchordates

Chordate neural anatomy is distinguished by dorsal position and a hollow tubular cord.

10. A mantle is characteristically associated with:

  1. Mollusca
  2. Porifera
  3. Nematoda
  4. Cnidaria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mollusca

The molluscan mantle covers the visceral mass and can secrete the shell.

11. Porifera are characterised by a body:

  1. Segmented backbone
  2. Jointed appendage system
  3. Canal system
  4. Closed vascular root
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Canal system

Water passage through pores and canals supports feeding and gas exchange.

12. The flagellated collar cells of sponges are:

  1. Osteocytes
  2. Choanocytes
  3. Cnidocytes
  4. Nephridia
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Choanocytes

Choanocytes drive water flow and trap food.

13. Cnidarians possess specialised stinging cells called:

  1. Cnidocytes
  2. Choanocytes
  3. Flame cells
  4. Chondrocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cnidocytes

These cells contain structures involved in prey capture and defence.

14. A cnidarian's basic tissue organisation is typically:

  1. Triploblastic with a true coelom
  2. Unicellular
  3. Without embryonic layers ever
  4. Diploblastic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Diploblastic

Two principal germ layers surround a mesogleal region.

15. Ctenophores move using rows of:

  1. Tube feet
  2. Parapodia
  3. Comb plates
  4. Jointed legs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Comb plates

The fused ciliary plates provide characteristic locomotion.

16. Bilateral symmetry means division into similar halves through:

  1. Only a horizontal cut
  2. One main plane
  3. Every possible plane
  4. No plane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. One main plane

Left and right sides correspond across a principal plane.

17. Platyhelminthes are generally:

  1. Acoelomate
  2. True coelomate
  3. Pseudocoelomate only
  4. Vertebrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acoelomate

They lack a body cavity between gut and body wall.

18. Flame cells function in flatworms mainly in:

  1. Only photosynthesis
  2. Only vision
  3. Only blood oxygen transport
  4. Excretion and osmoregulation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Excretion and osmoregulation

Protonephridial systems regulate fluids and remove wastes.

19. Nematodes are characteristically:

  1. Diploblastic
  2. Chordate
  3. Pseudocoelomate
  4. Acoelomate exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Pseudocoelomate

Their body cavity is not completely lined by mesoderm.

20. Annelids characteristically show:

  1. No mesoderm
  2. Metameric segmentation
  3. Only radial symmetry
  4. No organ systems
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Metameric segmentation

Repeated body units are a defining annelid feature.

21. The excretory organs of many annelids are:

  1. Nephridia
  2. Flame cells exclusively
  3. Malpighian tubules exclusively
  4. Kidneys with nephrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nephridia

Segmentally distributed nephridia process wastes and regulate fluid.

22. Arthropods possess:

  1. A notochord in every species
  2. Only unjointed cilia
  3. No exoskeleton
  4. Jointed appendages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Jointed appendages

Segmented appendages articulate at joints.

23. An arthropod exoskeleton commonly contains:

  1. Silica exclusively
  2. Bone only
  3. Chitin
  4. Cellulose exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Chitin

Chitin contributes to the protective external skeleton.

24. Molluscs typically possess a:

  1. Segmented annelid nephridia only
  2. Muscular foot
  3. Water vascular system
  4. Notochord
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Muscular foot

The foot is adapted for movement or other functions.

25. The mantle of a shelled mollusc commonly secretes the:

  1. Shell
  2. Notochord
  3. Comb plate
  4. Cnidocyst
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shell

Mantle tissue produces the characteristic shell material.

26. Adult echinoderms commonly exhibit:

  1. Only bilateral symmetry
  2. No body symmetry
  3. Metameric vertebral symmetry
  4. Radial symmetry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Radial symmetry

Many adult echinoderms have a five-part radial pattern.

27. Echinoderms have a characteristic:

  1. Canal system of choanocytes
  2. Closed plant xylem
  3. Water vascular system
  4. Tracheal system only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Water vascular system

Hydraulic structures support tube-foot movement and other functions.

28. A defining chordate feature at some life stage is a:

  1. Radula
  2. Notochord
  3. Chitinous jointed leg
  4. Cnidocyte
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Notochord

The supportive axial notochord distinguishes chordates.

29. A chordate nerve cord is typically:

  1. Dorsal and hollow
  2. Ventral and solid
  3. Lateral and woody
  4. Absent in all adults
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dorsal and hollow

The dorsal hollow neural structure is characteristic of chordates.

30. Mammals are distinguished by features including:

  1. Feathers
  2. Comb plates
  3. Malpighian tubules
  4. Mammary glands
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mammary glands

Milk-producing glands are the defining mammalian feature.
