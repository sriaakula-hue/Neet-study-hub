Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

The mole concept

A mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified entities. Always identify the entities: atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.

n = mass / molar mass

Use matching mass units. If mass is in grams, use molar mass in g/mol.

Worked example

Using a molar mass of 18 g/mol, 36 g of water is 2 mol of water molecules, approximately 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules.

Watch out

Each H₂O molecule contains three atoms. Two moles of water molecules therefore contain six moles of atoms.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Using molar mass 44 g/mol, 22 g of CO₂ contains:

0.5 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 22/44 = 0.5 mol.

2. A balanced chemical equation relates reactants through their:

Mole ratios

Stoichiometric coefficients count proportional numbers of entities, and therefore moles; they do not usually imply equal masses.

3. For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3 mol H₂ and 1 mol O₂ can form at most:

2 mol H₂O

One mole O₂ consumes two moles H₂ and forms two moles water, so oxygen limits the reaction.

4. The limiting reagent is the reactant that:

Restricts the amount of product formed

Compare available moles to the balanced reaction requirements rather than choosing by mass alone.

5. A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 56 g/mol. Molecular formula is:

C₄H₈

Empirical formula mass is 14 g/mol; 56/14 = 4, so multiply both subscripts by four.

6. Using H₂O molar mass 18 g/mol and oxygen contribution 16 g/mol, oxygen mass percent is approximately:

88.9%

The oxygen fraction is 16/18; multiplying by 100 gives approximately 88.9%.

7. The law of conservation of mass for an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system means:

Total mass remains constant

Atoms are rearranged in chemical reactions; the total mass is conserved to ordinary chemical accuracy.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Using molar mass 18 g/mol, how many moles are in 36 g of water?

  1. 0.5 mol
  2. 1 mol
  3. 2 mol
  4. 18 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 36/18 = 2 mol.

2. One mole of H₂O molecules contains how many moles of atoms?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3

Each molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom: 3 atoms in total.

3. One mole contains approximately how many specified entities?

  1. 6.022 × 10²³
  2. 6.022 × 10⁻²³
  3. 18
  4. 1000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6.022 × 10²³

The Avogadro constant is exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

4. Using molar mass 44 g/mol, 22 g of CO₂ contains:

  1. 0.5 mol
  2. 2 mol
  3. 1 mol
  4. 22 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.5 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 22/44 = 0.5 mol.

5. A balanced chemical equation relates reactants through their:

  1. Mole ratios
  2. Equal masses always
  3. Equal volumes in every phase
  4. Equal densities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mole ratios

Stoichiometric coefficients count proportional numbers of entities, and therefore moles; they do not usually imply equal masses.

6. For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3 mol H₂ and 1 mol O₂ can form at most:

  1. 2 mol H₂O
  2. 3 mol H₂O
  3. 4 mol H₂O
  4. 1 mol H₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 mol H₂O

One mole O₂ consumes two moles H₂ and forms two moles water, so oxygen limits the reaction.

7. The limiting reagent is the reactant that:

  1. Restricts the amount of product formed
  2. Has the greatest mass always
  3. Has the smallest molar mass always
  4. Is the catalyst
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Restricts the amount of product formed

Compare available moles to the balanced reaction requirements rather than choosing by mass alone.

8. A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 56 g/mol. Molecular formula is:

  1. C₄H₈
  2. CH₂
  3. C₂H₄
  4. C₃H₆
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. C₄H₈

Empirical formula mass is 14 g/mol; 56/14 = 4, so multiply both subscripts by four.

9. Using H₂O molar mass 18 g/mol and oxygen contribution 16 g/mol, oxygen mass percent is approximately:

  1. 88.9%
  2. 11.1%
  3. 50%
  4. 16%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 88.9%

The oxygen fraction is 16/18; multiplying by 100 gives approximately 88.9%.

10. The law of conservation of mass for an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system means:

  1. Total mass remains constant
  2. Every molecule remains unchanged
  3. Moles of all species are unchanged
  4. No heat can transfer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Total mass remains constant

Atoms are rearranged in chemical reactions; the total mass is conserved to ordinary chemical accuracy.
