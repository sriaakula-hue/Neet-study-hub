Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

The mole concept

A mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified entities. Always identify the entities: atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.

n = mass / molar mass

Use matching mass units. If mass is in grams, use molar mass in g/mol.

Worked example

Using a molar mass of 18 g/mol, 36 g of water is 2 mol of water molecules, approximately 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules.

Watch out

Each H₂O molecule contains three atoms. Two moles of water molecules therefore contain six moles of atoms.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Using molar mass 44 g/mol, 22 g of CO₂ contains:

0.5 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 22/44 = 0.5 mol.

2. A balanced chemical equation relates reactants through their:

Mole ratios

Stoichiometric coefficients count proportional numbers of entities, and therefore moles; they do not usually imply equal masses.

3. For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3 mol H₂ and 1 mol O₂ can form at most:

2 mol H₂O

One mole O₂ consumes two moles H₂ and forms two moles water, so oxygen limits the reaction.

4. The limiting reagent is the reactant that:

Restricts the amount of product formed

Compare available moles to the balanced reaction requirements rather than choosing by mass alone.

5. A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 56 g/mol. Molecular formula is:

C₄H₈

Empirical formula mass is 14 g/mol; 56/14 = 4, so multiply both subscripts by four.

6. Using H₂O molar mass 18 g/mol and oxygen contribution 16 g/mol, oxygen mass percent is approximately:

88.9%

The oxygen fraction is 16/18; multiplying by 100 gives approximately 88.9%.

7. The law of conservation of mass for an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system means:

Total mass remains constant

Atoms are rearranged in chemical reactions; the total mass is conserved to ordinary chemical accuracy.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Using molar mass 18 g/mol, how many moles are in 36 g of water?

  1. 0.5 mol
  2. 1 mol
  3. 2 mol
  4. 18 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 36/18 = 2 mol.

2. One mole of H₂O molecules contains how many moles of atoms?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3

Each molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom: 3 atoms in total.

3. One mole contains approximately how many specified entities?

  1. 6.022 × 10²³
  2. 6.022 × 10⁻²³
  3. 18
  4. 1000
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6.022 × 10²³

The Avogadro constant is exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

4. Using molar mass 44 g/mol, 22 g of CO₂ contains:

  1. 0.5 mol
  2. 2 mol
  3. 1 mol
  4. 22 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.5 mol

n = mass/molar mass = 22/44 = 0.5 mol.

5. A balanced chemical equation relates reactants through their:

  1. Mole ratios
  2. Equal masses always
  3. Equal volumes in every phase
  4. Equal densities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mole ratios

Stoichiometric coefficients count proportional numbers of entities, and therefore moles; they do not usually imply equal masses.

6. For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3 mol H₂ and 1 mol O₂ can form at most:

  1. 2 mol H₂O
  2. 3 mol H₂O
  3. 4 mol H₂O
  4. 1 mol H₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 mol H₂O

One mole O₂ consumes two moles H₂ and forms two moles water, so oxygen limits the reaction.

7. The limiting reagent is the reactant that:

  1. Restricts the amount of product formed
  2. Has the greatest mass always
  3. Has the smallest molar mass always
  4. Is the catalyst
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Restricts the amount of product formed

Compare available moles to the balanced reaction requirements rather than choosing by mass alone.

8. A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and molar mass 56 g/mol. Molecular formula is:

  1. C₄H₈
  2. CH₂
  3. C₂H₄
  4. C₃H₆
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. C₄H₈

Empirical formula mass is 14 g/mol; 56/14 = 4, so multiply both subscripts by four.

9. Using H₂O molar mass 18 g/mol and oxygen contribution 16 g/mol, oxygen mass percent is approximately:

  1. 88.9%
  2. 11.1%
  3. 50%
  4. 16%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 88.9%

The oxygen fraction is 16/18; multiplying by 100 gives approximately 88.9%.

10. The law of conservation of mass for an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system means:

  1. Total mass remains constant
  2. Every molecule remains unchanged
  3. Moles of all species are unchanged
  4. No heat can transfer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Total mass remains constant

Atoms are rearranged in chemical reactions; the total mass is conserved to ordinary chemical accuracy.

11. Using molar mass 32 g/mol, the mass of 0.25 mol O₂ is:

  1. 4 g
  2. 32 g
  3. 128 g
  4. 8 g
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 8 g

Mass=nM=0.25×32=8 g.

12. How many molecules are present in 0.5 mol CO₂? Use N_A=6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹.

  1. 1.2044×10²⁴
  2. 0.5
  3. 3.011×10²³
  4. 6.022×10²³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3.011×10²³

Molecule count=nN_A=0.5×6.022×10²³=3.011×10²³.

13. Two moles of NH₃ molecules contain how many moles of hydrogen atoms?

  1. 8 mol
  2. 6 mol
  3. 2 mol
  4. 3 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 6 mol

Each NH₃ molecule has three hydrogen atoms; 2×3=6 mol hydrogen atoms.

14. Using atomic masses C=12 and O=16, carbon mass percentage in CO₂ is approximately:

  1. 27.3%
  2. 72.7%
  3. 12%
  4. 50%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 27.3%

Molar mass is 44 g/mol; carbon mass fraction is 12/44. Multiply by 100 to obtain 27.3%.

15. A compound contains 40% C, 6.67% H and 53.33% O by mass. Using atomic masses 12, 1 and 16, its empirical formula is:

  1. C₂H₂O
  2. CHO₂
  3. C₂H₆O
  4. CH₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CH₂O

For 100 g, mole amounts are approximately 3.33, 6.67 and 3.33. Dividing by 3.33 gives C:H:O=1:2:1.

16. A compound has empirical formula CH₂O and molar mass 180 g/mol. Its molecular formula is:

  1. C₃H₆O₃
  2. C₁₂H₂₄O₁₂
  3. C₆H₁₂O₆
  4. CH₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. C₆H₁₂O₆

Empirical formula mass is 30 g/mol. The multiplier 180/30=6 gives C₆H₁₂O₆.

17. For N₂+3H₂→2NH₃, 2 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂ react completely as far as possible. The limiting reactant is:

  1. Neither reactant
  2. H₂
  3. N₂
  4. NH₃
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. H₂

Compare n/coefficient: N₂ gives 2/1=2, H₂ gives 3/3=1. Hydrogen has the smaller value and limits reaction.

18. For N₂+3H₂→2NH₃, 2 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂ can produce at most:

  1. 2 mol NH₃
  2. 4 mol NH₃
  3. 3 mol NH₃
  4. 6 mol NH₃
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2 mol NH₃

Three moles H₂ react with one mole N₂ to form two moles NH₃; one mole N₂ remains.

19. A reaction has theoretical yield 20 g and actual isolated yield 15 g. Percentage yield is:

  1. 133.3%
  2. 25%
  3. 5%
  4. 75%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 75%

Percentage yield=100×actual/theoretical=100×15/20=75%.

20. 0.2 mol solute is dissolved to make 500 mL solution. Molarity is:

  1. 100 mol/L
  2. 2.5 mol/L
  3. 0.4 mol/L
  4. 0.1 mol/L
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 0.4 mol/L

Convert 500 mL to 0.5 L. M=n/V=0.2/0.5=0.4 mol/L.

21. 0.5 mol solute is dissolved in 250 g solvent. Molality is:

  1. 500 mol/kg
  2. 2 mol/kg
  3. 0.125 mol/kg
  4. 0.5 mol/kg
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 mol/kg

Molality uses solvent mass in kilograms: 0.5/0.250=2 mol/kg.

22. A mixture contains 2 mol ethanol and 3 mol water. Mole fraction of ethanol is:

  1. 0.4
  2. 0.6
  3. 2/3
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.4

Total amount is 5 mol; ethanol mole fraction is 2/5=0.4.

23. 100 mL of 2 mol/L solution is diluted to 500 mL without reaction. Final molarity is:

  1. 10 mol/L
  2. 1 mol/L
  3. 2 mol/L
  4. 0.4 mol/L
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.4 mol/L

M₁V₁=M₂V₂, so M₂=2×100/500=0.4 mol/L.

24. 10 g solute mixed with 90 g solvent gives a solution with solute mass percentage:

  1. 90%
  2. 1%
  3. 10%
  4. 11.1%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10%

Solution mass=10+90=100 g. Solute mass percentage=100×10/100=10%.

25. At 273.15 K and 1 atm, an ideal gas has molar volume approximately 22.4 L/mol. Volume of 0.5 mol is:

  1. 5.6 L
  2. 11.2 L
  3. 22.4 L
  4. 44.8 L
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 11.2 L

At these stated conditions, V=nV_m=0.5×22.4=11.2 L.

26. At the same temperature and pressure, 1 L ideal H₂ and 1 L ideal O₂ contain:

  1. Equal numbers of molecules
  2. Equal masses
  3. Equal numbers of oxygen atoms
  4. Equal mass densities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Equal numbers of molecules

Avogadro's law gives equal molecule counts for equal ideal-gas volumes at the same T and P, despite differing molar masses.

27. The number of significant figures in 0.00450 is:

  1. 2
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3

Leading zeros are placeholders; 4, 5 and the final decimal zero are significant.

28. To the correct decimal place, 12.11+0.3 equals:

  1. 12
  2. 12.410
  3. 12.4
  4. 12.41
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 12.4

Addition is rounded to the least precise decimal place. The 0.3 term has one decimal place, so 12.41 rounds to 12.4.

29. Using atomic masses Ca=40, C=12 and O=16, molar mass of CaCO₃ is:

  1. 116 g/mol
  2. 100 g/mol
  3. 68 g/mol
  4. 84 g/mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 100 g/mol

Add 40+12+3×16=100 g/mol.

30. A pure sample contains 3.011×10²³ helium atoms. Using N_A=6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹, its amount is:

  1. 0.5 mol
  2. 2 mol
  3. 1 mol
  4. 3.011 mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.5 mol

n=N/N_A=(3.011×10²³)/(6.022×10²³)=0.5 mol.
