Purification and Characterisation of Organic Compounds

Purification and Characterisation of Organic Compounds

Key concepts

  • Purification methods include crystallisation, distillation, sublimation, extraction and chromatography; the suitable method depends on physical properties.
  • Chromatography separates components through differences in interaction with stationary and mobile phases.
  • An empirical formula gives the simplest atom ratio. A molecular formula is an integer multiple of the empirical formula.

Worked connection

If the empirical formula is CH₂O, its formula mass is about 30 g/mol. A molar mass of 180 g/mol gives molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆.

Watch out

Separation methods do not all suit every substance. A substance may decompose before reaching a useful boiling temperature.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Crystallisation separates substances mainly by differences in:

Solubility under chosen conditions

A suitable solvent dissolves the target when hot and allows selective crystallisation on cooling.

2. Sublimation can purify a solid that:

Vaporises directly and recondenses as a solid

The sublimable component can be separated from nonvolatile impurities under suitable conditions.

3. Simple distillation is especially suitable when a liquid must be separated from:

Nonvolatile dissolved impurities

Vaporising and condensing the volatile liquid can leave nonvolatile impurities behind.

4. Fractional distillation improves separation of liquids with:

Relatively close boiling points

Repeated vaporisation–condensation in a fractionating column improves enrichment when boiling points are close.

5. In extraction, a solute distributes between:

Two suitable immiscible phases

Solvent extraction relies on different affinities for the phases, often two immiscible liquids.

6. A chromatographic spot moves 3 cm while solvent front moves 6 cm. Rf is:

0.5

Rf is spot distance divided by solvent-front distance from the same origin: 3/6 = 0.5.

7. Qualitative elemental analysis determines chiefly:

Which elements are present

Qualitative tests detect presence; quantitative analysis measures amounts or percentages.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. An empirical formula expresses the:

  1. Actual molecule size always
  2. Simplest whole-number atom ratio
  3. Only the charge
  4. Only boiling point
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Simplest whole-number atom ratio

It represents relative atom counts in their simplest ratio.

2. CH₂O has formula mass 30 g/mol. A compound of molar mass 180 g/mol has molecular formula:

  1. CH₂O
  2. C₂H₄O₂
  3. C₆H₁₂O₆
  4. C₁₈H₃₆O₁₈
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. C₆H₁₂O₆

The multiplier is 180/30 = 6.

3. Chromatographic separation depends on differing:

  1. Phase interactions
  2. Nuclear masses only
  3. Numbers of protons only
  4. Gravitational directions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Phase interactions

Components interact differently with mobile and stationary phases.

4. Crystallisation separates substances mainly by differences in:

  1. Solubility under chosen conditions
  2. Nuclear charge alone
  3. Magnetic poles only
  4. Only gas pressure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Solubility under chosen conditions

A suitable solvent dissolves the target when hot and allows selective crystallisation on cooling.

5. Sublimation can purify a solid that:

  1. Vaporises directly and recondenses as a solid
  2. Only melts without vaporising
  3. Is permanently ionic in water
  4. Cannot change phase
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Vaporises directly and recondenses as a solid

The sublimable component can be separated from nonvolatile impurities under suitable conditions.

6. Simple distillation is especially suitable when a liquid must be separated from:

  1. Nonvolatile dissolved impurities
  2. Two liquids with almost identical boiling points only
  3. Every azeotrope completely
  4. A substance that always decomposes immediately
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nonvolatile dissolved impurities

Vaporising and condensing the volatile liquid can leave nonvolatile impurities behind.

7. Fractional distillation improves separation of liquids with:

  1. Relatively close boiling points
  2. Identical chemical identity only
  3. No volatility
  4. Only different colours
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Relatively close boiling points

Repeated vaporisation–condensation in a fractionating column improves enrichment when boiling points are close.

8. In extraction, a solute distributes between:

  1. Two suitable immiscible phases
  2. Only one uniform phase
  3. Only two solids at rest
  4. A nucleus and an electron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two suitable immiscible phases

Solvent extraction relies on different affinities for the phases, often two immiscible liquids.

9. A chromatographic spot moves 3 cm while solvent front moves 6 cm. Rf is:

  1. 0.5
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.5

Rf is spot distance divided by solvent-front distance from the same origin: 3/6 = 0.5.

10. Qualitative elemental analysis determines chiefly:

  1. Which elements are present
  2. Only molecular geometry
  3. Only exact molar mass
  4. Only boiling point
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Which elements are present

Qualitative tests detect presence; quantitative analysis measures amounts or percentages.

11. A solid that changes directly from solid to vapour can be purified by:

  1. Hydrolysis
  2. Neutralisation only
  3. Sublimation
  4. Electrophoresis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sublimation

Sublimation separates a volatile solid from nonvolatile impurities.

12. Recrystallisation is most effective when a solute is:

  1. Always gaseous
  2. Much more soluble hot than cold
  3. Insoluble at every temperature
  4. Identical in solubility to all impurities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Much more soluble hot than cold

Cooling a hot saturated solution allows the desired solid to crystallise preferentially.

13. Hot filtration during recrystallisation principally removes:

  1. Insoluble impurities
  2. All dissolved product
  3. Only protons
  4. All solvent molecules
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Insoluble impurities

Filtering the hot solution removes solids while keeping the desired solute dissolved.

14. Simple distillation is suitable when liquid components have:

  1. Exactly identical volatility
  2. No boiling point
  3. Only solid phases
  4. Sufficiently different boiling points
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sufficiently different boiling points

A sufficiently large volatility difference permits useful separation without a fractionating column.

15. The purpose of a fractionating column is repeated:

  1. Only solvent freezing
  2. Only crystal weighing
  3. Vapour-liquid equilibration
  4. Only neutron separation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Vapour-liquid equilibration

Repeated vaporisation and condensation improve separation of volatile components.

16. Steam distillation is useful for compounds that are:

  1. Only elemental metals
  2. Steam volatile and largely immiscible with water
  3. Always ionic salts
  4. Nonvolatile at every temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Steam volatile and largely immiscible with water

Joint vapour pressure allows suitable organic compounds to distil below their usual boiling point.

17. Distillation under reduced pressure helps protect compounds that:

  1. Decompose near their normal boiling point
  2. Have no vapour pressure
  3. Are all insoluble gases
  4. Contain no electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Decompose near their normal boiling point

Reduced external pressure lowers the boiling temperature.

18. Solvent extraction relies mainly on differing:

  1. Atomic numbers
  2. Nuclear spin counts
  3. Conservation of light
  4. Distribution between immiscible solvents
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Distribution between immiscible solvents

A solute partitions between two liquid phases according to its relative affinity.

19. In paper chromatography, separation depends on differing:

  1. Only container colour
  2. Only nucleus size
  3. Interactions with stationary and mobile phases
  4. Only sample mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Interactions with stationary and mobile phases

Different affinities cause different migration rates.

20. In TLC, the stationary phase commonly includes:

  1. Pure methane
  2. Silica gel
  3. Only oxygen gas
  4. Molten sodium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Silica gel

A thin adsorbent coating provides the stationary surface.

21. The chromatographic R_f value is measured relative to the:

  1. Solvent-front travel distance
  2. Plate width
  3. Sample mass
  4. Beaker diameter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Solvent-front travel distance

R_f is solute distance from origin divided by solvent-front distance.

22. A solute travels 3 cm while the solvent front travels 6 cm. Its R_f is:

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 6
  4. 0.5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.5

Using the common origin, R_f=3/6=0.5.

23. An R_f comparison is meaningful only when conditions such as solvent and adsorbent are:

  1. Unrecorded by definition
  2. Always absent
  3. Comparable
  4. Completely unrelated
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Comparable

Migration depends on the experimental system, so conditions must be matched.

24. Lassaigne's sodium-fusion test converts covalently bound heteroatoms into:

  1. Only oxygen gas
  2. Water-soluble ionic salts
  3. Only carbon dioxide
  4. Only free radicals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Water-soluble ionic salts

Fusion with sodium enables detection of nitrogen, sulfur and halogens in ionic forms.

25. In the nitrogen sodium-fusion test, the characteristic final colour is:

  1. Prussian blue
  2. Brick red
  3. Violet iodine
  4. White silver mirror
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prussian blue

Cyanide-derived iron chemistry can produce ferric ferrocyanide, the Prussian-blue material.

26. Sulfur in a sodium-fusion extract can give a violet colour with:

  1. Benedict reagent
  2. Only ethanol
  3. Dilute sodium chloride
  4. Sodium nitroprusside
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sodium nitroprusside

Sulfide reacts with nitroprusside to give the characteristic coloured complex.

27. Before a silver-nitrate halogen test, acidification and treatment help remove interference from:

  1. Only water
  2. Only inert nitrogen gas
  3. Cyanide and sulfide
  4. Only carbon atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cyanide and sulfide

These ions can also precipitate silver salts and confuse halide detection.

28. AgCl precipitate is usually:

  1. Black in fresh pure form
  2. White
  3. Yellow-orange
  4. Deep blue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. White

Silver chloride is a white precipitate under the standard qualitative test.

29. AgI precipitate is usually:

  1. Yellow
  2. White
  3. Red
  4. Green
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Yellow

Silver iodide has the familiar yellow appearance in halide testing.

30. Combustion analysis determines carbon and hydrogen from measured:

  1. N₂ and NaCl
  2. O₂ and HCl only
  3. Only residual ash
  4. CO₂ and H₂O
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CO₂ and H₂O

Complete oxidation converts carbon to carbon dioxide and hydrogen to water for quantification.
