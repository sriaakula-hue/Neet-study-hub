Biotechnology and Its Applications

Biotechnology and Its Applications

Key concepts

  • Biotechnology applications include recombinant therapeutic proteins, molecular diagnostics and genetically modified crops.
  • Recombinant human insulin can be produced using engineered microorganisms. Bt crops express insecticidal proteins derived from Bacillus thuringiensis.
  • Gene therapy aims to address disease by modifying or supplying genetic material. Applications require appropriate testing and biosafety oversight.

Connect the ideas

Bt proteins target susceptible insects; they do not make a crop universally resistant to every pest or disease.

Common mistake

Biotechnology effects depend on the trait, organism and context. A crop label alone does not imply every possible benefit.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Specific nucleic-acid sequence

Gut environment

Certain Bt proteins become active in susceptible insect gut conditions and damage the gut epithelium.

3. RNA interference can reduce gene expression through:

Sequence-specific RNA-mediated silencing

Small RNA pathways target matching RNA, reducing its expression or availability.

4. A transgenic organism contains:

Introduced genetic material from an engineering procedure

The introduced sequence can confer or study a trait, with expression depending on the construct and host.

5. Recombinant protein production needs not only the coding sequence but also suitable:

Expression and processing conditions

Regulatory sequences, host machinery and protein processing determine production and functional form.

6. Biosafety assessment considers:

Potential effects on people and the environment

Assessment depends on the engineered trait, exposure and use context, rather than a label alone.

7. Biopiracy concerns unauthorised exploitation of biological resources or associated knowledge without appropriate:

Consent or benefit arrangements

It is an ethical and legal resource-use issue; specific requirements depend on applicable rules and agreements.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Bt in Bt crops refers to:

  1. Bacillus thuringiensis
  2. Blood transfusion
  3. Botanical taxonomy
  4. Bicarbonate transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bacillus thuringiensis

Bt genes originate from Bacillus thuringiensis.

2. Recombinant human insulin production is an application of:

  1. Genetic engineering
  2. Only photosynthesis
  3. Only classical anatomy
  4. Only mineral nutrition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Genetic engineering

Engineered organisms can express human insulin components.

3. Gene therapy primarily involves:

  1. Modifying or supplying genetic material
  2. Replacing every cell with bacteria
  3. Only changing diet
  4. Only measuring pulse
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Modifying or supplying genetic material

Gene therapy uses genetic material to address disease mechanisms.

4. PCR-based detection can identify a target by amplifying its:

  1. Specific nucleic-acid sequence
  2. Only protein weight
  3. Only tissue colour
  4. Only whole organ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Specific nucleic-acid sequence

Amplification can make a low-abundance target detectable; controls and specificity are essential.

5. Bt toxin activity depends on the susceptible insect and can involve activation in its:

  1. Gut environment
  2. Blood-group system
  3. Lung
  4. Kidney
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gut environment

Certain Bt proteins become active in susceptible insect gut conditions and damage the gut epithelium.

6. RNA interference can reduce gene expression through:

  1. Sequence-specific RNA-mediated silencing
  2. Only chromosome doubling
  3. Only membrane thickening
  4. Only glucose oxidation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sequence-specific RNA-mediated silencing

Small RNA pathways target matching RNA, reducing its expression or availability.

7. A transgenic organism contains:

  1. Introduced genetic material from an engineering procedure
  2. No DNA
  3. Only identical copies of every original gene
  4. Only natural spores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Introduced genetic material from an engineering procedure

The introduced sequence can confer or study a trait, with expression depending on the construct and host.

8. Recombinant protein production needs not only the coding sequence but also suitable:

  1. Expression and processing conditions
  2. Only a labelled bottle
  3. Only the protein colour
  4. Only an empty vector
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Expression and processing conditions

Regulatory sequences, host machinery and protein processing determine production and functional form.

9. Biosafety assessment considers:

  1. Potential effects on people and the environment
  2. Only product packaging
  3. Only the organism’s name
  4. Only purchase price
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Potential effects on people and the environment

Assessment depends on the engineered trait, exposure and use context, rather than a label alone.

10. Biopiracy concerns unauthorised exploitation of biological resources or associated knowledge without appropriate:

  1. Consent or benefit arrangements
  2. Only electron transfer
  3. Only DNA replication
  4. Only a microscope
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Consent or benefit arrangements

It is an ethical and legal resource-use issue; specific requirements depend on applicable rules and agreements.

11. Bt crops contain genes derived from:

  1. Rhizobium exclusively
  2. Saccharomyces only
  3. Bacillus thuringiensis
  4. Plasmodium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Bacillus thuringiensis

Certain bacterial insect-active proteins are expressed in the engineered plant.

12. A Bt toxin's action depends on the:

  1. Only soil colour
  2. Susceptible insect and relevant toxin type
  3. All animals being equally susceptible
  4. Only plant height
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Susceptible insect and relevant toxin type

Different Bt proteins target particular susceptible insect groups.

13. In the standard textbook account, an ingested Bt protoxin is activated in the insect's:

  1. Alkaline gut environment
  2. Human stomach by definition
  3. Leaf chloroplast
  4. Pollen nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Alkaline gut environment

Activation and receptor interactions enable toxic action in susceptible larvae.

14. RNA interference involves sequence-specific reduction of expression through:

  1. Only protein digestion
  2. Only isotope separation
  3. Only membrane rupture
  4. RNA-mediated mechanisms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. RNA-mediated mechanisms

RNA recognition can silence matching gene expression.

15. Double-stranded RNA can trigger:

  1. Only DNA fertilisation
  2. Only mitosis in every cell
  3. RNA interference
  4. Only aerobic respiration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. RNA interference

Processing into regulatory RNAs supports sequence-specific silencing.

16. Recombinant human insulin production uses microorganisms engineered to express:

  1. Only viral capsid proteins
  2. Human insulin-related sequences
  3. Only plant cellulose genes
  4. Only bacterial photosynthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Human insulin-related sequences

Appropriate expression and processing provide the therapeutic protein.

17. Mature insulin contains polypeptide chains commonly designated:

  1. A and B
  2. X and Y
  3. Alpha, beta and gamma as three chains
  4. Heavy and light antibody chains
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A and B

Two chains are linked through disulfide bonds.

18. The connecting peptide present in proinsulin is the:

  1. A peptide only
  2. B peptide only
  3. Z peptide
  4. C peptide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. C peptide

Processing removes the connecting segment to produce mature insulin.

19. Gene therapy aims to modify genetic function in:

  1. Only food minerals
  2. Only unrelated rocks
  3. Appropriate patient cells
  4. Only rainfall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Appropriate patient cells

The strategy addresses a biological defect through genetic intervention.

20. A standard textbook gene-therapy example concerns deficiency of:

  1. Nitrogenase
  2. Adenosine deaminase
  3. Amylase only
  4. Rubisco
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Adenosine deaminase

ADA deficiency affects immune-cell function in the familiar example.

21. A molecular diagnostic PCR test detects suitable:

  1. Nucleic-acid sequences
  2. Only total body mass
  3. Only skin colour
  4. Only pulse rate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nucleic-acid sequences

Amplification can reveal a target genetic sequence.

22. ELISA relies on specific interaction between:

  1. Only DNA and cellulose
  2. Only fats and starch
  3. Only calcium and iron
  4. Antigen and antibody
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Antigen and antibody

Immunological recognition supports target detection.

23. Transgenic animals carry introduced genetic material that can be:

  1. Always absent from every nucleus
  2. Only dietary protein
  3. Expressed in their cells
  4. Only outside their body forever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Expressed in their cells

The engineered sequence can alter a suitable biological function.

24. Transgenic research can help study:

  1. Only atomic weights
  2. Gene function and disease mechanisms
  3. Only geological age
  4. Only rainfall totals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gene function and disease mechanisms

Controlled genetic modifications provide experimental models.

25. A genetically modified crop can be designed for a particular:

  1. Agronomic trait
  2. Guaranteed benefit in every environment
  3. Complete immunity to all pests
  4. Unlimited growth without nutrients
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Agronomic trait

Performance depends on the engineered trait and field context.

26. Biofortification aims to improve a crop's:

  1. Only its taxonomic name
  2. Only its isotope ratio
  3. Only its seed count with no nutrition effect
  4. Nutritional qualities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nutritional qualities

Enhanced nutrient content or quality is the central goal.

27. A biosafety assessment evaluates possible effects on health and the:

  1. Only calendar
  2. Only astronomical distance
  3. Environment
  4. Only currency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Environment

Safety evaluation examines relevant biological risks and exposure.

28. Biopiracy concerns unauthorised or inequitable use of biological resources and associated:

  1. Only space dust
  2. Traditional knowledge
  3. Only electrical circuits
  4. Only synthetic glass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Traditional knowledge

Resource and knowledge use raises rights and benefit-sharing issues.

29. A patent concerns a legally defined claim to an:

  1. Invention
  2. Entire natural ecosystem automatically
  3. Every known plant species automatically
  4. All traditional knowledge without conditions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Invention

Patent protection is specific and subject to applicable requirements.

30. Genetic engineering differs from ordinary selection because it can directly manipulate:

  1. Only external labels
  2. Only seasonal rainfall
  3. Only body weight after growth
  4. DNA sequences
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. DNA sequences

Targeted DNA modification changes the genetic information itself.
