Physics and Measurement

Physics and Measurement

Key concepts

  • SI uses base units such as metre, kilogram and second. Derived quantities combine base dimensions.
  • Force has dimensions MLT⁻²; energy has dimensions ML²T⁻². Equations must be dimensionally consistent.
  • Least count is the smallest readable increment of an instrument. Significant figures reflect how measurements are reported.

Worked example

For F = ma, mass has dimension M and acceleration LT⁻², giving MLT⁻². The result agrees with the dimension of force.

Watch out

Dimensional consistency is necessary but does not prove an equation correct or determine dimensionless numerical factors.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The dimensional formula of pressure is:

ML⁻¹T⁻²

Pressure is force per area: MLT⁻² divided by L² gives ML⁻¹T⁻².

2. A length is (20.0 ± 0.2) cm. Percentage uncertainty is:

1%

Relative uncertainty is 0.2/20.0 = 0.01; multiply by 100 for 1%.

3. The number of significant figures in 0.00450 is:

3

Leading zeros do not count; the digits 4, 5 and the trailing decimal zero are significant.

4. For independent worst-case bounds in a product xy, approximate relative error is:

Δx/x + Δy/y

For small bounded uncertainties, maximum fractional errors add in multiplication; this is not the statistical root-sum-square rule.

5. A zero error of +0.02 cm requires a reading correction of:

−0.02 cm

Corrected reading equals observed reading minus signed zero error.

6. The SI unit of frequency is:

Hertz

Frequency is cycles per second, expressed in hertz (s⁻¹).

7. Adding a length directly to a time in a physical equation is:

Dimensionally invalid

Terms combined by addition must have the same dimensions.

Practice questions and explanations

1. The SI base unit of mass is:

  1. Gram
  2. Kilogram
  3. Newton
  4. Joule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Kilogram

The kilogram is the SI base unit of mass.

2. The dimensional formula of energy is:

  1. MLT⁻²
  2. ML²T⁻²
  3. ML⁻¹T⁻²
  4. LT⁻¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. ML²T⁻²

Work is force times displacement, giving ML²T⁻².

3. Dimensional analysis alone cannot determine a:

  1. Dimensionless numerical coefficient
  2. Quantity dimension
  3. Unit consistency
  4. Required dimension of a constant
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dimensionless numerical coefficient

A numerical factor such as ½ carries no dimensions.

4. The dimensional formula of pressure is:

  1. ML⁻¹T⁻²
  2. MLT⁻²
  3. ML²T⁻²
  4. LT⁻¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ML⁻¹T⁻²

Pressure is force per area: MLT⁻² divided by L² gives ML⁻¹T⁻².

5. A length is (20.0 ± 0.2) cm. Percentage uncertainty is:

  1. 10%
  2. 4%
  3. 1%
  4. 0.1%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1%

Relative uncertainty is 0.2/20.0 = 0.01; multiply by 100 for 1%.

6. A balance displays 7.020 g. How many significant figures are reported?

  1. 4
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4

The digits 7 and 2, the zero between nonzero digits, and the final decimal zero are all significant: four figures.

7. For independent worst-case bounds in a product xy, approximate relative error is:

  1. Δx/x + Δy/y
  2. Δx + Δy only
  3. Always zero
  4. Δx/x − Δy/y
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Δx/x + Δy/y

For small bounded uncertainties, maximum fractional errors add in multiplication; this is not the statistical root-sum-square rule.

8. A zero error of +0.02 cm requires a reading correction of:

  1. −0.02 cm
  2. +0.02 cm
  3. Zero always
  4. Double the reading
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. −0.02 cm

Corrected reading equals observed reading minus signed zero error.

9. The SI unit of frequency is:

  1. Hertz
  2. Newton
  3. Pascal
  4. Watt
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hertz

Frequency is cycles per second, expressed in hertz (s⁻¹).

10. Adding a length directly to a time in a physical equation is:

  1. Dimensionally invalid
  2. Always valid
  3. Valid if both are positive
  4. Valid only in SI
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dimensionally invalid

Terms combined by addition must have the same dimensions.

11. A length is measured as 20 cm with maximum absolute error 0.2 cm. Percentage error is:

  1. 2 %
  2. 1 %
  3. 0.5 %
  4. 1.5 %
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1 %

Percentage error is 100×absolute error/measured value. Answer: 1 %.

12. A square has side 2 m with small maximum relative uncertainty 1%. Maximum absolute uncertainty in area is approximately:

  1. 0.08 m²
  2. 0.04 m²
  3. 0.12 m²
  4. 0.16 m²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.08 m²

Since A=L², relative area uncertainty is approximately 2%; multiply 0.02 by area. Answer: 0.08 m².
