Breathing and Exchange of Gases

Breathing and Exchange of Gases

Key concepts

  • Inspiration usually involves diaphragm contraction, increased thoracic volume and decreased intrapulmonary pressure.
  • Gas exchange across alveoli occurs by diffusion along partial-pressure gradients. Most oxygen is carried bound to haemoglobin.
  • Most carbon dioxide is transported as bicarbonate ions. Breathing is ventilation; cellular respiration is a different process.

Connect the ideas

When alveolar oxygen partial pressure exceeds that of arriving venous blood, oxygen diffuses into blood.

Common mistake

Arteries and veins are defined by direction of flow relative to the heart, not by oxygen content.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Alveoli support gas exchange partly because of their:

Large area and thin exchange barrier

Large surface area, thin diffusion distance and capillary supply support efficient exchange.

2. Normal quiet expiration is chiefly associated with:

Relaxation of inspiratory muscles and elastic recoil

Quiet expiration is mostly passive; forced expiration recruits additional muscles.

3. Gas diffusion direction is governed mainly by differences in:

Partial pressure

Each gas diffuses down its own partial-pressure gradient across the exchange membrane.

4. At metabolically active tissues, oxygen tends to move from:

Blood to tissues

Tissue oxygen consumption lowers local oxygen partial pressure relative to arriving arterial blood.

5. Carbonic anhydrase accelerates conversion involving CO₂, water and:

Carbonic acid/bicarbonate system

Rapid reversible CO₂ hydration supports bicarbonate transport and gas unloading.

6. Tidal volume refers to air moved during:

A normal quiet breath

It is the volume inspired or expired in an ordinary breathing cycle.

7. Residual volume is air remaining after:

Maximum forced expiration

Lungs retain air even after maximal expiration; it is not measured directly by simple spirometry.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. During normal inspiration, the diaphragm:

  1. Relaxes and moves upward
  2. Contracts and flattens
  3. Stops moving permanently
  4. Closes the trachea
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Contracts and flattens

Diaphragm contraction increases thoracic volume.

2. Most oxygen in blood is transported:

  1. As bicarbonate
  2. Bound to haemoglobin
  3. As carbonic acid
  4. Only dissolved in plasma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Bound to haemoglobin

Most oxygen is carried by haemoglobin.

3. Most carbon dioxide in blood is carried as:

  1. Bicarbonate ions
  2. Oxyhaemoglobin
  3. Pure gas bubbles
  4. Glucose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bicarbonate ions

Bicarbonate is the major transport form of CO₂.

4. Alveoli support gas exchange partly because of their:

  1. Large area and thin exchange barrier
  2. Thick cartilage walls
  3. Complete lack of blood supply
  4. High sugar storage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Large area and thin exchange barrier

Large surface area, thin diffusion distance and capillary supply support efficient exchange.

5. Normal quiet expiration is chiefly associated with:

  1. Relaxation of inspiratory muscles and elastic recoil
  2. Active diaphragm contraction
  3. No volume change
  4. Closing every alveolus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Relaxation of inspiratory muscles and elastic recoil

Quiet expiration is mostly passive; forced expiration recruits additional muscles.

6. Gas diffusion direction is governed mainly by differences in:

  1. Partial pressure
  2. Only total fluid mass
  3. Only temperature in every case
  4. Only blood type
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Partial pressure

Each gas diffuses down its own partial-pressure gradient across the exchange membrane.

7. At metabolically active tissues, oxygen tends to move from:

  1. Blood to tissues
  2. Tissues to blood always
  3. Air directly to cells without blood
  4. Only red cells to plasma and never tissue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Blood to tissues

Tissue oxygen consumption lowers local oxygen partial pressure relative to arriving arterial blood.

8. Carbonic anhydrase accelerates conversion involving CO₂, water and:

  1. Carbonic acid/bicarbonate system
  2. Only glucose
  3. Only haemoglobin synthesis
  4. Only urea
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carbonic acid/bicarbonate system

Rapid reversible CO₂ hydration supports bicarbonate transport and gas unloading.

9. Tidal volume refers to air moved during:

  1. A normal quiet breath
  2. Maximum forced expiration only
  3. Only the air remaining after maximum expiration
  4. A complete day
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A normal quiet breath

It is the volume inspired or expired in an ordinary breathing cycle.

10. Residual volume is air remaining after:

  1. Maximum forced expiration
  2. A normal inspiration only
  3. Every blood transfusion
  4. Complete lung removal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Maximum forced expiration

Lungs retain air even after maximal expiration; it is not measured directly by simple spirometry.

11. The principal muscular sheet involved in normal inspiration is the:

  1. Alveolus
  2. Diaphragm
  3. Pleura
  4. Epiglottis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Diaphragm

Its contraction enlarges thoracic volume.

12. During normal inspiration, thoracic volume:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases
  3. Stays fixed
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increases

Volume increase reduces pressure below atmospheric pressure.

13. Normal quiet expiration is largely:

  1. Only active diaphragm contraction
  2. Only heart pumping
  3. Only swallowing
  4. Passive recoil
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Passive recoil

Relaxation and elastic recoil reduce lung volume.

14. The gas-exchange surfaces of lungs are primarily the:

  1. Nasal hairs
  2. Vocal cords
  3. Alveoli
  4. Tracheal cartilage rings
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alveoli

Thin alveolar and capillary barriers support diffusion.

15. Surfactant helps reduce alveolar:

  1. Blood osmotic pressure only
  2. Surface tension
  3. Haemoglobin concentration
  4. Chromosome count
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Surface tension

Reduced tension helps stabilise small air spaces.

16. Gas diffusion across the respiratory membrane follows differences in:

  1. Partial pressure
  2. Only blood group
  3. Only nucleus size
  4. Only body mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Partial pressure

Gases move down their own partial-pressure gradients.

17. Most oxygen in blood is transported bound to:

  1. Albumin exclusively
  2. Urea
  3. Glucose
  4. Haemoglobin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Haemoglobin

Haemoglobin binds oxygen reversibly in red cells.

18. Most carbon dioxide is transported chiefly as:

  1. Only carbaminohaemoglobin
  2. Only carbonate crystals
  3. Bicarbonate
  4. Only dissolved CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Bicarbonate

Conversion to bicarbonate accounts for the largest fraction.

19. The enzyme accelerating CO₂ hydration in red cells is:

  1. Lipase
  2. Carbonic anhydrase
  3. Amylase
  4. Pepsin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Carbonic anhydrase

It speeds interconversion of carbon dioxide and carbonic-acid-related species.

20. Oxygen dissociation from haemoglobin is favoured by increased tissue:

  1. CO₂ and acidity
  2. Only oxygen pressure
  3. Only pH increase
  4. Only low temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CO₂ and acidity

These conditions shift binding towards oxygen unloading.

21. The usual oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve is:

  1. Straight through all pressures
  2. Rectangular
  3. Perfectly flat
  4. Sigmoid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Sigmoid

Cooperative binding creates the familiar S-shaped relation.

22. Air passes from the larynx into the:

  1. Aorta
  2. Ureter
  3. Trachea
  4. Oesophagus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Trachea

The trachea conducts inhaled air towards the branching bronchial system.

23. Residual volume remains after:

  1. Every breath with no expiration
  2. Maximal forced expiration
  3. An ordinary inspiration only
  4. Only birth
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Maximal forced expiration

Even maximal expiration does not empty all lung air.

24. Vital capacity excludes:

  1. Residual volume
  2. Tidal volume
  3. Inspiratory reserve
  4. Expiratory reserve
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Residual volume

Vital capacity spans the maximal usable expiratory excursion.

25. Total lung capacity equals vital capacity plus:

  1. Only tidal volume
  2. Only dead space
  3. Only inspiratory reserve
  4. Residual volume
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Residual volume

Adding retained air gives the total maximal lung volume.

26. Anatomical dead space is air in conducting passages that does not directly participate in:

  1. All temperature regulation
  2. All filtering
  3. Alveolar gas exchange
  4. All air movement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alveolar gas exchange

These passages conduct air without alveolar exchange surfaces.

27. The epiglottis helps prevent swallowed material entering the:

  1. Large intestine
  2. Glottis and airway
  3. Stomach
  4. Oesophagus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Glottis and airway

Its movement protects the respiratory entry during swallowing.

28. Respiratory rhythm regulation prominently involves centres in the:

  1. Medulla
  2. Cerebellar cortex alone
  3. Retina
  4. Pancreas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Medulla

Brainstem networks regulate the breathing rhythm.

29. Respiratory chemosensitive responses are strongly influenced by:

  1. Only blood sodium isotope
  2. Only melanin
  3. Only starch concentration
  4. CO₂-related H⁺ changes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CO₂-related H⁺ changes

Carbon dioxide affects acidity, providing an important regulatory signal.

30. Respiratory gas exchange in a healthy alveolus is facilitated by a barrier that is:

  1. Dry and avascular
  2. Made only of bone
  3. Thin and well perfused
  4. Thick and impermeable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Thin and well perfused

Short diffusion distance and capillary supply support rapid exchange.
