Body Fluids and Circulation

Body Fluids and Circulation

Key concepts

  • The human heart has two atria and two ventricles. Pulmonary circulation connects heart and lungs; systemic circulation connects heart and the rest of the body.
  • The sinoatrial node normally initiates the heartbeat. Valves help prevent backward flow.
  • Red blood cells transport respiratory gases, white blood cells contribute to defence and platelets contribute to haemostasis.

Connect the ideas

Blood flows from the right ventricle to the lungs through pulmonary arteries, then returns to the left atrium through pulmonary veins.

Common mistake

Pulmonary arteries carry relatively deoxygenated blood; pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The left ventricle pumps blood into the:

Aorta

Systemic circulation begins with left-ventricular ejection into the aorta.

2. The right ventricle pumps blood into the:

Pulmonary artery

Pulmonary arterial flow carries blood toward the lungs.

3. The tricuspid valve lies between the:

Right atrium and right ventricle

It prevents backward flow to the right atrium during right-ventricular contraction.

4. The mitral valve lies between the:

Left atrium and left ventricle

The bicuspid or mitral valve is the left atrioventricular valve.

5. Systole refers to a chamber’s:

Contraction phase

Diastole is relaxation; systole is contraction. State which chamber is meant when timing events.

6. In an ECG, the QRS complex chiefly represents:

Ventricular depolarisation

The electrical depolarisation of ventricles precedes their mechanical contraction.

7. Human mature red blood cells normally lack:

A nucleus

Loss of the nucleus and other organelles accompanies maturation and supports the specialised transport role.

1. The normal pacemaker of the human heart is the:

  1. AV valve
  2. Sinoatrial node
  3. Aorta
  4. Left ventricle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sinoatrial node

The SA node normally initiates cardiac impulses.

2. Pulmonary veins return blood to the:

  1. Right atrium
  2. Right ventricle
  3. Left atrium
  4. Left ventricle directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Left atrium

Oxygenated blood from lungs enters the left atrium.

3. Platelets mainly contribute to:

  1. Oxygen binding
  2. Blood clotting
  3. Bile secretion
  4. Glucose absorption
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Blood clotting

Platelets participate in haemostasis and clot formation.

4. The left ventricle pumps blood into the:

  1. Aorta
  2. Pulmonary vein
  3. Right atrium
  4. Vena cava
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Aorta

Systemic circulation begins with left-ventricular ejection into the aorta.

5. The right ventricle pumps blood into the:

  1. Pulmonary artery
  2. Aorta
  3. Pulmonary vein
  4. Left atrium directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pulmonary artery

Pulmonary arterial flow carries blood toward the lungs.

6. The tricuspid valve lies between the:

  1. Right atrium and right ventricle
  2. Left atrium and left ventricle
  3. Aorta and left atrium
  4. Two atria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Right atrium and right ventricle

It prevents backward flow to the right atrium during right-ventricular contraction.

7. The mitral valve lies between the:

  1. Left atrium and left ventricle
  2. Right atrium and right ventricle
  3. Two ventricles
  4. Vena cava and right atrium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Left atrium and left ventricle

The bicuspid or mitral valve is the left atrioventricular valve.

8. Systole refers to a chamber’s:

  1. Contraction phase
  2. Relaxation phase
  3. Valve destruction
  4. Permanent rest
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Contraction phase

Diastole is relaxation; systole is contraction. State which chamber is meant when timing events.

9. In an ECG, the QRS complex chiefly represents:

  1. Ventricular depolarisation
  2. Only atrial repolarisation
  3. Ventricular repolarisation
  4. Blood oxygen content
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ventricular depolarisation

The electrical depolarisation of ventricles precedes their mechanical contraction.

10. Human mature red blood cells normally lack:

  1. A nucleus
  2. Haemoglobin
  3. A membrane
  4. Cytoplasm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A nucleus

Loss of the nucleus and other organelles accompanies maturation and supports the specialised transport role.

11. The fluid matrix of blood is:

  1. Platelet
  2. Plasma
  3. Lymphocyte
  4. Haemoglobin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plasma

Plasma carries blood cells and dissolved substances.

12. A major plasma protein contributing to colloid osmotic balance is:

  1. Albumin
  2. Cellulose
  3. Myosin
  4. Chlorophyll
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Albumin

Albumin helps maintain plasma osmotic pressure.

13. Fibrinogen is associated with:

  1. Only oxygen binding
  2. Only vision
  3. Only bone formation
  4. Blood clotting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Blood clotting

It is converted into fibrin during clot formation.

14. Platelets are primarily associated with:

  1. Only photosynthesis
  2. Only antibody secretion
  3. Haemostasis
  4. Only oxygen storage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Haemostasis

They help limit blood loss at damaged vessels.

15. Mature human erythrocytes contain abundant:

  1. DNA chromosomes
  2. Haemoglobin
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. Keratin fibres only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Haemoglobin

Haemoglobin is their principal oxygen-carrying protein.

16. White blood cells are chiefly involved in:

  1. Defence
  2. Only oxygen transport
  3. Only glucose digestion
  4. Only bone mineral storage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Defence

Their varied roles support immune responses.

17. Arteries carry blood:

  1. Always towards the heart
  2. Always without oxygen
  3. Only to lungs
  4. Away from the heart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Away from the heart

Direction defines an artery, not oxygenation level.

18. Veins carry blood:

  1. Always fully oxygenated
  2. Only to kidneys
  3. Towards the heart
  4. Always away from the heart
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Towards the heart

Their defining feature is return towards the heart.

19. The pulmonary artery carries blood from the:

  1. Body to right atrium
  2. Right ventricle to lungs
  3. Left ventricle to body
  4. Lungs to left atrium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Right ventricle to lungs

It transports relatively deoxygenated blood for pulmonary exchange.

20. Pulmonary veins normally return blood to the:

  1. Left atrium
  2. Right atrium
  3. Right ventricle
  4. Aorta directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Left atrium

They bring oxygenated blood from the lungs.

21. The chamber pumping systemic arterial blood is the:

  1. Right atrium
  2. Left atrium
  3. Right ventricle
  4. Left ventricle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Left ventricle

Its thick muscular wall supplies systemic pressure.

22. The usual pacemaker region of the human heart is the:

  1. Bicuspid valve
  2. Pulmonary vein
  3. Sinoatrial node
  4. Aortic valve
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sinoatrial node

SA-node activity normally initiates the heartbeat.

23. The atrioventricular node helps provide a brief:

  1. Oxygen-production step
  2. Conduction delay
  3. Closure of every vessel
  4. Loss of all electrical signals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conduction delay

The delay supports ventricular filling before contraction.

24. The tricuspid valve separates the:

  1. Right atrium and right ventricle
  2. Left atrium and left ventricle
  3. Aorta and left atrium
  4. Pulmonary vein and right atrium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Right atrium and right ventricle

Its location prevents reverse flow during ventricular systole.

25. The bicuspid or mitral valve lies between the:

  1. Right atrium and right ventricle
  2. Aorta and right ventricle
  3. Both atria
  4. Left atrium and left ventricle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Left atrium and left ventricle

It guards the left atrioventricular opening.

26. An ECG P wave primarily represents:

  1. Only valve opening
  2. Only coronary blood flow
  3. Atrial depolarisation
  4. Ventricular repolarisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Atrial depolarisation

Electrical activation of the atria produces the P wave.

27. The QRS complex primarily represents:

  1. Only diastolic pressure
  2. Ventricular depolarisation
  3. Only atrial repolarisation visible separately
  4. Only oxygen transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ventricular depolarisation

Ventricular activation dominates the complex.

28. The ECG T wave primarily represents:

  1. Ventricular repolarisation
  2. Atrial depolarisation
  3. Only contraction of valves
  4. Only lung inflation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ventricular repolarisation

It reflects ventricular electrical recovery.

29. Double circulation means blood normally passes through the heart:

  1. Only once per circuit
  2. Four times per heartbeat
  3. Not at all
  4. Twice per complete circuit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Twice per complete circuit

Pulmonary and systemic circuits form two linked routes.

30. Lymph helps return excess tissue fluid to the:

  1. Only alveolar air
  2. Only bone cavities
  3. Blood circulation
  4. Only stomach lumen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Blood circulation

Lymphatic drainage supports fluid balance and other functions.
