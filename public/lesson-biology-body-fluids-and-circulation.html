Body Fluids and Circulation

Body Fluids and Circulation

Key concepts

  • The human heart has two atria and two ventricles. Pulmonary circulation connects heart and lungs; systemic circulation connects heart and the rest of the body.
  • The sinoatrial node normally initiates the heartbeat. Valves help prevent backward flow.
  • Red blood cells transport respiratory gases, white blood cells contribute to defence and platelets contribute to haemostasis.

Connect the ideas

Blood flows from the right ventricle to the lungs through pulmonary arteries, then returns to the left atrium through pulmonary veins.

Common mistake

Pulmonary arteries carry relatively deoxygenated blood; pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The left ventricle pumps blood into the:

Aorta

Systemic circulation begins with left-ventricular ejection into the aorta.

2. The right ventricle pumps blood into the:

Pulmonary artery

Pulmonary arterial flow carries blood toward the lungs.

3. The tricuspid valve lies between the:

Right atrium and right ventricle

It prevents backward flow to the right atrium during right-ventricular contraction.

4. The mitral valve lies between the:

Left atrium and left ventricle

The bicuspid or mitral valve is the left atrioventricular valve.

5. Systole refers to a chamber’s:

Contraction phase

Diastole is relaxation; systole is contraction. State which chamber is meant when timing events.

6. In an ECG, the QRS complex chiefly represents:

Ventricular depolarisation

The electrical depolarisation of ventricles precedes their mechanical contraction.

7. Human mature red blood cells normally lack:

A nucleus

Loss of the nucleus and other organelles accompanies maturation and supports the specialised transport role.
