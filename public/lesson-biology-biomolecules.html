Biomolecules

Biomolecules

Key concepts

  • Proteins are polymers of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides.
  • Enzymes lower activation energy without changing the equilibrium constant of the reaction. Most enzymes are proteins; some RNA molecules are catalytic.
  • Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids perform different storage, structural and regulatory roles.

Connect the ideas

Starch and glycogen store carbohydrate; cellulose contributes to plant cell walls. Their structures explain their different biological roles.

Common mistake

An enzyme changes how quickly equilibrium is approached, not the final equilibrium position.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The main carbohydrate storage polymer in many animals is:

Glycogen

Glycogen stores glucose, especially in liver and muscle in mammals.

2. Cellulose is chiefly a structural component of:

Plant cell walls

Cellulose fibres contribute to strength in plant cell walls.

3. A nucleoside differs from a nucleotide because a nucleoside lacks the:

Phosphate component

A nucleoside has base and sugar; a nucleotide additionally includes phosphate.

4. An enzyme’s active site binds its:

Substrate

The active site recognises a substrate and provides the chemical environment for catalysis.

5. Extreme temperature or pH can reduce enzyme activity by changing its:

Structure and active-site properties

Altered folding or ionisation can disrupt binding and catalytic function.

6. A competitive inhibitor typically competes with substrate for the:

Active site

In the simple model, increasing substrate concentration can reduce competitive inhibition.

7. Lipids differ from ordinary repeating-unit macromolecules because they are not generally:

True polymers of one repeating monomer

Many lipids are assembled from components such as glycerol and fatty acids rather than long repeating monomer chains.
