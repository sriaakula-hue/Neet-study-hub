Biomolecules

Biomolecules

Key concepts

  • Proteins are polymers of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides.
  • Enzymes lower activation energy without changing the equilibrium constant of the reaction. Most enzymes are proteins; some RNA molecules are catalytic.
  • Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids perform different storage, structural and regulatory roles.

Connect the ideas

Starch and glycogen store carbohydrate; cellulose contributes to plant cell walls. Their structures explain their different biological roles.

Common mistake

An enzyme changes how quickly equilibrium is approached, not the final equilibrium position.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The main carbohydrate storage polymer in many animals is:

Glycogen

Glycogen stores glucose, especially in liver and muscle in mammals.

2. Cellulose is chiefly a structural component of:

Plant cell walls

Cellulose fibres contribute to strength in plant cell walls.

3. A nucleoside differs from a nucleotide because a nucleoside lacks the:

Phosphate component

A nucleoside has base and sugar; a nucleotide additionally includes phosphate.

4. An enzyme’s active site binds its:

Substrate

The active site recognises a substrate and provides the chemical environment for catalysis.

5. Extreme temperature or pH can reduce enzyme activity by changing its:

Structure and active-site properties

Altered folding or ionisation can disrupt binding and catalytic function.

6. A competitive inhibitor typically competes with substrate for the:

Active site

In the simple model, increasing substrate concentration can reduce competitive inhibition.

7. Lipids differ from ordinary repeating-unit macromolecules because they are not generally:

True polymers of one repeating monomer

Many lipids are assembled from components such as glycerol and fatty acids rather than long repeating monomer chains.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The bond joining amino acids in a polypeptide is a:

  1. Peptide bond
  2. Glycosidic bond
  3. Hydrogen bond only
  4. Phosphodiester bond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Peptide bond

A peptide bond links amino-acid residues.

2. An enzyme increases reaction rate by:

  1. Raising activation energy
  2. Lowering activation energy
  3. Changing the equilibrium constant
  4. Being consumed completely
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Lowering activation energy

Enzymes provide a lower-activation-energy pathway.

3. The monomer units of nucleic acids are:

  1. Amino acids
  2. Fatty acids
  3. Nucleotides
  4. Monosaccharides
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Nucleotides

DNA and RNA consist of nucleotide units.

4. The main carbohydrate storage polymer in many animals is:

  1. Glycogen
  2. Cellulose
  3. Chitin
  4. DNA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Glycogen

Glycogen stores glucose, especially in liver and muscle in mammals.

5. Cellulose is chiefly a structural component of:

  1. Plant cell walls
  2. Animal plasma membranes
  3. Viral envelopes only
  4. Haemoglobin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Plant cell walls

Cellulose fibres contribute to strength in plant cell walls.

6. A nucleoside differs from a nucleotide because a nucleoside lacks the:

  1. Phosphate component
  2. Base
  3. Sugar
  4. Every atom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Phosphate component

A nucleoside has base and sugar; a nucleotide additionally includes phosphate.

7. An enzyme’s active site binds its:

  1. Substrate
  2. Nucleus
  3. Entire ecosystem
  4. Only another enzyme always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Substrate

The active site recognises a substrate and provides the chemical environment for catalysis.

8. Extreme temperature or pH can reduce enzyme activity by changing its:

  1. Structure and active-site properties
  2. Atomic number
  3. Neutron number
  4. Fundamental genetic code universally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Structure and active-site properties

Altered folding or ionisation can disrupt binding and catalytic function.

9. A competitive inhibitor typically competes with substrate for the:

  1. Active site
  2. Cell nucleus
  3. Entire membrane
  4. DNA sequence only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Active site

In the simple model, increasing substrate concentration can reduce competitive inhibition.

10. Lipids differ from ordinary repeating-unit macromolecules because they are not generally:

  1. True polymers of one repeating monomer
  2. Organic substances
  3. Energy stores
  4. Membrane components
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. True polymers of one repeating monomer

Many lipids are assembled from components such as glycerol and fatty acids rather than long repeating monomer chains.

11. The monomer units of proteins are:

  1. Amino acids
  2. Nucleotides
  3. Monosaccharides exclusively
  4. Fatty acids exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Amino acids

Peptide chains are assembled from amino-acid residues.

12. Purine bases in nucleic acids include:

  1. Cytosine and thymine
  2. Uracil and thymine
  3. Ribose and deoxyribose
  4. Adenine and guanine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Adenine and guanine

Purines have a two-ring structure unlike the single-ring pyrimidines.

13. Starch is a polymer built chiefly from:

  1. Ribose
  2. Amino acids
  3. Glucose
  4. Fructose exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Glucose

Glucose residues constitute this major plant reserve polysaccharide.

14. Glycogen is a major storage carbohydrate in:

  1. Only mineral rocks
  2. Animals and many fungi
  3. Only higher plants
  4. Only viruses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Animals and many fungi

Its branched glucose polymer stores readily available energy.

15. Cellulose is a major structural component of:

  1. Plant cell walls
  2. Animal bones
  3. Bacterial capsules in every case
  4. Mammalian blood plasma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Plant cell walls

Beta-linked glucose chains provide structural support.

16. Chitin is structurally associated with:

  1. Only vertebrate blood
  2. Only plant starch grains
  3. Only RNA
  4. Fungal walls and arthropod exoskeletons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fungal walls and arthropod exoskeletons

Its nitrogen-containing sugar polymer provides structural material.

17. A peptide bond connects the carboxyl group of one residue to the other's:

  1. Lipid tail
  2. Nitrogenous base
  3. Amino group
  4. Phosphate group only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Amino group

Condensation creates an amide linkage.

18. A protein's alpha helix is an example of:

  1. Nucleotide structure
  2. Secondary structure
  3. Primary sequence alone
  4. Quaternary arrangement only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Secondary structure

Regular backbone folding is stabilised by hydrogen bonding.

19. A protein's overall three-dimensional folded chain represents:

  1. Tertiary structure
  2. Only primary structure
  3. Only codon order
  4. Only nucleosome structure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Tertiary structure

Tertiary organisation describes the spatial arrangement of one chain.

20. Association of multiple polypeptide subunits is:

  1. Only primary structure
  2. Only an ester bond
  3. Only a nucleotide
  4. Quaternary structure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Quaternary structure

Subunit organisation is a higher level of protein structure.

21. Most enzymes accelerate reactions by lowering:

  1. Reactant mass
  2. The number of atoms
  3. Activation energy
  4. Overall equilibrium constant necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Activation energy

An alternative catalytic pathway reduces the kinetic barrier.

22. An enzyme's active site binds its:

  1. Only chromosome set
  2. Substrate
  3. Only neutron
  4. Only cell wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Substrate

Specific interactions position reactants for catalysis.

23. A competitive inhibitor often competes with substrate for the:

  1. Active site
  2. Nuclear pore
  3. Cell wall
  4. Ribosomal exit tunnel in every enzyme
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Active site

Binding can prevent substrate access without permanent enzyme destruction.

24. Enzyme activity commonly changes with pH because pH affects:

  1. Only atomic mass
  2. Only proton number in nuclei
  3. Only isotope abundance
  4. Ionisation and structure of catalytic groups
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ionisation and structure of catalytic groups

Charge changes can alter substrate binding and catalysis.

25. High temperature can reduce enzyme activity by causing:

  1. Infinite activation energy removal
  2. Creation of new nuclei
  3. Denaturation
  4. Extra codons automatically
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Denaturation

Protein structural disruption can impair the active site.

26. Some RNA molecules acting as catalysts are called:

  1. Ribonucleotides only
  2. Ribozymes
  3. Ribosomes only
  4. Ribose crystals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ribozymes

Catalytic activity is not restricted to protein molecules.

27. A coenzyme is generally an organic:

  1. Nonprotein helper molecule
  2. Cellulose wall
  3. Only metal nucleus
  4. Whole organelle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nonprotein helper molecule

It assists enzyme reactions, often carrying groups or electrons.

28. An apoenzyme plus its necessary cofactor constitutes a:

  1. Nucleosome
  2. Polysaccharide
  3. Virion
  4. Holoenzyme
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Holoenzyme

The complete catalytically competent combination is the holoenzyme.

29. A triglyceride commonly contains glycerol esterified with:

  1. Two amino acids
  2. One ribosome
  3. Three fatty acids
  4. Three nucleotides
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Three fatty acids

Three ester linkages connect the glycerol backbone to fatty acids.

30. ATP transfers usable energy through reactions involving its:

  1. Only protein unfolding
  2. Phosphate groups
  3. Only base replacement
  4. Only DNA replication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Phosphate groups

Coupled phosphate-transfer and hydrolysis reactions support cellular work.
