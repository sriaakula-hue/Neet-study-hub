Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Current

Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Current

Key concepts

  • Faraday’s law relates induced emf to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage. For N identical turns, emf = −N dΦ/dt.
  • Lenz’s law gives a direction opposing the change causing induction. Magnetic flux through a flat loop is BA cos θ for uniform B.
  • For a sinusoidal current, Irms = Ipeak/√2. Ideal transformer voltage ratio equals its turns ratio.

Worked example

A 10-turn coil whose per-turn flux changes by 0.02 Wb in 0.1 s has average emf magnitude 2 V.

Watch out

A constant nonzero flux does not by itself induce emf. RMS-to-peak ratios quoted here assume sinusoidal waveforms.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Magnetic flux through a plane loop in uniform B is:

BA cos θ

θ is the angle between B and the area normal, not the plane itself.

2. For a purely resistive sinusoidal AC circuit, current and voltage are:

In phase

An ideal resistor has no reactive phase shift, so instantaneous current tracks voltage.

3. In an ideal inductor, sinusoidal current:

Lags voltage by 90°

Voltage is proportional to rate of current change, producing a quarter-cycle current lag.

4. Capacitive reactance is:

1/(ωC)

XC decreases as angular frequency or capacitance increases.

5. At series LCR resonance, which relation holds?

ωL = 1/(ωC)

Inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, so net impedance is R in the ideal series circuit.

6. An ideal transformer has Ns/Np = 2 and primary voltage 50 V. Secondary voltage is:

100 V

Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, giving Vs = 2 × 50 = 100 V.

7. The SI unit of magnetic flux is:

Weber

Magnetic flux is measured in webers; tesla is flux density.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. N = 10, ΔΦ = 0.02 Wb per turn and Δt = 0.1 s give emf magnitude:

  1. 0.2 V
  2. 2 V
  3. 20 V
  4. 200 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 V

NΔΦ/Δt = 10 × 0.02/0.1 = 2 V.

2. If sinusoidal Ipeak = 2√2 A, Irms is:

  1. 1 A
  2. 2 A
  3. 4 A
  4. 2√2 A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 A

Irms = Ipeak/√2 = 2 A.

3. Lenz’s law determines induced-current direction so that it opposes the:

  1. Causing flux change
  2. Entire field always
  3. Resistance
  4. Number of turns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Causing flux change

The induced effect opposes the change, consistent with energy conservation.

4. Magnetic flux through a plane loop in uniform B is:

  1. BA cos θ
  2. BA sin θ always
  3. B/A
  4. A/B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. BA cos θ

θ is the angle between B and the area normal, not the plane itself.

5. For a purely resistive sinusoidal AC circuit, current and voltage are:

  1. In phase
  2. 90° apart
  3. 180° apart always
  4. Unrelated
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. In phase

An ideal resistor has no reactive phase shift, so instantaneous current tracks voltage.

6. In an ideal inductor, sinusoidal current:

  1. Lags voltage by 90°
  2. Leads voltage by 90°
  3. Always in phase
  4. Is always zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lags voltage by 90°

Voltage is proportional to rate of current change, producing a quarter-cycle current lag.

7. Capacitive reactance is:

  1. 1/(ωC)
  2. ωC
  3. ω/C
  4. C/ω
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1/(ωC)

XC decreases as angular frequency or capacitance increases.

8. At series LCR resonance, which relation holds?

  1. ωL = 1/(ωC)
  2. ωL = R always
  3. C = L in all units
  4. Impedance is infinite
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ωL = 1/(ωC)

Inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, so net impedance is R in the ideal series circuit.

9. An ideal transformer has Ns/Np = 2 and primary voltage 50 V. Secondary voltage is:

  1. 50 V
  2. 200 V
  3. 100 V
  4. 25 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 100 V

Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, giving Vs = 2 × 50 = 100 V.

10. The SI unit of magnetic flux is:

  1. Weber
  2. Tesla
  3. Henry
  4. Ohm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Weber

Magnetic flux is measured in webers; tesla is flux density.
