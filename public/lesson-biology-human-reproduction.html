Human Reproduction

Human Reproduction

Key concepts

  • Sperm are produced in seminiferous tubules. Ovarian follicles support oocyte development; ovulation is associated with an LH surge.
  • Human fertilisation usually occurs near the ampullary–isthmic junction of the oviduct. Implantation occurs in the uterine endometrium.
  • The placenta supports exchange and has endocrine functions. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone important for maintaining the endometrium.

Connect the ideas

The sequence is fertilisation, early cleavage, blastocyst formation and implantation; these events occur at different sites and times.

Common mistake

Fertilisation and implantation are distinct processes. Human reproduction also varies between individuals; textbook timelines are approximations.

1. Sperm production occurs in the:

  1. Seminiferous tubules
  2. Ureter
  3. Prostate only
  4. Urinary bladder
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Seminiferous tubules

Spermatogenesis occurs in seminiferous tubules of the testes.

2. Implantation occurs in the:

  1. Ovarian cortex
  2. Uterine endometrium
  3. Vagina
  4. Testis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Uterine endometrium

The blastocyst implants into the endometrium.

3. The hormone surge closely associated with ovulation is:

  1. LH
  2. Insulin
  3. ADH
  4. Melatonin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. LH

A surge of luteinising hormone triggers ovulation.

4. Sperm acquire maturation-related functions during passage through the:

  1. Epididymis
  2. Ureter
  3. Urinary bladder
  4. Ovary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Epididymis

The epididymis supports sperm maturation and storage.

5. The sperm acrosome helps with:

  1. Penetration of egg coverings
  2. Only ATP storage
  3. Only flagellar movement
  4. Only chromosome doubling
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Penetration of egg coverings

Acrosomal components assist interactions and penetration during fertilisation.

6. The sperm middle piece is rich in:

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Chloroplasts
  3. Large vacuoles
  4. Cell walls
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mitochondria

Mitochondria provide energy supporting motility.

7. The corpus luteum forms from the ovarian follicle after:

  1. Ovulation
  2. Implantation only
  3. Menstruation before every follicle grows
  4. Fertilisation in the testis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ovulation

The ruptured follicle develops into a progesterone-secreting corpus luteum.

8. A human secondary oocyte is generally arrested at metaphase II until:

  1. Fertilisation triggers completion
  2. Puberty ends
  3. Birth occurs
  4. It divides by mitosis repeatedly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fertilisation triggers completion

The second meiotic division normally completes after sperm-related activation.

9. Early cleavage increases embryo cell number mainly without a comparable immediate increase in:

  1. Overall embryo size
  2. Chromosome segregation
  3. Mitotic events
  4. Cell number
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Overall embryo size

The early divisions partition the cytoplasm into progressively smaller blastomeres.

10. The placenta supports exchange between maternal and fetal systems without normally requiring:

  1. Direct mixing of all maternal and fetal blood
  2. An exchange barrier
  3. Blood supply
  4. Transport processes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Direct mixing of all maternal and fetal blood

The exchange interface allows transfer while maintaining distinct circulations under normal conditions.

11. Sperm production occurs within the:

  1. Prostate
  2. Urethra
  3. Seminiferous tubules
  4. Epididymal lumen as the main site
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Seminiferous tubules

The testicular tubules contain the developing germ-cell lineage.

12. Sertoli cells provide support to:

  1. Only placental tissue
  2. Developing male germ cells
  3. Only ova
  4. Only prostate cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Developing male germ cells

They maintain the specialised environment of spermatogenesis.

13. Leydig cells are located mainly in the:

  1. Testicular interstitial tissue
  2. Ovarian follicles
  3. Uterine endometrium
  4. Cervical canal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Testicular interstitial tissue

They secrete androgens between seminiferous tubules.

14. Sperm commonly mature and are stored in the:

  1. Prostate
  2. Bladder
  3. Seminal vesicle only
  4. Epididymis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Epididymis

The duct supports sperm maturation and storage.

15. The vas deferens transports sperm from the epididymis towards the:

  1. Kidney
  2. Stomach
  3. Ejaculatory pathway
  4. Ovary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ejaculatory pathway

It forms a principal sperm-conducting duct.

16. The sperm acrosome contains materials important for:

  1. Only urine formation
  2. Penetrating egg-associated coverings
  3. Only tail movement
  4. Only DNA replication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Penetrating egg-associated coverings

Its contents assist fertilisation-related interactions.

17. The sperm midpiece contains abundant:

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Chloroplasts
  3. Large vacuoles only
  4. Cellulose fibres
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mitochondria

Energy production supports flagellar movement.

18. The sperm head principally contains a:

  1. Diploid egg nucleus
  2. Triploid endosperm
  3. Four embryonic cells
  4. Haploid nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Haploid nucleus

It carries the paternal chromosome contribution.

19. Spermiogenesis is the transformation of spermatids into:

  1. Sertoli cells
  2. Oogonia
  3. Spermatozoa
  4. Primary spermatocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Spermatozoa

Differentiation creates the specialised sperm structure.

20. Spermiation refers to release of sperm from:

  1. Only the prostate nucleus
  2. Sertoli-associated tubule tissue into the lumen
  3. Only the ovary
  4. Only the uterus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sertoli-associated tubule tissue into the lumen

Mature sperm leave their support-cell association.

21. The female gonads are the:

  1. Ovaries
  2. Uterine tubes
  3. Cervix
  4. Vagina
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ovaries

Ovaries contain developing oocytes and secrete hormones.

22. The finger-like projections near the uterine tube opening are:

  1. Villi of placenta
  2. Seminiferous tubules
  3. Cilia of sperm
  4. Fimbriae
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fimbriae

They help collect the ovulated oocyte.

23. Fertilisation commonly occurs near the:

  1. Vagina
  2. Ovarian cortex
  3. Ampullary-isthmic region of the uterine tube
  4. Cervix
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ampullary-isthmic region of the uterine tube

The familiar textbook site is within the uterine tube.

24. Ovulation is triggered principally by a surge in:

  1. Glucagon
  2. LH
  3. ADH
  4. Calcitonin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. LH

The gonadotropin surge supports release from the mature follicle.

25. After ovulation, the remaining follicular tissue forms the:

  1. Corpus luteum
  2. Blastocyst
  3. Placenta immediately
  4. Morula
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Corpus luteum

Luteal tissue becomes an important hormone-secreting structure.

26. The uterine lining is the:

  1. Myometrium
  2. Perimetrium
  3. Pericardium
  4. Endometrium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Endometrium

This layer undergoes cyclic changes and supports implantation.

27. The muscular middle uterine layer is the:

  1. Pericardium
  2. Epididymis
  3. Myometrium
  4. Endometrium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Myometrium

Smooth muscle in this layer contracts during labour.

28. Repeated early mitotic divisions of the zygote are called:

  1. Transcription
  2. Cleavage
  3. Spermiation
  4. Ovulation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cleavage

They produce smaller embryonic cells during early development.

29. An implantation-stage embryo is commonly a:

  1. Blastocyst
  2. Spermatid
  3. Primary oocyte
  4. Single unfertilised ovum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Blastocyst

Its structure includes an inner cell mass and trophoblast.

30. The inner cell mass principally contributes to the:

  1. Only maternal blood cells
  2. Only uterine muscle
  3. Only sperm tail
  4. Embryo proper
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Embryo proper

These cells form the developing embryonic body.
