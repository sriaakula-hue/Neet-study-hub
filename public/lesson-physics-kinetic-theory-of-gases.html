Kinetic Theory of Gases

Kinetic Theory of Gases

Key concepts

  • The ideal-gas equation is pV = nRT. Pressure arises from molecular momentum transfer to container walls.
  • The mean translational kinetic energy per molecule is 3kBT/2. Temperature must be in kelvin.
  • RMS speed is √(3RT/M), where molar mass M must be in kg/mol when SI units are used.

Worked example

For the same gas, increasing temperature from 300 K to 1,200 K doubles its RMS speed because the ratio is √4.

Watch out

RMS speed is not the same as mean velocity. In a gas at equilibrium, vector average velocity can be zero while RMS speed is nonzero.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. At fixed ideal-gas temperature, doubling volume halves:

Pressure

pV = nRT; fixed n and T make p inversely proportional to V.

2. For the same temperature, lighter gas molecules generally have RMS speed:

Higher

vrms = √(3RT/M), so lower molar mass gives higher RMS speed.

3. An ideal monatomic gas has molar Cv:

3R/2

Three translational quadratic degrees of freedom give U = 3nRT/2 and Cv = 3R/2.

4. For an ideal gas, Cp − Cv equals:

R

The ideal-gas molar heat-capacity relation is Mayer’s relation Cp − Cv = R.

5. Mean free path is an average distance between:

Successive molecular collisions

It describes molecular travel between collisions; it depends on number density and collision cross-section.

6. Absolute temperature rises from 200 K to 800 K for the same gas. RMS-speed ratio is:

2

The ratio is √(800/200) = √4 = 2.

7. Ideal-gas assumptions neglect intermolecular forces except during:

Collisions

The simple kinetic model treats molecules as free between brief elastic collisions.

1. Absolute temperature in gas equations is measured in:

  1. Celsius only
  2. Kelvin
  3. Fahrenheit only
  4. Joules
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Kelvin

Use the thermodynamic kelvin scale.

2. For the same gas, quadrupling absolute temperature changes RMS speed by:

  1. Four times
  2. Two times
  3. Half
  4. No change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two times

RMS speed is proportional to √T.

3. Mean translational kinetic energy per molecule is:

  1. kBT/2
  2. kBT
  3. 3kBT/2
  4. 3RT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3kBT/2

There are three translational degrees of freedom, each contributing kBT/2.

4. At fixed ideal-gas temperature, doubling volume halves:

  1. Pressure
  2. Molecular mass
  3. Kelvin temperature
  4. Gas constant
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pressure

pV = nRT; fixed n and T make p inversely proportional to V.

9. Absolute temperature rises from 200 K to 800 K for the same gas. RMS-speed ratio is:

Show answer and explanation

10. Ideal-gas assumptions neglect intermolecular forces except during:

  4. Only cooling
