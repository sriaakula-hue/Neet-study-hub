Human Health and Disease

Human Health and Disease

Key concepts

  • Innate immunity uses barriers and broad defence mechanisms. Adaptive immunity involves specific responses and immunological memory.
  • B cells can differentiate into antibody-secreting plasma cells; T cells contribute to cell-mediated responses and regulation.
  • Vaccination stimulates protective immune responses. Malaria is caused by Plasmodium and transmitted by infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

Connect the ideas

An antibody response after vaccination prepares the immune system for later exposure; the vaccine is not the same as an antibiotic.

Common mistake

Antibiotics do not treat every type of infection. These are exam notes, not a guide to choosing medicines.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Skin and mucosal barriers are components of:

Innate immunity

They provide broad early protection rather than antigen-specific acquired memory.

2. Cell-mediated immune responses particularly involve:

T lymphocytes

T cells have roles in recognition, regulation and destruction of infected or abnormal cells.

3. Receiving preformed antibodies provides:

Passive immunity

Passive protection is transferred rather than generated by the recipient’s own adaptive response.

4. Vaccination generally aims to induce:

Active protective immunity

The recipient develops an immune response and often memory to the relevant antigen.

5. HIV primarily targets immune cells including:

CD4 T cells

Loss or dysfunction of these immune cells contributes to acquired immunodeficiency.

6. Uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal cells is characteristic of:

Cancer

Cancer involves dysregulated cell growth, with additional features varying by tumour type.

7. Metastasis means spread of cancer cells to:

Other body sites

Cells can migrate and establish secondary growths beyond the original tumour site.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Antibody-secreting plasma cells develop from:

  1. B lymphocytes
  2. Red blood cells
  3. Platelets
  4. Neurons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. B lymphocytes

Activated B cells differentiate into plasma cells.

2. Malaria is caused by:

  1. Plasmodium
  2. A vitamin deficiency
  3. A fungus only
  4. Insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Plasmodium

Plasmodium is the protozoan parasite responsible.

3. Immunological memory is a major feature of:

  1. Adaptive immunity
  2. Only skin thickness
  3. Only stomach acid
  4. Red-cell production
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Adaptive immunity

Adaptive responses include antigen-specific memory cells.

4. Skin and mucosal barriers are components of:

  1. Innate immunity
  2. Only antibody memory
  3. Only endocrine signalling
  4. Only meiosis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Innate immunity

They provide broad early protection rather than antigen-specific acquired memory.

5. Cell-mediated immune responses particularly involve:

  1. T lymphocytes
  2. Only mature red blood cells
  3. Only platelets
  4. Only calcium crystals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. T lymphocytes

T cells have roles in recognition, regulation and destruction of infected or abnormal cells.

6. Receiving preformed antibodies provides:

  1. Passive immunity
  2. Only active immune training
  3. No immunity ever
  4. Genetic immunity by mutation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Passive immunity

Passive protection is transferred rather than generated by the recipient’s own adaptive response.

7. Vaccination generally aims to induce:

  1. Active protective immunity
  2. Only dehydration
  3. A permanent antibiotic supply
  4. Loss of all immune memory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Active protective immunity

The recipient develops an immune response and often memory to the relevant antigen.

8. HIV primarily targets immune cells including:

  1. CD4 T cells
  2. Only red blood cells
  3. Only bone-forming cells
  4. Only hair cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CD4 T cells

Loss or dysfunction of these immune cells contributes to acquired immunodeficiency.

9. Uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal cells is characteristic of:

  1. Cancer
  2. All normal mitosis
  3. Every vaccination
  4. Every fever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cancer

Cancer involves dysregulated cell growth, with additional features varying by tumour type.

10. Metastasis means spread of cancer cells to:

  1. Other body sites
  2. Only the original cell membrane
  3. Only a laboratory slide
  4. Only adjacent chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Other body sites

Cells can migrate and establish secondary growths beyond the original tumour site.

11. A pathogen is an agent that can cause:

  1. Disease
  2. Only photosynthesis
  3. Only oxygen transport
  4. Only food storage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Disease

Pathogenic agents disrupt normal host function.

12. Innate immunity is generally present without requiring:

  1. Any cells
  2. Any barriers
  3. Any genetic basis
  4. Prior specific exposure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Prior specific exposure

Its broad defensive features are available before antigen-specific memory develops.

13. Acquired immunity is characterised by specificity and:

  1. No cells
  2. No antigen recognition
  3. Memory
  4. No receptors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Memory

Prior exposure can strengthen a later response.

14. Antibodies are produced by plasma cells derived from:

  1. Osteocytes
  2. B lymphocytes
  3. Red blood cells
  4. Platelets
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. B lymphocytes

Activated B-cell lineages differentiate into antibody-secreting cells.

15. Cell-mediated immunity prominently involves:

  1. T lymphocytes
  2. Only erythrocytes
  3. Only platelets
  4. Only adipocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. T lymphocytes

T-cell functions target infected or altered cells and regulate responses.

16. An antigen can be recognised by appropriate immune:

  1. Only kidney filters
  2. Only bone calcium
  3. Only stomach acid
  4. Receptors or antibodies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Receptors or antibodies

Specific molecular recognition initiates or participates in immune responses.

17. A vaccination aims to induce protective immune:

  1. Universal sterility
  2. Loss of all lymphocytes
  3. Memory
  4. Permanent pathogen production
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Memory

Antigen exposure can prepare future specific responses.

18. Passive immunity provides:

  1. Only neutrons
  2. Preformed antibodies
  3. Only antigen DNA replication
  4. Only new stem cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Preformed antibodies

Transferred antibodies give protection without the recipient initially making them.

19. Antibodies crossing from mother to fetus are an example of:

  1. Natural passive immunity
  2. Artificial active immunity
  3. Only innate inflammation
  4. Only genetic drift
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Natural passive immunity

The fetal recipient receives maternal antibody molecules.

20. The primary immune response is usually slower than the secondary because the latter has:

  1. No antigens
  2. No lymphocytes
  3. Only red cells
  4. Memory cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Memory cells

Previously selected cell populations respond more rapidly.

21. An allergy is an exaggerated response to an otherwise often harmless:

  1. Chromosome count
  2. ATP molecule in every case
  3. Allergen
  4. Neutron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Allergen

Hypersensitive immune responses can produce symptoms.

22. Histamine is involved in many:

  1. Only photosynthesis
  2. Allergic and inflammatory responses
  3. Only DNA replication
  4. Only renal filtration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Allergic and inflammatory responses

Its actions include changes in vessels and local tissues.

23. Autoimmunity involves an immune response against:

  1. The body's own components
  2. Only a foreign bacterium
  3. Only an inert textbook
  4. Only carbon dioxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The body's own components

Self-tolerance failure permits immune attack on host structures.

24. Malaria is caused by parasites of the genus:

  1. Salmonella
  2. Entamoeba only
  3. Ascaris
  4. Plasmodium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Plasmodium

Plasmodium parasites infect humans through a complex life cycle.

25. The mosquito transmitting human malaria is typically a female:

  1. Aedes exclusively
  2. Housefly
  3. Anopheles
  4. Culex in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Anopheles

The standard human malaria vector is an infected female Anopheles.

26. Typhoid fever is associated with:

  1. Rhinovirus
  2. Salmonella Typhi
  3. Plasmodium falciparum
  4. Wuchereria bancrofti
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Salmonella Typhi

The bacterium causes the familiar systemic infection.

27. A common cold is commonly caused by:

  1. Viruses such as rhinoviruses
  2. Only tapeworms
  3. Only fungi
  4. Only vitamin deficiency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Viruses such as rhinoviruses

Respiratory viruses are responsible for the familiar syndrome.

28. Ringworm is associated with:

  1. A literal ring-shaped worm
  2. Malaria parasite
  3. A bacterial spore only
  4. Fungal infection
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fungal infection

Dermatophyte fungi cause the typical skin infection.

29. Cancer metastasis refers to:

  1. Only local DNA repair
  2. Only benign encapsulation
  3. Spread to distant sites
  4. Only normal cell division
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Spread to distant sites

Malignant cells can invade and establish growth elsewhere.

30. An oncogene is a gene whose altered or inappropriate activity can promote:

  1. Only leaf photosynthesis
  2. Cancer-associated cell growth
  3. Only normal digestion
  4. Only calcium excretion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cancer-associated cell growth

Growth-regulatory pathways can become abnormally activated.
