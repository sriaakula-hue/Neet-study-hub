Electric charges and fields

Electric charges and fields

Core idea

The magnitude of the electrostatic force between two point charges is F = k|q₁q₂|/r². Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Electric field is force per unit positive test charge.

Worked connection

Doubling the separation while keeping charges fixed makes the force one-quarter of its original value.

Watch out

Coulomb’s law in this form assumes point charges, or spherical charge distributions with suitable symmetry, in a uniform medium.

Potential and capacitance

A point charge has electric potential V = kq/r with zero at infinity. Capacitance is C = Q/V. Capacitors add directly in parallel; reciprocals add in series. Capacitor energy is ½CV².

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Electric field lines of an isolated positive point charge point:

Outward

A positive test charge would be repelled outward; field direction is defined using a positive test charge.

2. A 2 C test charge experiences an electric force of 6 N. Field magnitude is:

3 N/C

E = F/q = 6/2 = 3 N/C.

3. A capacitor stores 12 μC at 6 V. Capacitance is:

2 μF

C = Q/V = 12/6 = 2 μF, using microcoulombs per volt.

4. Capacitors 2 μF and 3 μF in parallel have equivalent capacitance:

5 μF

Parallel capacitors share voltage and their stored charges add, so capacitances add directly.

5. A 2 F capacitor at 3 V stores energy:

9 J

Energy is ½CV² = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.

6. Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, electric field is:

Zero

Free charges redistribute until the internal electric field vanishes in electrostatic equilibrium.

7. Electric potential is a:

Scalar

Potential is potential energy per unit charge; potentials add algebraically, unlike vector electric fields.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The separation of two point charges doubles. Their force magnitude becomes:

  1. Twice
  2. Half
  3. One-quarter
  4. Four times
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. One-quarter

The force varies as 1/r²; doubling r divides the force by four.

2. The SI unit of electric field is:

  1. N/C
  2. C/N
  3. J/s
  4. C/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. N/C

Electric field E = F/q has unit newton per coulomb.

3. Two positive point charges:

  1. Attract
  2. Repel
  3. Have no force
  4. Always cancel each other
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Repel

Like charges repel electrostatically.

4. Electric field lines of an isolated positive point charge point:

  1. Outward
  2. Inward
  3. Only sideways
  4. In closed circles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Outward

A positive test charge would be repelled outward; field direction is defined using a positive test charge.

  2. 8 N/C
  3. 0.33 N/C
  4. 3 N/C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3 N/C

E = F/q = 6/2 = 3 N/C.

9. Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, electric field is:

  1. Zero
  2. Always infinite

  4. Dependent only on mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zero

Free charges redistribute until the internal electric field vanishes in electrostatic equilibrium.

10. Electric potential is a:

  1. Scalar
  2. Vector with three components
  3. Force
  4. Charge density
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Scalar

Potential is potential energy per unit charge; potentials add algebraically, unlike vector electric fields.

11. In a uniform field of 200 N/C, a positive charge of 2 μC experiences force of what magnitude?

  1. 0.0008 N
  2. 0.0004 N
  3. 0.0002 N
  4. 0.0006 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.0004 N

Use F=qE, converting μC to 10⁻⁶ C. Answer: 0.0004 N.
