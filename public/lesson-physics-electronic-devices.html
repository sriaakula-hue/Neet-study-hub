Electronic Devices

Electronic Devices

Key concepts

  • A p–n junction has a depletion region and a built-in potential. Forward bias generally promotes conduction; reverse bias generally suppresses it until breakdown.
  • A diode can rectify alternating input. A Zener diode can be used for voltage regulation in a suitable reverse-breakdown circuit.
  • Logic gates map binary inputs to outputs. AND outputs 1 only when all inputs are 1; OR outputs 1 if at least one input is 1; NOT inverts its input.

Worked example

For inputs 1 and 0, AND gives 0 while OR gives 1. NOT applied to 1 gives 0.

Watch out

A real diode is not an ideal switch at every voltage, and Zener regulation requires appropriate current limiting.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

1

OR outputs 1 when at least one input is 1.

2. A NAND gate with inputs 1 and 1 gives:

0

AND would give 1; NAND inverts that result to 0.

3. A NOR gate with inputs 0 and 0 gives:

1

OR would give 0; NOR inverts that result to 1.

4. A p-type semiconductor’s majority charge carriers are:

Holes

Acceptor doping makes holes the majority mobile carriers; electrons remain minority carriers.

5. An n-type semiconductor’s majority charge carriers are:

Electrons

Donor doping supplies mobile electrons; the bulk material remains approximately electrically neutral.

6. A rectifier primarily converts:

AC input into unidirectional output

Diode rectification permits current preferentially in one direction; smoothing is a separate circuit function.

7. A light-emitting diode emits light principally during:

Forward-bias carrier recombination

Recombination releases energy as photons in an appropriate semiconductor junction.

1. A two-input AND gate with inputs 1 and 0 gives:

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. Undefined always

  2. 0
  3. 2
  4. −1

11. An ideal forward conducting diode is in series with a 200 Ω resistor across 10 V. Neglect diode voltage drop. Current is:

  1. 0.075 A
  2. 0.1 A
  3. 0.05 A
  4. 0.025 A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 0.05 A

An ideal conducting diode drops zero voltage, leaving I=10/R. Answer: 0.05 A.

12. A silicon diode model uses constant forward drop 0.7 V. A 200 Ω resistor and diode are in series across 10.7 V. Current is:

  1. 0.1 A
  2. 0.05 A
  3. 0.025 A
  4. 0.075 A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.05 A

The resistor voltage is 10.7−0.7=10 V; divide by resistance. Answer: 0.05 A.

13. A 5 V Zener regulator supplies a 2000 Ω load at its regulated voltage. Load current is:

  1. 2.5 mA
  2. 1.25 mA
  3. 3.75 mA
  4. 5 mA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2.5 mA

Load current is V/R; 5 V divided by k kΩ is 5/k mA. Answer: 2.5 mA.

14. In a simplified intrinsic semiconductor sample, electron density is 2e+15 m⁻³. Hole density is:

  1. 1e+15 m⁻³
  2. 3e+15 m⁻³
  3. 4e+15 m⁻³
  4. 2e+15 m⁻³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2e+15 m⁻³

An intrinsic semiconductor in equilibrium has equal free-electron and hole densities. Answer: 2e+15 m⁻³.

15. A simplified semiconductor has n_i=2e+10 cm⁻³ and equilibrium electron density n=10¹² cm⁻³. Using np=n_i², hole density is:

  1. 6e+08 cm⁻³
  2. 8e+08 cm⁻³
  3. 4e+08 cm⁻³
  4. 2e+08 cm⁻³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4e+08 cm⁻³

Mass-action law in thermal equilibrium gives p=n_i²/n; all densities use the same units. Answer: 4e+08 cm⁻³.
