Principles of Inheritance and Variation

Mendelian inheritance

In a simple Mendelian model, alleles segregate during gamete formation. A dominant allele determines the heterozygote phenotype under complete dominance.

Monohybrid cross

Aa × Aa → 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa

With complete dominance, the corresponding phenotype ratio is 3 dominant : 1 recessive. This assumes normal segregation, random fertilization and equal viability.

Watch out

Dominant does not mean more common or better. Incomplete dominance and codominance do not produce this 3:1 phenotype ratio.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A test cross commonly pairs an individual showing a dominant phenotype with a:

Homozygous recessive individual

Offspring phenotypes can reveal whether the dominant-phenotype parent is heterozygous.

2. For Aa × aa with complete dominance, expected phenotype ratio is:

1:1

Half the offspring are Aa and half aa under ordinary Mendelian assumptions.

3. In incomplete dominance, the heterozygote phenotype is commonly:

Intermediate

The heterozygote shows an intermediate expression in the classic incomplete-dominance model.

4. In codominance, a heterozygote can express:

Both allele-associated traits

Both allelic products or phenotypic features are detectably expressed, as in the AB blood-group example.

5. Independent assortment is most applicable to genes that are:

Unlinked or sufficiently separated

Physical linkage can cause departures from independent assortment; unlinked loci segregate independently.

6. An X-linked recessive trait is often expressed in an XY individual with one relevant allele because the individual is:

Hemizygous at that X locus

There is generally no second equivalent X-linked allele on the Y to mask the recessive expression.

7. A pedigree is used to study:

Inheritance patterns across family generations

Family relationships and trait distributions can suggest inheritance modes, though inference may require additional evidence.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. In Aa × Aa with complete dominance, the expected phenotype ratio is:

  1. 1:1
  2. 1:2:1
  3. 3:1
  4. 9:3:3:1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3:1

AA and Aa show the dominant phenotype, while aa shows the recessive phenotype: 3:1.

2. For Aa × Aa, the expected genotype ratio is:

  1. 3:1
  2. 1:1
  3. 1:2:1
  4. 9:3:3:1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1:2:1

The products are AA, Aa, Aa and aa, giving 1:2:1.

3. Under complete dominance, a dominant allele:

  1. Must be more common
  2. Is expressed in a heterozygote
  3. Is always beneficial
  4. Cannot occur in a homozygote
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Is expressed in a heterozygote

Dominance describes phenotype expression, not frequency or benefit.

4. A test cross commonly pairs an individual showing a dominant phenotype with a:

  1. Homozygous recessive individual
  2. Homozygous dominant only
  3. Different species
  4. Sterile individual only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Homozygous recessive individual

Offspring phenotypes can reveal whether the dominant-phenotype parent is heterozygous.

5. For Aa × aa with complete dominance, expected phenotype ratio is:

  1. 1:1
  2. 3:1
  3. 1:2:1
  4. 9:3:3:1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1:1

Half the offspring are Aa and half aa under ordinary Mendelian assumptions.

6. In incomplete dominance, the heterozygote phenotype is commonly:

  1. Intermediate
  2. Identical to one homozygote always
  3. Absent
  4. Always lethal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intermediate

The heterozygote shows an intermediate expression in the classic incomplete-dominance model.

7. In codominance, a heterozygote can express:

  1. Both allele-associated traits
  2. Neither trait always
  3. Only a recessive trait
  4. Only one trait in every cell necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both allele-associated traits

Both allelic products or phenotypic features are detectably expressed, as in the AB blood-group example.

8. Independent assortment is most applicable to genes that are:

  1. Unlinked or sufficiently separated
  2. Always tightly linked
  3. Always mitochondrial
  4. Always identical alleles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unlinked or sufficiently separated

Physical linkage can cause departures from independent assortment; unlinked loci segregate independently.

9. An X-linked recessive trait is often expressed in an XY individual with one relevant allele because the individual is:

  1. Hemizygous at that X locus
  2. Homozygous for both X copies
  3. Without chromosomes
  4. Always female
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hemizygous at that X locus

There is generally no second equivalent X-linked allele on the Y to mask the recessive expression.

10. A pedigree is used to study:

  1. Inheritance patterns across family generations
  2. Only enzyme rates
  3. Only atmospheric gases
  4. Only DNA length
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inheritance patterns across family generations

Family relationships and trait distributions can suggest inheritance modes, though inference may require additional evidence.

11. For an Aa×Aa cross with ordinary segregation, the probability of an aa offspring is:

  1. 1/2
  2. 3/4
  3. 1
  4. 1/4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1/4

Each parent passes allele a with probability 1/2; the probability of receiving a from both is 1/2×1/2=1/4.

12. For an AA×aa cross, the expected offspring genotype is:

  1. All aa
  2. Half AA and half aa
  3. All Aa
  4. All AA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. All Aa

The AA parent contributes A to every offspring and the aa parent contributes a, producing heterozygous Aa offspring.

13. Under independent assortment, an AaBb individual produces how many distinct allele combinations in gametes?

  1. One
  2. Four
  3. Two
  4. Eight
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Four

The two heterozygous independently assorting loci give 2²=4 gamete combinations: AB, Ab, aB and ab.

14. Under independent assortment, an AaBbCc individual can form how many distinct allele combinations in gametes?

  1. Eight
  2. Six
  3. Three
  4. Sixteen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Eight

For three independently assorting heterozygous loci, combinations=2³=8.

15. For AaBb×AaBb with independent assortment, the probability of aabb offspring is:

  1. 1/4
  2. 3/16
  3. 9/16
  4. 1/16
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1/16

At each locus the recessive homozygote probability is 1/4. Multiply independent probabilities: 1/4×1/4=1/16.

16. For AaBb×aabb with independent assortment, the probability of AaBb offspring is:

  1. 1/2
  2. 3/4
  3. 1/4
  4. 1/16
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 1/4

The AaBb parent makes AB gametes with probability 1/4; the aabb parent makes only ab gametes, so AaBb occurs with probability 1/4.

17. An individual is heterozygous at four independently assorting loci. Number of allele combinations in gametes is:

  1. 32
  2. 16
  3. 8
  4. 4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 16

The number of combinations is 2ⁿ with n=4 heterozygous independent loci, giving 16.

18. In snapdragons with incomplete dominance, red RR×white rr produces F₁ flowers that are:

  1. Pink Rr
  2. All red RR
  3. All white rr
  4. Half red and half white
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pink Rr

All offspring are Rr and show the intermediate pink phenotype in the standard snapdragon example.

19. Crossing pink Rr snapdragons with white rr under incomplete dominance gives which expected phenotype ratio?

  1. 3 pink : 1 white
  2. All pink
  3. 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white
  4. 1 pink : 1 white
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1 pink : 1 white

The Rr parent supplies R or r equally and the rr parent only r, giving half Rr pink and half rr white.

20. In the ABO blood-group system, alleles Iᴬ and Iᴮ are:

  1. Identical alleles
  2. Always lethal together
  3. Codominant to each other
  4. Both recessive to i
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Codominant to each other

IᴬIᴮ individuals express both A and B antigens. Both alleles are dominant to i in the standard ABO model.

21. A parent with genotype Iᴬi and a parent with genotype Iᴮi can have children of which ABO phenotypes?

  1. Only O
  2. A, B, AB and O
  3. Only A and B
  4. Only AB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A, B, AB and O

Combining Iᴬ or i with Iᴮ or i gives IᴬIᴮ, Iᴬi, Iᴮi and ii, corresponding to AB, A, B and O.

22. In the usual ABO model, a blood-group O individual has genotype:

  1. ii
  2. IᴬIᴮ
  3. Iᴬi
  4. Iᴮi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ii

O results from homozygous ii; Iᴬ and Iᴮ encode the standard A and B antigen-producing activities.

23. An IᴬIᴮ parent and an ii parent can have children with which usual ABO blood groups?

  1. AB or O
  2. Only AB
  3. A, B, AB or O
  4. A or B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A or B

The AB parent contributes Iᴬ or Iᴮ and the O parent only i, producing Iᴬi or Iᴮi offspring.

24. In human XY sex determination, a typical sperm carries:

  1. Only an X chromosome
  2. Neither X nor Y chromosome
  3. An X chromosome or a Y chromosome
  4. Both X and Y chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An X chromosome or a Y chromosome

Normal haploid sperm carry either X or Y. Typical ova carry X, so the sperm determines XX or XY at fertilisation.

25. A carrier mother XᴴXʰ and unaffected father XᴴY have sons. Assuming equal segregation, the probability that a son has the recessive X-linked trait is:

  1. 1
  2. 1/2
  3. 1/4
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1/2

Conditional on being a son, the child receives Y from the father and one maternal X. Half receive Xʰ and express the recessive trait.

26. For an X-linked recessive trait, an affected father XʰY and homozygous unaffected mother XᴴXᴴ have daughters who are:

  1. All carriers XᴴXʰ
  2. All affected XʰXʰ
  3. All homozygous unaffected XᴴXᴴ
  4. Half affected and half unaffected
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. All carriers XᴴXʰ

Every daughter receives Xʰ from the father and Xᴴ from the mother, becoming an unaffected carrier under the stated recessive model.

27. Two genes very close together on the same chromosome ordinarily show:

  1. Exactly 100% recombination
  2. No inheritance
  3. Independent assortment in every meiosis
  4. Low recombination frequency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Low recombination frequency

Close loci have fewer crossovers between them and therefore lower recombinant frequency than widely separated loci.

28. A genetic cross produces 40 recombinant offspring among 200 total offspring. Recombination frequency is:

  1. 5%
  2. 80%
  3. 20%
  4. 40%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20%

Recombination frequency=100×40/200=20%. For short intervals this can approximate map distance in centimorgans.

29. The law of segregation concerns the separation of:

  1. All linked loci in every meiosis
  2. The two alleles at a locus during gamete formation
  3. All proteins from DNA
  4. Dominant phenotypes from recessive phenotypes after birth
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The two alleles at a locus during gamete formation

A diploid individual's two alleles at a locus separate during gamete formation so each typical gamete receives one allele.

30. Sickle-cell anaemia illustrates pleiotropy because a change in one gene can:

  1. Affect multiple phenotypic features
  2. Create only one phenotype under all conditions
  3. Prevent allele segregation
  4. Make every trait polygenic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Affect multiple phenotypic features

Pleiotropy means a single gene influences multiple features. The haemoglobin gene variant can affect red-cell shape and several physiological outcomes.
