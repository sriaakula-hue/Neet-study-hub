Principles of Practical Chemistry

Principles of Practical Chemistry

Key concepts

  • Titration compares measured amounts of reactants using a suitable reaction and endpoint. Endpoint and equivalence point are related but distinct concepts.
  • Qualitative tests identify ions or functional groups through characteristic reactions. Controls, conditions and interfering species matter.
  • In acidic permanganate titrations, MnO₄⁻ is reduced to Mn²⁺; permanganate can serve as its own indicator.

Worked connection

For HCl and NaOH reacting 1:1, 20 mL of 0.10 M HCl requires 10 mL of 0.20 M NaOH at equivalence.

Watch out

These are theoretical notes. Practical work requires supervised laboratory procedures, appropriate equipment and proper chemical handling.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A volumetric pipette is primarily used to deliver a:

Specified accurately measured volume

A properly calibrated pipette transfers its marked volume using the specified technique.

2. A burette is useful for measuring:

Volume delivered during titration

The difference between initial and final readings gives delivered liquid volume.

3. An equivalence point is defined by:

Stoichiometric completion of the titration reaction

It is the theoretical point where reactant amounts satisfy the balanced reaction proportions.

4. Why rinse a burette with the intended titrant before filling?

To avoid dilution or contamination from residual liquid

Residual water or another solution can alter titrant concentration, introducing systematic error.

5. In acidic permanganate reduction to Mn²⁺, Mn oxidation state changes from:

+7 to +2

In MnO₄⁻, oxygen totals −8, so Mn is +7; the product ion has +2.

6. For a 1:1 titration, 10 mL of 0.10 M acid requires 20 mL base. Base molarity is:

0.05 M

Acid moles = 0.001; dividing by 0.020 L gives 0.05 mol/L base.

7. A reliable qualitative test should be interpreted with attention to:

Conditions and possible interfering species

Similar observations can have different causes; conditions, controls and complementary tests improve identification.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. At equivalence, 20 mL of 0.10 M HCl requires what volume of 0.20 M NaOH?

  1. 5 mL
  2. 10 mL
  3. 20 mL
  4. 40 mL
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 mL

The 1:1 reaction requires 0.002 mol NaOH; volume = 0.002/0.20 L = 10 mL.

2. Permanganate titrations can use KMnO₄ as a:

  1. Self-indicator
  2. Gas collector
  3. Buffer always
  4. Neutral solvent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Self-indicator

Its intense colour allows detection of a small persistent excess.

3. The titration endpoint is:

  1. The observed indicator change
  2. Always exactly pH 7
  3. Always the first drop
  4. Always complete evaporation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The observed indicator change

Endpoint is the observed signal used to stop titration, ideally close to equivalence.

4. A volumetric pipette is primarily used to deliver a:

  1. Specified accurately measured volume
  2. Continuously variable volume read from a burette
  3. Known mass only
  4. Gas pressure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Specified accurately measured volume

A properly calibrated pipette transfers its marked volume using the specified technique.

5. A burette is useful for measuring:

  1. Volume delivered during titration
  2. Only sample mass
  3. Only flame temperature
  4. Only orbital radius
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Volume delivered during titration

The difference between initial and final readings gives delivered liquid volume.

6. An equivalence point is defined by:

  1. Stoichiometric completion of the titration reaction
  2. Any first colour change
  3. Always pH 7
  4. A completely empty burette
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Stoichiometric completion of the titration reaction

It is the theoretical point where reactant amounts satisfy the balanced reaction proportions.

7. Why rinse a burette with the intended titrant before filling?

  1. To avoid dilution or contamination from residual liquid
  2. To increase its graduation spacing
  3. To change stoichiometry
  4. To eliminate every chemical hazard
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To avoid dilution or contamination from residual liquid

Residual water or another solution can alter titrant concentration, introducing systematic error.

8. In acidic permanganate reduction to Mn²⁺, Mn oxidation state changes from:

  1. +7 to +2
  2. +2 to +7
  3. 0 to +2
  4. −1 to +2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +7 to +2

In MnO₄⁻, oxygen totals −8, so Mn is +7; the product ion has +2.

9. For a 1:1 titration, 10 mL of 0.10 M acid requires 20 mL base. Base molarity is:

  1. 0.10 M
  2. 0.20 M
  3. 0.50 M
  4. 0.05 M
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.05 M

Acid moles = 0.001; dividing by 0.020 L gives 0.05 mol/L base.

10. A reliable qualitative test should be interpreted with attention to:

  1. Conditions and possible interfering species
  2. Colour alone without controls
  3. Only container size
  4. Only sample name
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Conditions and possible interfering species

Similar observations can have different causes; conditions, controls and complementary tests improve identification.

11. A burette is primarily used to deliver:

  1. Only a fixed solid mass
  2. Only compressed gas
  3. Only light
  4. A measured variable liquid volume
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A measured variable liquid volume

Its graduations permit controlled liquid delivery during titration.

12. A volumetric pipette is designed to deliver a:

  1. Measured solid mass
  2. Temperature value
  3. Specified calibrated volume
  4. Variable volume without limits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Specified calibrated volume

A volumetric pipette provides a fixed accurately calibrated liquid volume.

13. A volumetric flask is used to prepare a solution at a specified:

  1. Electrical current
  2. Final calibrated volume
  3. Unknown mass density only
  4. Boiling temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Final calibrated volume

The calibration mark sets the final solution volume.

14. The meniscus of a clear aqueous solution is usually read at its:

  1. Bottom at eye level
  2. Top from above
  3. Centre from below
  4. Any position without parallax concern
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bottom at eye level

Eye-level reading of the lower meniscus minimises parallax error.

15. A burette is rinsed with the solution it will contain mainly to avoid:

  1. Changing atomic numbers
  2. Heating every drop
  3. Removing all dissolved solute
  4. Dilution by residual water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Dilution by residual water

Residual rinse water would alter the delivered reagent concentration.

16. The equivalence point corresponds to:

  1. Equal solution volumes always
  2. Equal masses always
  3. Stoichiometrically matched reactant amounts
  4. A chosen colour in every possible reaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stoichiometrically matched reactant amounts

Balanced chemical requirements determine equivalence, not necessarily equal volumes.

17. An indicator endpoint is an observed change used to estimate the:

  1. Critical temperature
  2. Equivalence point
  3. Melting point
  4. Atomic radius
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Equivalence point

The endpoint should occur close to the chemical equivalence condition.

18. Phenolphthalein is ordinarily colourless in acidic solution and pink in:

  1. An appropriately basic solution
  2. Every neutral salt solution
  3. All concentrated acids
  4. Pure benzene always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An appropriately basic solution

Its indicator transition lies in the basic pH range.

19. Methyl orange changes colour in an:

  1. Extremely alkaline-only range
  2. Absolute zero range
  3. Unchanging colour at all pH
  4. Acidic transition range
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acidic transition range

Its acid-base transition is useful for suitable acidic endpoints.

20. A primary standard should ideally be:

  1. Always hygroscopic
  2. Rapidly decomposing
  3. Pure, stable and accurately weighable
  4. Highly volatile and impure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Pure, stable and accurately weighable

These properties allow reliable preparation of known amounts.

21. In acid-base calculations, equivalents depend on:

  1. Only its atomic number
  2. The specified reaction
  3. Only the substance's colour
  4. Only container volume
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The specified reaction

The reactive proton or hydroxide capacity may vary with the reaction considered.

22. For full neutralisation, one mole H₂SO₄ reacts with how many moles NaOH?

  1. 2
  2. 1
  3. 3
  4. 4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2

H₂SO₄+2NaOH→Na₂SO₄+2H₂O uses two hydroxide equivalents per acid molecule.

23. A white precipitate with AgNO₃ that dissolves in dilute ammonia suggests:

  1. Iodide
  2. Nitrate
  3. Sulfate exclusively
  4. Chloride
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Chloride

Silver chloride commonly dissolves through silver-ammonia complex formation.

24. A carbonate sample treated with dilute acid commonly releases a gas that turns limewater:

  1. Colourless from blue
  2. Purple
  3. Milky
  4. Deep blue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Milky

Carbon dioxide forms calcium carbonate in limewater.

25. The flame colour associated with sodium is characteristically:

  1. Blue-green
  2. Yellow
  3. Lilac
  4. Brick red
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Yellow

Sodium's intense yellow emission is a familiar qualitative signal.

26. Potassium's characteristic flame is commonly described as:

  1. Lilac
  2. Intense sodium yellow
  3. Brick red
  4. Crimson red
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lilac

Potassium gives a lilac flame, sometimes masked by sodium contamination.

27. Calcium salts commonly give a flame described as:

  1. Lilac
  2. Sodium yellow
  3. Green-blue
  4. Brick red
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Brick red

Calcium's qualitative flame colour is traditionally described as brick red.

28. A positive ammonium-ion test with warm alkali releases:

  1. Sulfur dioxide
  2. Hydrogen chloride
  3. Ammonia
  4. Carbon dioxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ammonia

OH⁻ converts NH₄⁺ to NH₃, which can affect moist indicator paper.

29. An acidified sulfite can release:

  1. N₂ only
  2. SO₂
  3. NH₃
  4. O₂ only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. SO₂

Acidification of sulfite produces sulfur dioxide.

30. For reliable repeated titrations, concordant readings should be:

  1. Close to one another
  2. Deliberately widely different
  3. Unrecorded
  4. Always exactly zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Close to one another

Agreement supports precision, though it alone does not prove accuracy.
