Excretory Products and Their Elimination

Excretory Products and Their Elimination

Key concepts

  • The nephron is the functional unit of a kidney. Urine formation involves filtration, selective reabsorption and secretion.
  • Glomerular filtration moves water and small solutes into Bowman’s capsule while normally retaining blood cells and most large proteins.
  • ADH increases water reabsorption in the distal nephron, especially collecting ducts. Humans primarily excrete nitrogen as urea.

Connect the ideas

A filtered glucose molecule is normally reabsorbed rather than excreted. Filtration alone does not determine final urine composition.

Common mistake

Excretion removes metabolic wastes; egestion removes unabsorbed material from the digestive tract.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

1. Bowman’s capsule surrounds the:

Glomerulus

The glomerular capillary tuft and Bowman’s capsule form the renal corpuscle.

2. Most filtered glucose is normally reabsorbed in the:

Proximal convoluted tubule

The proximal tubule reabsorbs glucose under normal filtered loads using transport mechanisms.

3. The loop of Henle contributes to formation of a renal medullary:

Osmotic gradient

Countercurrent processes support the gradient needed for water conservation.

4. The ureter carries urine from the kidney to the:

Urinary bladder

The bladder stores urine arriving through the ureters.

5. The urethra carries urine from the bladder to the:

Exterior

Do not confuse the urethra with a ureter.

6. Ammonia is generally more toxic and requires more water for excretion than:

Urea

Converting ammonia to urea reduces toxicity and permits excretion with less water than direct ammonia elimination.

7. Aldosterone commonly increases renal reabsorption of:

Sodium

Aldosterone promotes sodium retention and potassium secretion in the distal nephron, influencing fluid balance.

1. The functional unit of the kidney is the:

  1. Neuron
  2. Nephron
  3. Alveolus
  4. Sarcomere
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nephron

Nephrons perform filtration and tubular processing.

2. Humans are primarily:

  1. Ammonotelic
  2. Uricotelic
  3. Ureotelic
  4. Non-excretory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ureotelic

Urea is the principal nitrogenous waste in humans.

3. ADH generally increases renal:

  1. Water reabsorption
  2. Glucose oxidation
  3. Protein filtration
  4. Red-cell excretion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Water reabsorption

ADH increases water permeability and reabsorption in the distal nephron.

4. Bowman’s capsule surrounds the:

  1. Glomerulus
  2. Entire kidney
  3. Urinary bladder
  4. Ureter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Glomerulus

The glomerular capillary tuft and Bowman’s capsule form the renal corpuscle.

5. Most filtered glucose is normally reabsorbed in the:

  1. Proximal convoluted tubule
  2. Collecting duct only
  3. Ureter
  4. Urinary bladder
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Proximal convoluted tubule

The proximal tubule reabsorbs glucose under normal filtered loads using transport mechanisms.

6. The loop of Henle contributes to formation of a renal medullary:

  1. Osmotic gradient
  2. Air-pressure gradient
  3. DNA sequence
  4. Bone matrix
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Osmotic gradient

Countercurrent processes support the gradient needed for water conservation.

7. The ureter carries urine from the kidney to the:

  1. Urinary bladder
  2. Liver
  3. Heart
  4. Urethra directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Urinary bladder

The bladder stores urine arriving through the ureters.

8. The urethra carries urine from the bladder to the:

  1. Exterior
  2. Kidney cortex
  3. Liver
  4. Glomerulus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Exterior

Do not confuse the urethra with a ureter.

9. Ammonia is generally more toxic and requires more water for excretion than:

  1. Urea
  2. Every nutrient
  3. Oxygen
  4. Glucose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Urea

Converting ammonia to urea reduces toxicity and permits excretion with less water than direct ammonia elimination.

10. Aldosterone commonly increases renal reabsorption of:

  1. Sodium
  2. Only glucose
  3. Only proteins
  4. Red blood cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sodium

Aldosterone promotes sodium retention and potassium secretion in the distal nephron, influencing fluid balance.

11. The basic functional unit of the kidney is the:

  1. Nephron
  2. Alveolus
  3. Neuron
  4. Sarcomere
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nephron

Nephrons filter and modify fluid to form urine.

12. The glomerulus is a network of:

  2. Muscle fibres
  3. Lymph ducts only
  4. Capillaries

Answer: D. Capillaries

Its pressure supports filtration into the capsule.

13. The glomerulus is enclosed by:

  1. Pericardium
  2. Joint capsule
  3. Bowman's capsule
  4. Pleura
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Bowman's capsule

The capsule collects the filtered fluid.

14. The vessel entering a glomerulus is the:

  1. Ureter
  2. Afferent arteriole
  3. Efferent arteriole
  4. Renal vein only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Afferent arteriole

Afferent blood flow supplies the glomerular capillaries.

15. The vessel leaving a glomerulus is the:

  1. Efferent arteriole
  2. Afferent arteriole
  3. Urethra
  4. Vena cava directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Efferent arteriole

It carries blood onwards to further capillary networks.

16. Normal glomerular filtration largely excludes:

  1. Water
  2. Small salts
  3. Glucose
  4. Blood cells and most large proteins
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Blood cells and most large proteins

The filtration barrier restricts large or cellular components.

17. Most filtered glucose is normally reclaimed primarily in the:

  1. Bladder
  2. Ureter
  3. Proximal convoluted tubule
  4. Collecting duct exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Proximal convoluted tubule

Transport systems in the early tubule reclaim glucose under normal conditions.

18. The descending limb of the loop of Henle is relatively permeable to:

  1. Only platelets
  2. Water
  3. Only large proteins
  4. Only red cells

Water movement contributes to tubular concentration.

19. The ascending limb ordinarily has low permeability to:

  1. Water
  2. All ions without exception

  4. Only glucose

Answer: A. Water

Salt transport can occur without corresponding water movement.

20. The countercurrent mechanism helps establish a concentration gradient in the:

  1. Bladder lumen
  2. Renal pelvis only
  3. Urethra
  4. Renal medulla

Answer: D. Renal medulla

Loop and vascular arrangements support medullary osmotic organisation.

21. ADH increases water reabsorption chiefly through effects on the:

  1. Bladder muscles only
  2. Renal artery wall only
  3. Late distal tubule and collecting ducts
  4. Glomerular red cells

Answer: C. Late distal tubule and collecting ducts

It increases water permeability in responsive nephron regions.

22. Aldosterone promotes reabsorption of:

  1. Only oxygen
  2. Sodium
  3. Only glucose
  4. Only haemoglobin

Answer: B. Sodium

Its tubular actions also influence potassium handling.

23. Renin release participates in regulation of:

  1. Blood pressure and salt-water balance

Answer: A. Blood pressure and salt-water balance

The renin-angiotensin system helps coordinate renal and vascular responses.

24. Urea is formed chiefly in the:

  1. Nonexcretory
