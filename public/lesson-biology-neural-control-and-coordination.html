Neural Control and Coordination

Neural Control and Coordination

Key concepts

  • Neurons receive and transmit signals. The central nervous system consists of the brain and spinal cord.
  • Action potentials involve rapid changes in membrane ion permeability. Depolarisation commonly involves sodium entry, followed by potassium-mediated repolarisation.
  • Chemical synapses use neurotransmitters across a synaptic cleft. Reflex pathways can produce rapid responses without initial conscious processing.

Connect the ideas

In a withdrawal reflex, sensory input is processed through a spinal pathway, producing a rapid motor response.

Common mistake

An action potential is not a flow of electrons down a wire; it is a propagated membrane-potential change.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Dendrites commonly conduct signals toward the neuron’s:

Cell body

Dendrites receive input; the axon generally carries output away from the soma.

2. A myelin sheath generally helps increase impulse-conduction speed by supporting:

Saltatory conduction

Action potentials are regenerated at nodes of Ranvier, reducing the extent of membrane activation along the axon.

3. Nodes of Ranvier are gaps in the:

Myelin sheath

These exposed axon regions contain channels important for impulse regeneration.

4. Repolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

Potassium leaving

Voltage-gated potassium conductance helps restore the negative membrane potential.

5. The cerebellum is especially associated with:

Coordination of movement and balance

It helps coordinate motor activity and maintain posture and balance.

6. Photoreceptor cells in the retina include:

Rods and cones

Rods support low-light vision; cones support colour and high-acuity vision under suitable light.

7. The cochlea is associated primarily with:

Hearing

Mechanical vibrations in the cochlea stimulate sensory hair cells for auditory signalling.

1. The central nervous system consists of:

  1. All nerves only
  2. Brain and spinal cord
  3. Muscles and bones
  4. Eyes and ears only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Brain and spinal cord

The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord.

2. At a chemical synapse, the signal crosses the cleft via:

  1. Neurotransmitter
  2. Bone tissue
  3. Red blood cells
  4. DNA replication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Neurotransmitter

Neurotransmitters are released and bind to postsynaptic receptors.

3. The rapid depolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

  1. Sodium entering
  2. Sodium leaving only
  3. No ion movement
  4. Protein entering
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sodium entering

Voltage-gated sodium channels allow inward sodium movement.

4. Dendrites commonly conduct signals toward the neuron’s:

  1. Cell body
  2. Axon terminal only
  3. Myelin sheath only
  4. Synaptic vesicles only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell body

Dendrites receive input; the axon generally carries output away from the soma.

5. A myelin sheath generally helps increase impulse-conduction speed by supporting:

  1. Saltatory conduction
  2. Only continuous diffusion through cytoplasm
  3. Permanent membrane depolarisation
  4. Removal of nodes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Saltatory conduction

Action potentials are regenerated at nodes of Ranvier, reducing the extent of membrane activation along the axon.

6. Nodes of Ranvier are gaps in the:

  1. Myelin sheath
  2. Nuclear membrane
  3. Bone
  4. Retina only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Myelin sheath

These exposed axon regions contain channels important for impulse regeneration.

7. Repolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

  1. Potassium leaving
  2. Sodium entering continuously
  3. Calcium leaving every time
  4. No ion flux

Answer: A. Potassium leaving

Voltage-gated potassium conductance helps restore the negative membrane potential.

Rods support low-light vision; cones support colour and high-acuity vision under suitable light.

  1. Hearing
