Neural Control and Coordination

Neural Control and Coordination

Key concepts

  • Neurons receive and transmit signals. The central nervous system consists of the brain and spinal cord.
  • Action potentials involve rapid changes in membrane ion permeability. Depolarisation commonly involves sodium entry, followed by potassium-mediated repolarisation.
  • Chemical synapses use neurotransmitters across a synaptic cleft. Reflex pathways can produce rapid responses without initial conscious processing.

Connect the ideas

In a withdrawal reflex, sensory input is processed through a spinal pathway, producing a rapid motor response.

Common mistake

An action potential is not a flow of electrons down a wire; it is a propagated membrane-potential change.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Dendrites commonly conduct signals toward the neuron’s:

Cell body

Dendrites receive input; the axon generally carries output away from the soma.

2. A myelin sheath generally helps increase impulse-conduction speed by supporting:

Saltatory conduction

Action potentials are regenerated at nodes of Ranvier, reducing the extent of membrane activation along the axon.

3. Nodes of Ranvier are gaps in the:

Myelin sheath

These exposed axon regions contain channels important for impulse regeneration.

4. Repolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

Potassium leaving

Voltage-gated potassium conductance helps restore the negative membrane potential.

5. The cerebellum is especially associated with:

Coordination of movement and balance

It helps coordinate motor activity and maintain posture and balance.

6. Photoreceptor cells in the retina include:

Rods and cones

Rods support low-light vision; cones support colour and high-acuity vision under suitable light.

7. The cochlea is associated primarily with:

Hearing

Mechanical vibrations in the cochlea stimulate sensory hair cells for auditory signalling.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The central nervous system consists of:

  1. All nerves only
  2. Brain and spinal cord
  3. Muscles and bones
  4. Eyes and ears only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Brain and spinal cord

The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord.

2. At a chemical synapse, the signal crosses the cleft via:

  1. Neurotransmitter
  2. Bone tissue
  3. Red blood cells
  4. DNA replication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Neurotransmitter

Neurotransmitters are released and bind to postsynaptic receptors.

3. The rapid depolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

  1. Sodium entering
  2. Sodium leaving only
  3. No ion movement
  4. Protein entering
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sodium entering

Voltage-gated sodium channels allow inward sodium movement.

4. Dendrites commonly conduct signals toward the neuron’s:

  1. Cell body
  2. Axon terminal only
  3. Myelin sheath only
  4. Synaptic vesicles only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell body

Dendrites receive input; the axon generally carries output away from the soma.

5. A myelin sheath generally helps increase impulse-conduction speed by supporting:

  1. Saltatory conduction
  2. Only continuous diffusion through cytoplasm
  3. Permanent membrane depolarisation
  4. Removal of nodes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Saltatory conduction

Action potentials are regenerated at nodes of Ranvier, reducing the extent of membrane activation along the axon.

6. Nodes of Ranvier are gaps in the:

  1. Myelin sheath
  2. Nuclear membrane
  3. Bone
  4. Retina only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Myelin sheath

These exposed axon regions contain channels important for impulse regeneration.

7. Repolarisation of a typical neuronal action potential mainly involves:

  1. Potassium leaving
  2. Sodium entering continuously
  3. Calcium leaving every time
  4. No ion flux
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Potassium leaving

Voltage-gated potassium conductance helps restore the negative membrane potential.

8. The cerebellum is especially associated with:

  1. Coordination of movement and balance
  2. Only blood filtration
  3. Only insulin secretion
  4. Only hearing receptors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Coordination of movement and balance

It helps coordinate motor activity and maintain posture and balance.

9. Photoreceptor cells in the retina include:

  1. Rods and cones
  2. Only guard cells
  3. Only red blood cells
  4. Only osteocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rods and cones

Rods support low-light vision; cones support colour and high-acuity vision under suitable light.

10. The cochlea is associated primarily with:

  1. Hearing
  2. Smell
  3. Taste
  4. Blood pressure regulation only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hearing

Mechanical vibrations in the cochlea stimulate sensory hair cells for auditory signalling.

11. The central nervous system includes the:

  1. Only sensory receptors
  2. Brain and spinal cord
  3. Only peripheral nerves
  4. Only adrenal glands
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Brain and spinal cord

These structures form the central processing system.

12. The peripheral nervous system includes nerves outside the:

  1. Brain and spinal cord
  2. Whole body
  3. Heart only
  4. Skin only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Brain and spinal cord

Peripheral nerves connect the CNS with tissues.

13. Sensory or afferent neurons carry information:

  1. Only away from the CNS
  2. Only between bones
  3. Only through arteries
  4. Towards the CNS
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Towards the CNS

They convey receptor signals to central processing sites.

14. Motor or efferent neurons carry commands:

  1. Only towards receptors
  2. Only inside capillaries
  3. From the CNS towards effectors
  4. Only towards the CNS
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. From the CNS towards effectors

They activate muscles or other responsive tissues.

15. A resting neuron's interior is ordinarily:

  1. Without ions
  2. Negative relative to outside
  3. Positive relative to outside
  4. Always exactly neutral
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Negative relative to outside

Selective permeability and ion gradients establish resting polarity.

16. Rapid depolarisation in a typical axon involves increased entry of:

  1. Na⁺
  2. Only large proteins
  3. Only glucose
  4. Only bicarbonate crystals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Na⁺

Opening voltage-gated sodium channels drives the upstroke.

17. Repolarisation prominently involves outward movement of:

  1. Na⁺ exclusively
  2. DNA
  3. Haemoglobin
  4. K⁺
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. K⁺

Potassium conductance helps restore negative membrane voltage.

18. An action potential is generally described as:

  1. Only a chemical diffusion front
  2. Only an ATP molecule
  3. All-or-none at a given excitable site
  4. Continuously variable in peak with every stimulus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. All-or-none at a given excitable site

Once threshold is reached, a full electrical event occurs.

19. Myelin increases conduction efficiency by promoting:

  1. Only ATP elimination
  2. Saltatory conduction
  3. Only slower continuous leakage
  4. Only synaptic digestion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Saltatory conduction

Electrical excitation effectively jumps between exposed nodes.

20. Gaps between myelin segments are:

  1. Nodes of Ranvier
  2. Synaptic vesicles
  3. Nucleoli
  4. Sarcomeres
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nodes of Ranvier

These exposed regions contain concentrated excitable channels.

21. A chemical synapse transmits a signal using:

  1. Only DNA transfer
  2. Only direct bone contact
  3. Only blood flow
  4. Neurotransmitter release
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Neurotransmitter release

Released molecules act on receptors on the next cell.

22. Neurotransmitter is released from presynaptic:

  1. Lysosomal nuclei
  2. Myelin plates
  3. Vesicles
  4. Chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Vesicles

Fusion with the terminal membrane delivers transmitter to the cleft.

23. The small gap at a chemical synapse is the:

  1. Sarcomere
  2. Synaptic cleft
  3. Nuclear envelope
  4. Intercalated disc
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Synaptic cleft

The transmitter crosses this extracellular space.

24. A reflex arc typically links a receptor to an:

  1. Effector through neural pathways
  2. Only endocrine chromosome
  3. Only alveolus
  4. Only root hair
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Effector through neural pathways

It produces a rapid patterned response through neural circuitry.

25. The corpus callosum connects the two:

  1. Kidneys
  2. Atria
  3. Retinal layers
  4. Cerebral hemispheres
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cerebral hemispheres

This major fibre tract enables communication between the cerebral hemispheres.

26. The hypothalamus contributes to regulation of:

  1. Only bone hardness
  2. Only erythrocyte shape
  3. Homeostasis and endocrine functions
  4. Only external hearing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Homeostasis and endocrine functions

It links physiological regulation with autonomic and hormonal responses.

27. The medulla contains important centres regulating:

  1. Only starch digestion
  2. Breathing and cardiovascular activity
  3. Only voluntary finger choices
  4. Only colour recognition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Breathing and cardiovascular activity

Brainstem circuits control crucial involuntary functions.

28. The protective membranes surrounding the brain are:

  1. Meninges
  2. Pleura
  3. Peritoneum only
  4. Tonoplast
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meninges

Layered coverings protect central nervous tissue.

29. The autonomic nervous system chiefly regulates:

  1. Only voluntary skeletal movement
  2. Only DNA replication
  3. Only taste perception
  4. Involuntary visceral activity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Involuntary visceral activity

Its pathways control smooth muscle, cardiac muscle and glands.

30. The sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions often have:

  1. Only identical effects everywhere
  2. Only sensory roles
  3. Coordinated contrasting effects
  4. No functional relationship
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Coordinated contrasting effects

Their balance adjusts organ activity to physiological needs.
