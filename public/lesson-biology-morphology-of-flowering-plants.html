Morphology of Flowering Plants

Morphology of Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Morphology studies external structures: roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.
  • A flower has sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. The androecium consists of stamens and the gynoecium of carpels.
  • After fertilisation, ovules usually form seeds and the ovary forms the fruit. Roots and stems can be modified for storage or other functions.

Connect the ideas

A potato tuber is a modified stem, identifiable by its buds or “eyes”; a sweet potato is a modified root.

Common mistake

Underground storage organs are not all roots. Position alone does not identify an organ.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Leaf venation in many dicots is:

Reticulate

A network pattern is common in dicots; use this as a general trait with known exceptions.

2. A typical monocot seed contains:

One cotyledon

Monocotyledons are named for the single cotyledon in the embryo.

3. In a racemose inflorescence, the main axis usually:

Continues to grow

The main axis is indeterminate and flowers commonly show acropetal succession.

4. In a cymose inflorescence, the main axis characteristically:

Terminates in a flower

The terminal flower limits the main axis; younger flowers develop laterally.

5. The calyx consists of:

Sepals

Sepals form the calyx; petals form the corolla.

6. A flower with the ovary above the attachment of other floral whorls is typically:

Hypogynous

A hypogynous flower has a superior ovary, with other whorls inserted below it.

7. Arrangement of ovules within the ovary is called:

Placentation

Placentation describes ovule arrangement; the other terms describe leaf veins, leaf arrangement and floral-bud overlap.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The androecium is made up of:

  1. Sepals
  2. Petals
  3. Stamens
  4. Carpels
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stamens

Stamens are the male reproductive floral organs.

2. After fertilisation, an ovule usually develops into a:

  1. Fruit
  2. Seed
  3. Petal
  4. Stigma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seed

The ovule develops into a seed; the ovary usually develops into fruit.

3. A potato tuber is a modified:

  1. Root
  2. Leaf
  3. Stem
  4. Flower
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stem

Its buds or eyes identify it as a stem modification.

4. Leaf venation in many dicots is:

  1. Reticulate
  2. Always parallel
  3. Absent
  4. Only circular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reticulate

A network pattern is common in dicots; use this as a general trait with known exceptions.

5. A typical monocot seed contains:

  1. One cotyledon
  2. Two cotyledons
  3. No embryo
  4. Four cotyledons always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One cotyledon

Monocotyledons are named for the single cotyledon in the embryo.

6. In a racemose inflorescence, the main axis usually:

  1. Continues to grow
  2. Ends immediately in a terminal flower
  3. Becomes a root
  4. Has no flowers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Continues to grow

The main axis is indeterminate and flowers commonly show acropetal succession.

7. In a cymose inflorescence, the main axis characteristically:

  1. Terminates in a flower
  2. Grows indefinitely
  3. Forms only seeds
  4. Has no lateral branching
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Terminates in a flower

The terminal flower limits the main axis; younger flowers develop laterally.

8. The calyx consists of:

  1. Sepals
  2. Stamens
  3. Carpels
  4. Roots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sepals

Sepals form the calyx; petals form the corolla.

9. A flower with the ovary above the attachment of other floral whorls is typically:

  1. Hypogynous
  2. Epigynous
  3. Always unisexual
  4. Always sterile
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hypogynous

A hypogynous flower has a superior ovary, with other whorls inserted below it.

10. Arrangement of ovules within the ovary is called:

  1. Placentation
  2. Venation
  3. Phyllotaxy
  4. Aestivation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Placentation

Placentation describes ovule arrangement; the other terms describe leaf veins, leaf arrangement and floral-bud overlap.
