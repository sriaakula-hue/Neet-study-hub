Morphology of Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Morphology studies external structures: roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.
  • A flower has sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. The androecium consists of stamens and the gynoecium of carpels.
  • After fertilisation, ovules usually form seeds and the ovary forms the fruit. Roots and stems can be modified for storage or other functions.

Connect the ideas

A potato tuber is a modified stem, identifiable by its buds or “eyes”; a sweet potato is a modified root.

Common mistake

Underground storage organs are not all roots. Position alone does not identify an organ.

Hypogynous

Placentation

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The androecium is made up of:

  1. Sepals
  2. Petals
  3. Stamens
  4. Carpels
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stamens

Stamens are the male reproductive floral organs.

2. After fertilisation, an ovule usually develops into a:

  1. Fruit
  2. Seed
  3. Petal
  4. Stigma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Seed

The ovule develops into a seed; the ovary usually develops into fruit.

3. A potato tuber is a modified:

  1. Root
  2. Leaf
  3. Stem
  4. Flower
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stem

Its buds or eyes identify it as a stem modification.

4. Leaf venation in many dicots is:

  1. Reticulate
  2. Always parallel
  3. Absent
  4. Only circular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reticulate

A network pattern is common in dicots; use this as a general trait with known exceptions.

5. A typical monocot seed contains:

  1. One cotyledon
  2. Two cotyledons
  3. No embryo
  4. Four cotyledons always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One cotyledon

Monocotyledons are named for the single cotyledon in the embryo.

6. In a racemose inflorescence, the main axis usually:

  1. Continues to grow
  2. Ends immediately in a terminal flower
  3. Becomes a root
  4. Has no flowers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Continues to grow

The main axis is indeterminate and flowers commonly show acropetal succession.

7. In a cymose inflorescence, the main axis characteristically:

  1. Terminates in a flower
  2. Grows indefinitely
  3. Forms only seeds
  4. Has no lateral branching
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Terminates in a flower

The terminal flower limits the main axis; younger flowers develop laterally.

8. The calyx consists of:

  1. Sepals
  2. Stamens
  3. Carpels
  4. Roots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sepals

Sepals form the calyx; petals form the corolla.

9. A flower with the ovary above the attachment of other floral whorls is typically:

  1. Hypogynous
  2. Epigynous
  3. Always unisexual
  4. Always sterile
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hypogynous

A hypogynous flower has a superior ovary, with other whorls inserted below it.

10. Arrangement of ovules within the ovary is called:

  1. Placentation
  2. Venation
  3. Phyllotaxy
  4. Aestivation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Placentation

Placentation describes ovule arrangement; the other terms describe leaf veins, leaf arrangement and floral-bud overlap.

11. A typical primary root develops from the embryo's:

  1. Cotyledon tip
  2. Endosperm
  3. Radicle
  4. Plumule
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Radicle

The radicle is the embryonic root axis.

12. A typical shoot develops principally from the embryo's:

  1. Micropyle
  2. Plumule
  3. Radicle
  4. Seed coat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plumule

The plumule contains the embryonic shoot region.

13. A tap-root system is commonly associated with:

  1. Many dicots
  2. All monocots without exception
  3. All algae
  4. All fungi
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Many dicots

The persistent primary root produces lateral roots in many dicot plants.

14. A fibrous root system is commonly associated with:

  1. All gymnosperms exclusively
  2. Only mosses
  3. Only parasites
  4. Many monocots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Many monocots

Many roots of similar calibre replace the short-lived primary root.

15. Roots arising from parts other than the radicle are called:

  1. Pollen roots
  2. Endosperm roots
  3. Adventitious roots
  4. Only lateral primary roots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Adventitious roots

They originate from stems, leaves or other nonradicle structures.

16. Prop roots are classically illustrated by:

  1. Onion bulb
  2. Banyan
  3. Pea
  4. Potato tuber
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Banyan

Descending supporting roots develop from banyan branches.

17. Pneumatophores in some mangroves aid:

  1. Gas exchange
  2. Only sugar storage
  3. Only pollination
  4. Only seed dispersal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gas exchange

Upward roots help aerate tissues in waterlogged substrates.

18. A pea tendril is a modification of the:

  1. Root
  2. Fruit wall
  3. Seed coat
  4. Leaf
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Leaf

Leaf-derived tendrils help the plant climb and obtain support.

19. The nodes of a stem are regions where:

  1. Only flowers lose nuclei
  2. No tissues grow
  3. Leaves or branches originate
  4. Only root caps occur
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Leaves or branches originate

Nodes mark attachment or initiation points of lateral structures.

20. The region between two successive nodes is an:

  1. Sepal
  2. Internode
  3. Petiole
  4. Stigma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Internode

Internodes form the intervening stem lengths.

21. The stalk connecting a leaf blade to the stem is the:

  1. Petiole
  2. Pedicel
  3. Style
  4. Filament
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Petiole

A petiole attaches and supports the lamina.

22. Reticulate venation is commonly seen in:

  1. All monocots only
  2. All roots
  3. All petals without exception
  4. Many dicot leaves
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Many dicot leaves

The veins form a branching network.

23. Parallel venation is commonly seen in:

  1. All woody roots
  2. All fern rhizoids
  3. Many monocot leaves
  4. All dicot leaves only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Many monocot leaves

Veins run roughly alongside one another in many monocot laminae.

24. Leaf arrangement on a stem is termed:

  1. Vernation only
  2. Phyllotaxy
  3. Placentation
  4. Aestivation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Phyllotaxy

Phyllotaxy describes the arrangement of leaves around the stem.

25. The outermost floral whorl is ordinarily the:

  1. Calyx
  2. Androecium
  3. Gynoecium
  4. Corolla
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Calyx

Sepals collectively form the calyx.

26. Petals collectively constitute the:

  1. Calyx
  2. Androecium
  3. Placenta
  4. Corolla
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Corolla

The petal whorl is termed the corolla.

27. Stamens collectively form the:

  1. Calyx
  2. Pericarp
  3. Androecium
  4. Gynoecium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Androecium

The androecium is the male reproductive floral whorl.

28. Carpels collectively form the:

  1. Calyx
  2. Gynoecium
  3. Androecium
  4. Corolla
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gynoecium

The female floral structures constitute the gynoecium.

29. Attachment of ovules within an ovary is called:

  1. Placentation
  2. Phyllotaxy
  3. Aestivation
  4. Germination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Placentation

The ovule-bearing arrangement varies among floral groups.

30. In an epigynous flower, the ovary is described as:

  1. Superior
  2. Always absent
  3. Only lateral
  4. Inferior
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Inferior

Other floral parts appear attached above the ovary.
