Periodicity and Classification of Elements

Periodicity and Classification of Elements

Key concepts

  • The modern periodic table is ordered by atomic number. Elements in a group often share related valence-shell configurations.
  • Atomic radius generally decreases across a period and increases down a group. Ionisation enthalpy generally follows the opposite trend, with exceptions.
  • Cations are usually smaller than their parent atoms; anions are usually larger. Isoelectronic species differ in nuclear charge.

Worked connection

Among isoelectronic O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺ and Mg²⁺, increasing nuclear charge generally decreases ionic radius.

Watch out

Periodic trends are general patterns rather than exception-free rules. Subshell energies and electron pairing cause deviations.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Across a period, effective nuclear attraction generally:

Increases

Increasing nuclear charge with incomplete shielding contributes to contraction and generally higher ionisation enthalpy.

2. Down a main-group column, added electron shells generally cause atomic size to:

Increase

Outer electrons occupy higher principal shells, usually farther from the nucleus.

3. Relative to a neutral sodium atom, Na⁺ is usually:

Smaller

Losing the outer electron removes the outer shell and increases attraction per remaining electron.

4. First ionisation enthalpy is the energy for removing an electron from a:

Gaseous neutral atom

The first ionisation process is M(g) → M⁺(g) + e⁻.

5. A noble-gas closed shell generally gives:

High ionisation enthalpy

A filled valence shell is relatively stable, making removal energetically costly.

6. The s block generally involves filling the outer:

s subshell

The differentiating electron occupies an s orbital in the standard block classification.

7. Which ion has ten electrons?

Na⁺

Sodium has atomic number 11; losing one electron gives ten.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Modern periodic ordering is based on:

  1. Atomic mass only
  2. Atomic number
  3. Neutron number
  4. Density
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Atomic number

The modern periodic law relates properties to atomic number.

2. Atomic radius generally changes across a period from left to right by:

  1. Increasing
  2. Decreasing
  3. Remaining exactly constant
  4. Becoming zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Decreasing

Increasing effective nuclear attraction generally contracts atomic size.

3. Which is smallest among these isoelectronic ions?

  1. O²⁻
  2. F⁻
  3. Na⁺
  4. Mg²⁺
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mg²⁺

Mg²⁺ has the largest nuclear charge among these ten-electron ions.

4. Across a period, effective nuclear attraction generally:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases always
  3. Is exactly zero
  4. Becomes gravitational
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increases

Increasing nuclear charge with incomplete shielding contributes to contraction and generally higher ionisation enthalpy.

5. Down a main-group column, added electron shells generally cause atomic size to:

  1. Increase
  2. Decrease always
  3. Remain exactly constant
  4. Become zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increase

Outer electrons occupy higher principal shells, usually farther from the nucleus.

6. Relative to a neutral sodium atom, Na⁺ is usually:

  1. Smaller
  2. Larger
  3. Identical in radius exactly
  4. Without a nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Smaller

Losing the outer electron removes the outer shell and increases attraction per remaining electron.

7. First ionisation enthalpy is the energy for removing an electron from a:

  1. Gaseous neutral atom
  2. Liquid ion only
  3. Solid nucleus
  4. Gaseous anion only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gaseous neutral atom

The first ionisation process is M(g) → M⁺(g) + e⁻.

8. A noble-gas closed shell generally gives:

  1. High ionisation enthalpy
  2. Zero ionisation enthalpy
  3. No electrons
  4. Maximum metallic conductivity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. High ionisation enthalpy

A filled valence shell is relatively stable, making removal energetically costly.

9. The s block generally involves filling the outer:

  1. s subshell
  2. p subshell
  3. d subshell only
  4. f subshell only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. s subshell

The differentiating electron occupies an s orbital in the standard block classification.

10. Which ion has ten electrons?

  1. Na⁺
  2. Na neutral
  3. Mg neutral
  4. K⁺
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Na⁺

Sodium has atomic number 11; losing one electron gives ten.

11. The modern periodic table orders elements by increasing:

  1. Neutron count
  2. Density
  3. Atomic number
  4. Atomic mass alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Atomic number

Atomic number determines nuclear charge and the characteristic electron configuration.

12. Elements in the same group commonly share a similar:

  1. Number of neutrons
  2. Valence-shell electron pattern
  3. Number of occupied shells
  4. Atomic mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Valence-shell electron pattern

Similar valence configurations explain many shared chemical properties.

13. Across a typical period, atomic radius generally:

  1. Decreases
  2. Increases continuously
  3. Remains identical
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Decreases

Increasing effective nuclear attraction draws valence electrons closer across a period.

14. Down a typical group, atomic radius generally:

  1. Decreases
  2. Remains identical
  3. Depends only on isotope mass
  4. Increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Increases

Additional occupied shells and shielding increase the outer-electron distance.

15. First ionisation enthalpy measures energy needed to remove an electron from:

  1. A solid metal lattice directly
  2. A gas-phase cation forming an atom
  3. An isolated gaseous atom
  4. An aqueous anion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An isolated gaseous atom

The first ionisation step converts a gaseous neutral atom to a gaseous cation.

16. Which atom usually has the largest radius among these period-3 choices?

  1. Cl
  2. Na
  3. Mg
  4. Si
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Na

Atomic size generally decreases from left to right across period 3.

17. Which element has the greatest electronegativity on the usual Pauling scale?

  1. Fluorine
  2. Oxygen
  3. Chlorine
  4. Nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fluorine

Fluorine strongly attracts shared electron density in a chemical bond.

18. An atom losing electrons usually forms an ion that is:

  1. Larger in every case
  2. Uncharged
  3. Equal in size to every anion
  4. Smaller than the parent atom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Smaller than the parent atom

Cations typically have reduced electron repulsion and stronger attraction per remaining electron.

19. An atom gaining an electron usually forms an ion that is:

  1. A neutral molecule
  2. A nucleus without electrons
  3. Larger than the parent atom
  4. Smaller in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Larger than the parent atom

Additional electron repulsion expands the electron cloud of a typical anion.

20. Within an isoelectronic series, radius usually decreases as:

  1. Nuclear attraction decreases
  2. Nuclear charge increases
  3. Proton number decreases
  4. Electron count increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Nuclear charge increases

Equal electron counts allow nuclear charge to dominate relative ionic size.

21. Which is the smallest ion in the isoelectronic sequence O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺?

  1. Mg²⁺
  2. O²⁻
  3. F⁻
  4. Na⁺
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mg²⁺

All have ten electrons; Mg has the greatest proton number and strongest attraction.

22. The period number of a main-group element usually reflects its:

  1. Total electron count
  2. Number of isotope species
  3. Oxidation state in every compound
  4. Highest occupied principal shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Highest occupied principal shell

The outermost occupied principal energy level identifies the period.

23. The block of an element is based primarily on its:

  1. Common isotope
  2. Physical colour
  3. Differentiating electron's subshell
  4. Atomic mass digit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Differentiating electron's subshell

The subshell receiving the differentiating electron defines s, p, d or f block.

24. An s-block valence configuration is typically:

  1. nf¹⁴ only
  2. ns¹ or ns²
  3. np⁶ only
  4. nd¹⁰ only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. ns¹ or ns²

The characteristic differentiating valence electrons enter an s subshell.

25. The usual valence configuration of group 17 is:

  1. ns²np⁵
  2. ns²np³
  3. ns¹
  4. ns²np⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ns²np⁵

Halogens generally have seven outer-shell electrons and need one for an octet.

26. Which pair lies in the same period?

  1. Li and Na
  2. F and Cl
  3. He and Ne
  4. Na and Cl
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Na and Cl

Sodium and chlorine both belong to period 3.

27. The jump after successive ionisation of all valence electrons is large because the next electron is:

  1. In a higher empty orbital
  2. Unaffected by nuclear charge
  3. A more tightly bound core electron
  4. A neutron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A more tightly bound core electron

Removing a core electron requires substantially greater energy than a valence electron.

28. Compared with magnesium, aluminium's first ionisation enthalpy is slightly lower mainly because its removed electron is:

  1. In a completely filled 3p subshell
  2. A higher-energy 3p electron
  3. A 1s electron
  4. A nuclear proton
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A higher-energy 3p electron

Al loses a 3p electron more readily than Mg loses its 3s electron.

29. Nitrogen has a higher first ionisation enthalpy than oxygen mainly because nitrogen has a:

  1. Half-filled 2p subshell
  2. Completely filled 2p subshell
  3. Larger proton count
  4. Vacant valence shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Half-filled 2p subshell

Nitrogen's half-filled p arrangement is relatively stable; oxygen has paired-electron repulsion.

30. Metallic character generally becomes stronger:

  1. Up a group and towards the right
  2. Only with increasing neutron number
  3. Only in noble gases
  4. Down a group and towards the left
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Down a group and towards the left

Larger size and easier valence-electron loss favour metallic behaviour.
