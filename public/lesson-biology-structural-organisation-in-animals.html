Structural Organisation in Animals

Structural Organisation in Animals

Key concepts

  • Epithelial, connective, muscular and neural tissues have distinct structures and functions. Blood is a connective tissue.
  • Skeletal muscle is striated and generally voluntary; smooth muscle is non-striated and involuntary; cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary.
  • An adult frog has a three-chambered heart, paired kidneys and moist skin that contributes to respiration.

Connect the ideas

A frog’s heart has two atria and one ventricle. This differs from the four-chambered human heart.

Common mistake

Striation does not always mean voluntary control: cardiac muscle is striated but involuntary.

Intercellular matrix

5. Smooth muscle is commonly found in walls of:

Hollow internal organs

Its involuntary contraction supports functions such as intestinal movement and regulation of vessel diameter.

6. A frog is classified among:

Moist skin

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Blood is classified as which tissue?

  1. Epithelial
  2. Connective
  3. Muscular
  4. Neural
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Connective

Blood is a fluid connective tissue.

2. Cardiac muscle is:

  1. Non-striated and voluntary
  2. Striated and involuntary
  3. Striated and voluntary
  4. Non-striated and voluntary only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Striated and involuntary

Cardiac muscle is striated and functions involuntarily.

3. An adult frog heart has:

  1. One chamber
  2. Two chambers
  3. Three chambers
  4. Four chambers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Three chambers

It has two atria and one ventricle.

4. An epithelium typically has closely arranged cells with little:

  1. Intercellular matrix
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Genetic material
  4. Cytoplasm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intercellular matrix

Closely packed epithelial cells cover surfaces or line cavities; a basal support layer helps organise the tissue.

5. Simple squamous epithelium is suited to rapid:

  1. Diffusion across a thin lining
  2. Bone strengthening
  3. Powerful voluntary contraction
  4. Neural impulse generation only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Diffusion across a thin lining

A single thin layer reduces diffusion distance, as in appropriate exchange surfaces.

6. Ciliated epithelium can help move material along a:

  1. Surface
  2. Bone marrow only
  3. DNA strand
  4. Muscle sarcomere
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Surface

Coordinated ciliary beating transports mucus or other material along suitable epithelial linings.

7. Adipose tissue is a specialised connective tissue that stores:

  1. Fat
  2. Only chlorophyll
  3. Only DNA
  4. Only air
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fat

Adipose cells store lipids and contribute to insulation, cushioning and metabolic functions.

8. Smooth muscle is commonly found in walls of:

  1. Hollow internal organs
  2. Only long bones
  3. Only skin keratin
  4. Only the outer tooth enamel
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hollow internal organs

Its involuntary contraction supports functions such as intestinal movement and regulation of vessel diameter.

9. A frog is classified among:

  1. Amphibians
  2. Reptiles
  3. Mammals
  4. Arthropods
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Amphibians

Frogs are amphibian vertebrates, with life-history and anatomical features distinct from reptiles and mammals.

10. An adult frog can exchange respiratory gases through lungs and:

  1. Moist skin
  2. Only dry scales
  3. Only feathers
  4. Only its shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Moist skin

Cutaneous gas exchange depends on a suitably moist, vascularised skin surface.

11. Epithelial tissue generally has cells that are:

  1. Always multinucleate fibres
  2. Only suspended in plasma
  3. Closely packed with little intercellular material
  4. Widely separated in a mineral matrix
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Closely packed with little intercellular material

A compact covering or lining is characteristic of epithelium.

12. A single layer of flattened epithelial cells is:

  1. Cardiac muscle
  2. Simple squamous epithelium
  3. Stratified cuboidal epithelium
  4. Dense regular connective tissue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Simple squamous epithelium

Thin flattened cells support rapid exchange and delicate lining.

13. Kidney tubules commonly contain:

  1. Cuboidal epithelium
  2. Only bone cells
  3. Only smooth muscle
  4. Only cartilage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cuboidal epithelium

Cuboidal epithelial cells support absorption and secretion.

14. Ciliated epithelium helps move:

  1. Bones around joints
  2. Only chromosomes
  3. Only mineral crystals
  4. Material along a surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Material along a surface

Coordinated ciliary beating moves mucus or other materials.

15. A glandular epithelium is specialised for:

  1. Only contraction
  2. Only skeletal support
  3. Secretion
  4. Only impulse conduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Secretion

Its cells produce and release substances.

16. Connective tissue is distinguished by a relatively abundant:

  1. Nuclear envelope around the whole tissue
  2. Extracellular matrix
  3. Photosynthetic apparatus
  4. Thylakoid system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Extracellular matrix

Cells are distributed within a specialised supporting matrix.

17. Tendons connect:

  1. Muscle to bone
  2. Bone to bone
  3. Neuron to neuron only
  4. Artery to vein only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Muscle to bone

Dense connective fibres transmit muscle force to the skeleton.

18. Ligaments commonly connect:

  1. Muscle to skin only
  2. Only neurons
  3. Only blood cells
  4. Bone to bone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Bone to bone

They stabilise connections at joints.

19. Adipose tissue mainly stores:

  1. Only uric acid
  2. Only cellulose
  3. Fat
  4. Only starch
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Fat

Adipocytes contain lipid reserves and provide insulation.

20. Cartilage cells are called:

  1. Erythrocytes
  2. Chondrocytes
  3. Osteocytes
  4. Neurons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chondrocytes

Chondrocytes maintain the cartilage matrix.

21. Mature bone cells are called:

  1. Osteocytes
  2. Chondrocytes
  3. Leucocytes
  4. Hepatocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Osteocytes

Osteocytes occupy spaces within mineralised bone matrix.

22. Blood is classified as a specialised:

  1. Epithelial sheet
  2. Skeletal muscle
  3. Nervous cord
  4. Connective tissue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Connective tissue

Cells occur within its fluid extracellular matrix, plasma.

23. Striated skeletal muscle is usually:

  1. Noncontractile
  2. Unicellular with no fibres
  3. Voluntary
  4. Always involuntary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Voluntary

Somatic motor control regulates skeletal muscle contraction.

24. Smooth muscle is commonly found in:

  1. Only brain grey matter
  2. Walls of hollow visceral organs
  3. Only long bones
  4. Only tendons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Walls of hollow visceral organs

It supports involuntary movements in structures such as the gut.

25. Cardiac muscle has characteristic:

  1. Intercalated discs
  2. No contractile proteins
  3. Only one continuous giant neuron
  4. Cellulose walls
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Intercalated discs

Cell junctions help coordinate heart-muscle contraction.

26. The basic signalling cell of nervous tissue is the:

  1. Adipocyte
  2. Osteocyte
  3. Chondrocyte
  4. Neuron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Neuron

Neurons receive and transmit electrical and chemical signals.

27. A neuron's axon principally carries signals:

  1. Only between bone cells
  2. Only through blood plasma
  3. Away from the cell body
  4. Only into the nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Away from the cell body

The axon conducts impulses towards target cells.

28. In the standard frog example, the adult heart has:

  1. Five chambers
  2. Three chambers
  3. Two chambers
  4. Four chambers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Three chambers

Two atria and one ventricle form the familiar amphibian heart.

29. Adult frogs can exchange gases through:

  1. Skin and lungs
  2. Only gills
  3. Only tracheae
  4. Only feathers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Skin and lungs

Moist vascular skin and lungs provide respiratory pathways.

30. In the standard frog example, the urinary bladder opens into the:

  1. Trachea
  2. Oesophagus
  3. Aorta
  4. Cloaca
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cloaca

The common posterior chamber receives relevant digestive and urogenital outlets.
