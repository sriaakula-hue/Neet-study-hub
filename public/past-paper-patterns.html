Physics · 4 verified year examples

Photoelectric threshold and stopping potential

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q32 · PDF p4 · 2017 · B · Q158 · PDF p18 · 2020 · H6 · English · PDF p16 · 2025 · 45 · Q38 · PDF p12

Practice 1: A metal has a work function of 3.6 eV. Light supplies photons of energy 8.1 eV. What stopping-potential magnitude is needed to stop the fastest emitted electrons?

  1. 3.6 V
  2. 4.5 V
  3. 8.1 V
  4. 11.7 V
Show answer and explanation

B. 4.5 V

Einstein's equation gives Kmax = 8.1 − 3.6 = 4.5 eV. Since eVs = Kmax, the stopping-potential magnitude is 4.5 V.

Practice 2: Monochromatic light has frequency 0.90 times a metal's threshold frequency. Its intensity is then tripled without changing frequency. In the ordinary single-photon photoelectric model, what happens?

  1. Emission starts because intensity is higher
  2. Maximum kinetic energy triples
  3. No photoelectrons are emitted
  4. The threshold frequency becomes smaller
Show answer and explanation

C. No photoelectrons are emitted

Each photon's energy remains below the work function. Increasing intensity increases the number of photons, not the energy of each photon.

Physics · 4 verified year examples

Radioactive half-life calculations

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q34 · PDF p4 · 2018 · AA · Q13 · PDF p5 · 2021 · M4 · Q34 · PDF p6 · 2023 · G6 · Q28 · PDF p7

Practice 1: An isotope's half-life is 18 minutes. What percentage of its initial undecayed nuclei remains after 72 minutes?

  1. 25%
  2. 12.5%
  3. 6.25%
  4. 3.125%
Show answer and explanation

C. 6.25%

72/18 = 4 half-lives. The remaining fraction is (1/2)^4 = 1/16, or 6.25%.

Practice 2: A radioactive sample's activity falls from 960 counts per minute to 120 counts per minute in 60 minutes. What is its half-life?

  1. 10 minutes
  2. 15 minutes
  3. 20 minutes
  4. 30 minutes
Show answer and explanation

C. 20 minutes

The activity falls to 1/8 = (1/2)^3. Three half-lives take 60 minutes, so one half-life is 20 minutes.

Physics · 4 verified year examples

Hydrogen energy levels and spectral series

Past-paper examples: 2017 · B · Q168 · PDF p20 · 2018 · AA · Q11 · PDF p5 · 2022 · R6 · PDF p5 · 2023 · G6 · PDF p4

Practice 1: Use En = −13.6/n² eV for hydrogen. What photon energy is emitted when an electron moves from n = 4 to n = 2?

  1. 0.85 eV
  2. 2.55 eV
  3. 3.40 eV
  4. 12.75 eV
Show answer and explanation

B. 2.55 eV

E4 = −0.85 eV and E2 = −3.40 eV. The emitted photon carries E4 − E2 = 2.55 eV; this transition belongs to the Balmer series.

Practice 2: A hydrogen atom is initially in its n = 2 state. Using En = −13.6/n² eV, what minimum energy ionises it from that state?

  1. 13.6 eV
  2. 6.8 eV
  3. 3.4 eV
  4. 1.7 eV
Show answer and explanation

C. 3.4 eV

The n = 2 energy is −3.4 eV. Reaching the ionisation limit at 0 eV requires 3.4 eV.

Physics · 2 verified year examples

Charge sharing between capacitors

Past-paper examples: 2017 · B · Q153 · PDF p17 · 2022 · R6 · Q50 · PDF p7

Practice 1: An 8 μF capacitor at 24 V is disconnected from its battery and connected in parallel to an uncharged 4 μF capacitor. With like terminals joined, what is the final common voltage?

  1. 8 V
  2. 12 V
  3. 16 V
  4. 24 V
Show answer and explanation

C. 16 V

Charge is conserved: Qinitial = 8 × 24 = 192 μC. Total capacitance is 12 μF, so Vfinal = 192/12 = 16 V.

Practice 2: A 10 μF capacitor charged to 40 V is isolated and connected in parallel to an identical uncharged capacitor. What electrostatic energy remains after redistribution?

  1. 2 mJ
  2. 4 mJ
  3. 8 mJ
  4. 16 mJ
Show answer and explanation

B. 4 mJ

The final voltage is 20 V and combined capacitance is 20 μF. Ufinal = ½ × 20 × 10⁻⁶ × 20² = 0.004 J = 4 mJ.

Physics · 3 verified year examples

Spring oscillations and elastic energy

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q12 · PDF p2 · 2021 · M4 · Q15 · PDF p3 · 2023 · G6 · Q29 · PDF p7

Practice 1: A 0.36 kg mass oscillates on an ideal spring with stiffness 100 N m⁻¹. What is its period?

  1. 0.06π s
  2. 0.12π s
  3. 0.36π s
  4. 0.72π s
Show answer and explanation

B. 0.12π s

T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.36/100) = 2π × 0.06 = 0.12π s.

Practice 2: A spring of stiffness 150 N m⁻¹ is extended by 4.0 cm within its elastic range. How much elastic potential energy is stored?

  1. 0.012 J
  2. 0.060 J
  3. 0.120 J
  4. 1.20 J
Show answer and explanation

C. 0.120 J

Convert extension to 0.040 m. U = ½kx² = ½ × 150 × 0.040² = 0.120 J.

Physics · 3 verified year examples

Thin lenses in contact

Past-paper examples: 2019 · P1 · Q12 · PDF p4 · 2023 · G6 · Q46 · PDF p11 · 2025 · 45 · PDF p5

Practice 1: Thin lenses of powers +8 D and −3 D are placed in contact in air. What is the equivalent focal length?

  1. +0.20 m
  2. +0.125 m
  3. −0.20 m
  4. +0.50 m
Show answer and explanation

A. +0.20 m

For thin lenses in contact, Ptotal = +8 − 3 = +5 D. Hence f = 1/P = +0.20 m.

Practice 2: A convex lens of focal length +15 cm is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length −30 cm. What is the combination's focal length?

  1. +10 cm
  2. +30 cm
  3. −30 cm
  4. Infinite
Show answer and explanation

B. +30 cm

1/F = 1/15 − 1/30 = 1/30 cm⁻¹. Thus F = +30 cm, so the combination is converging.

Chemistry · 5 verified year examples

First-order kinetics and completion time

Past-paper examples: 2017 · B · Q25 · PDF p4 · 2018 · AA · Q79 · PDF p22 · 2019 · P1 · Q63 · PDF p17 · 2020 · H6 · English · Q99 · PDF p12 · 2025 · 45 · Q56 · PDF p17

Practice 1: A first-order reaction has a half-life of 12 seconds. How long does it take for 87.5% of the reactant to disappear?

  1. 12 s
  2. 24 s
  3. 36 s
  4. 48 s
Show answer and explanation

C. 36 s

87.5% disappearance leaves 12.5% = 1/8. That is three half-lives: 3 × 12 = 36 s.

Practice 2: A first-order reaction has rate constant 0.020 s⁻¹. How long is needed for the reactant concentration to fall to 10% of its initial value? Use ln 10 = 2.303.

  1. 34.65 s
  2. 50.0 s
  3. 115.15 s
  4. 230.3 s
Show answer and explanation

C. 115.15 s

The integrated law is t = ln([A]0/[A]t)/k. Therefore t = ln 10/0.020 = 115.15 s.

Chemistry · 3 verified year examples

Gibbs energy and feasibility

Past-paper examples: 2017 · B · PDF p2 · 2019 · P1 · Q53 · PDF p15 · 2020 · H6 · English · Q98 · PDF p12

Practice 1: For a reaction, ΔH = +24.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +80.0 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Treat both as constant. At which temperatures is ΔG negative?

  1. T < 300 K
  2. T > 300 K
  3. Only at T = 300 K
  4. At every positive temperature
Show answer and explanation

B. T > 300 K

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Convert ΔH to 24000 J mol⁻¹; ΔG < 0 when T > 24000/80 = 300 K. At 300 K, ΔG is zero.

Practice 2: A standard cell transfers two moles of electrons per mole of reaction and has E°cell = +0.350 V. With F = 96500 C mol⁻¹, what is ΔG°?

  1. −67.55 kJ mol⁻¹
  2. +67.55 kJ mol⁻¹
  3. −33.775 kJ mol⁻¹
  4. +33.775 kJ mol⁻¹
Show answer and explanation

A. −67.55 kJ mol⁻¹

ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 0.350 = −67550 J mol⁻¹ = −67.55 kJ mol⁻¹.

Chemistry · 3 verified year examples

Colligative properties and particle concentration

Past-paper examples: 2020 · H6 · English · Q105 · PDF p12 · 2021 · M4 · Q67 · PDF p11 · 2024 · S6 · PDF p24

Practice 1: An ideal dilute solution contains a non-electrolyte at molality 0.150 mol kg⁻¹. The solvent has Kf = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹. What is the freezing-point depression?

  1. 0.124 K
  2. 0.279 K
  3. 1.86 K
  4. 2.79 K
Show answer and explanation

B. 0.279 K

For a non-electrolyte, i = 1. ΔTf = iKfm = 1 × 1.86 × 0.150 = 0.279 K.

Practice 2: At 300 K, an ideal dilute non-electrolyte solution has concentration 0.0300 mol L⁻¹. Using R = 0.0820 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, find its osmotic pressure.

  1. 0.0738 atm
  2. 0.246 atm
  3. 0.738 atm
  4. 7.38 atm
Show answer and explanation

C. 0.738 atm

π = cRT = 0.0300 × 0.0820 × 300 = 0.738 atm; i = 1 for the stated non-electrolyte.

Chemistry · 3 verified year examples

Molecular shape and lone pairs

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q150 · PDF p15 · 2021 · M4 · Q77 · PDF p12 · 2024 · S6 · Q52 · PDF p14

Practice 1: A central atom has three bonding pairs and one lone pair in a four-domain VSEPR arrangement. What molecular shape is predicted?

  1. Trigonal planar
  2. Trigonal pyramidal
  3. Square planar
  4. Linear
Show answer and explanation

B. Trigonal pyramidal

Four electron domains have tetrahedral electron-pair geometry. With one lone pair, the positions of the three bonded atoms give a trigonal pyramidal molecular shape.

Practice 2: XeF4 has four Xe–F bonds and two lone pairs on xenon. Which molecular shape follows from its six electron domains?

  1. Tetrahedral
  2. Trigonal bipyramidal
  3. Square planar
  4. Square pyramidal
Show answer and explanation

C. Square planar

Six domains adopt an octahedral electron-domain arrangement. The two lone pairs occupy opposite positions, leaving four F atoms in a square plane.

Chemistry · 3 verified year examples

Ligand denticity and complex classification

Past-paper examples: 2021 · M4 · Q72 · PDF p11 · 2023 · G6 · Q71 · PDF p16 · 2024 · S6 · PDF p23

Practice 1: Each ethane-1,2-diamine ligand (en) bonds through two nitrogen atoms. What is chromium's coordination number in [Cr(en)3]³⁺?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 6
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

C. 6

Three bidentate en ligands provide 3 × 2 = 6 donor atoms bonded to chromium. Coordination number counts donor atoms, not the number of ligand molecules.

Practice 2: Which complex contains only one type of coordinated ligand and is therefore homoleptic?

  1. [Co(NH3)5Cl]²⁺
  2. [Co(NH3)6]³⁺
  3. [Pt(NH3)2Cl2]
  4. [Cr(H2O)5Cl]²⁺
Show answer and explanation

B. [Co(NH3)6]³⁺

[Co(NH3)6]³⁺ has only ammine ligands. Each other listed complex contains two ligand types and is heteroleptic.

Chemistry · 2 verified year examples

Qualitative carbonyl tests

Past-paper examples: 2017 · B · Q43 · PDF p6 · 2024 · S6 · Q64 · PDF p17

Practice 1: Two labelled pure samples contain propanal and propanone. Under the usual Tollens' test conditions, which sample can reduce the reagent to give a silver mirror?

  1. Only propanal
  2. Only propanone
  3. Both samples
  4. Neither sample
Show answer and explanation

A. Only propanal

Propanal is an aldehyde and is oxidised while Ag(I) is reduced to silver. The ordinary ketone propanone does not give the usual positive Tollens' test.

Practice 2: A lab technician prepares the blue Fehling's A stock solution before mixing it with Fehling's B. Which solute supplies the copper(II) ions in solution A?

  1. Sodium potassium tartrate
  2. Copper(II) sulfate
  3. Sodium hydroxide alone
  4. Silver nitrate
Show answer and explanation

B. Copper(II) sulfate

Fehling's A is aqueous copper(II) sulfate. Fehling's B supplies alkaline sodium potassium tartrate; the two solutions are mixed for the test.

Biology · 4 verified year examples

Restriction sites and joining DNA

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q69 · PDF p8 · 2019 · P1 · PDF p34 · 2022 · R6 · Q145 · PDF p20 · 2025 · 45 · PDF p44

Practice 1: Consider 5′-GAATTC-3′ paired with 3′-CTTAAG-5′. What makes this double-stranded DNA site palindromic?

  1. Both strands have the same sequence when read 5′ to 3′
  2. Both strands have the same sequence when read left to right
  3. Only one strand contains adenine
  4. Its sugar-phosphate backbone is absent
Show answer and explanation

A. Both strands have the same sequence when read 5′ to 3′

Read the lower strand from right to left to follow its 5′ → 3′ direction: GAATTC. Matching sequences in the same directional reading define this DNA palindrome.

Practice 2: A cloning experiment has already produced compatible sticky ends in a vector and insert. Which enzyme seals the sugar-phosphate backbone after their ends pair?

  1. DNA helicase
  2. DNA ligase
  3. RNA polymerase
  4. A restriction endonuclease
Show answer and explanation

B. DNA ligase

DNA ligase forms phosphodiester bonds to seal nicks in the backbone. Restriction enzymes perform cutting; ligase performs joining.

Biology · 4 verified year examples

PCR steps and amplification

Past-paper examples: 2018 · AA · Q101 · PDF p26 · 2021 · M4 · Q133 · PDF p18 · 2023 · G6 · Q141 · PDF p33 · 2025 · 45 · Q92 · PDF p28

Practice 1: An ideal PCR starts with three double-stranded target DNA molecules. With perfect doubling in each cycle, how many target molecules are present after five cycles?

  1. 15
  2. 32
  3. 48
  4. 96
Show answer and explanation

D. 96

With ideal amplification, Nfinal = Ninitial × 2^n. Thus 3 × 2^5 = 96 double-stranded target molecules.

Practice 2: During the first high-temperature step of PCR, what change allows primers to bind in the following step?

  1. DNA strands separate as base-pair hydrogen bonds are disrupted
  2. All phosphodiester bonds in the target are destroyed
  3. Primers are converted into amino acids
  4. DNA ligase joins the two template strands
Show answer and explanation

A. DNA strands separate as base-pair hydrogen bonds are disrupted

Denaturation separates complementary strands without destroying the template backbone. Primers then anneal on cooling, followed by polymerase extension.

Biology · 2 verified year examples

Lac operon genes and regulation

Past-paper examples: 2019 · P1 · Q166 · PDF p38 · 2024 · S6 · Q143 · PDF p34

Practice 1: In an otherwise functional lac operon, a mutation prevents the repressor from binding the operator. If glucose repression is absent, what is expected even when lactose is absent?

  1. The structural genes can be transcribed
  2. All structural genes are deleted
  3. The promoter becomes a ribosome
  4. DNA replication must stop
Show answer and explanation

A. The structural genes can be transcribed

The repressor normally blocks transcription in the absence of inducer. Failure to bind the operator removes that block, permitting constitutive expression under the stated conditions.

Practice 2: A mutation disrupts lacY while lacZ remains functional. Which function is directly affected?

  1. Synthesis of the repressor
  2. Hydrolysis of lactose by β-galactosidase
  3. Membrane transport of lactose by permease
  4. Synthesis of transacetylase
Show answer and explanation

C. Membrane transport of lactose by permease

lacY codes for permease, which facilitates lactose entry. lacZ codes for β-galactosidase, while lacA codes for transacetylase.

Biology · 4 verified year examples

Semiconservative replication and strand synthesis

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q63 · PDF p7 · 2017 · B · Q88 · PDF p10 · 2018 · AA · Q106 · PDF p27 · 2024 · S6 · Q143 · PDF p34

Practice 1: Bacteria with fully ¹⁵N-labelled DNA are transferred to ¹⁴N medium. After two generations of semiconservative replication, what proportions of DNA molecules are hybrid and light?

  1. 100% hybrid, 0% light
  2. 75% hybrid, 25% light
  3. 50% hybrid, 50% light
  4. 0% hybrid, 100% light
Show answer and explanation

C. 50% hybrid, 50% light

Generation one produces all hybrid DNA. In generation two, each hybrid molecule makes one hybrid and one light molecule, giving equal proportions.

Practice 2: Why does the lagging strand form short DNA fragments at a replication fork?

  1. DNA polymerase synthesises new DNA only 5′ → 3′ on antiparallel templates
  2. DNA polymerase synthesises the lagging strand only 3′ → 5′
  3. The lagging strand has no template
  4. RNA primers permanently replace all DNA
Show answer and explanation

A. DNA polymerase synthesises new DNA only 5′ → 3′ on antiparallel templates

Polymerase extends a strand at its 3′ end, so synthesis proceeds 5′ → 3′. On the oppositely oriented template, synthesis occurs discontinuously as Okazaki fragments.

Biology · 2 verified year examples

Hardy–Weinberg frequencies and assumptions

Past-paper examples: 2016 · E4 · Phase II · Q112 · PDF p11 · 2024 · S6 · Q174 · PDF p41

Practice 1: In a Hardy–Weinberg population with two alleles, the recessive homozygote frequency is 0.09. What is the heterozygote frequency?

  1. 0.09
  2. 0.21
  3. 0.42
  4. 0.49
Show answer and explanation

C. 0.42

q² = 0.09 gives q = 0.30. Therefore p = 0.70 and heterozygote frequency 2pq = 2 × 0.70 × 0.30 = 0.42.

Practice 2: A previously isolated population receives many migrants whose allele frequencies differ from its own. Which Hardy–Weinberg assumption has been violated directly?

  1. No gene flow
  2. Two alleles must have identical frequencies
  3. Every individual is homozygous
  4. No individuals can reproduce
Show answer and explanation

A. No gene flow

The no-migration/no-gene-flow assumption is violated. Migration can change the recipient population's allele frequencies.

Biology · 3 verified year examples

Ecological energy and biomass pyramids

Past-paper examples: 2018 · AA · Q115 · PDF p28 · 2019 · P1 · PDF p32 · 2021 · M4 · Q123 · PDF p17

Practice 1: A marine ecosystem has a small standing crop of rapidly reproducing phytoplankton and a larger standing biomass of zooplankton. Which statement can be correct?

  1. Both energy and biomass pyramids must be inverted
  2. The biomass pyramid can be inverted while the energy pyramid stays upright
  3. The energy pyramid is inverted whenever producers are small
  4. No producers are needed
Show answer and explanation

B. The biomass pyramid can be inverted while the energy pyramid stays upright

Rapid producer turnover can support a greater consumer standing biomass. Energy transfer still loses usable energy at each trophic step, so the energy pyramid remains upright.

Practice 2: Why is an ecological pyramid of energy upright when energy flow is measured over the same area and time?

  1. Each trophic level creates energy
  2. All organisms store equal energy
  3. Usable energy is lost between successive trophic levels
  4. Consumers receive more energy than producers capture
Show answer and explanation

C. Usable energy is lost between successive trophic levels

Energy is spent in metabolism and dissipated as heat, with other losses during transfer. Less energy is available to each successive trophic level.
