Coordination Compounds

Coordination Compounds

Key concepts

  • A ligand donates an electron pair to a central metal atom or ion. Coordination number counts donor atoms directly attached to the metal.
  • Ligand charge and overall complex charge determine the metal oxidation state. Neutral ligands include NH₃ and H₂O.
  • Complexes can show geometrical or optical isomerism depending on coordination geometry and ligand arrangement.

Worked connection

In [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, the six NH₃ ligands are neutral, so cobalt is +3 and the coordination number is six.

Watch out

Coordination number counts donor attachments, not necessarily ligand molecules. A bidentate ligand binds through two donor atoms.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. NH₃ acts as a ligand chiefly by donating the nitrogen:

Lone pair

The nitrogen lone pair can form a coordinate bond to a suitable metal centre.

2. In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, iron oxidation state is:

+2

Six cyanide ligands each contribute −1: x − 6 = −4, giving x = +2.

3. For a complex containing three bidentate ligands each bound through both donor sites, coordination number is:

6

Each of the three ligands supplies two attached donor atoms, giving six.

4. A ligand that attaches through a single donor atom is:

Monodentate

Denticity counts available attaching donor sites, not overall ligand charge.

5. In square-planar MA₂B₂, adjacent A ligands define the:

Cis form

Cis means adjacent identical ligands; trans places them opposite.

6. A chelating ligand commonly forms:

A ring with the metal centre

A multidentate ligand attaches through multiple sites, creating one or more rings containing the metal.

7. Coordination number and oxidation number are:

Distinct quantities

Coordination number counts donor attachments; oxidation number is formal charge bookkeeping.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The oxidation state of Co in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is:

  1. 0
  2. +1
  3. +2
  4. +3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +3

NH₃ is neutral, so cobalt accounts for the +3 charge.

2. The coordination number in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is:

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6

Six donor nitrogen atoms are attached to the metal.

3. A bidentate ligand binds through:

  1. One donor atom
  2. Two donor atoms
  3. Three protons
  4. No donor atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two donor atoms

Bidentate means two donor sites can attach to one metal centre.

4. NH₃ acts as a ligand chiefly by donating the nitrogen:

  1. Lone pair
  2. Nucleus
  3. Neutron
  4. Entire molecule as a proton
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lone pair

The nitrogen lone pair can form a coordinate bond to a suitable metal centre.

5. In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, iron oxidation state is:

  1. −2
  2. +3
  3. +4
  4. +2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +2

Six cyanide ligands each contribute −1: x − 6 = −4, giving x = +2.

6. For a complex containing three bidentate ligands each bound through both donor sites, coordination number is:

  1. 6
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6

Each of the three ligands supplies two attached donor atoms, giving six.

7. A ligand that attaches through a single donor atom is:

  1. Monodentate
  2. Bidentate
  3. Hexadentate
  4. Always uncharged
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Monodentate

Denticity counts available attaching donor sites, not overall ligand charge.

8. In square-planar MA₂B₂, adjacent A ligands define the:

  1. Cis form
  2. Trans form
  3. No isomer
  4. Optical rotation necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cis form

Cis means adjacent identical ligands; trans places them opposite.

9. A chelating ligand commonly forms:

  1. A ring with the metal centre
  2. Only an ionic lattice without donor bonds
  3. Only a gas bubble
  4. A new nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A ring with the metal centre

A multidentate ligand attaches through multiple sites, creating one or more rings containing the metal.

10. Coordination number and oxidation number are:

  1. Distinct quantities
  2. Always equal
  3. Always both zero
  4. Both counts of electrons only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Distinct quantities

Coordination number counts donor attachments; oxidation number is formal charge bookkeeping.

11. A ligand is a species that donates:

  1. Only a proton
  2. Its whole nucleus
  3. An electron pair to a metal centre
  4. A neutron pair
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. An electron pair to a metal centre

Coordinate bonding uses a donor electron pair and a metal acceptor orbital.

12. A monodentate ligand coordinates through:

  1. No atoms
  2. One donor atom
  3. Two donor atoms always
  4. Four nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. One donor atom

Denticity counts donor atoms binding from a single ligand.

13. Ethylenediamine normally acts as a:

  1. Bidentate ligand
  2. Monodentate ligand
  3. Hexadentate ligand
  4. Noncoordinating gas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bidentate ligand

Its two nitrogen donors can bind the same metal.

14. EDTA commonly binds a suitable metal as a:

  1. Monodentate ligand
  2. Bidentate ligand
  3. Tridentate ligand
  4. Hexadentate ligand
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Hexadentate ligand

The usual EDTA complex uses six donor atoms.

15. In [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, cobalt's oxidation state is:

  1. +6
  2. −3
  3. +3
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. +3

Ammonia is neutral, so the metal carries the complex's +3 charge.

16. In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, iron's oxidation state is:

  1. −2
  2. +2
  3. +3
  4. +6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. +2

Six cyanides contribute −6; total −4 requires Fe=+2.

17. The coordination number in [Co(en)₃]³⁺ is:

  1. 6
  2. 3
  3. 9
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6

Each of three bidentate en ligands donates two atoms, giving six coordinating donors.

18. The coordination number in [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is:

  1. 1
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 2

Two monodentate ammonia ligands provide two donor atoms.

19. The usual geometry of a coordination number 6 complex is:

  1. Trigonal planar
  2. Tetrahedral
  3. Octahedral
  4. Linear
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Octahedral

Six ligands commonly occupy octahedral directions around a metal centre.

20. A common coordination-number-4 geometry is:

  1. Always pentagonal bipyramidal
  2. Tetrahedral or square planar
  3. Always octahedral
  4. Always linear
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tetrahedral or square planar

Four-coordinate complexes can adopt tetrahedral or square-planar structures depending on the metal and ligands.

21. In an octahedral field, the lower-energy d set is:

  1. t₂g
  2. e_g
  3. All five unsplit
  4. Only the s orbital
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. t₂g

The t₂g orbitals avoid the ligand axes more effectively than e_g.

22. In an octahedral field, the e_g set contains:

  1. Three orbitals
  2. Five orbitals
  3. One orbital
  4. Two orbitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two orbitals

The d_z² and d_x²−y² orbitals form e_g.

23. In an octahedral field, the t₂g set contains:

  1. Four orbitals
  2. Six orbitals
  3. Three orbitals
  4. Two orbitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Three orbitals

d_xy, d_yz and d_xz form the triply represented t₂g set.

24. A strong-field ligand can favour a low-spin arrangement by producing:

  1. Always more unpaired electrons
  2. Splitting greater than pairing energy
  3. Zero splitting
  4. Loss of the metal nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Splitting greater than pairing energy

When splitting is large, electron pairing in lower orbitals becomes energetically favourable.

25. Which is usually the stronger-field ligand in the standard spectrochemical comparison?

  1. CN⁻
  2. I⁻
  3. Br⁻
  4. Cl⁻
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CN⁻

Cyanide lies much higher in the spectrochemical series than halide ligands.

26. Chelation involves a ligand forming:

  1. Only a hydrogen bond
  2. Only an ionic lattice
  3. A new atomic nucleus
  4. A ring with the metal through multiple donor atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A ring with the metal through multiple donor atoms

Multidentate ligands bind at more than one site and generate metal-containing rings.

27. Cis and trans forms of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] illustrate:

  1. Chain isomerism of hydrocarbons
  2. Only ionisation enthalpy
  3. Geometrical isomerism
  4. Isotopic decay
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Geometrical isomerism

Square-planar placement allows adjacent or opposite identical ligands.

28. Linkage isomerism can occur with a ligand that has:

  1. An unchanging nuclear mass only
  2. Alternative donor atoms
  3. No donor atom
  4. Only one possible donor site
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Alternative donor atoms

Ambidentate ligands such as nitrite can coordinate through different atoms.

29. In [Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]²⁺, an ambidentate ligand is:

  1. NO₂⁻
  2. NH₃
  3. Co
  4. The overall complex charge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. NO₂⁻

Nitrite can bind through nitrogen or oxygen, enabling linkage isomerism.

30. Complex ions dissolved in water do not necessarily release all coordinated ligands because:

  1. Every ligand becomes a proton
  2. Every complex is insoluble
  3. Metal atoms vanish
  4. The coordination sphere can remain intact
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The coordination sphere can remain intact

The inner coordination entity can persist while outer counterions dissociate.
