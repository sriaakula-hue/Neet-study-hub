Coordination Compounds

Coordination Compounds

Key concepts

  • A ligand donates an electron pair to a central metal atom or ion. Coordination number counts donor atoms directly attached to the metal.
  • Ligand charge and overall complex charge determine the metal oxidation state. Neutral ligands include NH₃ and H₂O.
  • Complexes can show geometrical or optical isomerism depending on coordination geometry and ligand arrangement.

Worked connection

In [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, the six NH₃ ligands are neutral, so cobalt is +3 and the coordination number is six.

Watch out

Coordination number counts donor attachments, not necessarily ligand molecules. A bidentate ligand binds through two donor atoms.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. NH₃ acts as a ligand chiefly by donating the nitrogen:

Lone pair

The nitrogen lone pair can form a coordinate bond to a suitable metal centre.

2. In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, iron oxidation state is:

+2

Six cyanide ligands each contribute −1: x − 6 = −4, giving x = +2.

3. For a complex containing three bidentate ligands each bound through both donor sites, coordination number is:

6

Each of the three ligands supplies two attached donor atoms, giving six.

4. A ligand that attaches through a single donor atom is:

Monodentate

Denticity counts available attaching donor sites, not overall ligand charge.

5. In square-planar MA₂B₂, adjacent A ligands define the:

Cis form

Cis means adjacent identical ligands; trans places them opposite.

6. A chelating ligand commonly forms:

A ring with the metal centre

A multidentate ligand attaches through multiple sites, creating one or more rings containing the metal.

7. Coordination number and oxidation number are:

Distinct quantities

Coordination number counts donor attachments; oxidation number is formal charge bookkeeping.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The oxidation state of Co in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is:

  1. 0
  2. +1
  3. +2
  4. +3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +3

NH₃ is neutral, so cobalt accounts for the +3 charge.

2. The coordination number in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is:

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6

Six donor nitrogen atoms are attached to the metal.

3. A bidentate ligand binds through:

  1. One donor atom
  2. Two donor atoms
  3. Three protons
  4. No donor atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two donor atoms

Bidentate means two donor sites can attach to one metal centre.

4. NH₃ acts as a ligand chiefly by donating the nitrogen:

  1. Lone pair
  2. Nucleus
  3. Neutron
  4. Entire molecule as a proton
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lone pair

The nitrogen lone pair can form a coordinate bond to a suitable metal centre.

5. In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, iron oxidation state is:

  1. −2
  2. +3
  3. +4
  4. +2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +2

Six cyanide ligands each contribute −1: x − 6 = −4, giving x = +2.

6. For a complex containing three bidentate ligands each bound through both donor sites, coordination number is:

  1. 6
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6

Each of the three ligands supplies two attached donor atoms, giving six.

7. A ligand that attaches through a single donor atom is:

  1. Monodentate
  2. Bidentate
  3. Hexadentate
  4. Always uncharged
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Monodentate

Denticity counts available attaching donor sites, not overall ligand charge.

8. In square-planar MA₂B₂, adjacent A ligands define the:

  1. Cis form
  2. Trans form
  3. No isomer
  4. Optical rotation necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cis form

Cis means adjacent identical ligands; trans places them opposite.

9. A chelating ligand commonly forms:

  1. A ring with the metal centre
  2. Only an ionic lattice without donor bonds
  3. Only a gas bubble
  4. A new nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A ring with the metal centre

A multidentate ligand attaches through multiple sites, creating one or more rings containing the metal.

10. Coordination number and oxidation number are:

  1. Distinct quantities
  2. Always equal
  3. Always both zero
  4. Both counts of electrons only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Distinct quantities

Coordination number counts donor attachments; oxidation number is formal charge bookkeeping.
