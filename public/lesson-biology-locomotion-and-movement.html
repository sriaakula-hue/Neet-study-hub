Locomotion and Movement

Locomotion and Movement

Key concepts

  • A sarcomere is the contractile unit between adjacent Z lines in a striated muscle fibre.
  • In sliding-filament contraction, actin and myosin filaments slide rather than shorten. Calcium binds to troponin and exposes binding sites.
  • ATP is needed for cross-bridge cycling, including detachment of myosin from actin. Joints permit different ranges and types of movement.

Connect the ideas

During contraction, the I band and H zone narrow, while the A band remains essentially constant in length.

Common mistake

Muscle contraction does not shorten the individual thick and thin filaments.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Thin muscle filaments are chiefly associated with:

Actin

Actin forms the main thin-filament framework, with associated regulatory proteins.

2. Thick muscle filaments are chiefly composed of:

Myosin

Myosin heads form cross-bridges with actin during contraction.

3. ATP binding to a myosin head helps it:

Detach from actin

ATP attachment reduces actin affinity; subsequent hydrolysis supports the cross-bridge cycle.

4. A tendon connects:

Muscle to bone

Tendons transmit muscle pull to skeletal attachment points.

5. A ligament typically connects:

Bone to bone

Ligaments support joints by connecting bones and limiting excessive movement.

6. A ball-and-socket joint is found at the human:

Shoulder

The shoulder permits movement in multiple planes through a ball-and-socket arrangement.

7. The H zone mainly contains:

Thick filaments without thin-filament overlap

It is the central thick-filament region lacking actin overlap in the stated sarcomere arrangement.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A sarcomere lies between two adjacent:

  1. A bands
  2. Z lines
  3. H zones
  4. Nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Z lines

Z lines delimit the sarcomere.

2. During skeletal muscle contraction, which band remains approximately constant?

  1. I band
  2. A band
  3. H zone
  4. All shrink equally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A band

The A band corresponds to thick-filament length, which stays constant.

3. In skeletal muscle, calcium binds to:

  1. Troponin
  2. Haemoglobin
  3. Insulin
  4. Cellulose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Troponin

Calcium binding to troponin permits exposure of actin binding sites.

4. Thin muscle filaments are chiefly associated with:

  1. Actin
  2. Myosin only
  3. Cellulose
  4. DNA only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Actin

Actin forms the main thin-filament framework, with associated regulatory proteins.

5. Thick muscle filaments are chiefly composed of:

  1. Myosin
  2. Actin only
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. Insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Myosin

Myosin heads form cross-bridges with actin during contraction.

6. ATP binding to a myosin head helps it:

  1. Detach from actin
  2. Bind permanently forever
  3. Destroy troponin
  4. Become a neuron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Detach from actin

ATP attachment reduces actin affinity; subsequent hydrolysis supports the cross-bridge cycle.

7. A tendon connects:

  1. Muscle to bone
  2. Bone to bone usually
  3. Neuron to neuron
  4. Blood to skin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Muscle to bone

Tendons transmit muscle pull to skeletal attachment points.

8. A ligament typically connects:

  1. Bone to bone
  2. Muscle to bone
  3. Only blood vessels
  4. Only nerve cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bone to bone

Ligaments support joints by connecting bones and limiting excessive movement.

9. A ball-and-socket joint is found at the human:

  1. Shoulder
  2. Elbow hinge only
  3. Skull suture
  4. Intervertebral disc only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shoulder

The shoulder permits movement in multiple planes through a ball-and-socket arrangement.

10. The H zone mainly contains:

  1. Thick filaments without thin-filament overlap
  2. Only thin filaments
  3. Only Z lines
  4. No filaments ever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thick filaments without thin-filament overlap

It is the central thick-filament region lacking actin overlap in the stated sarcomere arrangement.

11. The repeating contractile unit of a striated muscle fibre is the:

  1. Alveolus
  2. Sarcomere
  3. Neuron
  4. Nephron
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sarcomere

Adjacent Z lines delimit the sarcomere.

12. Thin muscle filaments are built mainly from:

  1. Actin
  2. Myosin only
  3. Collagen
  4. Keratin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Actin

Actin forms the principal thin-filament framework.

13. Thick muscle filaments contain primarily:

  1. Actin only
  2. Cellulose
  3. Tubulin only
  4. Myosin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Myosin

Myosin heads interact with thin filaments to generate force.

14. During contraction, the A band generally:

  1. Doubles in length
  2. Becomes the Z line
  3. Maintains its length
  4. Disappears completely
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Maintains its length

Thick-filament length remains essentially unchanged.

15. During contraction, the I band generally:

  1. Becomes bone
  2. Shortens
  3. Always lengthens
  4. Remains the only fixed band
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Shortens

Greater filament overlap reduces the actin-only region.

16. The H zone generally becomes smaller as:

  1. Filament overlap increases
  2. Myosin elongates
  3. Actin disappears
  4. Calcium vanishes from every cell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Filament overlap increases

The thick-only central region shrinks during contraction.

17. Calcium initiates skeletal contraction by binding:

  1. Only myosin ATPase
  2. Collagen
  3. Haemoglobin
  4. Troponin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Troponin

The resulting change displaces tropomyosin from binding sites.

18. Tropomyosin at rest helps cover:

  1. Only calcium channels
  2. Only collagen fibres
  3. Myosin-binding sites on actin
  4. Only nuclear pores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Myosin-binding sites on actin

It prevents strong cross-bridge binding until activation.

19. ATP binding to myosin promotes:

  1. Formation of a new sarcomere nucleus
  2. Detachment from actin
  3. Permanent locking to actin
  4. Destruction of actin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Detachment from actin

The cross-bridge cycle requires ATP-dependent release.

20. ATP hydrolysis helps:

  1. Energise the myosin head
  2. Only dissolve bones
  3. Only make oxygen
  4. Only delete troponin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Energise the myosin head

Hydrolysis primes the head for the next force-generating interaction.

21. Rigor after ATP depletion reflects failure of cross-bridges to:

  1. Form at all
  2. Bind any calcium
  3. Contain any proteins
  4. Detach normally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Detach normally

Without ATP, strongly attached bridges persist.

22. The neurotransmitter at a typical skeletal neuromuscular junction is:

  1. Thyroxine
  2. Glucagon
  3. Acetylcholine
  4. Insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Acetylcholine

Motor-neuron release activates the muscle membrane.

23. The axial skeleton includes:

  1. Only pelvic girdle
  2. Skull, vertebral column and thoracic cage
  3. Only limbs
  4. Only shoulder girdles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Skull, vertebral column and thoracic cage

These structures form the body's central supporting axis.

24. The appendicular skeleton includes:

  1. Limbs and their girdles
  2. Only skull
  3. Only vertebrae
  4. Only ribs and sternum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Limbs and their girdles

The limb skeleton and attachment girdles are appendicular.

25. A human skull connects to the first vertebra through:

  1. Patellae
  2. Metacarpals
  3. Scapular spines
  4. Occipital condyles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Occipital condyles

These articular surfaces connect skull and atlas.

26. The first cervical vertebra is the:

  1. Sacrum
  2. Coccyx
  3. Atlas
  4. Axis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Atlas

Atlas supports the skull at the top of the vertebral column.

27. The second cervical vertebra is the:

  1. Humerus
  2. Axis
  3. Atlas
  4. Sternum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Axis

Its structure supports rotational movement with the atlas.

28. The shoulder joint is commonly classified as:

  1. Ball-and-socket
  2. Hinge only
  3. Pivot only
  4. Immovable suture
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ball-and-socket

Its structure permits movement in multiple directions.

29. The elbow is primarily a:

  1. Ball-and-socket
  2. Immovable suture
  3. Gliding-only joint
  4. Hinge joint
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Hinge joint

Flexion and extension dominate its movement.

30. A tendon attaches skeletal muscle to:

  1. A neuron nucleus
  2. A blood cell
  3. Bone
  4. Another bone only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Bone

It transmits contraction force to the skeleton.
