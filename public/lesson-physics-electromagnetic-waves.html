Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic Waves

Key concepts

  • Electromagnetic waves in vacuum have transverse electric and magnetic fields, mutually perpendicular and perpendicular to propagation.
  • All electromagnetic waves travel at c in vacuum. Frequency and wavelength satisfy c = fλ.
  • The spectrum includes radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma radiation. Photon energy increases with frequency.

Worked example

In vacuum, a wave of frequency 3 × 10⁸ Hz has wavelength 1 m, using c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

Watch out

Equal vacuum speed does not mean equal frequency, wavelength or photon energy. Speeds can differ in material media.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. In a vacuum electromagnetic wave, E and B are:

Mutually perpendicular

E, B and propagation form perpendicular directions for the transverse wave.

2. An electromagnetic wave can propagate through:

Vacuum

Unlike mechanical waves, electromagnetic waves need no material medium.

3. From lower to higher frequency, the correct order is:

Radio → infrared → ultraviolet → X-ray

The listed progression follows increasing electromagnetic frequency.

4. The vacuum field-magnitude relation is:

E = cB

In SI units a plane vacuum wave has E/B = c.

5. Microwaves generally have wavelengths shorter than radio waves but longer than:

Infrared

The conventional spectrum places microwaves between longer radio bands and infrared.

6. A vacuum wave has λ = 0.10 m and c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Frequency is:

3 × 10⁹ Hz

f = c/λ = 3 × 10⁸/0.10 = 3 × 10⁹ Hz.

7. Electromagnetic waves transfer:

Energy and momentum

Fields carry energy and momentum, which can be exchanged with matter.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Electromagnetic waves in vacuum are:

  1. Longitudinal only
  2. Transverse
  3. Stationary only
  4. Dependent on air
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Transverse

Both fields oscillate perpendicular to propagation.

2. Using c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, f = 3 × 10⁸ Hz gives wavelength:

  1. 0.1 m
  2. 1 m
  3. 10 m
  4. 100 m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1 m

Wavelength equals wave speed divided by frequency: λ = c/f = (3 × 10⁸)/(3 × 10⁸) = 1 m.

3. Which has higher photon energy: radio or ultraviolet?

  1. Radio
  2. Ultraviolet
  3. Always equal
  4. Cannot compare frequency ranges
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ultraviolet

Ultraviolet has much higher frequency, and E = hf.

4. In a vacuum electromagnetic wave, E and B are:

  1. Mutually perpendicular
  2. Always parallel
  3. Always opposite
  4. Unrelated to propagation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mutually perpendicular

E, B and propagation form perpendicular directions for the transverse wave.

5. An electromagnetic wave can propagate through:

  1. Vacuum
  2. Only air
  3. Only water
  4. Only a stretched string
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Vacuum

Unlike mechanical waves, electromagnetic waves need no material medium.

6. From lower to higher frequency, the correct order is:

  1. Radio → infrared → ultraviolet → X-ray
  2. X-ray → radio → ultraviolet → infrared
  3. Ultraviolet → infrared → radio → X-ray
  4. Infrared → radio → X-ray → ultraviolet
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Radio → infrared → ultraviolet → X-ray

The listed progression follows increasing electromagnetic frequency.

7. The vacuum field-magnitude relation is:

  1. E = cB
  2. B = cE
  3. E = B in SI always
  4. E = c²B
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. E = cB

In SI units a plane vacuum wave has E/B = c.

8. Microwaves generally have wavelengths shorter than radio waves but longer than:

  1. Infrared
  2. All visible and radio together
  3. Every sound wave necessarily
  4. All electromagnetic waves
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Infrared

The conventional spectrum places microwaves between longer radio bands and infrared.

9. A vacuum wave has λ = 0.10 m and c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Frequency is:

  1. 3 × 10⁷ Hz
  2. 3 × 10⁸ Hz
  3. 3 × 10¹⁰ Hz
  4. 3 × 10⁹ Hz
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3 × 10⁹ Hz

f = c/λ = 3 × 10⁸/0.10 = 3 × 10⁹ Hz.

10. Electromagnetic waves transfer:

  1. Energy and momentum
  2. Neither energy nor momentum
  3. Only matter in bulk
  4. Only electric charge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Energy and momentum

Fields carry energy and momentum, which can be exchanged with matter.
