Cell: The Unit of Life

Cell structure and organelles

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus. Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.

Key functions

Ribosomes synthesize proteins. Mitochondria carry out much of aerobic cellular respiration and ATP production in eukaryotic cells. Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis in plant and algal cells that contain them.

Watch out

Ribosomes are not membrane-bound and occur in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack nuclei and mitochondria.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The plasma membrane is described as selectively permeable because it:

Regulates movement of substances

The lipid bilayer and membrane proteins determine how different substances cross.

2. The Golgi apparatus commonly participates in:

Modification and packaging of cell products

Golgi cisternae process, sort and package products arriving from the endoplasmic reticulum.

3. Rough endoplasmic reticulum is associated with attached:

Ribosomes

Attached ribosomes synthesise proteins entering the secretory or membrane pathway.

4. Lysosomes characteristically contain:

Hydrolytic enzymes

They support intracellular digestion and recycling under their appropriate internal conditions.

5. A nucleolus is strongly associated with:

Ribosome-component production

The nucleolus supports rRNA synthesis and ribosomal-subunit assembly.

6. Prokaryotic chromosomal material is generally located in the:

Nucleoid region

Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; their DNA occupies a nucleoid region.

7. Mitochondria and chloroplasts possess:

Their own DNA and ribosomes

These organelles retain genetic and protein-synthesis machinery, although many required proteins are encoded in the nucleus.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Which structure synthesizes proteins?

  1. Lysosome
  2. Ribosome
  3. Vacuole
  4. Cell wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ribosome

Ribosomes translate mRNA into polypeptides during protein synthesis.

2. Which is characteristic of a prokaryotic cell?

  1. No ribosomes
  2. No cell membrane
  3. No membrane-bound nucleus
  4. Always multicellular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. No membrane-bound nucleus

Prokaryotes have genetic material without a membrane-bound nucleus, and they do have ribosomes.

3. Which organelle carries out much aerobic ATP production in eukaryotic cells?

  1. Mitochondrion
  2. Golgi apparatus
  3. Cell wall
  4. Nucleolus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mitochondrion

Mitochondria host the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

4. The plasma membrane is described as selectively permeable because it:

  1. Regulates movement of substances
  2. Allows everything equally
  3. Allows nothing ever
  4. Is made only of cellulose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Regulates movement of substances

The lipid bilayer and membrane proteins determine how different substances cross.

5. The Golgi apparatus commonly participates in:

  1. Modification and packaging of cell products
  2. Only DNA replication
  3. Only ATP synthesis
  4. Only photosynthetic oxygen release
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Modification and packaging of cell products

Golgi cisternae process, sort and package products arriving from the endoplasmic reticulum.

6. Rough endoplasmic reticulum is associated with attached:

  1. Ribosomes
  2. Chloroplasts
  3. Whole nuclei
  4. Centrioles only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ribosomes

Attached ribosomes synthesise proteins entering the secretory or membrane pathway.

7. Lysosomes characteristically contain:

  1. Hydrolytic enzymes
  2. Only chlorophyll
  3. Only DNA polymerase
  4. Only glucose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Hydrolytic enzymes

They support intracellular digestion and recycling under their appropriate internal conditions.

8. A nucleolus is strongly associated with:

  1. Ribosome-component production
  2. Only lipid storage
  3. Only ATP transport
  4. Only cell-wall synthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ribosome-component production

The nucleolus supports rRNA synthesis and ribosomal-subunit assembly.

9. Prokaryotic chromosomal material is generally located in the:

  1. Nucleoid region
  2. Membrane-bound nucleus
  3. Golgi lumen
  4. Chloroplast stroma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nucleoid region

Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; their DNA occupies a nucleoid region.

10. Mitochondria and chloroplasts possess:

  1. Their own DNA and ribosomes
  2. No membranes
  3. Only cellulose
  4. No genetic material
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Their own DNA and ribosomes

These organelles retain genetic and protein-synthesis machinery, although many required proteins are encoded in the nucleus.

11. In the fluid mosaic model, the main framework of the plasma membrane is:

  1. A cellulose sheet
  2. A DNA double helix
  3. A peptidoglycan bilayer
  4. A phospholipid bilayer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. A phospholipid bilayer

Amphipathic phospholipids form a bilayer with hydrophilic heads towards water and hydrophobic tails inward; proteins are embedded or associated.

12. The hydrophobic fatty-acid tails of membrane phospholipids mainly point:

  1. Only towards cytoplasm
  2. Towards ribosomal RNA
  3. Towards the bilayer interior
  4. Only towards extracellular water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Towards the bilayer interior

Nonpolar tails avoid water and face inward, while polar heads face aqueous environments on both sides.

13. Transport against a concentration gradient generally requires:

  1. A cellulose pump
  2. Energy input and transport machinery
  3. Only simple diffusion
  4. No membrane protein under any condition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Energy input and transport machinery

Active transport uses energy directly or through a coupled gradient to move substances against their gradients.

14. Facilitated diffusion differs from simple diffusion because it:

  1. Uses membrane transport proteins
  2. Always consumes ATP directly
  3. Moves only against a gradient
  4. Occurs only across cell walls
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Uses membrane transport proteins

Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a gradient through channels or carriers; it does not require direct ATP hydrolysis.

15. Most bacterial ribosomes are described as:

  1. 80S
  2. 60S
  3. 40S
  4. 70S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 70S

Bacterial ribosomes are 70S particles formed from 50S and 30S subunits. Sedimentation coefficients are not arithmetically additive.

16. Typical eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes contain which subunits?

  1. 40S and 30S
  2. 70S and 80S
  3. 60S and 40S
  4. 50S and 30S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 60S and 40S

Eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S, consisting of 60S and 40S subunits.

17. Which cell structure is not enclosed by a membrane?

  1. Golgi cisterna
  2. Ribosome
  3. Lysosome
  4. Mitochondrion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Ribosome

Ribosomes are ribonucleoprotein complexes without a bounding membrane; the other listed structures are membrane bounded.

18. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum is especially associated with:

  1. Lipid synthesis
  2. Chromosome segregation exclusively
  3. Formation of bacterial peptidoglycan
  4. Storage of genetic information
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lipid synthesis

Smooth ER participates in lipid synthesis and, in specialised cells, detoxification and calcium handling.

19. A secreted protein is commonly processed along which route?

  1. Chloroplast → nucleus → cell wall
  2. Nucleolus → mitochondrion → ribosome
  3. Lysosome → DNA → centriole
  4. Rough ER → Golgi → secretory vesicle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Rough ER → Golgi → secretory vesicle

Proteins entering the secretory pathway are synthesised into the rough ER, processed in the Golgi and transported in vesicles.

20. The inward folds of the mitochondrial inner membrane are called:

  1. Cisternae of Golgi
  2. Nuclear pores
  3. Cristae
  4. Grana
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cristae

Cristae increase the inner membrane surface area that supports respiratory electron transport and ATP synthesis.

21. Stacks of thylakoids in chloroplasts are called:

  1. Nucleoli
  2. Grana
  3. Cristae
  4. Centrioles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Grana

A granum is a stack of thylakoids; grana is the plural. Thylakoid membranes house photosynthetic light-reaction machinery.

22. The fluid region of a chloroplast surrounding thylakoids is the:

  1. Stroma
  2. Matrix of mitochondrion
  3. Periplasm of bacteria
  4. Nucleoplasm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Stroma

The chloroplast stroma contains enzymes for carbon fixation as well as chloroplast DNA and ribosomes.

23. The large central vacuole of a mature plant cell is bounded by the:

  1. Plasmalemma only
  2. Nuclear envelope
  3. Middle lamella
  4. Tonoplast
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Tonoplast

The tonoplast is the vacuolar membrane and controls exchange between vacuole contents and cytoplasm.

24. The middle lamella between adjacent plant cells is chiefly rich in:

  1. Peptidoglycan
  2. Glycogen
  3. Calcium pectate
  4. Chitin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Calcium pectate

The pectin-rich middle lamella, commonly described as calcium pectate, helps cement neighbouring plant cells together.

25. Channels connecting the cytoplasm of neighbouring plant cells are:

  1. Ribosomal subunits
  2. Plasmodesmata
  3. Cristae
  4. Desmosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plasmodesmata

Plasmodesmata traverse cell walls and provide cytoplasmic connections for communication and transport.

26. The nuclear envelope of a typical eukaryotic cell consists of:

  1. Two membranes with nuclear pores
  2. A single cellulose layer
  3. No membranes
  4. A peptidoglycan shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two membranes with nuclear pores

A double membrane separates nuclear contents from cytoplasm; pores regulate nucleocytoplasmic exchange.

27. A typical motile eukaryotic cilium has an axonemal arrangement of:

  1. Nine peripheral triplets and no central pair
  2. Nine single microtubules only
  3. Two peripheral doublets and nine central singlets
  4. Nine peripheral microtubule doublets and two central singlets
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Nine peripheral microtubule doublets and two central singlets

The conventional motile axoneme has a 9+2 arrangement. Some specialised cilia differ from this typical pattern.

28. A typical centriole shows which microtubule arrangement?

  1. Two central triplets only
  2. Eight peripheral single microtubules
  3. Nine peripheral triplets with no central pair
  4. Nine peripheral doublets plus two central singlets
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Nine peripheral triplets with no central pair

A centriole is typically organised as nine peripheral triplets, conventionally described as 9+0.

29. Which group typically has a cell wall containing chitin?

  1. Most bacteria
  2. Fungi
  3. Animals
  4. Flowering plants
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fungi

Fungal cell walls characteristically contain chitin. Plant walls chiefly contain cellulose; bacterial walls commonly contain peptidoglycan.

30. A mature mammalian red blood cell differs from most animal cells because it lacks:

  1. A nucleus and mitochondria
  2. A plasma membrane
  3. Haemoglobin
  4. Cytoplasm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A nucleus and mitochondria

During maturation, mammalian erythrocytes lose their nucleus and mitochondria but retain plasma membrane, cytoplasm and haemoglobin.
