Oscillations and Waves

Key concepts

  • Simple harmonic motion has acceleration a = −ω²x. For an ideal mass–spring system, T = 2π√(m/k).
  • For small-angle oscillations of an ideal simple pendulum, T = 2π√(L/g). Frequency is the reciprocal of period.
  • A travelling wave has speed v = fλ. Superposition allows interference and standing-wave patterns.

Worked example

A wave of frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 2 m has speed 100 m/s. Quadrupling an ideal pendulum length doubles its small-angle period.

Watch out

The simple pendulum formula assumes small amplitude; it is not exact for arbitrary angles.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A periodic motion has period 0.25 s. Frequency is:

4 Hz

f = 1/T = 1/0.25 = 4 Hz.

2. In ideal SHM, speed is maximum at:

Equilibrium

At equilibrium, potential energy is minimum and kinetic energy maximum.

3. In ideal SHM, acceleration magnitude is greatest at:

The extremes

|a| = ω²|x|, so maximum displacement gives maximum acceleration magnitude.

4. A mass–spring oscillator has m = 1 kg and k = 4 N/m. Period is:

π s

T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(1/4) = π s.

5. Two waves of frequencies 256 Hz and 260 Hz produce beat frequency:

4 Hz

Beat frequency is the absolute frequency difference, |260 − 256| = 4 Hz.

6. In a transverse wave, particle displacement is:

Perpendicular to propagation

Transverse oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.

7. For a string fixed at both ends, fundamental wavelength is:

2L

The fundamental has two end nodes and one antinode; the length contains half a wavelength.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A wave with f = 50 Hz and λ = 2 m has speed:

  1. 25 m/s
  2. 50 m/s
  3. 100 m/s
  4. 200 m/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 100 m/s

v = fλ = 50 × 2 = 100 m/s.

2. Quadrupling an ideal pendulum length changes its small-angle period by:

  1. Four times
  2. Two times
  3. Half
  4. No change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two times

T is proportional to √L.

3. SHM acceleration is directed:

  1. Away from equilibrium always
  2. Toward equilibrium
  3. Along velocity always
  4. In one fixed direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Toward equilibrium

a = −ω²x points opposite displacement.

4. A periodic motion has period 0.25 s. Frequency is:

  1. 4 Hz
  2. 0.25 Hz
  3. 2 Hz
  4. 8 Hz
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 Hz

f = 1/T = 1/0.25 = 4 Hz.

5. In ideal SHM, speed is maximum at:

  1. Equilibrium
  2. Either extreme
  3. Every point equally
  4. Only maximum positive displacement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Equilibrium

At equilibrium, potential energy is minimum and kinetic energy maximum.

6. In ideal SHM, acceleration magnitude is greatest at:

  1. The extremes
  2. Equilibrium
  3. Every point equally
  4. Only zero amplitude
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The extremes

|a| = ω²|x|, so maximum displacement gives maximum acceleration magnitude.

7. A mass–spring oscillator has m = 1 kg and k = 4 N/m. Period is:

  1. π s
  2. 2π s
  3. 4π s
  4. π/2 s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. π s

T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(1/4) = π s.

8. Two waves of frequencies 256 Hz and 260 Hz produce beat frequency:

  1. 4 Hz
  2. 516 Hz
  3. 258 Hz
  4. 2 Hz
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 Hz

Beat frequency is the absolute frequency difference, |260 − 256| = 4 Hz.

9. In a transverse wave, particle displacement is:

  1. Perpendicular to propagation
  2. Parallel to propagation
  3. Always zero
  4. Only rotational
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Perpendicular to propagation

Transverse oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.

10. For a string fixed at both ends, fundamental wavelength is:

  1. 2L
  2. L
  3. L/2
  4. 4L
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2L

The fundamental has two end nodes and one antinode; the length contains half a wavelength.
