Photosynthesis in Higher Plants

Photosynthesis foundations

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy. In plants, light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.

Two linked processes

The light reactions produce ATP and NADPH and release oxygen from water. The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma and uses ATP and NADPH to help fix carbon dioxide into organic compounds.

Watch out

The oxygen released during plant photosynthesis comes from water, not carbon dioxide. The Calvin cycle does not directly use light, but depends on products and regulation linked to the light reactions.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. The principal reaction-centre pigment in plant photosynthesis is:

Chlorophyll a

Chlorophyll a in reaction centres participates directly in photochemical charge separation.

2. The initial carbon-fixing enzyme in the C₃ Calvin cycle is:

RuBisCO

RuBisCO catalyses CO₂ addition to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate in the carboxylation stage.

3. The first stable product of C₃ carbon fixation is:

3-phosphoglycerate

The unstable six-carbon intermediate splits into two three-carbon 3-PGA molecules.

4. In a typical C₄ pathway, initial CO₂ fixation forms a four-carbon compound via:

PEP carboxylase

PEP carboxylase initially forms oxaloacetate from phosphoenolpyruvate and bicarbonate.

5. Cyclic photophosphorylation principally produces:

ATP without net NADPH or O₂ production

Electrons cycle through a pathway associated with photosystem I, supporting ATP formation.

6. The Calvin cycle consumes products of light reactions including:

ATP and NADPH

ATP supplies energy and NADPH supplies reducing power for carbon assimilation.

7. Photorespiration is promoted when RuBisCO acts as an:

Oxygenase

RuBisCO can add O₂ rather than CO₂, leading to the photorespiratory pathway.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The oxygen released during plant photosynthesis originates from:

  1. Water
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Glucose
  4. Chlorophyll
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Water

Water is split during the light reactions, releasing oxygen.

2. The Calvin cycle occurs in the chloroplast’s:

  1. Outer membrane
  2. Thylakoid lumen
  3. Inner membrane only
  4. Stroma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Stroma

The Calvin cycle enzymes operate in the chloroplast stroma.

3. Light-dependent reactions in chloroplasts occur in the:

  1. Stroma only
  2. Thylakoid membranes
  3. Nucleus
  4. Cell wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Thylakoid membranes

Thylakoid membranes contain the photosynthetic electron-transport systems.

4. The principal reaction-centre pigment in plant photosynthesis is:

  1. Chlorophyll a
  2. Haemoglobin
  3. Melanin
  4. Insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Chlorophyll a

Chlorophyll a in reaction centres participates directly in photochemical charge separation.

5. The initial carbon-fixing enzyme in the C₃ Calvin cycle is:

  1. RuBisCO
  2. DNA ligase
  3. Pepsin
  4. Trypsin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. RuBisCO

RuBisCO catalyses CO₂ addition to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate in the carboxylation stage.

6. The first stable product of C₃ carbon fixation is:

  1. 3-phosphoglycerate
  2. Oxaloacetate
  3. Ethanol
  4. Acetyl-CoA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3-phosphoglycerate

The unstable six-carbon intermediate splits into two three-carbon 3-PGA molecules.

7. In a typical C₄ pathway, initial CO₂ fixation forms a four-carbon compound via:

  1. PEP carboxylase
  2. RuBisCO only as the first step
  3. DNA polymerase
  4. Amylase
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. PEP carboxylase

PEP carboxylase initially forms oxaloacetate from phosphoenolpyruvate and bicarbonate.

8. Cyclic photophosphorylation principally produces:

  1. ATP without net NADPH or O₂ production
  2. Only oxygen
  3. Only NADPH
  4. Glucose directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ATP without net NADPH or O₂ production

Electrons cycle through a pathway associated with photosystem I, supporting ATP formation.

9. The Calvin cycle consumes products of light reactions including:

  1. ATP and NADPH
  2. Only oxygen
  3. Only nitrogen gas
  4. Only ethanol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ATP and NADPH

ATP supplies energy and NADPH supplies reducing power for carbon assimilation.

10. Photorespiration is promoted when RuBisCO acts as an:

  1. Oxygenase
  2. Amylase
  3. Ligase
  4. Protease
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Oxygenase

RuBisCO can add O₂ rather than CO₂, leading to the photorespiratory pathway.

11. The primary electron donor replacing electrons lost by photosystem II is:

  1. Molecular nitrogen
  2. Water
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Glucose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Water

Water splitting supplies electrons and releases oxygen and protons.

12. The reaction-centre chlorophyll of photosystem II is called:

  1. P680
  2. P700
  3. RuBisCO
  4. PEP
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. P680

Its characteristic absorption maximum is near 680 nm.

13. The reaction-centre chlorophyll of photosystem I is called:

  1. P680
  2. ATP synthase
  3. NADP reductase
  4. P700
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. P700

Photosystem I has the reaction centre conventionally designated P700.

14. Noncyclic electron flow normally involves:

  1. Photosystem II alone without any acceptor
  2. Neither photosystem
  3. Both photosystems II and I
  4. Photosystem I alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Both photosystems II and I

Electron transfer through both systems supports ATP and NADPH production.

15. The electron acceptor reduced at the end of normal linear photosynthetic flow is:

  1. ADP directly
  2. NADP⁺
  3. O₂
  4. CO₂ directly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. NADP⁺

NADP⁺ accepts electrons to form NADPH.

16. During light reactions, protons accumulate primarily in the:

  1. Thylakoid lumen
  2. Chloroplast outer cytoplasm
  3. Nuclear lumen
  4. Golgi lumen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thylakoid lumen

Proton pumping and water splitting establish the thylakoid gradient.

17. Photosynthetic ATP synthase uses proton flow from the lumen towards the:

  1. Outer cell wall
  2. Nucleolus
  3. Vacuole
  4. Stroma
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Stroma

Down-gradient proton flow powers ATP formation on the stromal side.

18. The Calvin-cycle CO₂ acceptor in a C₃ plant is:

  1. Oxaloacetate
  2. Acetyl-CoA
  3. RuBP
  4. PEP
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. RuBP

RuBisCO adds CO₂ to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate.

19. RuBP contains how many carbon atoms?

  1. 6
  2. 5
  3. 3
  4. 4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5

Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate is a five-carbon sugar phosphate.

20. A first stable C₃ fixation product is:

  1. 3-Phosphoglycerate
  2. Oxaloacetate
  3. Pyruvate
  4. Acetyl-CoA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3-Phosphoglycerate

The initial unstable six-carbon product yields two three-carbon molecules.

21. The initial CO₂ acceptor in the usual C₄ mesophyll pathway is:

  1. RuBP
  2. Glucose
  3. Ribose
  4. PEP
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. PEP

Phosphoenolpyruvate accepts inorganic carbon during initial C₄ fixation.

22. Oxaloacetate in the C₄ pathway contains how many carbons?

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 4
  4. 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 4

Oxaloacetate is the initial four-carbon product.

23. Kranz anatomy is characteristically associated with:

  1. Only fungal cells
  2. Many C₄ leaves
  3. All roots
  4. All C₃ leaves
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Many C₄ leaves

Mesophyll surrounds bundle-sheath cells in the characteristic C₄ arrangement.

24. In a typical C₄ leaf, the Calvin cycle occurs principally in:

  1. Bundle-sheath chloroplasts
  2. Root hairs
  3. Only guard-cell nuclei
  4. Xylem vessels
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bundle-sheath chloroplasts

CO₂ is concentrated near RuBisCO in bundle-sheath cells.

25. Photorespiration involves oxygenation of RuBP by:

  1. PEP carboxylase
  2. DNA polymerase
  3. Amylase
  4. RuBisCO
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. RuBisCO

RuBisCO can act as an oxygenase when conditions favour oxygenation.

26. A photosynthesis action spectrum measures:

  1. Only leaf thickness
  2. Only chromosome number
  3. Photosynthetic effectiveness of different wavelengths
  4. Only pigment concentration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Photosynthetic effectiveness of different wavelengths

It relates wavelength to the biological rate of photosynthesis.

27. An absorption spectrum measures:

  1. Only oxygen mass
  2. Light absorbed across wavelengths
  3. Only sugar exported
  4. Only stomatal density
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Light absorbed across wavelengths

It records wavelength-dependent absorption by a pigment or sample.

28. Accessory pigments benefit photosynthesis by:

  1. Extending light capture and providing photoprotection
  2. Replacing all chlorophyll a
  3. Creating CO₂ from nitrogen
  4. Eliminating electron transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Extending light capture and providing photoprotection

They transfer captured energy and help limit light-induced damage.

29. The rate-limiting factor concept means that photosynthetic rate is constrained by:

  1. Always oxygen abundance
  2. Always leaf mass alone
  3. Every factor equally at all times
  4. The factor most limiting under the conditions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The factor most limiting under the conditions

Improving a nonlimiting factor may not increase the rate.

30. For one net three-carbon sugar equivalent, the Calvin cycle fixes:

  1. 2 CO₂
  2. 6 CO₂
  3. 3 CO₂
  4. 1 CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3 CO₂

Three fixed carbons are needed for one net triose-phosphate equivalent.
