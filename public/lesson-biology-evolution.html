Evolution

Evolution

Key concepts

  • Evolution involves heritable changes in populations across generations. Natural selection acts through differences in survival and reproduction.
  • Homologous structures share an evolutionary origin even when functions differ; analogous structures have similar functions but different origins.
  • In a simple two-allele Hardy–Weinberg model, p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1 under specified equilibrium assumptions.

Connect the ideas

If p = 0.6 and q = 0.4, the expected heterozygote frequency is 2pq = 0.48 under equilibrium assumptions.

Common mistake

Individuals do not evolve new inherited traits because they need them. Selection changes trait frequencies in populations over generations.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Analogous structures have similar function but typically:

Different evolutionary origins

Analogy reflects convergent functional similarity rather than the same ancestral structure.

2. Genetic drift describes allele-frequency change due to:

Chance sampling

Random changes are especially prominent in small populations.

3. A founder effect can occur when a new population starts from:

A small sample of an original population

The sample may carry allele frequencies different from the source population by chance.

4. Gene flow involves transfer of alleles through:

Migration and reproduction

Individuals or gametes moving between populations can introduce alleles.

5. If q = 0.2 under a two-allele Hardy–Weinberg model, the recessive homozygote frequency is:

0.04

The recessive homozygote fraction is q² = 0.2² = 0.04.

6. Antibiotic resistance can spread because selection favours resistant variants already present or arising by mutation, rather than:

Bacteria deliberately deciding to resist

Selection acts on variation; it does not require organisms to consciously acquire the needed trait.

7. A Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium assumption includes:

Random mating

The ideal model also assumes a very large population and no mutation, migration or selection affecting the locus.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. For p = 0.6 and q = 0.4, Hardy–Weinberg heterozygote frequency is:

  1. 0.24
  2. 0.36
  3. 0.48
  4. 0.60
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 0.48

2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.48.

2. Structures with shared ancestry but potentially different functions are:

  1. Homologous
  2. Always analogous
  3. Always vestigial
  4. Non-heritable
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Homologous

Homology reflects common evolutionary origin.

3. Natural selection directly involves differences in:

  1. Survival and reproductive success
  2. Conscious desire
  3. Individual intention
  4. Only body size
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Survival and reproductive success

Heritable variants differ in fitness under particular environmental conditions.

4. Analogous structures have similar function but typically:

  1. Different evolutionary origins
  2. Identical ancestry in every case
  3. No function
  4. No environmental relation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Different evolutionary origins

Analogy reflects convergent functional similarity rather than the same ancestral structure.

5. Genetic drift describes allele-frequency change due to:

  1. Chance sampling
  2. Only conscious adaptation
  3. Only directed mutation
  4. Only perfect selection
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Chance sampling

Random changes are especially prominent in small populations.

6. A founder effect can occur when a new population starts from:

  1. A small sample of an original population
  2. Every individual in the original population equally
  3. Only identical ecosystems
  4. No reproduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A small sample of an original population

The sample may carry allele frequencies different from the source population by chance.

7. Gene flow involves transfer of alleles through:

  1. Migration and reproduction
  2. Only weather changes
  3. Only fossil formation
  4. Only nonliving particles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Migration and reproduction

Individuals or gametes moving between populations can introduce alleles.

8. If q = 0.2 under a two-allele Hardy–Weinberg model, the recessive homozygote frequency is:

  1. 0.8
  2. 0.04
  3. 0.2
  4. 0.4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.04

The recessive homozygote fraction is q² = 0.2² = 0.04.

9. Antibiotic resistance can spread because selection favours resistant variants already present or arising by mutation, rather than:

  1. Bacteria deliberately deciding to resist
  2. Heritable variation
  3. Differential reproduction
  4. Changes in allele frequency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bacteria deliberately deciding to resist

Selection acts on variation; it does not require organisms to consciously acquire the needed trait.

10. A Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium assumption includes:

  1. Random mating
  2. Strong selection
  3. Large migration
  4. Frequent directed mutation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Random mating

The ideal model also assumes a very large population and no mutation, migration or selection affecting the locus.

11. Biological evolution concerns changes in inherited population characteristics across:

  1. Only minutes within one cell
  2. Only daily food intake
  3. Only individual exercise
  4. Generations
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Generations

Population inheritance patterns change over successive generations.

12. Natural selection acts through differences in:

  1. Only conscious desire
  2. Only individual age
  3. Survival and reproduction
  4. Only geographical names
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Survival and reproduction

Heritable variants can contribute different numbers of offspring.

13. A source of new heritable variation is:

  1. Only temporary sweating
  2. Mutation
  3. Only exercise
  4. Only learning a song
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mutation

DNA changes can introduce new inherited alleles.

14. Genetic recombination can produce new combinations of:

  1. Existing alleles
  2. Only new atomic elements
  3. Only identical clones
  4. Only nonheritable injuries
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Existing alleles

Meiosis reshuffles inherited variants.

15. Genetic drift is change in allele frequency due to:

  1. Only purposeful adaptation
  2. Only a universal optimum
  3. Only exercise
  4. Random sampling effects
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Random sampling effects

Chance effects are especially influential in small populations.

16. Reproductive isolation supports speciation by reducing:

  1. All mutation
  2. All cell division
  3. Gene flow between diverging populations
  4. All DNA replication
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Gene flow between diverging populations

Restricted interbreeding permits populations to diverge genetically over generations.

17. A bottleneck can reduce variation after a:

  1. Temporary temperature measurement
  2. Sharp population-size reduction
  3. Universal population expansion
  4. Single ordinary meal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sharp population-size reduction

Few survivors can retain only part of the previous gene pool.

18. Gene flow involves movement of alleles through:

  1. Migration and reproduction
  2. Only rainfall
  3. Only rock erosion
  4. Only enzyme denaturation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Migration and reproduction

Migrants can contribute their variants to another population.

19. Homologous structures share a common:

  1. Function necessarily
  2. Colour necessarily
  3. Size necessarily
  4. Evolutionary origin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Evolutionary origin

Common ancestry can yield structures adapted to different uses.

20. Analogous structures chiefly share a similar:

  1. DNA sequence in every gene
  2. Embryonic origin necessarily
  3. Function despite different origins
  4. Ancestral skeletal pattern necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Function despite different origins

Convergent adaptation can produce similar functional solutions.

21. Bird and insect wings are a classic example of:

  1. Identical embryonic construction
  2. Analogy
  3. Homology of the entire wing structure
  4. No evolutionary comparison
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Analogy

Their flight function is similar but overall origins differ.

22. Forelimbs of humans and whales illustrate:

  1. Homology
  2. Analogy of origin
  3. Independent invention of every bone
  4. No common ancestry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Homology

Their underlying skeletal pattern reflects common vertebrate ancestry.

23. Adaptive radiation involves diversification from a lineage into:

  1. Only one unchanged niche
  2. Only a single clone
  3. Only nonheritable traits
  4. Different ecological roles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Different ecological roles

Different niches favour divergent adaptations.

24. Darwin's finches are a familiar example of:

  1. Only nuclear mutation in rocks
  2. No speciation
  3. Adaptive radiation
  4. Only artificial cloning
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Adaptive radiation

Related birds diversified into different ecological forms.

25. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium assumes random mating and absence of substantial:

  1. All genetic diversity
  2. Selection, mutation, migration and drift
  3. All reproduction
  4. All chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Selection, mutation, migration and drift

These forces would change the predicted stable allele frequencies.

26. For two alleles with p=0.7 and q=0.3, expected heterozygote frequency is:

  1. 0.42
  2. 0.49
  3. 0.09
  4. 0.21
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.42

Under Hardy–Weinberg assumptions, 2pq=2×0.7×0.3.

27. With q=0.25 under Hardy–Weinberg conditions, recessive homozygote frequency is:

  1. 0.0625
  2. 0.25
  3. 0.5
  4. 0.75
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.0625

The expected aa frequency is q²=0.25²=0.0625.

28. Stabilising selection commonly favours:

  1. Only one extreme always
  2. Every phenotype equally
  3. Intermediate phenotypes
  4. Both extremes only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Intermediate phenotypes

Individuals near the intermediate can have greater fitness.

29. Directional selection commonly shifts a distribution towards:

  1. No inherited trait
  2. One phenotypic extreme
  3. Both extremes equally
  4. Only the original mean
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. One phenotypic extreme

One side gains a reproductive advantage.

30. Disruptive selection commonly favours:

  1. Both extremes over intermediates
  2. Only intermediates
  3. Only identical genotypes
  4. No phenotype
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both extremes over intermediates

Different extreme phenotypes can outperform intermediate forms.
