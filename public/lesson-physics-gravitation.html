Gravitation

Gravitation

Key concepts

  • For point masses, F = Gm₁m₂/r². Outside a spherically symmetric body, the field is equivalent to that of its mass at its centre.
  • At distance r from the centre of mass M, gravitational potential per unit mass is −GM/r when zero is chosen at infinity.
  • For an ideal circular orbit, speed is √(GM/r). Escape speed from radius R is √(2GM/R), neglecting atmosphere and rotation.

Worked example

At twice a planet’s surface radius, the ideal gravitational acceleration is one-quarter of its surface value.

Watch out

Radius in these formulas is measured from the centre, not simply altitude above the surface.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Gravitational interaction between two positive masses is:

Attractive

Newtonian gravitational force attracts each mass toward the other.

2. For a circular orbit around a fixed central mass, period scales with radius as:

r³ᐟ²

Kepler’s circular-orbit relation T² ∝ r³ gives T ∝ r³ᐟ².

3. Gravitational potential with zero at infinity outside a spherical mass M is:

−GM/r

At finite radius the potential is negative because work must be supplied to move a unit mass to infinity.

4. An astronaut in an orbiting spacecraft feels weightless mainly because both are:

In continuous free fall

The astronaut and spacecraft share gravitational free fall; gravity is generally substantial at orbital altitude.

5. At radius 3R outside a spherical planet, acceleration compared with surface g is:

g/9

Inverse-square dependence gives g(R/r)² = g/9.

6. Ideal escape speed from a given planet radius depends on the escaping object’s mass:

Not at all

Object mass cancels between kinetic and gravitational potential-energy terms.

7. For a circular gravitational orbit, total mechanical energy is:

−GMm/(2r)

K = GMm/(2r) and U = −GMm/r; their sum is −GMm/(2r).

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. At r = 2R outside a spherical planet, g compared with its surface value is:

  1. 2g
  2. g/2
  3. g/4
  4. 4g
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. g/4

g varies as 1/r², giving one-quarter.

2. The SI unit of G is:

  1. N m² kg⁻²
  2. N kg² m⁻²
  3. m s⁻¹
  4. kg m⁻²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. N m² kg⁻²

From G = Fr²/(m₁m₂), the unit is N m² kg⁻².

3. Escape speed relative to circular orbital speed at the same radius is:

  1. Equal
  2. √2 times
  3. Half
  4. Twice
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. √2 times

The respective expressions are √(2GM/r) and √(GM/r).

4. Gravitational interaction between two positive masses is:

  1. Attractive
  2. Repulsive
  3. Zero always
  4. Only magnetic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Attractive

Newtonian gravitational force attracts each mass toward the other.

5. For a circular orbit around a fixed central mass, period scales with radius as:

  1. r³ᐟ²
  2. r
  3. r⁻¹
  4. r²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. r³ᐟ²

Kepler’s circular-orbit relation T² ∝ r³ gives T ∝ r³ᐟ².

6. Gravitational potential with zero at infinity outside a spherical mass M is:

  1. −GM/r
  2. +GM/r
  3. −GMr
  4. Zero everywhere
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. −GM/r

At finite radius the potential is negative because work must be supplied to move a unit mass to infinity.

7. An astronaut in an orbiting spacecraft feels weightless mainly because both are:

  1. In continuous free fall
  2. Outside every gravitational field
  3. Without mass
  4. At zero velocity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. In continuous free fall

The astronaut and spacecraft share gravitational free fall; gravity is generally substantial at orbital altitude.

8. At radius 3R outside a spherical planet, acceleration compared with surface g is:

  1. g/9
  2. g/3
  3. 3g
  4. 9g
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. g/9

Inverse-square dependence gives g(R/r)² = g/9.

9. Ideal escape speed from a given planet radius depends on the escaping object’s mass:

  1. Not at all
  2. Linearly
  3. As its square root
  4. As its square
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Not at all

Object mass cancels between kinetic and gravitational potential-energy terms.

10. For a circular gravitational orbit, total mechanical energy is:

  1. −GMm/(2r)
  2. +GMm/r
  3. Zero
  4. −2GMm/r
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. −GMm/(2r)

K = GMm/(2r) and U = −GMm/r; their sum is −GMm/(2r).
