Chemical kinetics: first order

Chemical kinetics: first order

Core idea

For a first-order reaction, rate = k[A]. Its integrated rate law is ln([A]₀/[A]t) = kt, and half-life t½ = ln 2/k. The rate constant has units of inverse time.

Worked connection

A first-order reaction with k = 0.693 min⁻¹ has half-life 1 min. After two half-lives, one-quarter of the starting reactant remains.

Watch out

Reaction order is determined experimentally. It cannot generally be read from the balanced equation.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A first-order rate constant is 0.231 min⁻¹. Approximate half-life is:

3 min

t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/0.231 = 3 min.

2. If [A] doubles in a first-order reaction, its instantaneous rate:

Doubles

Rate = k[A], so rate is directly proportional to reactant concentration at fixed conditions.

3. For a zero-order reaction, rate is:

Independent of reactant concentration in its valid regime

Zero order means rate = k within the range where that rate law holds, not absence of reaction.

4. A catalyst commonly accelerates a reaction by:

Providing a lower-activation-energy pathway

A different mechanism can lower the barrier without altering the overall reaction thermodynamics.

5. The Arrhenius relation is:

k = A exp(−Ea/RT)

The exponential factor represents the effect of an activation barrier on the rate constant.

6. Molecularity is defined for:

An elementary reaction step

It counts reacting entities in an elementary event; overall reaction order is determined experimentally.

7. A first-order reactant has 80% remaining initially and halves once. Remaining fraction of the original initial amount is:

40%

One half-life halves the amount present: 0.80 × 0.5 = 0.40 of the original reference amount.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The unit of a first-order rate constant, using seconds, is:

  1. mol/L
  2. s⁻¹
  3. L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
  4. mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. s⁻¹

Rate/[A] has units s⁻¹ for a first-order process.

2. A first-order reactant remaining after two half-lives is:

  1. 75%
  2. 50%
  3. 25%
  4. 0%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 25%

The fraction remaining is (½)² = ¼.

3. For first-order kinetics, half-life depends on:

  1. Initial concentration only
  2. The rate constant
  3. Container colour
  4. Product mass only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The rate constant

t½ = ln 2/k, independent of initial concentration.

4. A first-order rate constant is 0.231 min⁻¹. Approximate half-life is:

  1. 6 min
  2. 3 min
  3. 1 min
  4. 0.333 min
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3 min

t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/0.231 = 3 min.

5. If [A] doubles in a first-order reaction, its instantaneous rate:

  1. Doubles
  2. Quadruples
  3. Halves
  4. Stays constant
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Doubles

Rate = k[A], so rate is directly proportional to reactant concentration at fixed conditions.

6. For a zero-order reaction, rate is:

  1. Independent of reactant concentration in its valid regime
  2. Always zero
  3. Proportional to [A]
  4. Proportional to [A]²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Independent of reactant concentration in its valid regime

Zero order means rate = k within the range where that rate law holds, not absence of reaction.

7. A catalyst commonly accelerates a reaction by:

  1. Providing a lower-activation-energy pathway
  2. Increasing equilibrium constant
  3. Making ΔG always zero
  4. Increasing every molecular mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Providing a lower-activation-energy pathway

A different mechanism can lower the barrier without altering the overall reaction thermodynamics.

8. The Arrhenius relation is:

  1. k = A exp(−Ea/RT)
  2. k = A exp(+Ea/RT) always
  3. k = Ea + RT
  4. k = RT/Ea always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. k = A exp(−Ea/RT)

The exponential factor represents the effect of an activation barrier on the rate constant.

9. Molecularity is defined for:

  1. An elementary reaction step
  2. Every overall equation uniquely
  3. Only first-order reactions
  4. Only solid substances
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An elementary reaction step

It counts reacting entities in an elementary event; overall reaction order is determined experimentally.

10. A first-order reactant has 80% remaining initially and halves once. Remaining fraction of the original initial amount is:

  1. 80%
  2. 40%
  3. 20%
  4. 60%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 40%

One half-life halves the amount present: 0.80 × 0.5 = 0.40 of the original reference amount.
