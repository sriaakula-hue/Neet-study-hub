Rotational Motion

Rotational Motion

Key concepts

  • Torque magnitude is τ = rF sin θ. For a rigid body about a fixed axis with constant moment of inertia, net torque is Iα.
  • Moment of inertia depends on the mass distribution and chosen axis. Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω².
  • Angular momentum is conserved when the net external torque is zero. For a point mass at perpendicular distance r, I = mr².

Worked example

A 2 kg point mass 0.5 m from an axis has I = 0.50 kg m². At ω = 4 rad/s its rotational kinetic energy is 4 J.

Watch out

Use the perpendicular distance from the axis; moment of inertia is not an intrinsic scalar independent of the axis.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A 6 N force acts perpendicular to a lever arm 0.5 m long. Torque is:

3 N m

τ = rF = 0.5 × 6 = 3 N m for perpendicular force.

2. I = 2 kg m² and net torque 8 N m give angular acceleration:

4 rad/s²

For fixed-axis rotation with constant I, α = τ/I = 8/2 = 4 rad/s².

3. For a uniform solid disc about its central perpendicular axis, I is:

½MR²

The standard mass integral for a uniform disc gives half MR².

4. The parallel-axis theorem is:

I = Icm + Md²

The second axis is parallel and distance d from the centre-of-mass axis; translating the axis adds Md².

5. A body has I = 4 kg m² and ω = 3 rad/s. Angular momentum magnitude about that axis is:

12 kg m²/s

For this fixed principal-axis rotation, L = Iω = 4 × 3 = 12 kg m²/s.

6. Pure rolling without slipping obeys:

vcm = ωR

The instantaneous contact point is at rest relative to the surface when vcm matches ωR.

7. If I halves while angular momentum remains constant, angular speed:

Doubles

L = Iω; at fixed L, ω is inversely proportional to I.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A 2 kg point mass at perpendicular distance 0.5 m has I:

  1. 0.25 kg m²
  2. 0.50 kg m²
  3. 1 kg m²
  4. 2 kg m²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.50 kg m²

I = mr² = 2 × 0.5² = 0.50 kg m².

2. A force applied along the radius gives torque magnitude:

  1. rF
  2. 2rF
  3. Zero
  4. F/r
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zero

The angle is 0° or 180°, so sin θ = 0.

3. Angular momentum is conserved if the net external:

  1. Force is always zero
  2. Torque is zero
  3. Speed is zero
  4. Mass is zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Torque is zero

The rate of change of angular momentum equals external torque.

4. A 6 N force acts perpendicular to a lever arm 0.5 m long. Torque is:

  1. 12 N m
  2. 6.5 N m
  3. 0.083 N m
  4. 3 N m
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 3 N m

τ = rF = 0.5 × 6 = 3 N m for perpendicular force.

5. I = 2 kg m² and net torque 8 N m give angular acceleration:

  1. 16 rad/s²
  2. 10 rad/s²
  3. 0.25 rad/s²
  4. 4 rad/s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 4 rad/s²

For fixed-axis rotation with constant I, α = τ/I = 8/2 = 4 rad/s².

6. For a uniform solid disc about its central perpendicular axis, I is:

  1. ½MR²
  2. MR²
  3. 2MR²
  4. ⅔MR²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ½MR²

The standard mass integral for a uniform disc gives half MR².

7. The parallel-axis theorem is:

  1. I = Icm + Md²
  2. I = Icm − Md²
  3. I = Md only
  4. I = Icm/d²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. I = Icm + Md²

The second axis is parallel and distance d from the centre-of-mass axis; translating the axis adds Md².

8. A body has I = 4 kg m² and ω = 3 rad/s. Angular momentum magnitude about that axis is:

  1. 6 kg m²/s
  2. 18 kg m²/s
  3. 36 kg m²/s
  4. 12 kg m²/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 12 kg m²/s

For this fixed principal-axis rotation, L = Iω = 4 × 3 = 12 kg m²/s.

9. Pure rolling without slipping obeys:

  1. vcm = ωR
  2. vcm = ω/R
  3. vcm = 0 always
  4. ω = 0 always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. vcm = ωR

The instantaneous contact point is at rest relative to the surface when vcm matches ωR.

10. If I halves while angular momentum remains constant, angular speed:

  1. Doubles
  2. Halves
  3. Stays constant
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Doubles

L = Iω; at fixed L, ω is inversely proportional to I.
