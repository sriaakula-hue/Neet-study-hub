Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Meristematic tissues divide actively; permanent tissues are differentiated. Parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma are simple tissue types.
  • Xylem conducts water and minerals and contributes mechanical support. Phloem transports organic solutes from sources to sinks.
  • Typical dicot stems have vascular bundles arranged in a ring; typical monocot stems have scattered bundles.

Connect the ideas

An actively photosynthesising leaf can be a sugar source, while a growing root is a sink. Phloem transport follows source–sink relations.

Common mistake

Phloem transport is not always downward. Different sieve tubes can transport toward different sinks.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Collenchyma usually provides support through cells with:

Unevenly thickened living cell walls

Collenchyma commonly has uneven thickening at cell corners and supports growing regions.

2. Sclerenchyma cells at maturity are generally:

Dead with thick lignified walls

Thick lignified walls provide mechanical support; mature cells are typically dead.

3. Lateral meristems primarily contribute to:

Increase in girth

Vascular and cork cambia contribute to secondary growth in suitable plants.

4. Stomata are pores regulated by:

Guard cells

Changes in guard-cell turgor regulate stomatal opening and gas exchange.

5. A vascular bundle containing cambium is described as:

Open

Open collateral bundles contain cambium and can support secondary vascular growth.

6. Casparian strips are associated with the root:

Endodermis

Their suberised barrier restricts apoplastic flow and promotes selective entry into the vascular cylinder.

7. Sieve-tube elements transport organic solutes and are closely associated with:

Companion cells

Companion cells support the function of the specialised sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Water and mineral transport primarily occurs through:

  1. Phloem
  2. Xylem
  3. Epidermis only
  4. Pith only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Xylem

Xylem is the main water-conducting vascular tissue.

2. Actively dividing plant tissue is called:

  1. Meristematic tissue
  2. Sclerenchyma only
  3. Permanent tissue only
  4. Cork only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meristematic tissue

Meristems contain actively dividing cells.

3. Typical monocot stem vascular bundles are:

  1. Arranged in one ring
  2. Absent
  3. Scattered in ground tissue
  4. Only in the epidermis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Scattered in ground tissue

A scattered vascular-bundle arrangement is typical of monocot stems.

4. Collenchyma usually provides support through cells with:

  1. Unevenly thickened living cell walls
  2. No cell walls
  3. Only lignified dead fibres
  4. Only sieve plates
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unevenly thickened living cell walls

Collenchyma commonly has uneven thickening at cell corners and supports growing regions.

5. Sclerenchyma cells at maturity are generally:

  1. Dead with thick lignified walls
  2. Always living and thin-walled
  3. Without walls
  4. Only photosynthetic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dead with thick lignified walls

Thick lignified walls provide mechanical support; mature cells are typically dead.

6. Lateral meristems primarily contribute to:

  1. Increase in girth
  2. Only root-tip elongation
  3. Only pollen formation
  4. Only water evaporation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increase in girth

Vascular and cork cambia contribute to secondary growth in suitable plants.

7. Stomata are pores regulated by:

  1. Guard cells
  2. Sieve tubes
  3. Xylem vessels only
  4. Root hairs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Guard cells

Changes in guard-cell turgor regulate stomatal opening and gas exchange.

8. A vascular bundle containing cambium is described as:

  1. Open
  2. Closed
  3. Radial only
  4. Acellular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Open

Open collateral bundles contain cambium and can support secondary vascular growth.

9. Casparian strips are associated with the root:

  1. Endodermis
  2. Pith only
  3. Epidermis only
  4. Root cap only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Endodermis

Their suberised barrier restricts apoplastic flow and promotes selective entry into the vascular cylinder.

10. Sieve-tube elements transport organic solutes and are closely associated with:

  1. Companion cells
  2. Guard cells
  3. Only vessel elements
  4. Only cork cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Companion cells

Companion cells support the function of the specialised sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem.

11. Apical meristems primarily increase plant:

  1. Only fruit sugar
  2. Length
  3. Only girth
  4. Only seed colour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Length

They generate primary growth at shoot and root tips.

12. Lateral meristems mainly contribute to increase in:

  1. Girth
  2. Only leaf length
  3. Only pollen count
  4. Only root-hair number
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Girth

Secondary growth adds tissues around stems and roots.

13. Permanent tissues arise largely through:

  1. Only fertilisation
  2. Only leaf shedding
  3. Only transpiration
  4. Differentiation of meristematic cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Differentiation of meristematic cells

Cells develop specialised structure and function.

14. Parenchyma cells are typically:

  1. Always lignified and empty
  2. Only multinucleate
  3. Living with relatively thin walls
  4. Dead with no wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Living with relatively thin walls

These versatile cells participate in storage and metabolism.

15. Collenchyma commonly provides:

  1. Only mineral absorption
  2. Flexible support in young organs
  3. Only water conduction
  4. Only reproduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Flexible support in young organs

Uneven wall thickening supports growing plant parts.

16. Sclerenchyma mature cells are commonly:

  1. Dead with thick lignified walls
  2. Always dividing
  3. Without walls
  4. Only photosynthetic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dead with thick lignified walls

Rigid walls provide mechanical strength.

17. Xylem chiefly conducts:

  1. Only sugars
  2. Only pollen
  3. Only hormones from flowers
  4. Water and dissolved minerals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Water and dissolved minerals

Its conduits transport fluid from roots through the plant.

18. Phloem chiefly transports:

  1. Only silica
  2. Only gametes
  3. Organic assimilates
  4. Only oxygen bubbles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Organic assimilates

Sugars and other organic products move between sources and sinks.

19. Tracheids and vessel elements belong to:

  1. Cork cambium exclusively
  2. Xylem
  3. Phloem
  4. Epidermis exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Xylem

They form water-conducting xylem structures.

20. Sieve-tube elements are associated with:

  1. Companion cells
  2. Guard cells only
  3. Root-cap cells only
  4. Tracheids only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Companion cells

Companion cells support their metabolic and transport functions.

21. The epidermis of an aerial organ often has a waxy:

  1. Casparian band everywhere
  2. Endodermal pericycle
  3. Middle xylem
  4. Cuticle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cuticle

The cuticle reduces uncontrolled water loss.

22. A stomatal pore is regulated by:

  1. Sieve plates
  2. Root-cap cells
  3. Guard cells
  4. Companion cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Guard cells

Changes in guard-cell turgor alter opening.

23. Root hairs are extensions of:

  1. Cork cells
  2. Epidermal cells
  3. Xylem vessels
  4. Endodermal nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Epidermal cells

They increase contact area for water and mineral uptake.

24. The root endodermis contains characteristic:

  1. Casparian strips
  2. Only chloroplast stacks
  3. Only stomatal pores
  4. Only pollen sacs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Casparian strips

These wall modifications restrict apoplastic passage.

25. The pericycle in many roots gives rise to:

  1. Only petals
  2. Only stomata
  3. Only anther pollen
  4. Lateral roots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Lateral roots

Internal pericycle cells initiate lateral-root development.

26. In a typical dicot stem, vascular bundles are arranged in a:

  1. Single central vessel
  2. Leaf-like network
  3. Ring
  4. Random pattern in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ring

The familiar ring pattern supports potential secondary growth.

27. In a typical monocot stem, vascular bundles are:

  1. Only outside epidermis
  2. Scattered
  3. Always in one cambial ring
  4. Absent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Scattered

Bundles occur throughout the ground tissue.

28. An open vascular bundle contains:

  1. Cambium between xylem and phloem
  2. No conducting tissue
  3. Only parenchyma
  4. Only dead phloem
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cambium between xylem and phloem

Cambium permits production of additional vascular tissues.

29. A closed vascular bundle lacks the usual:

  1. Xylem
  2. Phloem
  3. Bundle sheath necessarily
  4. Vascular cambium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Vascular cambium

Without intervening cambium, the bundle lacks ordinary cambial secondary growth.

30. In a dorsiventral leaf, palisade tissue is generally nearer the:

  1. Lower root cap
  2. Stem pericycle
  3. Upper epidermis
  4. Root surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Upper epidermis

Closely packed palisade cells optimise light capture.
