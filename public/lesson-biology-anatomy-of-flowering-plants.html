Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Key concepts

  • Meristematic tissues divide actively; permanent tissues are differentiated. Parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma are simple tissue types.
  • Xylem conducts water and minerals and contributes mechanical support. Phloem transports organic solutes from sources to sinks.
  • Typical dicot stems have vascular bundles arranged in a ring; typical monocot stems have scattered bundles.

Connect the ideas

An actively photosynthesising leaf can be a sugar source, while a growing root is a sink. Phloem transport follows source–sink relations.

Common mistake

Phloem transport is not always downward. Different sieve tubes can transport toward different sinks.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Collenchyma usually provides support through cells with:

Unevenly thickened living cell walls

Collenchyma commonly has uneven thickening at cell corners and supports growing regions.

2. Sclerenchyma cells at maturity are generally:

Dead with thick lignified walls

Thick lignified walls provide mechanical support; mature cells are typically dead.

3. Lateral meristems primarily contribute to:

Increase in girth

Vascular and cork cambia contribute to secondary growth in suitable plants.

4. Stomata are pores regulated by:

Guard cells

Changes in guard-cell turgor regulate stomatal opening and gas exchange.

5. A vascular bundle containing cambium is described as:

Open

Open collateral bundles contain cambium and can support secondary vascular growth.

6. Casparian strips are associated with the root:

Endodermis

Their suberised barrier restricts apoplastic flow and promotes selective entry into the vascular cylinder.

7. Sieve-tube elements transport organic solutes and are closely associated with:

Companion cells

Companion cells support the function of the specialised sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Water and mineral transport primarily occurs through:

  1. Phloem
  2. Xylem
  3. Epidermis only
  4. Pith only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Xylem

Xylem is the main water-conducting vascular tissue.

2. Actively dividing plant tissue is called:

  1. Meristematic tissue
  2. Sclerenchyma only
  3. Permanent tissue only
  4. Cork only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Meristematic tissue

Meristems contain actively dividing cells.

3. Typical monocot stem vascular bundles are:

  1. Arranged in one ring
  2. Absent
  3. Scattered in ground tissue
  4. Only in the epidermis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Scattered in ground tissue

A scattered vascular-bundle arrangement is typical of monocot stems.

4. Collenchyma usually provides support through cells with:

  1. Unevenly thickened living cell walls
  2. No cell walls
  3. Only lignified dead fibres
  4. Only sieve plates
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unevenly thickened living cell walls

Collenchyma commonly has uneven thickening at cell corners and supports growing regions.

5. Sclerenchyma cells at maturity are generally:

  1. Dead with thick lignified walls
  2. Always living and thin-walled
  3. Without walls
  4. Only photosynthetic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dead with thick lignified walls

Thick lignified walls provide mechanical support; mature cells are typically dead.

6. Lateral meristems primarily contribute to:

  1. Increase in girth
  2. Only root-tip elongation
  3. Only pollen formation
  4. Only water evaporation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Increase in girth

Vascular and cork cambia contribute to secondary growth in suitable plants.

7. Stomata are pores regulated by:

  1. Guard cells
  2. Sieve tubes
  3. Xylem vessels only
  4. Root hairs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Guard cells

Changes in guard-cell turgor regulate stomatal opening and gas exchange.

8. A vascular bundle containing cambium is described as:

  1. Open
  2. Closed
  3. Radial only
  4. Acellular
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Open

Open collateral bundles contain cambium and can support secondary vascular growth.

9. Casparian strips are associated with the root:

  1. Endodermis
  2. Pith only
  3. Epidermis only
  4. Root cap only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Endodermis

Their suberised barrier restricts apoplastic flow and promotes selective entry into the vascular cylinder.

10. Sieve-tube elements transport organic solutes and are closely associated with:

  1. Companion cells
  2. Guard cells
  3. Only vessel elements
  4. Only cork cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Companion cells

Companion cells support the function of the specialised sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem.
