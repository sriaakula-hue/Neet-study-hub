Organisms and Populations

Organisms and Populations

Key concepts

  • Population attributes include density, birth rate, death rate and age structure. These describe populations rather than single individuals.
  • Exponential growth under ideal conditions follows dN/dt = rN. Logistic growth includes carrying capacity K.
  • Interactions include mutualism (+/+), competition (−/−), predation (+/−), parasitism (+/−) and commensalism (+/0).

Connect the ideas

In logistic growth, as population size approaches K, the term (1 − N/K) decreases and growth slows.

Common mistake

Carrying capacity depends on environmental conditions and can change over time.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. In exponential growth, dN/dt = rN, r represents the:

Per-capita growth rate

The model’s total growth scales with population size multiplied by its per-capita rate.

2. In logistic growth, growth slows near K because:

Resource limitation reduces the growth term

The factor (1 − N/K) approaches zero as N approaches carrying capacity.

3. Competition is represented by:

−/−

Both populations can experience reduced performance from competing for limiting resources.

4. Commensalism is represented by:

+/0

One partner benefits while the other is not appreciably affected in the stated interaction.

5. Parasitism generally involves the parasite benefiting while the host is:

Harmed

Parasitic effects reduce host performance; immediate host death is not required.

6. Age structure can influence a population’s future:

Growth pattern

Relative numbers of prereproductive, reproductive and postreproductive individuals affect demographic prospects.

7. An adaptation is best understood as a trait that improves performance under:

A particular environmental context

A trait’s benefit depends on conditions and trade-offs; it is not universally optimal.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Both partners benefit in:

  1. Mutualism
  2. Competition
  3. Predation
  4. Parasitism
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mutualism

Mutualism is represented as +/+.

2. The symbol K in logistic population growth represents:

  1. Carrying capacity
  2. Mutation rate
  3. Body temperature
  4. Food-chain length
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carrying capacity

K is the carrying capacity under the specified conditions.

3. An attribute of a population rather than a single individual is:

  1. Age distribution
  2. Body weight
  3. Eye colour
  4. Blood type
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Age distribution

Age distribution describes the relative numbers in different age groups.

4. In exponential growth, dN/dt = rN, r represents the:

  1. Per-capita growth rate
  2. Carrying capacity
  3. Body mass
  4. Trophic level
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Per-capita growth rate

The model’s total growth scales with population size multiplied by its per-capita rate.

5. In logistic growth, growth slows near K because:

  1. Resource limitation reduces the growth term
  2. Every individual instantly dies
  3. Mutation becomes zero always
  4. Temperature must be zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Resource limitation reduces the growth term

The factor (1 − N/K) approaches zero as N approaches carrying capacity.

6. Competition is represented by:

  1. −/−
  2. +/+
  3. +/0
  4. −/0 only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. −/−

Both populations can experience reduced performance from competing for limiting resources.

7. Commensalism is represented by:

  1. +/0
  2. +/+
  3. −/−
  4. +/−
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +/0

One partner benefits while the other is not appreciably affected in the stated interaction.

8. Parasitism generally involves the parasite benefiting while the host is:

  1. Harmed
  2. Equally benefited
  3. Always unaffected
  4. Always killed immediately
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Harmed

Parasitic effects reduce host performance; immediate host death is not required.

9. Age structure can influence a population’s future:

  1. Growth pattern
  2. Atomic number
  3. Genetic code universally
  4. Only skin colour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Growth pattern

Relative numbers of prereproductive, reproductive and postreproductive individuals affect demographic prospects.

10. An adaptation is best understood as a trait that improves performance under:

  1. A particular environmental context
  2. Every possible environment equally
  3. Only artificial lighting
  4. No selection pressure ever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A particular environmental context

A trait’s benefit depends on conditions and trade-offs; it is not universally optimal.

11. An organism's habitat is its usual:

  1. Only its food preference
  2. Only its chromosome number
  3. Only its evolutionary date
  4. Place of living
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Place of living

Habitat describes the environment occupied.

12. An ecological niche includes an organism's:

  1. Only body colour
  2. Only atomic mass
  3. Role and resource use
  4. Only its address
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Role and resource use

The niche captures functional relationships and requirements.

13. A population consists of individuals of the same species in a defined:

  1. Only laboratory bottle always
  2. Area and time
  3. Entire universe regardless of time
  4. Only one family always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Area and time

Spatial and temporal context defines the sampled population.

14. Population density can be expressed as individuals per unit:

  1. Area or volume
  2. Only temperature
  3. Only chromosome
  4. Only wavelength
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Area or volume

Appropriate measurement depends on the organism and habitat.

15. Natality adds individuals through:

  1. Deaths
  2. Emigration
  3. Only predation
  4. Births
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Births

Birth contributions increase population numbers.

16. Mortality removes individuals through:

  1. Immigration
  2. Only reproduction
  3. Deaths
  4. Births
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Deaths

Death contributions reduce population size.

17. Immigration refers to movement:

  1. Only between genes
  2. Into a population
  3. Out of a population
  4. Only within one cell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Into a population

Arriving individuals increase the local population.

18. Emigration refers to movement:

  1. Out of a population
  2. Into a population
  3. Only between organelles
  4. Only during pollination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Out of a population

Leaving individuals reduce the local population.

19. Exponential growth assumes an idealised absence of limiting:

  1. DNA in every organism
  2. All reproduction
  3. All births
  4. Resources and constraints
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Resources and constraints

A constant per-capita rate produces unconstrained mathematical growth.

20. Logistic growth incorporates a carrying capacity denoted:

  1. Only q
  2. Only RQ
  3. K
  4. Only p
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. K

K represents the model's supported population level.

21. For logistic growth, increase is greatest near:

  1. N=2K
  2. N=K/2
  3. N=0 only
  4. N=K only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. N=K/2

The product N(1−N/K) peaks at half carrying capacity for positive r.

22. A population age pyramid displays relative numbers in:

  1. Age classes
  2. Only blood groups
  3. Only genotypes
  4. Only feeding levels
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Age classes

Age structure informs growth interpretation.

23. Mutualism benefits:

  1. One while harming the other
  2. Neither species
  3. Only the predator
  4. Both participating species
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Both participating species

Both partners receive a favourable effect.

24. Commensalism benefits one participant while the other is:

  1. Always strongly helped
  2. Always sterilised
  3. Largely unaffected
  4. Always killed
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Largely unaffected

The textbook interaction is represented as positive/neutral.

25. Parasitism benefits the parasite while:

  1. Removing every parasite's nutrition
  2. Harming the host
  3. Always benefiting the host
  4. Leaving every host unaffected
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Harming the host

The host supplies resources at a cost.

26. Competition commonly reduces access to shared:

  1. Limiting resources
  2. Only unlimited sunlight in every context
  3. Only chromosomes
  4. Only inherited codons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Limiting resources

Resource overlap can lower performance of competitors.

27. Predation involves one organism:

  1. Only sharing shelter
  2. Only fixing nitrogen
  3. Only carrying pollen
  4. Consuming another
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Consuming another

The consumer benefits while the prey is harmed.

28. A herbivore-resistant plant trait can include:

  1. Only chromosome duplication
  2. Only more oxygen
  3. Chemical defence
  4. Only photosynthetic pigments in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Chemical defence

Defensive compounds can deter or impair herbivores.

29. An ectotherm's body temperature depends strongly on:

  1. Only altitude name
  2. External thermal conditions
  3. Only constant internal heat regulation
  4. Only blood type
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. External thermal conditions

Behaviour and environment substantially influence temperature.

30. A diapause is a regulated period of:

  1. Developmental arrest
  2. Unlimited growth
  3. Continuous fertilisation
  4. Only food digestion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Developmental arrest

It can help organisms survive predictable adverse conditions.
