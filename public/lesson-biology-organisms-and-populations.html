Organisms and Populations

Organisms and Populations

Key concepts

  • Population attributes include density, birth rate, death rate and age structure. These describe populations rather than single individuals.
  • Exponential growth under ideal conditions follows dN/dt = rN. Logistic growth includes carrying capacity K.
  • Interactions include mutualism (+/+), competition (−/−), predation (+/−), parasitism (+/−) and commensalism (+/0).

Connect the ideas

In logistic growth, as population size approaches K, the term (1 − N/K) decreases and growth slows.

Common mistake

Carrying capacity depends on environmental conditions and can change over time.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. In exponential growth, dN/dt = rN, r represents the:

Per-capita growth rate

The model’s total growth scales with population size multiplied by its per-capita rate.

2. In logistic growth, growth slows near K because:

Resource limitation reduces the growth term

The factor (1 − N/K) approaches zero as N approaches carrying capacity.

3. Competition is represented by:

−/−

Both populations can experience reduced performance from competing for limiting resources.

4. Commensalism is represented by:

+/0

One partner benefits while the other is not appreciably affected in the stated interaction.

5. Parasitism generally involves the parasite benefiting while the host is:

Harmed

Parasitic effects reduce host performance; immediate host death is not required.

6. Age structure can influence a population’s future:

Growth pattern

Relative numbers of prereproductive, reproductive and postreproductive individuals affect demographic prospects.

7. An adaptation is best understood as a trait that improves performance under:

A particular environmental context

A trait’s benefit depends on conditions and trade-offs; it is not universally optimal.

  2. Mutation rate
  3. Body temperature
  4. Food-chain length

K is the carrying capacity under the specified conditions.

  1. Age distribution
  2. Body weight

Answer: A. Age distribution

Age distribution describes the relative numbers in different age groups.

4. In exponential growth, dN/dt = rN, r represents the:

  1. Per-capita growth rate
  2. Carrying capacity
  3. Body mass
  4. Trophic level

Answer: A. Per-capita growth rate

The model’s total growth scales with population size multiplied by its per-capita rate.

5. In logistic growth, growth slows near K because:

  1. Resource limitation reduces the growth term
  2. Every individual instantly dies
  3. Mutation becomes zero always

Answer: A. Resource limitation reduces the growth term

The factor (1 − N/K) approaches zero as N approaches carrying capacity.

Both populations can experience reduced performance from competing for limiting resources.
