d- and f-Block Elements

d- and f-Block Elements

Key concepts

  • Transition elements have an incomplete d subshell in the atom or in at least one of their cations.
  • Variable oxidation states, complex formation and magnetic behaviour are common features. Many, but not all, transition-metal ions are coloured.
  • Lanthanoid contraction is the gradual decrease in lanthanoid ionic sizes, associated with imperfect shielding by 4f electrons.

Worked connection

Cu²⁺ has an incomplete d subshell. Zn and Zn²⁺ have filled d subshells, so zinc is generally not classified as a transition element by this definition.

Watch out

Being in the d block is not identical to satisfying the transition-element definition. Colour also depends on the species and environment.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Variable oxidation states of transition metals relate partly to:

Similar energies of ns and (n−1)d electrons

Both outer s and nearby d electrons can participate in bonding or ion formation.

2. A d⁰ ion has how many d electrons?

Zero

The superscript gives the number of electrons occupying d orbitals in the stated species.

3. A d¹⁰ ion has a d subshell that is:

Completely filled

Five d orbitals each hold two electrons when the subshell is full.

4. Transition-metal complex colour can arise from:

Ligands split metal orbital energies; absorption of visible light can promote electrons, while charge-transfer transitions can also contribute.

5. The first-row transition series mainly involves filling:

3d orbitals

The first d series is associated with 3d filling alongside 4s occupancy.

6. A property commonly associated with many transition elements is:

Catalytic activity

Accessible oxidation states and surface interactions can provide catalytic reaction pathways.

7. Lanthanoids mainly involve progressive filling of:

4f orbitals

The series is associated with 4f filling, with electronic-configuration exceptions in individual elements.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Which is generally excluded as a transition element under the incomplete-d definition?

  1. Iron
  2. Copper
  3. Zinc
  4. Chromium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zinc

Zn and its common Zn²⁺ ion have filled d subshells.

2. Paramagnetism is associated with:

  1. Unpaired electrons
  2. Only paired electrons
  3. No electrons
  4. Only neutrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unpaired electrons

Unpaired electrons produce a net magnetic moment.

3. Lanthanoid contraction is primarily associated with poor shielding by:

  1. 4f electrons
  2. 1s electrons only
  3. Protons
  4. Neutrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4f electrons

Imperfect 4f shielding contributes to increasing effective nuclear attraction.

4. Variable oxidation states of transition metals relate partly to:

  1. Similar energies of ns and (n−1)d electrons
  2. Absence of electrons
  3. Only neutron changes
  4. Always filled p subshells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Similar energies of ns and (n−1)d electrons

Both outer s and nearby d electrons can participate in bonding or ion formation.

5. A d⁰ ion has how many d electrons?

  1. Zero
  2. One
  3. Five
  4. Ten
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zero

The superscript gives the number of electrons occupying d orbitals in the stated species.

6. A d¹⁰ ion has a d subshell that is:

  1. Completely filled
  2. Half-filled
  3. Empty
  4. Always with five unpaired electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Completely filled

Five d orbitals each hold two electrons when the subshell is full.

7. Transition-metal complex colour can arise from:

  1. Electronic transitions between split energy levels
  2. Only nuclear decay
  3. Only sound absorption
  4. Only mass loss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electronic transitions between split energy levels

Ligands split metal orbital energies; absorption of visible light can promote electrons, while charge-transfer transitions can also contribute.

8. The first-row transition series mainly involves filling:

  1. 3d orbitals
  2. 2p orbitals
  3. 4f orbitals
  4. 1s orbitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3d orbitals

The first d series is associated with 3d filling alongside 4s occupancy.

9. A property commonly associated with many transition elements is:

  1. Catalytic activity
  2. No complex formation
  3. Only one oxidation state in every compound
  4. No metallic behaviour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Catalytic activity

Accessible oxidation states and surface interactions can provide catalytic reaction pathways.

10. Lanthanoids mainly involve progressive filling of:

  1. 4f orbitals
  2. 2s orbitals
  3. 3p orbitals
  4. 5d only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4f orbitals

The series is associated with 4f filling, with electronic-configuration exceptions in individual elements.

11. Transition elements typically have an incompletely filled d subshell in:

  1. Only their anions
  2. Their atoms or common ions
  3. Only their nuclei
  4. Every noble-gas shell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Their atoms or common ions

The definition concerns incomplete d occupancy in the atom or a relevant cation.

12. Zinc is often excluded from transition elements because Zn and Zn²⁺ have:

  1. Filled d subshells
  2. Half-filled f subshells
  3. No electrons
  4. Incomplete p shells only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Filled d subshells

Both the atom and usual Zn²⁺ ion have d¹⁰ configurations.

13. Variable oxidation states of transition metals reflect involvement of:

  1. Only 1s electrons
  2. Only neutrons
  3. Only the outermost p⁶ shell
  4. ns and nearby (n−1)d electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. ns and nearby (n−1)d electrons

Similar energies allow different numbers of s and d electrons to participate.

14. The usual electronic configuration of chromium is:

  1. [Ar]3d⁶
  2. [Ne]3s²
  3. [Ar]3d⁵4s¹
  4. [Ar]3d⁴4s²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. [Ar]3d⁵4s¹

The half-filled d arrangement gives the familiar chromium ground-state exception.

15. The usual electronic configuration of copper is:

  1. [Ne]3p⁶
  2. [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹
  3. [Ar]3d⁹4s²
  4. [Ar]3d⁸4s³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹

Copper has the familiar filled-d ground-state configuration.

16. When a first-row transition atom forms its common cation, electrons are usually removed first from:

  1. 4s
  2. 3d in every case
  3. 2p
  4. 1s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4s

The occupied 4s electrons are generally lost before 3d electrons during ion formation.

17. The d-electron count of Fe²⁺ is:

  1. d⁴
  2. d⁵
  3. d⁸
  4. d⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. d⁶

Neutral Fe is [Ar]3d⁶4s²; removing two 4s electrons leaves d⁶.

18. The d-electron count of Fe³⁺ is:

  1. d⁷
  2. d³
  3. d⁵
  4. d⁶
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. d⁵

After losing the two 4s electrons and one 3d electron, Fe³⁺ has d⁵.

19. The d-electron count of Cu²⁺ is:

  1. d⁵
  2. d⁹
  3. d¹⁰
  4. d⁸
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. d⁹

Copper loses its 4s electron and one 3d electron to form Cu²⁺.

20. Many transition-metal ions are coloured because they can undergo:

  1. Electronic transitions between split d levels
  2. Nuclear fission
  3. Loss of all protons
  4. Only infrared lattice translation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electronic transitions between split d levels

Ligand fields split d orbitals; some transitions absorb visible wavelengths.

21. An ion with all electrons paired is:

  1. Paramagnetic necessarily
  2. Ferromagnetic necessarily
  3. Radioactive necessarily
  4. Diamagnetic
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Diamagnetic

Paired electron moments cancel, giving the elementary diamagnetic behaviour.

22. An ion with unpaired electrons is ordinarily:

  1. Electrically neutral necessarily
  2. Unable to form complexes
  3. Paramagnetic
  4. Diamagnetic necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Paramagnetic

Unpaired spins provide a net magnetic response in the elementary model.

23. The spin-only moment for one unpaired electron is approximately:

  1. 5.92 μB
  2. 1.73 μB
  3. 0 μB
  4. 2.83 μB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1.73 μB

The spin-only formula √[n(n+2)] gives √3 for n=1.

24. For five unpaired electrons, the spin-only moment is approximately:

  1. 5.92 μB
  2. 1.73 μB
  3. 2.83 μB
  4. 0 μB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 5.92 μB

The formula √[5(5+2)] gives √35≈5.92.

25. Lanthanoid contraction mainly arises from poor shielding by:

  1. 1s protons
  2. Valence neutrons
  3. Free water molecules
  4. 4f electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 4f electrons

As nuclear charge rises, ineffective 4f shielding causes a gradual size decrease.

26. The common oxidation state of many lanthanoids is:

  1. +7
  2. 0 only
  3. +3
  4. −3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. +3

Loss of outer electrons commonly produces the +3 oxidation state.

27. Compared with lanthanoids, actinoids often exhibit:

  1. No complex formation
  2. A wider range of oxidation states
  3. No radioactive isotopes
  4. Only completely empty f orbitals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A wider range of oxidation states

The 5f, 6d and 7s energies allow more varied electron participation.

28. The oxidation state of manganese in MnO₄⁻ is:

  1. +7
  2. +2
  3. +4
  4. +6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. +7

Four oxygens contribute −8; net charge −1 requires Mn=+7.

29. The oxidation state of chromium in Cr₂O₇²⁻ is:

  1. +3
  2. +2
  3. +7
  4. +6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. +6

Seven oxygens contribute −14; two Cr atoms total +12, so each is +6.

30. Many transition metals are useful catalysts because they provide:

  1. Only increased equilibrium constants
  2. No interaction with reactants
  3. Alternative pathways through variable oxidation states and adsorption
  4. Only unchanged activation energies
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alternative pathways through variable oxidation states and adsorption

Variable electron states and surface interactions can lower activation barriers without changing equilibrium.
