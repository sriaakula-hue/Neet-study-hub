Plant Growth and Development

Plant Growth and Development

Key concepts

  • Growth is an irreversible increase in size or mass. Differentiation gives cells specialised structures and functions.
  • Auxins can promote cell elongation; gibberellins can promote stem elongation; cytokinins promote cell division.
  • Ethylene is a gaseous regulator associated with fruit ripening. Abscisic acid contributes to seed dormancy and stress responses, including stomatal closure.

Connect the ideas

A plant under water stress can increase ABA signalling, reducing water loss by closing stomata.

Common mistake

The effect of a growth regulator depends on concentration, tissue and context; one hormone can have several roles.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The gaseous plant growth regulator is:

  1. Auxin
  2. Cytokinin
  3. Ethylene
  4. Abscisic acid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ethylene

Ethylene is a gaseous signalling molecule.

2. Which regulator is associated with stomatal closure under water stress?

  1. Abscisic acid
  2. Gibberellin only
  3. Ethylene only
  4. Cytokinin only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Abscisic acid

ABA promotes stomatal closure in water-stress responses.

3. Cytokinins are especially associated with:

  1. Cell division
  2. Only water conduction
  3. Only seed coat formation
  4. Electron transport
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell division

Cytokinins promote cell division.

4. Dedifferentiation involves mature cells regaining the capacity to:

  1. Divide
  2. Lose all DNA
  3. Become viruses
  4. Produce only oxygen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Divide

Some differentiated plant cells can regain division capacity under appropriate conditions.

5. Redifferentiation means newly dividing cells subsequently become:

  1. Specialised again
  2. Acellular
  3. Only gametes
  4. Only bacteria
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Specialised again

Cells produced by dedifferentiated tissues can acquire differentiated structure and function.

6. A sigmoid growth curve typically has an early slow phase followed by:

  1. Rapid growth and then slowing
  2. Only constant shrinkage
  3. No growth
  4. Immediate maximum size
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Rapid growth and then slowing

Many biological growth processes show lag, rapid growth and a slowing or plateau phase.

7. Photoperiodism concerns response to:

  1. Relative durations of light and dark
  2. Only soil pH
  3. Only wind speed
  4. Only gravity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Relative durations of light and dark

Flowering in many species depends on suitable day/night length patterns.

8. Vernalisation refers to promotion of flowering through suitable:

  1. Low-temperature exposure
  2. Only high salt
  3. Only darkness for one minute
  4. No environmental cue
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Low-temperature exposure

Certain plants require or respond to a period of cold before flowering.

9. Auxin is closely involved in:

  1. Apical dominance
  2. Only fungal spore formation
  3. Only nitrogen fixation
  4. Only blood clotting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Apical dominance

Auxin-related signalling from the shoot apex can suppress lateral-bud growth; interactions with other regulators also matter.

10. Gibberellins can promote:

  1. Stem elongation
  2. Only stomatal closure in every case
  3. Only seed dormancy
  4. Only membrane destruction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Stem elongation

Gibberellins have roles including elongation and germination processes, depending on tissue and conditions.

11. A growth curve limited by resources commonly appears:

  1. Sigmoid
  2. Perfectly linear forever
  3. Always circular
  4. Instantly flat from birth
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sigmoid

A lag, rapid phase and slowing phase create an S-shaped course.

12. A plant meristem supports growth through repeated:

  1. Only fertilisation
  2. Only leaf death
  3. Only evaporation
  4. Cell division
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cell division

Meristematic cells supply new tissue cells.

13. Differentiation involves acquisition of:

  1. Only more water
  2. Only new isotopes
  3. Specialised structure and function
  4. Only extra chromosomes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Specialised structure and function

Developing cells become adapted to particular roles.

14. Dedifferentiation allows suitably living specialised cells to regain:

  1. Only lignin
  2. Division capacity
  3. Only a lost nucleus in all cases
  4. Only pollen identity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Division capacity

Some plant cells can return to meristem-like activity.

15. Auxin is classically associated with promoting:

  1. Cell elongation in shoots
  2. Only stomatal closure
  3. Only seed dormancy
  4. Only protein destruction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell elongation in shoots

Its effects include elongation and developmental regulation.

16. Apical dominance is strongly associated with:

  1. Only cytokinin destruction of roots
  2. Only ethylene in all situations
  3. No hormonal influence
  4. Auxin-related inhibition of lateral buds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Auxin-related inhibition of lateral buds

The shoot apex influences lateral-bud growth through hormonal regulation.

17. Removal of a shoot tip can promote lateral growth by reducing:

  1. Root formation permanently
  2. All cytokinin activity
  3. Apical dominance
  4. All photosynthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Apical dominance

Reduced apical inhibition permits lateral buds to develop.

18. In germinating cereal grains, gibberellin can stimulate synthesis of:

  1. Only keratin
  2. Alpha-amylase
  3. Only haemoglobin
  4. Only insulin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Alpha-amylase

The enzyme mobilises starch reserves to support seedling growth.

19. Bolting in some rosette plants is associated with:

  1. Gibberellin action
  2. Only abscisic acid action
  3. Only ethylene inhibition
  4. Only mineral precipitation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gibberellin action

Rapid internode elongation precedes flowering in the familiar response.

20. In many tissue-culture systems, a high cytokinin-to-auxin ratio favours:

  1. Only root formation
  2. Only seed dormancy
  3. Only cell death
  4. Shoot formation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Shoot formation

The relative hormone balance helps direct organ formation in suitable plant cultures.

21. Cytokinins can delay leaf:

  1. Only chloroplast division
  2. Only water absorption
  3. Senescence
  4. Initial fertilisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Senescence

They help maintain metabolic activity in ageing tissues.

22. Ethylene is a plant hormone that is:

  1. A metal ion
  2. Gaseous
  3. A rigid cell-wall polymer
  4. A protein enzyme
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gaseous

The small gaseous molecule diffuses and signals developmental responses.

23. Ethylene is important in ripening of many:

  1. Climacteric fruits
  2. Only dry grains
  3. Only gymnosperm cones
  4. Only algae
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Climacteric fruits

Ethylene accompanies enhanced respiration and ripening in these fruits.

24. Abscisic acid is strongly associated with:

  1. Only continuous growth promotion
  2. Only pollen-tube nutrition
  3. Only nitrogen fixation
  4. Stress responses and dormancy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Stress responses and dormancy

ABA regulates dormancy and water-stress responses.

25. Water stress can trigger ABA-associated closure of:

  1. Only xylem pits
  2. Only pollen grains
  3. Stomata
  4. All roots permanently
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Stomata

Guard-cell responses reduce further water loss.

26. Photoperiodism concerns a response to the duration of:

  1. Only atmospheric oxygen
  2. Light and darkness
  3. Only soil pressure
  4. Only leaf width
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Light and darkness

Some developmental processes depend on day/night timing.

27. The photoperiodic stimulus is commonly perceived by:

  1. Leaves
  2. Only pollen grains
  3. Only root caps
  4. Only dead bark
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Leaves

Leaves perceive timing and generate signals affecting flowering.

28. Vernalisation refers to promotion of flowering by suitable:

  1. Continuous ultraviolet injury
  2. Only high salinity
  3. Only starvation
  4. Low-temperature exposure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Low-temperature exposure

Cold treatment can satisfy a developmental requirement.

29. Seed germination generally requires an appropriate combination of water, oxygen and:

  1. Only darkness in every species
  2. Only pollen
  3. Temperature
  4. Only pure nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Temperature

Metabolic reactivation depends on suitable environmental conditions.

30. Plant hormone effects depend importantly on concentration and:

  1. Only seed diameter
  2. Tissue and developmental context
  3. Only isotope mass
  4. Only flower colour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tissue and developmental context

The same hormone can produce different responses in different settings.
