Microbes in Human Welfare

Microbes in Human Welfare

Key concepts

  • Microbes support food processing, industrial production, sewage treatment and agriculture.
  • Saccharomyces cerevisiae is used in fermentation. Lactic acid bacteria help convert milk into curd.
  • Methanogenic microorganisms contribute to biogas production. Biological sewage treatment reduces organic load through microbial activity.

Connect the ideas

In fermentation, yeast can convert sugars into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Different processes use different microorganisms.

Common mistake

Not all microbes are pathogens; beneficial uses depend on choosing suitable organisms and conditions.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A yeast commonly used in alcoholic fermentation is:

  1. Saccharomyces cerevisiae
  2. Plasmodium vivax
  3. Anopheles
  4. Homo sapiens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Saccharomyces cerevisiae

S. cerevisiae is widely used for fermentation.

2. Curd production is associated with:

  1. Lactic acid bacteria
  2. Only viruses
  3. Mosquitoes
  4. Only protozoa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lactic acid bacteria

These bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid.

3. A major combustible component of biogas is:

  1. Oxygen
  2. Methane
  3. Nitrogen only
  4. Sulphur dioxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Methane

Methanogens produce methane, which provides biogas fuel value.

4. Biological sewage treatment commonly uses microbial:

  1. Flocs and metabolic activity
  2. Only sterile filters
  3. Only sunlight
  4. Only chemical dyes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Flocs and metabolic activity

Microbial aggregates degrade organic matter, reducing oxygen demand under suitable treatment conditions.

5. BOD is a measure related to oxygen required for:

  1. Biological decomposition of organic matter
  2. Only chemical colouring
  3. Only salt dissolution
  4. Only water evaporation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Biological decomposition of organic matter

Higher biochemical oxygen demand commonly indicates a greater biodegradable organic load.

6. Biogas methanogens belong chiefly to:

  1. Archaea
  2. Angiosperms
  3. Vertebrates
  4. Viruses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Archaea

Methanogenesis is characteristic of certain archaeal microorganisms in anaerobic settings.

7. Rhizobium can benefit leguminous plants by:

  1. Symbiotic nitrogen fixation
  2. Producing every required vitamin
  3. Making seeds directly
  4. Eliminating all pathogens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Symbiotic nitrogen fixation

Root-nodule associations convert atmospheric nitrogen into forms usable within the symbiosis.

8. A biofertiliser uses organisms that improve:

  1. Nutrient availability or uptake
  2. Only food colour
  3. Only packaging
  4. Only genetic identity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nutrient availability or uptake

Examples include nitrogen-fixing bacteria and beneficial mycorrhizal associations.

9. Mycorrhiza describes an association between fungi and plant:

  1. Roots
  2. Only petals
  3. Only fruits
  4. Only pollen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Roots

The partnership can improve mineral and water uptake while fungi receive organic products.

10. Penicillin was originally associated with a:

  1. Fungus
  2. Flowering plant
  3. Virus
  4. Mosquito
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fungus

Penicillium fungi produce penicillin-related compounds; antibiotics are distinct from vaccines.

11. Lactic acid bacteria help convert milk into:

  1. Only bread
  2. Only alcohol
  3. Only vinegar
  4. Curd
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Curd

Acid production changes milk proteins and texture.

12. The bacterium commonly associated with yoghurt or curd fermentation belongs to:

  1. Ascaris
  2. Pinus
  3. Lactobacillus-type LAB
  4. Plasmodium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Lactobacillus-type LAB

Lactic acid bacteria ferment milk sugars.

13. Baker's yeast is commonly:

  1. Nostoc
  2. Saccharomyces cerevisiae
  3. Penicillium notatum only
  4. Rhizobium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Saccharomyces cerevisiae

This yeast produces fermentation products useful in baking.

14. Bread dough rises largely due to microbial production of:

  1. CO₂
  2. Only N₂
  3. Only O₂
  4. Only NH₃
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CO₂

Gas becomes trapped in the dough structure.

15. Alcoholic fermentation by yeast produces ethanol and:

  1. Only oxygen
  2. Only urea
  3. Only calcium
  4. CO₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CO₂

Carbon dioxide accompanies conversion of sugars to ethanol.

16. Large fermentation vessels used industrially are called:

  1. Alveoli
  2. Spindle poles
  3. Fermenters
  4. Nephrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Fermenters

Controlled culture conditions support microbial production.

17. Cyclosporin A is classically obtained using the fungus:

  1. Rhizobium
  2. Trichoderma polysporum
  3. Aspergillus niger
  4. Saccharomyces cerevisiae
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Trichoderma polysporum

This microbial product is used for its immunosuppressive activity.

18. The major acid used in vinegar is:

  1. Acetic acid
  2. Citric acid only
  3. Lactic acid only
  4. Uric acid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Acetic acid

Acetic acid bacteria can oxidise ethanol during vinegar production.

19. Citric acid production is classically associated with:

  1. Anopheles
  2. Ascaris
  3. Euglena only
  4. Aspergillus niger
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Aspergillus niger

Industrial cultures of this fungus can produce citric acid.

20. Microbial lipases act on:

  1. Only cellulose
  2. Only proteins
  3. Lipids
  4. Only DNA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Lipids

Lipases catalyse reactions involving fat or ester substrates.

21. Microbial proteases act on:

  1. Only triglycerides
  2. Proteins
  3. Only starch
  4. Only DNA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Proteins

Proteases cleave peptide bonds.

22. Sewage treatment's primary stage chiefly uses:

  1. Physical separation
  2. Only DNA cloning
  3. Only vaccines
  4. Only photosynthesis
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Physical separation

Filtration and sedimentation remove larger or settleable material.

23. Secondary sewage treatment relies chiefly on:

  1. Only mineral counting
  2. Only chloroplast isolation
  3. Only chromatography
  4. Microbial biological processing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Microbial biological processing

Microorganisms consume and transform organic matter.

24. BOD measures oxygen required for microbial breakdown of:

  1. Only glass particles
  2. Only sodium chloride
  3. Biodegradable organic matter
  4. Only dissolved nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Biodegradable organic matter

Higher biodegradable load generally raises biochemical oxygen demand.

25. Activated-sludge flocs include bacteria associated with:

  1. Only bones
  2. Fungal filaments and aggregated material
  3. Only red cells
  4. Only pollen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fungal filaments and aggregated material

Microbial aggregates support organic-waste treatment.

26. Anaerobic sludge digesters can produce methane through:

  1. Methanogenic microorganisms
  2. Only nitrating plants
  3. Only viruses
  4. Only erythrocytes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Methanogenic microorganisms

Anaerobic microbial metabolism yields methane-rich gas.

27. Biogas contains a substantial proportion of:

  1. Only oxygen
  2. Only helium
  3. Only chlorine
  4. Methane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Methane

Methane is the principal combustible component.

28. Rhizobium benefits legume plants through:

  1. Only photosynthetic oxygen release
  2. Only starch digestion
  3. Symbiotic nitrogen fixation
  4. Only fruit pollination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Symbiotic nitrogen fixation

Root-nodule bacteria provide biologically available nitrogen.

29. Mycorrhiza is an association between a plant's roots and:

  1. Only fish
  2. Fungi
  3. Only viruses
  4. Only insects
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Fungi

The partners exchange resources and often improve mineral uptake.

30. Bacillus thuringiensis is used in biological control because it produces:

  1. Insect-active proteins
  2. Only human antibodies
  3. Only lignin
  4. Only plant hormones
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Insect-active proteins

Certain Bt proteins act against susceptible insect groups.
