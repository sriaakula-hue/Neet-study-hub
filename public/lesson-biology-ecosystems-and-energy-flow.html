Ecosystems and energy flow

Ecosystems and energy flow

Core idea

Producers capture energy and support consumers. Energy flows through trophic levels and is dissipated as heat. Nutrients cycle, but energy is not recycled through ecosystems in the same way.

Worked connection

Using the approximate 10% transfer rule, 10,000 J at a producer level supports about 1,000 J at the primary-consumer level and 100 J at the secondary-consumer level.

Watch out

The 10% rule is a teaching approximation, not a universal exact value. Pyramids of energy are always upright when expressed per unit area per unit time.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Gross primary productivity is the total rate of organic production by:

Producers before subtracting respiration

GPP includes the total photosynthetic fixation before producer respiratory losses are deducted.

2. Net primary productivity is:

GPP minus producer respiration

NPP is the rate available for producer growth and transfer into other trophic pathways.

3. Detritus includes:

Dead organic material

Dead remains and waste support detrital food webs and decomposition.

4. Decomposers contribute to:

Recycling nutrients from organic remains

They break down organic material and release inorganic nutrients, with energy losses during metabolism.

5. An inverted biomass pyramid can occur in:

Some aquatic ecosystems

Rapid producer turnover can support a larger standing consumer biomass despite a small instantaneous phytoplankton biomass.

6. Using the approximate 10% rule, 5,000 J at producers supports primary consumers with about:

500 J

Ten percent of 5,000 J is 500 J. The rule is an approximation, not an exact constant in every ecosystem.

7. A food web differs from a single food chain because it includes:

Multiple interconnected feeding pathways

Organisms can have multiple food sources and consumers, creating interconnected routes of energy transfer.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Using the approximate 10% rule, 1,000 J at producers gives primary consumers about:

  1. 10 J
  2. 100 J
  3. 1,000 J
  4. 10,000 J
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 100 J

About 10% of 1,000 J is 100 J.

2. Which ecological pyramid is always upright?

  1. Numbers
  2. Biomass
  3. Energy
  4. All pyramids
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Energy

Energy decreases between successive trophic levels.

3. Which statement is correct?

  1. Energy and nutrients both cycle identically
  2. Energy flows; nutrients cycle
  3. Nutrients never cycle
  4. All energy reaches top consumers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Energy flows; nutrients cycle

Energy dissipates as heat while materials are recycled through biogeochemical processes.

4. Gross primary productivity is the total rate of organic production by:

  1. Producers before subtracting respiration
  2. Only herbivores
  3. Only decomposers
  4. Only carnivores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Producers before subtracting respiration

GPP includes the total photosynthetic fixation before producer respiratory losses are deducted.

5. Net primary productivity is:

  1. GPP minus producer respiration
  2. GPP plus respiration
  3. Only sunlight intensity
  4. Always zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. GPP minus producer respiration

NPP is the rate available for producer growth and transfer into other trophic pathways.

6. Detritus includes:

  1. Dead organic material
  2. Only living predators
  3. Only sunlight
  4. Only dissolved oxygen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dead organic material

Dead remains and waste support detrital food webs and decomposition.

7. Decomposers contribute to:

  1. Recycling nutrients from organic remains
  2. Creation of unlimited energy
  3. Stopping every food web
  4. Only oxygen consumption without products
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Recycling nutrients from organic remains

They break down organic material and release inorganic nutrients, with energy losses during metabolism.

8. An inverted biomass pyramid can occur in:

  1. Some aquatic ecosystems
  2. Every terrestrial forest
  3. Every energy pyramid
  4. No ecosystem ever
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Some aquatic ecosystems

Rapid producer turnover can support a larger standing consumer biomass despite a small instantaneous phytoplankton biomass.

9. Using the approximate 10% rule, 5,000 J at producers supports primary consumers with about:

  1. 50,000 J
  2. 500 J
  3. 50 J
  4. 5,000 J
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 500 J

Ten percent of 5,000 J is 500 J. The rule is an approximation, not an exact constant in every ecosystem.

10. A food web differs from a single food chain because it includes:

  1. Multiple interconnected feeding pathways
  2. Only one species
  3. No producers
  4. No energy flow
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Multiple interconnected feeding pathways

Organisms can have multiple food sources and consumers, creating interconnected routes of energy transfer.

11. The ultimate energy input for most ecosystems is:

  1. Soil mass alone
  2. Moonlight exclusively
  3. Sunlight
  4. Nitrogen gas alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Sunlight

Photosynthetic producers capture solar energy for food webs.

12. Green plants usually occupy the:

  1. Highest predator level
  2. First trophic level
  3. Second trophic level
  4. Third trophic level
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. First trophic level

Primary producers introduce fixed organic energy into the food chain.

13. Herbivores ordinarily occupy the:

  1. Second trophic level
  2. First trophic level
  3. Only fourth trophic level
  4. No trophic level
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Second trophic level

Primary consumers feed directly on producers.

14. A carnivore feeding on herbivores is commonly a:

  1. Primary producer
  2. Primary consumer
  3. Decomposer only
  4. Secondary consumer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Secondary consumer

It consumes organisms from the second trophic level.

15. Net primary productivity equals gross primary productivity minus:

  1. Only rainfall
  2. All sunlight
  3. Producer respiration
  4. Consumer immigration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Producer respiration

Producer respiratory losses reduce the energy retained as new biomass.

16. Energy transfer through a typical food chain is:

  1. Independent of metabolism
  2. Unidirectional with dissipative losses
  3. A perfect closed energy cycle
  4. Always increasing at each level
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Unidirectional with dissipative losses

Metabolic transformations dissipate energy as heat.

17. A pyramid of energy is ordinarily:

  1. Upright
  2. Inverted
  3. Flat at every level
  4. Unrelated to transfer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Upright

Energy available per area and time decreases across trophic transfers.

18. An inverted biomass pyramid can occur in an aquatic system because:

  1. Consumers produce all sunlight
  2. Fish have no metabolism
  3. Water prevents producer growth
  4. Phytoplankton turn over rapidly
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Phytoplankton turn over rapidly

Small standing producer biomass can support greater consumer standing biomass through rapid production.

19. A food web contains:

  1. Only decomposers
  2. Only producers
  3. Interconnected feeding pathways
  4. Only one linear pathway
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Interconnected feeding pathways

Multiple diet relationships connect food chains.

20. Detritus consists mainly of:

  1. Only dissolved salt
  2. Dead organic material
  3. Only living apex predators
  4. Only atmospheric nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Dead organic material

Organic remains form the substrate of detrital pathways.

21. Decomposers commonly include:

  1. Fungi and bacteria
  2. Only mammals
  3. Only birds
  4. Only flowering plants
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fungi and bacteria

Microbial enzymes break down complex dead organic material.

22. Fragmentation of detritus can be aided by:

  1. Only clouds
  2. Only ultraviolet radiation
  3. Only nitrogen gas
  4. Detritivores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Detritivores

Organisms such as earthworms physically break organic remains into smaller pieces.

23. Leaching moves soluble substances from detritus through the action of:

  1. Only chromosomes
  2. Only ribosomes
  3. Water
  4. Only photosystems
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Water

Rain and percolating water can carry soluble compounds into soil.

24. Humification produces relatively resistant:

  1. Only nitrate gas
  2. Humus
  3. Only fresh glucose
  4. Only oxygen gas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Humus

Humus is a dark, relatively stable organic component.

25. Mineralisation releases inorganic nutrients from:

  1. Organic matter
  2. Only sunlight
  3. Only granite nuclei
  4. Only pure water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Organic matter

Microbial breakdown returns nutrients in mineral forms.

26. Ecological succession involves changes in:

  1. Only atomic mass
  2. Only the number of planets
  3. Only DNA codon length
  4. Community composition over time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Community composition over time

Species assemblages change as environments and interactions develop.

27. Primary succession begins on a site initially lacking developed:

  1. All water permanently
  2. All minerals
  3. Soil and an established community
  4. Sunlight in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Soil and an established community

New bare substrates support early colonists before soil develops.

28. Succession after disturbance where soil remains is commonly:

  1. Genetic translation
  2. Secondary succession
  3. Primary succession always
  4. No succession
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Secondary succession

Existing soil and propagules can speed community recovery.

29. A pioneer species on bare rock can be a:

  1. Lichen
  2. Large oak tree first
  3. Whale
  4. Earthworm in dry bare rock alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lichen

Lichens can colonise exposed rock and contribute to weathering.

30. Nutrient movement differs from energy flow because nutrients can be:

  1. Created from nothing at every level
  2. Destroyed completely by respiration
  3. Never reused
  4. Recycled between biotic and abiotic compartments
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Recycled between biotic and abiotic compartments

Biogeochemical cycles return matter for repeated biological use.
