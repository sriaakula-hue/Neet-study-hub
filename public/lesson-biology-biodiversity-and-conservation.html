Biodiversity and Conservation

Biodiversity and Conservation

Key concepts

  • Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity. Habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species and coextinctions contribute to losses.
  • In situ conservation protects organisms in natural habitats. Ex situ conservation protects them outside natural habitats.
  • Protected areas, seed banks, botanical gardens and captive programmes serve different conservation roles.

Connect the ideas

A national park supports in situ conservation; a seed bank is an ex situ method. Both can be part of a conservation strategy.

Common mistake

Counting species alone does not describe all biodiversity: variation within species and between ecosystems also matters.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Species diversity concerns variation in:

Kinds of species in an area

It differs from genetic diversity within species and ecosystem diversity across habitat systems.

2. Habitat fragmentation can reduce biodiversity by:

Dividing habitats and limiting connectivity

Isolation and smaller habitat patches can disrupt movement, reproduction and persistence.

3. An invasive alien species can threaten native biodiversity by:

Competition, predation or other ecological disruption

Introduced species can disrupt existing interactions; impacts depend on the species and context.

4. A biodiversity hotspot is associated with high endemism and substantial:

Habitat threat or loss

Hotspot frameworks prioritise biologically distinctive areas that have suffered considerable habitat loss.

5. An endemic species is native to and restricted to a:

Particular geographical area

Restricted natural distribution can increase vulnerability when that area’s habitat is damaged.

6. Coextinction can occur when loss of one species causes loss of a:

Dependent associated species

Specialised partners, hosts or dependent organisms may not persist after the linked species disappears.

7. A botanical garden conserving living plants outside their original habitat is an example of:

Ex situ conservation

It maintains organisms away from their natural habitat, complementing habitat-based protection.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Conservation in natural habitats is called:

  1. Ex situ
  2. In situ
  3. PCR
  4. Hybridisation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. In situ

In situ conservation protects organisms in their native habitats.

2. A seed bank is an example of:

  1. Ex situ conservation
  2. Only in situ conservation
  3. Predation
  4. Natural selection only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ex situ conservation

Seeds are maintained outside their original habitats.

3. Variation within a species contributes to:

  1. Genetic diversity
  2. Only ecosystem area
  3. Only trophic level
  4. No biodiversity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Genetic diversity

Genetic diversity describes heritable variation within species.

4. Species diversity concerns variation in:

  1. Kinds of species in an area
  2. Only alleles in one individual
  3. Only weather
  4. Only water molecules
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Kinds of species in an area

It differs from genetic diversity within species and ecosystem diversity across habitat systems.

5. Habitat fragmentation can reduce biodiversity by:

  1. Dividing habitats and limiting connectivity
  2. Always expanding every population
  3. Removing all competition beneficially
  4. Creating unlimited resources
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dividing habitats and limiting connectivity

Isolation and smaller habitat patches can disrupt movement, reproduction and persistence.

6. An invasive alien species can threaten native biodiversity by:

  1. Competition, predation or other ecological disruption
  2. Always improving every native species
  3. Always remaining harmless
  4. Changing all atomic masses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Competition, predation or other ecological disruption

Introduced species can disrupt existing interactions; impacts depend on the species and context.

7. A biodiversity hotspot is associated with high endemism and substantial:

  1. Habitat threat or loss
  2. Zero species
  3. No conservation concern
  4. Only ice cover
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Habitat threat or loss

Hotspot frameworks prioritise biologically distinctive areas that have suffered considerable habitat loss.

8. An endemic species is native to and restricted to a:

  1. Particular geographical area
  2. Every continent
  3. Laboratory only
  4. Single individual always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Particular geographical area

Restricted natural distribution can increase vulnerability when that area’s habitat is damaged.

9. Coextinction can occur when loss of one species causes loss of a:

  1. Dependent associated species
  2. Completely unrelated mineral
  3. Planet
  4. Chemical element
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dependent associated species

Specialised partners, hosts or dependent organisms may not persist after the linked species disappears.

10. A botanical garden conserving living plants outside their original habitat is an example of:

  1. Ex situ conservation
  2. Only in situ conservation
  3. Only natural selection
  4. Only predation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ex situ conservation

It maintains organisms away from their natural habitat, complementing habitat-based protection.

11. Biodiversity includes variation at genetic, species and:

  1. Only atomic levels
  2. Only orbital levels
  3. Only currency levels
  4. Ecosystem levels
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ecosystem levels

Diversity can be described across several biological scales.

12. Genetic diversity refers to variation:

  1. Only ecosystem climate
  2. Only taxonomic names
  3. Within a species' genetic makeup
  4. Only between continents' rocks
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Within a species' genetic makeup

Allelic and other genetic differences form this level of diversity.

13. Species diversity concerns variation in species composition and:

  1. Only mineral mass
  2. Relative abundance
  3. Only chromosome length
  4. Only individual age
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Relative abundance

Both richness and distribution of abundance matter.

14. Ecosystem diversity concerns differences among:

  1. Habitats and ecological systems
  2. Only genes of one clone
  3. Only body sizes
  4. Only base pairs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Habitats and ecological systems

Distinct communities and environments contribute diversity.

15. Species richness means the number of:

  1. Only individuals
  2. Only chromosomes
  3. Only food chains
  4. Species present
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Species present

It counts distinct species in the defined sample.

16. A common broad geographical pattern is greater species richness in:

  1. Only deserts
  2. Only the deepest mines
  3. Tropical regions
  4. Polar regions invariably
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Tropical regions

Many groups show increasing richness towards lower latitudes.

17. The species-area relationship commonly shows richness increasing with:

  1. Only individual size
  2. Area surveyed
  3. Only temperature unit
  4. Only chromosome count
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Area surveyed

Larger sampled areas often include more habitat and species.

18. In S=C Aᶻ, S denotes:

  1. Species richness
  2. Only survival rate
  3. Only salinity
  4. Only sunlight
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Species richness

The relation models species number as a function of area.

19. The exponent z describes the slope when the species-area relation is plotted on:

  1. Only a pie chart
  2. Only a flat map
  3. Only a chromosome diagram
  4. Log-log axes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Log-log axes

Taking logs gives logS=logC+zlogA.

20. Habitat fragmentation can isolate:

  1. Only solar rays
  2. Only proteins
  3. Previously connected populations
  4. Only atoms
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Previously connected populations

Reduced connectivity affects movement and reproduction.

21. Overexploitation threatens species when extraction exceeds their:

  1. Name length
  2. Capacity to replenish
  3. Colour intensity
  4. Atomic weight
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Capacity to replenish

Unsustainable harvesting can drive population decline.

22. An invasive alien species can threaten biodiversity through:

  1. Competition, predation or other disruptive interactions
  2. Always harmless coexistence
  3. Only taxonomic renaming
  4. Only extra rainfall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Competition, predation or other disruptive interactions

Introduced organisms can alter native ecological relationships.

23. Coextinction occurs when one species' loss causes loss of a dependent:

  1. Only a rock
  2. Only climate measurement
  3. Only chromosome band
  4. Associated species
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Associated species

Obligate ecological partners can disappear together.

24. In situ conservation protects organisms in their:

  1. Only frozen tubes
  2. Only museums
  3. Natural habitats
  4. Only laboratories
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Natural habitats

Habitat protection supports populations and interactions where they occur.

25. Ex situ conservation protects biological material:

  1. Only within a food chain
  2. Outside its natural habitat
  3. Only in untouched wilderness
  4. Only in open ocean
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Outside its natural habitat

Gardens, breeding facilities and storage collections are examples.

26. A national park is primarily an example of:

  1. In situ conservation
  2. Only ex situ cryopreservation
  3. Only laboratory culture
  4. Only herbarium storage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. In situ conservation

Protection occurs within a defined natural landscape.

27. A seed bank is primarily an example of:

  1. Only in situ wilderness protection
  2. Only trophic transfer
  3. Only community succession
  4. Ex situ conservation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ex situ conservation

Stored viable material is maintained outside the original habitat.

28. Cryopreservation uses:

  1. Only bright light
  2. Only high oxygen pressure
  3. Very low temperatures
  4. Only intense heat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Very low temperatures

Cold storage can preserve suitable biological material.

29. A biodiversity hotspot is recognised for high endemism and substantial:

  1. Only complete lack of disturbance
  2. Habitat loss or threat
  3. Only low species count
  4. Only absence of plants
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Habitat loss or threat

Conservation priorities combine unique diversity with risk.

30. An endemic species is naturally restricted to a particular:

  1. Geographical region
  2. Blood group
  3. Trophic number everywhere
  4. Atomic mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Geographical region

Its natural range is limited rather than globally widespread.
