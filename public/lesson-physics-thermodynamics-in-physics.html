Thermodynamics in Physics

Thermodynamics in Physics

Key concepts

  • Using work done by the system as positive, the first law is ΔU = Q − W. Heat and work describe energy transfer.
  • For an ideal gas, internal energy depends on temperature. An isothermal ideal-gas process has ΔU = 0.
  • An adiabatic process has Q = 0. A heat engine cannot convert all heat taken from a single reservoir into cyclic work without other effects.

Worked example

A gas absorbs 100 J of heat and does 40 J of work. Its internal-energy increase is 60 J.

Watch out

The sign convention differs from the common chemistry form ΔU = q + w, where w means work done on the system. Define W before calculating.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A gas does 30 J work adiabatically. ΔU is:

−30 J

Q = 0 and ΔU = Q − W, with work by the gas positive, gives −30 J.

2. An engine absorbs 500 J and does 100 J work per cycle. Efficiency is:

20%

η = W/Qhot = 100/500 = 0.20.

3. Over a complete thermodynamic cycle, ΔU is:

Zero

Internal energy is a state function; the system returns to its initial state.

4. The zeroth law supports the concept of:

Temperature

Transitivity of thermal equilibrium permits a consistent temperature measurement.

5. For an ideal gas in reversible isothermal expansion, absorbed heat equals:

Work done by the gas

Ideal-gas ΔU = 0 at constant temperature, so Q = W under the stated sign convention.

6. A Carnot engine operating between Th and Tc has efficiency:

1 − Tc/Th

Use absolute temperatures; η = 1 − Tc/Th is the reversible upper limit.

7. For Th = 600 K and Tc = 300 K, Carnot efficiency is:

50%

η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.50. Real irreversible engines between these reservoirs have lower efficiency.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Q = 100 J and work done by the gas W = 40 J give ΔU:

  1. 140 J
  2. 60 J
  3. −60 J
  4. −140 J
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 60 J

ΔU = Q − W = 100 − 40 = 60 J.

2. For an adiabatic process, which is zero?

  1. Heat transfer Q
  2. Work always
  3. Temperature change always
  4. Pressure always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Heat transfer Q

Adiabatic means no heat exchange.

3. For an isothermal process of an ideal gas, ΔU is:

  1. Positive always
  2. Negative always
  3. Zero
  4. Equal to pressure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Zero

Ideal-gas internal energy depends only on temperature.

4. A gas does 30 J work adiabatically. ΔU is:

  1. 0 J
  2. +60 J
  3. −30 J
  4. +30 J
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. −30 J

Q = 0 and ΔU = Q − W, with work by the gas positive, gives −30 J.

5. An engine absorbs 500 J and does 100 J work per cycle. Efficiency is:

  1. 80%
  2. 100%
  3. 20%
  4. 5%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20%

η = W/Qhot = 100/500 = 0.20.

6. Over a complete thermodynamic cycle, ΔU is:

  1. Zero
  2. Equal to heat input always
  3. Always positive
  4. Always negative
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zero

Internal energy is a state function; the system returns to its initial state.

7. The zeroth law supports the concept of:

  1. Temperature
  2. Electric charge
  3. Nuclear spin
  4. Refractive index
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Temperature

Transitivity of thermal equilibrium permits a consistent temperature measurement.

8. For an ideal gas in reversible isothermal expansion, absorbed heat equals:

  1. Work done by the gas
  2. Zero always
  3. Its entire internal energy
  4. Pressure only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Work done by the gas

Ideal-gas ΔU = 0 at constant temperature, so Q = W under the stated sign convention.

9. A Carnot engine operating between Th and Tc has efficiency:

  1. 1 − Tc/Th
  2. 1 − Th/Tc
  3. Th − Tc in degrees Celsius
  4. Tc/Th only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1 − Tc/Th

Use absolute temperatures; η = 1 − Tc/Th is the reversible upper limit.

10. For Th = 600 K and Tc = 300 K, Carnot efficiency is:

  1. 75%
  2. 100%
  3. 50%
  4. 25%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 50%

η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.50. Real irreversible engines between these reservoirs have lower efficiency.
