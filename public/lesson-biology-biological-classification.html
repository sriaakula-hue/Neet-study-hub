Biological Classification

Biological Classification

EXPANDED REVISION · LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Use cellular organisation and nutrition to compare kingdoms, and distinguish organisms from acellular infectious agents.

1. Ask questions in the right order

Start with cell type: does the cell have a membrane-bound nucleus? Next consider whether the organism is unicellular or multicellular. Then examine nutrition, cell-wall features and relationships. One visible character rarely supports a complete classification.

2. Compare the five-kingdom framework

Monera contains prokaryotes. Protista includes a broad range of mostly unicellular eukaryotes. Fungi are eukaryotic absorptive heterotrophs; many grow as hyphae forming a mycelium. Plants are typically multicellular photosynthetic eukaryotes. Animals are multicellular eukaryotes that generally obtain food by ingestion. This textbook framework is useful for comparisons but is not the only modern classification system.

3. Nutrition matters

A fungus can secrete digestive enzymes and absorb smaller molecules from its surroundings. This differs from an animal ingesting food before internal digestion. Cyanobacteria photosynthesise, yet remain prokaryotic bacteria: photosynthesis alone does not make an organism a plant.

4. Partnerships and special cases

Lichens combine a fungal partner with a photosynthetic partner. The partnership must be distinguished from a single organism performing every function alone. Mycoplasmas are bacteria that lack a cell wall; avoid assuming every bacterium has a rigid wall.

5. Acellular agents

Viruses have genetic material enclosed in a protein coat, sometimes with an envelope, and use host cells for replication. A viral genome may be DNA or RNA depending on the virus. Viroids are infectious RNA molecules lacking a protein coat. These do not fit ordinary cellular kingdom descriptions.

Worked comparison and pitfalls

Compare a cyanobacterium, yeast and virus: the first is a prokaryotic cell, the second a eukaryotic fungus, and the third acellular. “Small”, “photosynthetic” and “infectious” are not interchangeable classification criteria. Not all bacteria cause disease, and not all fungi are multicellular.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Cyanobacteria have which cellular organisation?

  1. Prokaryotic
  2. Multicellular eukaryotic only
  3. Acellular
  4. No DNA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prokaryotic

Cyanobacteria are bacteria and lack a membrane-bound nucleus.

2. The major structural polymer associated with fungal cell walls is:

  1. Glycogen
  2. Chitin
  3. Starch
  4. Haemoglobin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chitin

Chitin is a characteristic component of fungal cell walls.

3. A viroid consists mainly of:

  1. DNA inside a capsid
  2. RNA without a protein coat
  3. A bacterial cell
  4. A fungal spore
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. RNA without a protein coat

Viroids are small infectious RNA molecules without a capsid.

4. A membrane-bound nucleus is present in:

  1. Yeast
  2. A cyanobacterium
  3. A typical bacterium
  4. A viroid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Yeast

Yeast is a eukaryotic fungus; bacteria are prokaryotic and viroids are acellular.

5. Fungal nutrition commonly involves:

  1. External digestion followed by absorption
  2. Photosynthesis in every species
  3. Only ingestion with a mouth
  4. No nutrient uptake
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. External digestion followed by absorption

Many fungi release enzymes and absorb digestion products.

6. A fungal hypha is:

  1. A filamentous growth structure
  2. A viral genome
  3. A chloroplast
  4. A mammalian cell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A filamentous growth structure

Hyphae are threadlike fungal structures; a network forms a mycelium.

7. A lichen partnership usually includes a fungus and a:

  1. Photosynthetic partner
  2. Human neuron
  3. Mammalian red cell
  4. Naked DNA molecule only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Photosynthetic partner

The photosynthetic partner supplies organic products in the association.

8. Mycoplasmas are notable for lacking a:

  1. Cell wall
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Genome
  4. Ribosome
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell wall

They are cellular bacteria without a cell wall.

9. A typical viral genome can be:

  1. DNA or RNA, depending on the virus
  2. Always both DNA and RNA as its genome
  3. Only a protein
  4. Only cellulose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. DNA or RNA, depending on the virus

Different viruses use DNA or RNA genomes.

10. Which observation most clearly distinguishes a cyanobacterium from a unicellular eukaryotic alga?

  1. Membrane-bound nucleus in the alga
  2. Both can photosynthesise
  3. Both are small
  4. Both need water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Membrane-bound nucleus in the alga

Cellular organisation, rather than photosynthesis alone, distinguishes these cases.

11. Whittaker's five-kingdom proposal used features including cell structure and:

  1. Only body colour
  2. Only geographic longitude
  3. Only lifespan
  4. Mode of nutrition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mode of nutrition

Organisation, nutrition, reproduction and relationships were important criteria.

12. The five-kingdom group containing typical prokaryotes is:

  1. Fungi
  2. Plantae
  3. Monera
  4. Protista
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Monera

The historical Monera category includes organisms lacking a membrane-bounded nucleus.

13. Typical protists are primarily:

  1. Only mammals
  2. Unicellular eukaryotes
  3. Multicellular prokaryotes
  4. Only viruses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Unicellular eukaryotes

Protista grouped diverse mostly unicellular eukaryotic organisms.

14. Fungal nutrition is generally:

  1. Absorptive heterotrophy
  2. Photosynthetic autotrophy in all fungi
  3. Only chemosynthesis
  4. Only intracellular nitrogen fixation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Absorptive heterotrophy

Fungi digest externally and absorb dissolved nutrients.

15. The wall of many fungi contains:

  1. Peptidoglycan exclusively
  2. Only cellulose in every fungus
  3. Only lipid
  4. Chitin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Chitin

Chitin is characteristic of fungal cell walls.

16. Methanogenic archaea can be found in:

  1. Only dry steel
  2. Only plant chloroplasts
  3. Anaerobic habitats such as ruminant guts
  4. Only oxygen-rich clouds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Anaerobic habitats such as ruminant guts

Methanogens function in low-oxygen environments.

17. Cyanobacteria are:

  1. Typical fungal hyphae
  2. Photosynthetic prokaryotes
  3. Photosynthetic animals
  4. Noncellular viruses
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Photosynthetic prokaryotes

They perform oxygenic photosynthesis without a eukaryotic nucleus.

18. Heterocysts in some cyanobacteria are associated with:

  1. Nitrogen fixation
  2. Only sexual reproduction
  3. Only calcium storage
  4. Only locomotion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nitrogen fixation

Specialised cells provide conditions suitable for nitrogen-fixing activity.

19. Mycoplasma differs from ordinary wall-bearing bacteria by lacking a:

  1. Plasma membrane
  2. Genetic material
  3. Ribosome
  4. Cell wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cell wall

Its absence of a rigid wall influences shape and antibiotic sensitivity.

20. A diatom's cell wall contains substantial:

  1. Only peptidoglycan
  2. Only keratin
  3. Silica
  4. Only chitin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Silica

The silica-rich frustule is a characteristic diatom structure.

21. Diatomaceous earth is derived mainly from accumulated:

  1. Pollen tubes
  2. Diatom remains
  3. Whale bones
  4. Fern spores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Diatom remains

Siliceous shells persist and form deposits.

22. Many dinoflagellates have walls with:

  1. Cellulose plates
  2. Bone plates
  3. Only peptidoglycan
  4. Only chitin shells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cellulose plates

Their plate-bearing outer coverings are characteristic in many species.

23. Euglenoids typically possess a flexible:

  1. Woody secondary wall
  2. Peptidoglycan wall
  3. Silica shell
  4. Pellicle
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Pellicle

The protein-rich covering permits flexible body shape.

24. Slime moulds can form a multinucleate mass called a:

  1. Protonema
  2. Sporophyte cone
  3. Plasmodium
  4. Blastula
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Plasmodium

The feeding mass can spread over decaying organic material.

25. Amoeboid protozoans move using:

  1. Only wings
  2. Pseudopodia
  3. Only cilia
  4. Only spores
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Pseudopodia

Temporary cytoplasmic projections support movement and feeding.

26. Ciliated protozoans use:

  1. Cilia
  2. Hyphae
  3. Root hairs
  4. Pollen tubes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cilia

Short coordinated surface structures drive movement.

27. Fungal hyphae collectively form a:

  1. Protonema
  2. Gamete
  3. Virion
  4. Mycelium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Mycelium

The branching filament network constitutes the fungal body.

28. Lichens are associations between fungi and:

  1. Only viruses
  2. Only nitrifying rocks
  3. Photosynthetic partners
  4. Only mammalian cells
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Photosynthetic partners

Algal or cyanobacterial partners supply photosynthetic products.

29. A virus has genetic material enclosed in a:

  1. Chloroplast
  2. Protein capsid
  3. Cellulose nucleus
  4. Peptidoglycan nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Protein capsid

The capsid protects the nucleic acid; some viruses also have envelopes.

30. A viroid differs from a typical virus by being a small infectious:

  1. RNA lacking a protein coat
  2. Protein without RNA
  3. Bacterial cell
  4. Fungal spore
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. RNA lacking a protein coat

Viroids are infectious RNA agents without the usual capsid.
