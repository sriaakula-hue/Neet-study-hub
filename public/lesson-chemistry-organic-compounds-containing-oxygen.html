Organic Compounds Containing Oxygen

Organic Compounds Containing Oxygen

Key concepts

  • Alcohols contain an OH group attached to a saturated carbon; phenols contain OH attached directly to an aromatic ring; ethers contain a C–O–C linkage.
  • Aldehydes and ketones contain carbonyl groups. Carboxylic acids contain the COOH group.
  • Resonance stabilisation of phenoxide contributes to phenol being more acidic than a typical alcohol. Carbonyl compounds commonly undergo nucleophilic addition.

Worked connection

Ethanol is an alcohol, phenol has OH directly attached to a benzene ring, and ethanoic acid has a carboxyl group.

Watch out

An OH group alone does not establish an alcohol: its attached group determines the functional classification.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Aldehydes differ from ketones because the carbonyl carbon of an aldehyde is bonded to:

At least one hydrogen

An aldehyde group is R–CHO, while a typical ketone has two carbon-containing groups on its carbonyl carbon.

2. Oxidation of a suitable primary alcohol can initially form an:

Aldehyde

Controlled oxidation can give an aldehyde; stronger or prolonged conditions can yield a carboxylic acid.

3. Oxidation of a suitable secondary alcohol generally forms a:

Ketone

Removing two hydrogens from the secondary alcohol forms its carbonyl group.

4. The carbonyl carbon is a common target for:

Nucleophilic attack

The polar C=O bond leaves carbon relatively electrophilic, allowing electron-pair donation by a nucleophile.

5. Esterification of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol can form:

An ester and water

Under suitable conditions, acid and alcohol combine with elimination of water; the process is often reversible.

6. Intermolecular hydrogen bonding helps explain why alcohols often have higher boiling points than comparable:

Ethers

Alcohols can donate and accept hydrogen bonds; ethers accept but do not donate conventional OH hydrogen bonds.

7. Ethanoic acid has formula:

CH₃COOH

The carboxyl group COOH is attached to a methyl group in ethanoic acid.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. The functional group of a carboxylic acid is:

  1. COOH
  2. NH₂
  3. C–Cl
  4. C≡C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. COOH

A carboxyl group contains a carbonyl and hydroxyl on the same carbon.

2. An ether contains the linkage:

  1. C–O–C
  2. C–NH–C
  3. C–Cl
  4. O–O only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. C–O–C

Ether oxygen joins two carbon-containing groups.

3. Phenol is more acidic than a typical alcohol largely because its conjugate base is:

  1. Resonance stabilised
  2. Always positively charged
  3. A noble gas
  4. Without electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Resonance stabilised

The negative charge in phenoxide can be delocalised.

4. Aldehydes differ from ketones because the carbonyl carbon of an aldehyde is bonded to:

  1. At least one hydrogen
  2. Two carbon groups always
  3. Only oxygen atoms
  4. Only halogens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. At least one hydrogen

An aldehyde group is R–CHO, while a typical ketone has two carbon-containing groups on its carbonyl carbon.

5. Oxidation of a suitable primary alcohol can initially form an:

  1. Aldehyde
  2. Ketone always
  3. Ether only
  4. Alkane only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Aldehyde

Controlled oxidation can give an aldehyde; stronger or prolonged conditions can yield a carboxylic acid.

6. Oxidation of a suitable secondary alcohol generally forms a:

  1. Ketone
  2. Primary amine
  3. Alkane
  4. Ether necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ketone

Removing two hydrogens from the secondary alcohol forms its carbonyl group.

7. The carbonyl carbon is a common target for:

  1. Nucleophilic attack
  2. Only neutron attack
  3. Only helium addition
  4. No reactions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Nucleophilic attack

The polar C=O bond leaves carbon relatively electrophilic, allowing electron-pair donation by a nucleophile.

8. Esterification of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol can form:

  1. An ester and water
  2. An amine and nitrogen
  3. Only hydrogen gas
  4. Only an alkane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An ester and water

Under suitable conditions, acid and alcohol combine with elimination of water; the process is often reversible.

9. Intermolecular hydrogen bonding helps explain why alcohols often have higher boiling points than comparable:

  1. Ethers
  2. Ionic salts universally
  3. Metals universally
  4. All carboxylic acids
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ethers

Alcohols can donate and accept hydrogen bonds; ethers accept but do not donate conventional OH hydrogen bonds.

10. Ethanoic acid has formula:

  1. CH₃COOH
  2. CH₃OH
  3. CH₃CHO
  4. CH₃OCH₃
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CH₃COOH

The carboxyl group COOH is attached to a methyl group in ethanoic acid.

11. The functional group of a phenol is an OH group attached directly to:

  1. A saturated carbon only
  2. An aromatic carbon
  3. A carbonyl oxygen
  4. A nitrogen atom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. An aromatic carbon

Direct aromatic-ring attachment distinguishes phenols from ordinary alcohols.

12. A primary alcohol's OH-bearing carbon is attached to:

  1. One other carbon, or none for methanol
  2. Three other carbons
  3. Four other carbons
  4. Two carbonyl oxygens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One other carbon, or none for methanol

Classification counts carbon neighbours; methanol is the conventional zero-carbon-neighbour exception.

13. Controlled oxidation of a primary alcohol can yield an:

  1. Ketone necessarily
  2. Ether necessarily
  3. Amine
  4. Aldehyde
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Aldehyde

Stopping oxidation before further oxygen incorporation can isolate an aldehyde.

14. Oxidation of a secondary alcohol commonly forms a:

  1. Ether
  2. Alkane
  3. Ketone
  4. Primary amine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ketone

Removing hydrogen from the secondary alcohol yields a carbonyl group between two carbon groups.

15. Tertiary alcohols resist mild oxidation because the OH-bearing carbon lacks:

  1. Valence electrons
  2. A hydrogen needed for simple carbonyl formation
  3. All carbon neighbours
  4. An oxygen atom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A hydrogen needed for simple carbonyl formation

Direct oxidation to a carbonyl cannot occur without breaking a carbon-carbon bond.

16. Aldehydes contain the group:

  1. –CHO
  2. –COOH
  3. –O–
  4. –NH₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. –CHO

The carbonyl carbon of an aldehyde is bonded to hydrogen.

17. A ketone carbonyl carbon is bonded to:

  1. Two hydrogens
  2. Only hydroxide ions
  3. A halogen and hydrogen always
  4. Two carbon groups
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Two carbon groups

A ketone has carbon substituents on both sides of its carbonyl group.

18. Tollens' reagent gives a silver mirror with many:

  1. Alkanes
  2. Ordinary tertiary alcohols
  3. Aldehydes
  4. Simple ethers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Aldehydes

Aldehydes can reduce silver(I) while being oxidised under the test conditions.

19. Fehling's solution commonly gives a brick-red precipitate with:

  1. Most simple ketones
  2. Aliphatic aldehydes
  3. Benzene
  4. Diethyl ether
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Aliphatic aldehydes

Reduction of copper(II) can form copper(I) oxide in the standard aldehyde test.

20. A methyl ketone can give a positive:

  1. Iodoform test
  2. Lassaigne nitrogen test necessarily
  3. Flame test for sodium necessarily
  4. Only carbonate test
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Iodoform test

The CH₃CO– motif can yield yellow iodoform under the standard conditions.

21. The yellow solid in a positive iodoform test is:

  1. CHCl₃
  2. NaI
  3. I₂ only
  4. CHI₃
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. CHI₃

Triiodomethane forms the characteristic yellow precipitate.

22. Carboxylic acids contain the functional group:

  1. –O–
  2. –NH₂
  3. –COOH
  4. –CHO
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. –COOH

A carboxyl group combines a carbonyl and hydroxyl on the same carbon.

23. Carboxylic acids react with sodium hydrogen carbonate to release:

  1. NH₃
  2. CO₂
  3. H₂ only
  4. O₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. CO₂

Acid-carbonate chemistry forms carbon dioxide, water and a carboxylate salt.

24. Phenol is more acidic than ethanol mainly because phenoxide is stabilised by:

  1. Resonance
  2. Only greater molecular mass
  3. Loss of all electrons
  4. A carbon-metal bond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Resonance

Delocalisation stabilises the conjugate base of phenol.

25. Williamson ether synthesis commonly uses an alkoxide and a suitable:

  1. Tertiary amide
  2. Aromatic nitro compound
  3. Carboxylate ion only
  4. Primary alkyl halide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Primary alkyl halide

An SN2 reaction forms an ether, with primary substrates reducing competing elimination.

26. An ester is commonly formed from a carboxylic acid and an:

  1. Noble gas
  2. Alkyl nitrate only
  3. Alcohol
  4. Alkane
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Alcohol

Acid-catalysed esterification combines these partners with loss of water.

27. Hydrolysis of an ester produces, in acidic conditions, a carboxylic acid and an:

  1. Alkyne
  2. Alcohol
  3. Amide
  4. Alkene necessarily
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Alcohol

Breaking the acyl-oxygen linkage regenerates the acid and alcohol components.

28. Base hydrolysis of an ester is commonly called:

  1. Saponification
  2. Nitration
  3. Halogenation
  4. Polymerisation only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Saponification

The reaction gives a carboxylate salt and alcohol under basic conditions.

29. A carbonyl compound with an alpha hydrogen can commonly undergo:

  1. Only Cannizzaro reaction
  2. Only substitution at a halogen
  3. Wurtz coupling
  4. Aldol reaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Aldol reaction

Alpha-hydrogen removal allows enolate-based carbon-carbon bond formation.

30. Aldehydes without an alpha hydrogen can undergo the:

  1. Wurtz reaction
  2. Finkelstein reaction
  3. Cannizzaro reaction
  4. Ordinary aldol reaction alone
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cannizzaro reaction

Strong base can cause disproportionation into alcohol and carboxylate.
