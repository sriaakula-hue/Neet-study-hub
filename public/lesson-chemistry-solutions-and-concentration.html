Solutions and concentration

Core idea

Molarity is moles of solute per litre of solution. Molality is moles of solute per kilogram of solvent. The denominator matters: solution volume differs from solvent mass.

Worked connection

Dissolving 0.5 mol of solute and making the final solution volume 2 L gives 0.25 mol/L.

Watch out

Molarity can change with temperature because volume changes. Molality is based on mass and does not depend on thermal expansion.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A solution has 2 mol solute in 4 kg solvent. Molality is:

0.5 mol/kg

Molality is solute moles per kilogram of solvent: 2/4 = 0.5.

2. A mixture has 1 mol A and 3 mol B. Mole fraction of A is:

0.25

xA = nA/(nA + nB) = 1/4 = 0.25.

3. For an ideal solution of volatile liquids, Raoult’s law gives partial pressure as:

xi pi°

The component partial vapour pressure equals its liquid mole fraction times the pure-component vapour pressure at that temperature.

4. Colligative properties in an ideal dilute solution depend chiefly on:

Number of solute particles

They depend on particle concentration, not directly on particle identity, under the appropriate ideal assumptions.

5. An ideal dilute solution containing a nonvolatile solute generally has boiling point:

Higher than pure solvent

The lowered solvent vapour pressure requires a higher temperature to reach external pressure.

6. For complete ideal dissociation of NaCl, the van’t Hoff factor is approximately:

2

One formula unit yields Na⁺ and Cl⁻, two particles; real solutions can deviate due to interactions.

7. Ideal dilute osmotic pressure obeys:

π = CRT

C is molar particle concentration; use consistent units and account for dissociation or association where needed.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. 0.5 mol of solute in 2 L of solution has molarity:

  1. 0.25 M
  2. 1 M
  3. 2.5 M
  4. 4 M
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.25 M

M = n/V = 0.5/2 = 0.25 mol/L.

2. Molality uses which denominator?

  1. Litres of solution
  2. Kilograms of solvent
  3. Moles of solvent
  4. Grams of solution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Kilograms of solvent

Molality = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kilograms.

3. Which concentration is unaffected by thermal expansion at fixed composition?

  1. Molarity
  2. Molality
  3. Mass per volume
  4. Moles per litre
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Molality

Molality uses masses, which do not change through thermal expansion.

4. A solution has 2 mol solute in 4 kg solvent. Molality is:

  1. 2 mol/kg
  2. 8 mol/kg
  3. 4 mol/kg
  4. 0.5 mol/kg
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.5 mol/kg

Molality is solute moles per kilogram of solvent: 2/4 = 0.5.

5. A mixture has 1 mol A and 3 mol B. Mole fraction of A is:

  1. 0.75
  2. 0.33
  3. 1
  4. 0.25
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 0.25

xA = nA/(nA + nB) = 1/4 = 0.25.

6. For an ideal solution of volatile liquids, Raoult’s law gives partial pressure as:

  1. xi pi°
  2. pi°/xi
  3. xi + pi°
  4. Always zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. xi pi°

The component partial vapour pressure equals its liquid mole fraction times the pure-component vapour pressure at that temperature.

7. Colligative properties in an ideal dilute solution depend chiefly on:

  1. Number of solute particles
  2. Only solute colour
  3. Only solute chemical identity
  4. Only solvent mass regardless of concentration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Number of solute particles

They depend on particle concentration, not directly on particle identity, under the appropriate ideal assumptions.

8. An ideal dilute solution containing a nonvolatile solute generally has boiling point:

  1. Higher than pure solvent
  2. Lower than pure solvent
  3. Exactly unchanged always
  4. Zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Higher than pure solvent

The lowered solvent vapour pressure requires a higher temperature to reach external pressure.

9. For complete ideal dissociation of NaCl, the van’t Hoff factor is approximately:

  1. 2
  2. 1
  3. 3
  4. 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2

One formula unit yields Na⁺ and Cl⁻, two particles; real solutions can deviate due to interactions.

10. Ideal dilute osmotic pressure obeys:

  1. π = CRT
  2. π = C/R
  3. π = RT/C
  4. π = 0 always
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. π = CRT

C is molar particle concentration; use consistent units and account for dissociation or association where needed.
