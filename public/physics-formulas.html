Measurement

Dimensions

[v]=LT⁻¹; [a]=LT⁻²; [F]=MLT⁻²; [energy]=ML²T⁻²; [power]=ML²T⁻³

M, L and T denote mass, length and time dimensions.

Errors in sums and products

Δ(x±y)=Δx+Δy; Δz/|z|=|a|Δx/|x|+|b|Δy/|y| for z=xᵃyᵇ

Maximum absolute errors add for sums; maximum relative errors add with powers. Small uncertainties, x and y nonzero.

Percentage error

Percentage error = 100 × absolute error / |measured value

Use the same units in numerator and denominator; measured value must be nonzero.

Vectors and motion

Vector components

Aₓ=A cos θ; Aᵧ=A sin θ; |A|=√(Aₓ²+Aᵧ²)

θ is measured from the positive x axis in a plane.

Scalar and vector products

A·B=AB cos θ; |A×B|=AB sin θ

Dot product is scalar; cross-product direction follows the right-hand rule.

Average and instantaneous rates

v_avg=Δx/Δt; v=dx/dt; a=dv/dt

Speed uses distance, while velocity uses displacement. SI: m/s and m/s².

Uniform acceleration

v=u+at; s=ut+½at²; v²=u²+2as; s=(u+v)t/2

Only constant acceleration in the chosen direction; s is displacement, u initial velocity.

Relative velocity

v_AB=v_A−v_B

Velocity of A relative to B; subtract vectors, not just their magnitudes.

Projectile motion

T=2u sin θ/g; H=u²sin²θ/(2g); R=u²sin 2θ/g

Same launch and landing height; constant g; neglect air resistance. θ is angle above horizontal.

Projectile trajectory

y=x tan θ−gx²/(2u²cos²θ)

Origin at launch; horizontal x, upward y; cos θ nonzero.

Circular motion

v=ωr; a_c=v²/r=ω²r; T=2π/ω; f=1/T

Uniform circular motion; centripetal acceleration points inward. Angular speed in rad/s.

Laws of motion

Newton's second law and impulse

F_net=dp/dt; F_net=ma; J=∫F dt=Δp

F=ma requires constant mass; p=mv. Force N; impulse N·s.

Friction

f_s≤μ_sN; f_s,max=μ_sN; f_k=μ_kN

Static friction adjusts up to its limit; N is normal reaction and need not equal mg.

Inclined plane and banking

F_parallel=mg sin θ; N=mg cos θ; tan θ_bank=v²/(rg)

Incline expressions assume no other normal forces; banking formula is for a frictionless road.

Momentum conservation

Σp_initial=Σp_final

An isolated system, or zero net external impulse during the interaction.

Work, energy and power

Work and kinetic energy

W=∫F·dr; W=Fs cos θ; K=½mv²; W_net=ΔK

Constant-force form uses displacement s. Energy and work in joules.

Potential energy

U_gravity=mgh; U_spring=½kx²; Fₓ=−dU/dx

mgh near Earth with constant g; spring obeys Hooke's law within elastic limit.

Power and mechanical energy

P=W/t; P_inst=F·v; E=K+U

Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work. Power in watts.

Coefficient of restitution

e=(speed of separation)/(speed of approach)

Along the line of impact; standard passive collisions have 0≤e≤1.

Rotation

Centre of mass

r_CM=Σmᵢrᵢ/Σmᵢ

Vector mass-weighted average; total mass nonzero.

Torque, angular momentum and energy

τ=r×F; L=r×p; τ_net=dL/dt; L=Iω; K_rot=½Iω²

L=Iω is the fixed principal-axis form; torque N·m, I in kg·m².

Angular acceleration

ω=ω₀+αt; θ=ω₀t+½αt²; ω²=ω₀²+2αθ; τ=Iα

Constant α about a fixed axis with constant I for these forms.

Moments of inertia

Ring MR²; solid disc ½MR²; solid sphere ⅖MR²; thin spherical shell ⅔MR²

Axes through centres: ring/disc symmetry axis and sphere diameter.

Rod and axis theorems

I_rod,centre=ML²/12; I_rod,end=ML²/3; I=I_CM+Md²; I_z=I_x+I_y

Rod axis perpendicular to length; parallel-axis theorem uses distance d; perpendicular-axis theorem only for planar lamina.

Pure rolling

v_CM=ωR; K=½Mv_CM²+½I_CMω²

Rolling without slipping; includes translation and rotation.

Gravitation

Force, potential and field

F=Gm₁m₂/r²; V=−GM/r; g=GM/r²; U=−GMm/r

Spherical source outside its surface or point masses; potential zero at infinity.

Orbital and escape speed

v_orbit=√(GM/r); T=2π√(r³/GM); v_escape=√(2GM/R)

Circular orbit at radius r; escape from radius R without air drag; central mass dominates.

Variation of g

g_h=g₀[R/(R+h)]²; g_d=g₀(1−d/R)

Depth expression assumes uniform Earth density, ignoring rotation. h altitude, d depth.

Kepler and orbital energy

T²∝a³; E_circular=−GMm/(2r)

a is ellipse semimajor axis; energy expression for circular orbit radius r.

Solids and fluids

Elasticity

Stress=F/A; strain=ΔL/L; Y=(F/A)/(ΔL/L); bulk modulus=−ΔP/(ΔV/V)

Small elastic deformation; stress and moduli in pascals, strain dimensionless.

Pressure and buoyancy

P=P₀+ρgh; F_b=ρ_fluid V_displaced g

Hydrostatic uniform density; buoyancy equals displaced fluid's weight.

Continuity and Bernoulli

A₁v₁=A₂v₂; P+½ρv²+ρgh=constant

Steady incompressible flow; Bernoulli also assumes negligible viscosity and follows a streamline.

Viscosity and terminal speed

F_viscous=6πηrv; v_t=2r²(ρ_s−ρ_f)g/(9η)

Small sphere in creeping flow, far from boundaries; v_t magnitude for a denser sphere settling.

Surface tension

S=F/ℓ; ΔP_drop=2S/r; ΔP_soap bubble=4S/r

ℓ is total contact length; liquid drop has one surface, soap bubble two. S in N/m.

Capillary rise

h=2S cos θ/(ρgr)

Tube radius r; θ contact angle. Negative h indicates capillary depression.

Thermal physics

Thermal expansion

ΔL=αL₀ΔT; ΔA≈2αA₀ΔT; ΔV≈3αV₀ΔT

Small temperature change, isotropic solid; α approximately constant.

Heat and phase change

Q=mcΔT; Q=mL

Specific heat c in J/(kg·K); latent heat L in J/kg; phase change at constant temperature.

Conduction and radiation

P_cond=kAΔT/ℓ; P_net=εσA(T⁴−T_env⁴)

Steady slab conduction; radiative temperatures in kelvin, emissivity ε dimensionless.

First law and ideal gas work

ΔU=Q−W_by; W_by=∫P dV; W_isothermal=nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

Physics convention: W_by is work done by gas. Isothermal formula for reversible ideal-gas expansion/compression.

Ideal-gas processes

PV=nRT; PV^γ=constant; TV^(γ−1)=constant

Last two are reversible adiabatic ideal-gas relations, γ=C_P/C_V.

Heat capacities and engine

C_P−C_V=R; η=W/Q_hot; η_Carnot=1−T_cold/T_hot

Heat capacities molar for ideal gas; temperatures K; Carnot is a reversible upper bound.

Kinetic theory

Molecular speeds and pressure

v_rms=√(3RT/M); P=⅓ρv_rms²; mean translational K=3k_BT/2

M in kg/mol, T in K; classical ideal gas, equilibrium.

Degrees of freedom

U=(f/2)nRT; C_V=fR/2; C_P=(f+2)R/2; γ=(f+2)/f

Equipartition applies to active modes. Monatomic f=3; rigid diatomic f≈5 in usual temperature range.

Oscillations

Simple harmonic motion

x=A cos(ωt+φ); a=−ω²x; v²=ω²(A²−x²); T=2π/ω

SHM about equilibrium; angular frequency in rad/s.

Spring and pendulum

T_spring=2π√(m/k); T_pendulum=2π√(ℓ/g)

Ideal massless spring; simple pendulum for small-angle oscillations.

SHM energy

E=½kA²; U=½kx²; K=½k(A²−x²)

Undamped ideal spring oscillator, displacement measured from equilibrium.

Waves

Wave equation and speed

y=A sin(kx−ωt+φ); k=2π/λ; v=fλ=ω/k; v_string=√(Tension/μ)

Travelling sinusoidal wave; μ is string mass per unit length.

Standing-wave frequencies

f_n=n v/(2L); f_n=(2n−1)v/(4L)

First: string fixed at both ends or pipe open at both ends; second: pipe closed at one end. n=1,2,…; neglect end correction.

Beats and Doppler effect

f_beats=|f₁−f₂|; f'=f(v+v_o)/(v−v_s)

Sound: v_o positive toward source, v_s positive toward observer; medium stationary and speeds subsonic.

Electrostatics

Coulomb force and field

F=q₁q₂/(4πεr²); E=F/q_test; E_point=q/(4πεr²)

Force/field magnitudes use absolute charge where required; vector direction depends on signs. ε is medium permittivity.

Potential and energy

V_point=q/(4πεr); U=q₁q₂/(4πεr); E=−∇V

Point charges; zero potential at infinity. V in volts, E in N/C or V/m.

Electric dipole

p=q d; τ=p×E; U=−p·E

d vector from negative to positive charge, magnitude separation; uniform applied field.

Far-field dipole

E_axial=2p/(4πε₀r³); E_equatorial=p/(4πε₀r³)

Magnitudes for r much larger than charge separation; equatorial field opposite p.

Gauss's law

∮E·dA=Q_enclosed/ε₀

Closed surface in vacuum; outward area vector.

Symmetric fields

E_line=λ/(2πε₀r); E_sheet=σ/(2ε₀); E_conductor,out=σ/ε₀

Infinite uniform line, infinite nonconducting sheet, and just outside a conductor surface respectively.

Capacitance

Capacitance and plate capacitor

C=Q/V; C_parallel plate=κε₀A/d

Ideal large plates, separation d, fully filled dielectric κ, neglect edge effects. C in farads.

Combinations and stored energy

C_parallel=ΣCᵢ; 1/C_series=Σ1/Cᵢ; U=½CV²=Q²/(2C)=½QV

Series uses same charge, parallel same voltage.

Current electricity

Current, resistance and drift

I=ΔQ/Δt; I=neAv_d; R=ρL/A; V=IR

n carrier number density, e magnitude of carrier charge; Ohm's law for an ohmic conductor at fixed conditions.

Temperature and power

R≈R₀[1+α(T−T₀)]; P=VI=I²R=V²/R

Linear temperature approximation; power expressions for a resistor.

Networks and cells

R_series=ΣRᵢ; 1/R_parallel=Σ1/Rᵢ; I=ε/(R+r); V_terminal=ε−Ir

Cell discharging through R; r internal resistance, ε emf.

Kirchhoff and bridge

ΣI_at junction=0; ΣΔV_loop=0; P/Q=R/S

Use signed currents and potential changes; last relation is a balanced Wheatstone bridge with conventional arm labels.

Potentiometer and metre bridge

ε₁/ε₂=ℓ₁/ℓ₂; R/S=ℓ/(100−ℓ)

Potentiometer uniform potential gradient at balance; metre-bridge ℓ in cm, R on the ℓ side.

Magnetism

Magnetic force

F=q(v×B); |F|=|q|vB sin θ; F_wire=I(ℓ×B)

Magnetic force perpendicular to velocity; field B in tesla.

Charged-particle circular motion

r=mv_perp/(|q|B); T=2πm/(|q|B)

Uniform field, nonrelativistic particle; perpendicular motion circular, parallel motion leads to a helix.

Magnetic fields

B_wire=μ₀I/(2πr); B_loop,centre=μ₀NI/(2R); B_solenoid≈μ₀nI

Long straight wire, N-turn circular loop, long ideal solenoid in vacuum; n turns/metre.

Parallel wires and current loop

F/ℓ=μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd); m=NIA; τ=m×B; U=−m·B

Long parallel wires separation d; same-direction currents attract. m magnetic dipole moment.

Magnetic materials

M=χ_m H; B=μ₀(H+M)=μ₀(1+χ_m)H

Linear isotropic response; M magnetisation, H magnetic field intensity, χ_m susceptibility.

Electromagnetic induction

Flux, emf and motional emf

Φ=B A cos θ; ε=−N dΦ/dt; |ε_motion|=Bℓv

θ is between B and area normal; motional form for rod, velocity and B mutually perpendicular.

Self-induction and energy

ε_L=−L dI/dt; U_L=½LI²

Inductance L in henry; negative sign expresses opposition to change.

Alternating current

AC and reactance

I_rms=I₀/√2; V_rms=V₀/√2; X_L=ωL; X_C=1/(ωC)

Sinusoidal AC; reactances in ohms.

Series RLC and resonance

Z=√[R²+(X_L−X_C)²]; I_rms=V_rms/Z; ω₀=1/√(LC)

Ideal series RLC; impedance minimum at resonance.

AC power and transformer

P_avg=V_rmsI_rms cos φ; cos φ=R/Z; V_s/V_p=N_s/N_p=I_p/I_s

Power-factor expression for series RLC; transformer ratios for an ideal transformer.

Electromagnetic waves

Wave speed and energy

c=1/√(μ₀ε₀); c=fλ; E₀=cB₀

Vacuum electromagnetic waves; E and B perpendicular and in phase.

Ray optics

Reflection and refraction

i=r; n=c/v; n₁ sin i=n₂ sin r; sin C=n₂/n₁

Critical angle C only for incidence from n₁>n₂; angles from normal.

Mirrors and thin lenses

1/f=1/v+1/u (mirror); 1/f=1/v−1/u (lens); m_mirror=−v/u; m_lens=v/u

Cartesian sign convention: incident light direction positive, object usually u<0. f, u, v in same units.

Lens maker and power

1/f=(n_rel−1)(1/R₁−1/R₂); P=1/f; P_total=ΣPᵢ

Thin lens, n_rel lens/medium index; f in metres for power in dioptres; lenses in contact.

Prism

n=sin[(A+δ_min)/2]/sin(A/2); δ≈(n−1)A

Prism in air; last expression for a thin prism, small angles in radians.

Microscope and telescope

M_microscope≈(L/f_o)(D/f_e); |M_telescope|=f_o/f_e

Magnitudes at normal adjustment; microscope approximation L≫f_o, D≈25 cm; telescope final image at infinity.

Wave optics

Interference

β=λD/d; Δ=d sin θ; bright Δ=mλ; dark Δ=(m+½)λ

Young's double slit; coherent sources, D≫d, small angles for fringe width.

Diffraction and polarisation

a sin θ=mλ (m=1,2,…); I=I₀cos²θ; tan i_B=n₂/n₁

Single-slit dark fringes; Malus law for polarised input; Brewster angle for nonabsorbing dielectric interface.

Modern physics

Photons and photoelectric effect

E=hf=hc/λ; p=h/λ; K_max=hf−φ=eV_stop

φ work function, threshold f₀=φ/h; energies expressed consistently in J or eV.

de Broglie wavelength

λ=h/p; λ=h/√(2mK)

Second form nonrelativistic; particle kinetic energy K in joules with SI h and m.

Bohr model

r_n=a₀n²/Z; E_n=−13.6 Z²/n² eV; hν=E_i−E_f

Hydrogen-like one-electron ions, nuclear charge Z; emission E_i>E_f, n positive integer.

Spectral lines

1/λ=R_H Z²(1/n_f²−1/n_i²)

Emission n_i>n_f; hydrogen-like approximation, appropriate Rydberg constant.

Nuclear size and binding

R=R₀A^(1/3); E_binding=Δmc²; 1 u·c²≈931.5 MeV

Mass number A; R₀≈1.2 fm. Use consistent atomic/nuclear masses for mass defect.

Radioactive decay

N=N₀e^(−λt); activity=λN; t_half=ln 2/λ; mean life=1/λ

λ decay constant, distinct from wavelength; activity in becquerels.

Electronics

Transistor gains

I_E=I_C+I_B; α=I_C/I_E; β=I_C/I_B=α/(1−α)

DC active-region current magnitudes, leakage neglected; these are idealised relations.

Logic operations

NOT: Y=¬A; AND: Y=A·B; OR: Y=A+B

Boolean notation, not ordinary arithmetic; NAND and NOR invert AND and OR outputs.

Constants

Common constants

g≈9.8 m/s²; c≈3.00×10⁸ m/s; h≈6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s; e≈1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C

g varies with location. 1 eV≈1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J.

Gravitational and electrical constants

G≈6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²; ε₀≈8.854×10⁻¹² F/m; 1/(4πε₀)≈8.988×10⁹ N·m²/C²

Vacuum constants; use sensible rounding consistent with the question.
