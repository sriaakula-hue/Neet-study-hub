Cell biology

Microscope magnification

Magnification=image size/actual size

Use matching length units; 1 mm=1000 μm, 1 μm=1000 nm. Magnification dimensionless.

Surface area to volume

Sphere: SA/V=3/r; cube: SA/V=6/a

r radius and a cube edge; ratio has inverse-length units. Small cells have greater surface area relative to volume.

Chromosome and DNA content

G₁: 2n,2C; after S and G₂: 2n,4C; after meiosis I: n,2C; after meiosis II: n,1C

Typical diploid cell; n chromosome-set number, C haploid DNA amount. S phase doubles DNA, not centromere-counted chromosomes.

DNA length

Length≈number of base pairs×0.34 nm

Approximate B-DNA axial rise per base pair; 1 nm=10⁻⁹ m.

DNA base composition

A=T; G=C; A+T+G+C=100%

Double-stranded DNA base percentages; not a universal rule for single-stranded nucleic acids.

DNA hydrogen bonds

Hydrogen bonds=2×(A–T pairs)+3×(G–C pairs)

Watson–Crick pairing; counts between paired bases, not backbone covalent bonds.

Genetic code and translation

Possible triplets=4³=64; coding length=3×amino-acid count

61 sense codons plus 3 stop codons; add 3 bases for a stop codon when counting complete translated coding sequence including stop, excluding introns/UTRs.

PCR amplification

Ideal DNA copies=N₀×2ⁿ

n complete cycles with perfect doubling; real efficiency and plateau effects lower yield.

Photosynthesis and respiration

Photosynthesis

6CO₂+12H₂O→C₆H₁₂O₆+6O₂+6H₂O

Overall balanced textbook representation using light; net form uses 6H₂O on left; evolved oxygen comes from water.

Aerobic respiration

C₆H₁₂O₆+6O₂→6CO₂+6H₂O+energy

Overall glucose oxidation; ATP yield varies with organism, shuttle and cellular conditions.

Alcohol fermentation

C₆H₁₂O₆→2C₂H₅OH+2CO₂; glycolysis net=2 ATP/glucose

Yeast-type fermentation; regenerates NAD⁺ and does not add ATP beyond glycolysis.

Lactate formation

C₆H₁₂O₆→2C₃H₆O₃; glycolysis net=2 ATP/glucose

Simplified lactic-acid representation; cellular product mainly lactate at physiological pH.

Respiratory quotient

RQ=CO₂ produced/O₂ consumed

Use molar amounts or gas volumes at same T and P; carbohydrate RQ≈1, fats generally <1, organic acids can exceed 1.

Glycolysis and Calvin-cycle accounting

Per glucose: 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP, 2 NADH; per 3CO₂ fixed: 9 ATP+6 NADPH for one net triose phosphate

Calvin-cycle ideal accounting; two net triose phosphates correspond to one hexose equivalent.

Human physiology

Breathing volumes

IC=TV+IRV; EC=TV+ERV; FRC=ERV+RV; VC=IRV+TV+ERV; TLC=VC+RV

TV tidal, IRV inspiratory reserve, ERV expiratory reserve, RV residual volume; capacities in mL or L.

Ventilation

Minute ventilation=tidal volume×breathing frequency; alveolar ventilation=(TV−dead-space volume)×frequency

Same volume units; frequency per minute yields volume/minute.

Cardiac output

CO=heart rate×stroke volume

Example: 72 beats/min×70 mL/beat=5040 mL/min≈5.0 L/min. Values vary with physiology.

Filtration and urine

Urine excretion rate=filtration rate−reabsorption rate+secretion rate

For a particular substance, use matching amount/time units; not simply bulk water volumes. GFR≈125 mL/min in a typical healthy adult, about 180 L/day.

Genetics

Monohybrid ratios

Aa×Aa: genotype 1AA:2Aa:1aa; phenotype 3 dominant:1 recessive; Aa×aa test cross 1:1

Single locus, complete dominance, equal viability and ordinary Mendelian segregation.

Incomplete dominance and codominance

Heterozygote×heterozygote: genotype 1:2:1; phenotype 1:2:1

Distinct heterozygote phenotype; not the 3:1 phenotype of complete dominance.

Dihybrid ratios

AaBb×AaBb phenotype 9:3:3:1; AaBb×aabb test cross 1:1:1:1

Independent assortment, complete dominance at both loci and no viability effects; linkage changes ratios.

Gamete types

Number of gamete combinations=2ⁿ

n independently assorting heterozygous loci; does not assume linked genes assort independently.

Probability

Independent events P(A and B)=P(A)P(B); mutually exclusive P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)

Use multiplication/addition only when the stated conditions hold; general addition subtracts overlap.

Recombination frequency

RF=100×recombinant offspring/total offspring; map distance≈RF cM

Approximation for short intervals; observed RF≤50%; multiple crossovers make RF underestimate longer map distances.

Evolution

Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium

p+q=1; p²+2pq+q²=1

Two alleles in ideal random-mating population without selection, migration, mutation or appreciable drift. Frequencies AA=p², Aa=2pq, aa=q².

Recessive phenotype estimate

q=√(frequency of aa); carriers=2q(1−q)

Requires Hardy–Weinberg and fully expressed recessive phenotype; do not apply without assumptions.

Population ecology

Population change

N_(t+1)=N_t+B+I−D−E

B births, I immigration, D deaths, E emigration over the same interval; counts, not per-capita rates.

Exponential growth

dN/dt=rN; N_t=N₀e^(rt); doubling time=ln2/r

Constant intrinsic per-capita growth rate r; doubling formula only r>0, unconstrained ideal growth.

Logistic growth

dN/dt=rN(1−N/K)

K carrying capacity; continuous deterministic model. Growth rate maximum at N=K/2 for r>0.

Population density

Density=number of individuals/area or volume

Choose appropriate sampling units; biomass or cover may be more suitable than counts for some organisms.

Ecosystem

Productivity

NPP=GPP−R

Net primary production equals gross primary production minus producer respiration; use matching energy/biomass per area per time units.

Trophic transfer

Transfer efficiency=100×production at next level/production at previous level

About 10% is a textbook approximation, not a universal exact efficiency; repeated 10% transfers give E_n≈E₀(0.1)ⁿ.

Biodiversity

Species–area relationship

S=C Aᶻ; log S=log C+z log A

S richness, A area, C and z fitted constants; scales and region affect exponent; use consistent log base.

Reproduction

Flowering-plant fertilisation

n sperm+n egg→2n zygote; n sperm+2n central cell→3n primary endosperm nucleus

Typical angiosperm embryo sac with two haploid polar nuclei; exceptions exist. Double fertilisation combines syngamy and triple fusion.

Pollen and ovule accounting

One functional pollen grain supplies two sperm; one successful ovule can form one seed

Typical angiosperm reproduction, assuming successful fertilisation and development; one sperm forms zygote and the other participates in endosperm formation.
