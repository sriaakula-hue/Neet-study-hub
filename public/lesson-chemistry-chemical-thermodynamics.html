Chemical Thermodynamics

Chemical Thermodynamics

Key concepts

  • With the chemistry sign convention, ΔU = q + w, where w is work done on the system.
  • Enthalpy H = U + pV is a state function. At constant pressure with only pressure–volume work, heat transfer equals ΔH.
  • At constant temperature and pressure, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS determines thermodynamic favourability. A negative ΔG favours the forward process.

Worked connection

If ΔH = −100 kJ/mol, ΔS = −0.20 kJ/(mol K) and T = 300 K, ΔG = −40 kJ/mol.

Watch out

A thermodynamically favourable reaction need not be fast. Rate depends on activation barriers and kinetics.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. An extensive property is:

Internal energy

Extensive properties scale with system amount; temperature, density and pressure are intensive.

2. When a reaction is reversed, its enthalpy change:

Changes sign

The same energy difference is traversed in the opposite direction.

3. Hess’s law is based on enthalpy being a:

State function

The overall enthalpy change depends on endpoints, so intermediate reaction steps can be added.

4. At T = 300 K, ΔH = +20 kJ/mol and ΔS = +0.10 kJ/(mol K) give ΔG:

−10 kJ/mol

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = 20 − 300 × 0.10 = −10 kJ/mol.

5. Entropy commonly increases during melting because:

The liquid permits more accessible molecular arrangements

The liquid generally has greater configurational freedom than the ordered solid at the melting transition.

6. The standard Gibbs change and equilibrium constant satisfy:

ΔG° = −RT ln K

Large equilibrium K corresponds to negative standard Gibbs change under the specified standard-state convention.

7. At equilibrium, the actual reaction Gibbs energy change is:

Zero

The reaction has no net thermodynamic driving force at equilibrium; standard ΔG° need not be zero.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Using ΔU = q + w, q = +50 J and w = −20 J give ΔU:

  1. −70 J
  2. −30 J
  3. +30 J
  4. +70 J
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. +30 J

ΔU = 50 − 20 = +30 J.

2. At constant T and p, forward thermodynamic favourability corresponds to:

  1. ΔG < 0
  2. ΔG > 0
  3. Always ΔH > 0
  4. Always ΔS = 0
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ΔG < 0

A negative Gibbs energy change favours the forward process.

3. A state function depends on:

  1. Only the path
  2. Initial and final states
  3. Only elapsed time
  4. Only catalyst mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Initial and final states

Its change is independent of the path between states.

4. An extensive property is:

  1. Internal energy
  2. Temperature
  3. Density
  4. Pressure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Internal energy

Extensive properties scale with system amount; temperature, density and pressure are intensive.

5. When a reaction is reversed, its enthalpy change:

  1. Changes sign
  2. Stays identical
  3. Becomes zero necessarily
  4. Always becomes positive regardless of original sign
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Changes sign

The same energy difference is traversed in the opposite direction.

6. Hess’s law is based on enthalpy being a:

  1. State function
  2. Rate constant
  3. Vector
  4. Catalyst
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. State function

The overall enthalpy change depends on endpoints, so intermediate reaction steps can be added.

7. At T = 300 K, ΔH = +20 kJ/mol and ΔS = +0.10 kJ/(mol K) give ΔG:

  1. +10 kJ/mol
  2. +50 kJ/mol
  3. −50 kJ/mol
  4. −10 kJ/mol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. −10 kJ/mol

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = 20 − 300 × 0.10 = −10 kJ/mol.

8. Entropy commonly increases during melting because:

  1. The liquid permits more accessible molecular arrangements
  2. Every bond disappears
  3. Mass increases
  4. Temperature must rise throughout melting
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The liquid permits more accessible molecular arrangements

The liquid generally has greater configurational freedom than the ordered solid at the melting transition.

9. The standard Gibbs change and equilibrium constant satisfy:

  1. ΔG° = −RT ln K
  2. ΔG° = RT/K
  3. ΔG° = KRT
  4. ΔG° = 0 for every K
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ΔG° = −RT ln K

Large equilibrium K corresponds to negative standard Gibbs change under the specified standard-state convention.

10. At equilibrium, the actual reaction Gibbs energy change is:

  1. Zero
  2. Always negative
  3. Always positive
  4. Equal to activation energy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Zero

The reaction has no net thermodynamic driving force at equilibrium; standard ΔG° need not be zero.
