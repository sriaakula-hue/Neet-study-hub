Mole concept

Moles and particle count

n=m/M; N=nN_A; N_A≈6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹

m mass and M molar mass in consistent units; N counts specified atoms, molecules or ions.

Gas volume and density

PV=nRT; M=ρRT/P; n=V/V_m

Ideal gas; V_m depends on T and P. At 273.15 K and 1 atm V_m≈22.414 L/mol; at 1 bar ≈22.711 L/mol.

Mass percentages and empirical formula

Mass %=100m_element/m_compound; mole ratio ∝ mass/M_atomic

Divide mole amounts by the smallest and convert to whole numbers.

Molecular formula

Multiplier = molar mass / empirical-formula mass

Molecular formula is empirical formula multiplied by an integer.

Stoichiometry and yield

For aA+bB→cC, n_A/a=n_B/b=n_C/c; % yield=100 actual/theoretical

Ratios apply to amounts reacting/formed; smallest initial n/coefficient identifies limiting reactant.

Concentration

Molarity and molality

M=n_solute/V_solution(L); m=n_solute/m_solvent(kg)

Molarity changes with temperature; molality uses solvent mass. Context distinguishes m mass from molality.

Mole fraction and percentages

xᵢ=nᵢ/Σn; Σxᵢ=1; mass %=100m_solute/m_solution; volume %=100V_solute/V_solution

State whether a percentage is mass/mass, volume/volume or mass/volume.

Parts per million and dilution

ppm=10⁶×mass fraction; M₁V₁=M₂V₂

Dilution relation conserves solute moles with no reaction. For dilute aqueous solutions, mg/L≈ppm only when density≈1 kg/L.

Atomic structure

Photon and uncertainty

E=hν=hc/λ; ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2; ℏ=h/(2π)

SI energy in J; uncertainty is not instrument error.

Bohr energy and wavelength

E_n=−13.6Z²/n² eV; λ=h/(mv)

First for one-electron species; second nonrelativistic de Broglie form.

Orbitals and capacity

Orbitals in shell=n²; maximum electrons=2n²; subshell orbitals=2l+1

Quantum numbers: l=0,…,n−1; m_l=−l,…,+l; two electrons per orbital.

Chemical bonding

Formal charge

FC=valence electrons−nonbonding electrons−½bonding electrons

Lewis bookkeeping; formal charge is distinct from oxidation state.

Bond order

BO=(N_bonding−N_antibonding)/2

Molecular orbital electron counts; larger BO generally implies stronger shorter bonds within comparable species.

Dipole moment

μ=q r

Charge separation r; vector contributions add; 1 D≈3.336×10⁻³⁰ C·m.

Hybridisation count

Steric number = σ-bonded atoms + lone pairs on central atom

Common model: 2 sp, 3 sp², 4 sp³; count each multiple bond as one bonded atom. This is a structural guide, not universal for all complexes.

Thermodynamics

Chemistry first law and work

ΔU=q+w; w=−P_extΔV; w_rev,isothermal=−nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

Chemistry convention: w is work done on system. Constant external pressure for middle expression; ideal reversible gas for last.

Heat and enthalpy

q=mcΔT; H=U+PV; ΔH=ΔU+Δn_gRT

Last relation for ideal gaseous species at common T; Δn_g products minus reactants, with stoichiometric coefficients.

Reaction enthalpy and bonds

ΔH°_rxn=ΣνΔH°_f(products)−ΣνΔH°_f(reactants); ΔH≈Σbonds broken−Σbonds formed

Bond enthalpy estimate for gas-phase average bonds; breaking absorbs, forming releases energy.

Gibbs energy and entropy

ΔG=ΔH−TΔS; ΔS_surroundings=−ΔH_system/T

Constant temperature; second expression for a constant-pressure process with a thermal reservoir.

Equilibrium and free energy

ΔG=ΔG°+RT ln Q; ΔG°=−RT ln K

Dimensionless activity-based Q and K; at equilibrium ΔG=0 and Q=K, not necessarily ΔG°=0.

Equilibrium

Equilibrium expression

K_c=∏[products]^ν/∏[reactants]^ν; K_p=K_c(RT)^Δn_g

School concentration/pressure convention, consistent units; pure solids and liquids omitted, gases ideal for conversion.

Changing reaction equations

K_reverse=1/K; K_multiplied=Kᵃ; K_added=K₁K₂

Reverse reaction, multiply all coefficients by a, or add reactions respectively, at same T.

Ionic product and pH

K_w=[H⁺][OH⁻]; pH=−log₁₀[H⁺]; pOH=−log₁₀[OH⁻]; pH+pOH=pK_w

Dilute ideal aqueous concentration approximation. pK_w≈14 at 25°C; neutral pH=pK_w/2, not always 7.

Weak acid and weak base

[H⁺]≈√(K_aC); [OH⁻]≈√(K_bC); α≈√(K/C)

Monoprotic weak species, α≪1, water autoionisation negligible; C in mol/L in school convention.

Conjugate pairs and buffers

K_aK_b=K_w; pH=pK_a+log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA])

Conjugate acid/base pair; Henderson–Hasselbalch assumes an effective buffer and concentration/activity approximation.

Solubility products

K_sp(AB)=s²; K_sp(AB₂)=4s³; Q_sp>K_sp implies precipitation

Pure-water ideal dissolution without common ions or other equilibria; s molar solubility; AB₂→A²⁺+2B⁻.

Salt hydrolysis

pH≈½(pK_w+pK_a+log C) for weak-acid/strong-base salt

Small hydrolysis, ideal dilute solution, C in mol/L; corresponding strong-acid/weak-base salt pH≈½(pK_w−pK_b−log C).

Redox and electrochemistry

Oxidation numbers and equivalent mass

Σoxidation numbers=species charge; equivalent mass=molar mass/n_factor

n_factor is reaction-dependent: exchanged electrons for redox, H⁺ or OH⁻ equivalents for specified acid-base reaction.

Conductivity

G=1/R; κ=ℓ/(RA); Λ_m=κ/c

κ in S/m and c in mol/m³ gives Λ_m in S·m²/mol. If κ in S/cm and c in mol/L, Λ_m=1000κ/c in S·cm²/mol.

Kohlrausch and dissociation

Λ_m°=Σνᵢλᵢ°; α≈Λ_m/Λ_m°

Infinite-dilution ionic contributions; dissociation estimate used for weak electrolytes with suitable assumptions.

Cell potential and Nernst

E_cell=E_cathode−E_anode; E=E°−(RT/nF)ln Q

Electrode values are reduction potentials. n is transferred electron count; F≈96485 C/mol.

Nernst at 298 K

E=E°−(0.05916/n)log₁₀Q; ΔG°=−nFE°; log₁₀K=nE°/0.05916

E in volts; T≈298.15 K; Q and thermodynamic K dimensionless.

Faraday electrolysis

m=MIt/(nF)

m deposited mass, M molar mass, current I amperes and t seconds; n electrons per ion; assumes 100% current efficiency.

Solutions

Raoult and Henry

pᵢ=xᵢpᵢ°; p_total=Σpᵢ; p_gas=K_H x_gas

Ideal volatile solution for Raoult; Henry convention here is pressure equals K_H times dissolved mole fraction.

Relative vapour-pressure lowering

(p°−p)/p°=x_solute≈n_solute/n_solvent

Nonvolatile solute, ideal solution; final approximation for dilute solution.

Colligative properties

ΔT_b=iK_bm; ΔT_f=iK_fm; π=iCRT

Dilute ideal solution; m molality, C molarity, π osmotic pressure; use R units consistent with C and pressure.

van't Hoff factor

i=observed colligative effect/ideal nonelectrolyte effect; i=1+α(ν−1)

Dissociation into ν particles with fraction α. For association of a monomers, i=1−α(1−1/a).

Chemical kinetics

Reaction rate

Rate=−(1/a)d[A]/dt=(1/c)d[C]/dt

For aA→cC; rate positive for reaction proceeding forward.

Rate law and units

r=k[A]^m[B]^n; overall order=m+n; units of k=(concentration)^(1−order)/time

Orders determined experimentally, not generally from balanced coefficients.

Zero order

[A]=[A]₀−kt; t_half=[A]₀/(2k)

Valid during zero-order regime; concentration cannot become negative.

First order

ln([A]₀/[A])=kt; k=(2.303/t)log₁₀([A]₀/[A]); t_half=0.693/k

First-order decay, k in inverse time; fraction remaining=e^(−kt).

Arrhenius

k=Ae^(−E_a/RT); ln(k₂/k₁)=(E_a/R)(1/T₁−1/T₂)

Same reaction and approximately constant activation energy/prefactor; T K, E_a units consistent with R.

Coordination chemistry

Coordination and oxidation state

Metal oxidation state=complex charge−Σligand charges

Coordination number counts donor atoms, not necessarily number of ligands.

Spin-only magnetic moment

μ=√[n(n+2)] μ_B

n unpaired electrons; spin-only approximation, orbital contributions neglected.

Crystal-field splitting

Octahedral: t₂g −0.4Δ_o, e_g +0.6Δ_o; tetrahedral: e −0.6Δ_t, t₂ +0.4Δ_t

Energy per electron relative to barycentre; CFSE sums occupied energies; pairing energy treated separately. Δ_t≈4Δ_o/9 for comparable systems.

Organic chemistry

Degree of unsaturation

DBE=(2C+2+N−H−X)/2

Usual neutral closed-shell formulas; X halogen count, O and S ignored. Each ring or double bond contributes 1, triple bond 2.

Homologous series

Alkanes C_nH_(2n+2); acyclic monoalkenes C_nH_(2n); acyclic monoalkynes C_nH_(2n−2)

Hydrocarbons only, stated structural assumptions; cycloalkanes also C_nH_(2n).

Combustion

C_xH_y+(x+y/4)O₂→xCO₂+(y/2)H₂O

Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon; may multiply coefficients to obtain integers.

Common functional groups

Alcohol R−OH; aldehyde R−CHO; ketone R−CO−R′; acid R−COOH; ester R−COOR′; amine R−NH₂

Structural patterns rather than numerical formulas; R and R′ are organic groups.

Practical chemistry

Titration equivalence

aM_AV_A=bM_BV_B when a and b are reactive equivalents per mole

Conserves equivalents, not blindly M₁V₁=M₂V₂. For H₂SO₄+2NaOH, 2M_acidV_acid=M_baseV_base.

Elemental combustion analysis

m_C=(12/44)m_CO₂; m_H=(2/18)m_H₂O; %=100m_element/m_sample

Complete combustion, all relevant carbon collected as CO₂ and hydrogen as water; approximate integer molar masses.

Chromatography

R_f=distance travelled by solute/distance travelled by solvent front

Distances from the same origin; ratio dimensionless and dependent on conditions.

Constants

Gas constant and energy conversion

R≈8.314 J/(mol·K)=0.082057 L·atm/(mol·K)=0.08314 L·bar/(mol·K)

Choose the value matching pressure/volume units; 1 cal=4.184 J. Use natural log ln versus base-10 log consistently.
