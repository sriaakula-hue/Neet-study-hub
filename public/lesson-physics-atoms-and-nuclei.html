Atoms and Nuclei

Key concepts

  • In the Bohr model for hydrogen, En = −13.6 eV/n². Photon energies correspond to energy differences between levels.
  • Mass number counts protons and neutrons; atomic number counts protons. Isotopes share atomic number.
  • Binding energy is associated with mass defect through E = Δmc². Nuclear fission splits a heavy nucleus; fusion joins light nuclei.

Worked example

The hydrogen n = 2 level has energy −3.4 eV. A transition from n = 2 to n = 1 emits a photon of energy 10.2 eV.

Watch out

The quoted Bohr energy formula is for hydrogen; hydrogen-like ions require the appropriate Z² factor.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Rutherford scattering supported a nucleus that is:

Small, dense and positively charged

Rare large-angle deflections indicated a concentrated positive charge and much of the mass in a small nucleus.

2. In the Bohr model, hydrogen orbit radius scales with n as:

n²

For hydrogen, rn = a₀n²; hydrogen-like ions introduce division by Z.

3. Ionisation energy from hydrogen’s ground state is approximately:

13.6 eV

It requires raising energy from −13.6 eV to the continuum reference of zero.

4. A nucleus has atomic number 6 and mass number 14. Neutron count is:

8

Neutrons = A − Z = 14 − 6 = 8.

5. Greater binding energy per nucleon generally indicates:

Greater nuclear binding stability

More energy is required per nucleon to disassemble the nucleus, though decay stability also involves other factors.

6. Nuclear fusion refers to:

Combining light nuclei

Fusion joins nuclei; chemical bonding rearranges electrons without the same nuclear change.

7. The nuclear radius roughly scales with mass number A as:

A¹ᐟ³

The empirical relation R ≈ R₀A¹ᐟ³ reflects approximately constant nuclear density.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. For hydrogen, E₂ in the Bohr model is:

  1. −13.6 eV
  2. −6.8 eV
  3. −3.4 eV
  4. +3.4 eV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. −3.4 eV

−13.6/2² = −3.4 eV.

2. An n = 2 to n = 1 hydrogen transition emits energy:

  1. 3.4 eV
  2. 6.8 eV
  3. 10.2 eV
  4. 17 eV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 10.2 eV

The energy difference is −3.4 − (−13.6) = 10.2 eV.

3. Mass number equals the total number of:

  1. Protons and neutrons
  2. Electrons only
  3. Protons and electrons
  4. Neutrons only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Protons and neutrons

Mass number counts nuclear nucleons.

4. Rutherford scattering supported a nucleus that is:

  1. Small, dense and positively charged
  2. Spread uniformly through the atom
  3. Negatively charged
  4. Made only of electrons
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Small, dense and positively charged

Rare large-angle deflections indicated a concentrated positive charge and much of the mass in a small nucleus.

5. In the Bohr model, hydrogen orbit radius scales with n as:

  1. n²
  2. n
  3. 1/n
  4. 1/n²
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. n²

For hydrogen, rn = a₀n²; hydrogen-like ions introduce division by Z.

6. Ionisation energy from hydrogen’s ground state is approximately:

  1. 13.6 eV
  2. 3.4 eV
  3. 10.2 eV
  4. 0 eV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 13.6 eV

It requires raising energy from −13.6 eV to the continuum reference of zero.

7. A nucleus has atomic number 6 and mass number 14. Neutron count is:

  1. 20
  2. 8
  3. 6
  4. 14
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 8

Neutrons = A − Z = 14 − 6 = 8.

8. Greater binding energy per nucleon generally indicates:

  1. Greater nuclear binding stability
  2. More electrons always
  3. Higher atomic number necessarily
  4. No mass defect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Greater nuclear binding stability

More energy is required per nucleon to disassemble the nucleus, though decay stability also involves other factors.

9. Nuclear fusion refers to:

  1. Combining light nuclei
  2. Splitting only heavy nuclei
  3. Removing orbital electrons
  4. Chemical bonding only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Combining light nuclei

Fusion joins nuclei; chemical bonding rearranges electrons without the same nuclear change.

10. The nuclear radius roughly scales with mass number A as:

  1. A¹ᐟ³
  2. A
  3. A²
  4. 1/A
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A¹ᐟ³

The empirical relation R ≈ R₀A¹ᐟ³ reflects approximately constant nuclear density.
