Respiration in Plants

Respiration in Plants

Key concepts

  • Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and converts one glucose into two pyruvate molecules, with net formation of two ATP.
  • In eukaryotes, the citric acid cycle occurs mainly in the mitochondrial matrix and the respiratory electron-transport chain is in the inner membrane.
  • Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiratory electron transport. Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ without that oxygen-dependent chain.

Connect the ideas

The net ATP from glycolysis is two, because four are produced but two were invested.

Common mistake

Total ATP yield per glucose is not a single universal value; it depends on cellular conditions and accounting assumptions.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. One glucose molecule yields how many pyruvate molecules in glycolysis?

Two

The six-carbon glucose is converted into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules.

2. The citric acid cycle in a typical eukaryotic cell occurs mainly in the mitochondrial:

Matrix

The matrix contains most citric acid-cycle enzymes.

3. The respiratory electron-transport chain is located in the mitochondrial:

Inner membrane

The inner membrane hosts electron carriers and ATP synthase, supporting a proton gradient.

4. Fermentation helps maintain glycolysis by regenerating:

NAD⁺

Oxidising NADH back to NAD⁺ allows glycolytic oxidation steps to continue.

5. Alcoholic fermentation in yeast commonly produces ethanol and:

Carbon dioxide

Pyruvate is decarboxylated and the resulting acetaldehyde reduced to ethanol.

6. The proton gradient used for mitochondrial ATP synthesis is chiefly across the:

Inner membrane

Protons return through ATP synthase from the intermembrane space to the matrix.

7. For carbohydrate oxidation, a respiratory quotient near 1 means:

CO₂ released and O₂ consumed are approximately equal

RQ is the ratio of CO₂ production to O₂ consumption; complete carbohydrate oxidation commonly gives approximately one.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. Glycolysis takes place in the:

  1. Chloroplast stroma
  2. Cytosol
  3. Mitochondrial matrix only
  4. Nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Cytosol

The glycolytic pathway is cytosolic.

2. Net ATP yield from glycolysis per glucose is:

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 36
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2

Four ATP are formed and two are used, yielding two net ATP.

3. The final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration is:

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Glucose
  3. Oxygen
  4. Pyruvate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Oxygen

Oxygen accepts electrons and protons to form water.

4. One glucose molecule yields how many pyruvate molecules in glycolysis?

  1. Two
  2. One
  3. Four
  4. Six
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Two

The six-carbon glucose is converted into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules.

5. The citric acid cycle in a typical eukaryotic cell occurs mainly in the mitochondrial:

  1. Matrix
  2. Outer membrane only
  3. Intermembrane space only
  4. Nucleus
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Matrix

The matrix contains most citric acid-cycle enzymes.

6. The respiratory electron-transport chain is located in the mitochondrial:

  1. Inner membrane
  2. Outer cell wall
  3. Nucleolus
  4. Cytosolic ribosome
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inner membrane

The inner membrane hosts electron carriers and ATP synthase, supporting a proton gradient.

7. Fermentation helps maintain glycolysis by regenerating:

  1. NAD⁺
  2. Only oxygen
  3. DNA
  4. Chlorophyll
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. NAD⁺

Oxidising NADH back to NAD⁺ allows glycolytic oxidation steps to continue.

8. Alcoholic fermentation in yeast commonly produces ethanol and:

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Oxygen
  3. Nitrogen only
  4. Urea
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carbon dioxide

Pyruvate is decarboxylated and the resulting acetaldehyde reduced to ethanol.

9. The proton gradient used for mitochondrial ATP synthesis is chiefly across the:

  1. Inner membrane
  2. Nuclear envelope
  3. Cell wall
  4. Golgi membrane only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Inner membrane

Protons return through ATP synthase from the intermembrane space to the matrix.

10. For carbohydrate oxidation, a respiratory quotient near 1 means:

  1. CO₂ released and O₂ consumed are approximately equal
  2. No oxygen is consumed
  3. Only protein is oxidised
  4. No respiration occurs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CO₂ released and O₂ consumed are approximately equal

RQ is the ratio of CO₂ production to O₂ consumption; complete carbohydrate oxidation commonly gives approximately one.

11. Glycolysis occurs chiefly in the:

  1. Mitochondrial matrix exclusively
  2. Thylakoid lumen
  3. Nucleus
  4. Cytosol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cytosol

The initial glucose-splitting pathway does not require mitochondrial localisation.

12. One glucose molecule normally yields how many pyruvate molecules in glycolysis?

  1. 3
  2. 6
  3. 2
  4. 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2

The six-carbon substrate is converted into two three-carbon products.

13. The net ATP yield from glycolysis per glucose is:

  1. 38 ATP net
  2. 2 ATP
  3. 4 ATP net
  4. 8 ATP net
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 ATP

Four ATP are formed but two are invested.

14. Glycolysis produces how many NADH per glucose?

  1. 2
  2. 1
  3. 4
  4. 6
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2

Two oxidation events reduce two NAD⁺ molecules.

15. The bridge from pyruvate to the Krebs cycle produces:

  1. RuBP
  2. Lactose
  3. Oxaloacetate directly in every case
  4. Acetyl-CoA
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acetyl-CoA

Oxidative decarboxylation supplies a two-carbon acetyl group.

16. Pyruvate oxidation releases carbon as:

  1. CO exclusively
  2. Pure carbon
  3. CO₂
  4. CH₄
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. CO₂

Decarboxylation removes one carbon from each pyruvate.

17. In eukaryotes, the Krebs cycle occurs primarily in the:

  1. Nucleolus
  2. Mitochondrial matrix
  3. Thylakoid lumen
  4. Golgi cisternae
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Mitochondrial matrix

Matrix enzymes carry out most cycle steps.

18. Acetyl-CoA enters the cycle by combining with:

  1. Oxaloacetate
  2. Glucose
  3. RuBP
  4. Ethanol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Oxaloacetate

The four-carbon acceptor combines with the acetyl group.

19. The first six-carbon cycle intermediate is:

  1. Pyruvate
  2. Succinate
  3. Fumarate
  4. Citrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Citrate

Citrate forms from acetyl-CoA and oxaloacetate.

20. The electron-transport chain of mitochondria is located in the:

  1. Nuclear chromatin
  2. Golgi envelope
  3. Inner membrane
  4. Outer cell wall
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Inner membrane

Membrane complexes establish the proton gradient.

21. The terminal electron acceptor in aerobic mitochondrial respiration is:

  1. Nitrogen gas
  2. Oxygen
  3. CO₂
  4. Glucose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Oxygen

Oxygen is reduced to water at the end of the chain.

22. Oxidative phosphorylation uses energy stored in a:

  1. Proton electrochemical gradient
  2. Cellulose fibre
  3. Chromosome count
  4. Calcium crystal only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Proton electrochemical gradient

ATP synthase couples proton flow to ATP formation.

23. During mitochondrial proton pumping, protons accumulate in the:

  1. Nucleolus
  2. Thylakoid stroma
  3. Cytosolic nucleus
  4. Intermembrane space
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Intermembrane space

The inner membrane separates the pumped protons from the matrix.

24. Substrate-level phosphorylation forms ATP through:

  1. Only membrane proton flow
  2. Only sunlight
  3. Direct phosphate transfer from a metabolic intermediate
  4. Only oxygen reduction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Direct phosphate transfer from a metabolic intermediate

A high-energy substrate transfers phosphate to ADP.

25. Alcoholic fermentation converts pyruvate via an intermediate called:

  1. RuBP
  2. Acetaldehyde
  3. Oxaloacetate
  4. Citrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Acetaldehyde

Decarboxylation creates acetaldehyde before ethanol formation.

26. A major purpose of fermentation is regeneration of:

  1. NAD⁺
  2. O₂
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. RuBisCO
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. NAD⁺

Restoring NAD⁺ allows glycolytic oxidation to continue.

27. For complete oxidation of carbohydrate, respiratory quotient is commonly:

  1. 0
  2. 2
  3. 0.1
  4. 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1

CO₂ production and O₂ use have equal stoichiometric amounts for glucose.

28. Fats generally have respiratory quotient:

  1. Always zero
  2. Greater than 3
  3. Less than 1
  4. Exactly 2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Less than 1

Their relatively reduced carbon requires more oxygen per carbon dioxide produced.

29. The respiratory pathway is called amphibolic because it supports:

  1. Only photosynthesis
  2. Both breakdown and biosynthesis
  3. Only protein degradation
  4. Only gas diffusion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Both breakdown and biosynthesis

Intermediates participate in both catabolic and anabolic processes.

30. Per acetyl-CoA, the usual Krebs-cycle accounting includes:

  1. 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 GTP or ATP
  2. Only 2 NADH
  3. 4 FADH₂ and no CO₂
  4. Only glucose production
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 GTP or ATP

One turn also releases two CO₂ and regenerates oxaloacetate.
