Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen

Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen

Key concepts

  • Amines are derivatives of ammonia. Primary, secondary and tertiary amines differ in the number of carbon-containing groups attached to nitrogen.
  • The nitrogen lone pair contributes to basicity, but resonance, induction, solvation and steric effects influence relative strength.
  • Aromatic primary amines can form diazonium salts under suitable cold conditions; these are useful in substitution and coupling reactions.

Worked connection

CH₃NH₂ is primary, (CH₃)₂NH is secondary and (CH₃)₃N is tertiary. This classification counts groups attached to nitrogen.

Watch out

Aniline’s nitrogen lone pair can delocalise into the ring; amine basicity cannot be ranked solely by counting alkyl groups.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. Aniline has an amino group attached directly to:

A benzene ring

Aniline is C₆H₅NH₂, an aromatic primary amine.

2. The lone pair in aniline can interact with the ring through:

Resonance

Delocalisation makes the nitrogen lone pair less available for protonation than in many aliphatic amines.

3. A quaternary ammonium ion has nitrogen bonded to:

Four carbon-containing groups

Four substituents give a positively charged nitrogen without the ordinary amine lone pair.

4. A primary aromatic amine forms a diazonium salt by suitable:

Diazotisation

Reaction with nitrous acid generated under cold acidic conditions gives the aromatic diazonium salt.

5. Azo coupling characteristically produces a linkage:

–N=N–

The azo group joins suitable aromatic systems and often gives strongly coloured compounds.

6. An amide differs from an amine because its nitrogen is associated with a:

Carbonyl group

An amide contains a C(=O)–N linkage and has different resonance and basicity from an ordinary amine.

7. In aqueous amine basicity comparisons, one must consider:

Solvation, induction and steric effects

The protonated and unprotonated species are differently stabilised by these factors, so a simple universal counting rule is insufficient.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. CH₃NH₂ is a:

  1. Primary amine
  2. Secondary amine
  3. Tertiary amine
  4. Quaternary ammonium salt
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Primary amine

One carbon-containing group is attached to nitrogen.

2. Amine basicity is associated with the nitrogen:

  1. Lone pair
  2. Neutron count only
  3. No electrons
  4. Only bond length
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lone pair

The lone pair can accept a proton.

3. (CH₃)₃N is classified as a:

  1. Primary amine
  2. Secondary amine
  3. Tertiary amine
  4. Amide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Tertiary amine

Three methyl groups are bonded to nitrogen.

4. Aniline has an amino group attached directly to:

  1. A benzene ring
  2. Only a methyl carbon
  3. A carboxyl carbon
  4. A silicon atom
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A benzene ring

Aniline is C₆H₅NH₂, an aromatic primary amine.

5. The lone pair in aniline can interact with the ring through:

  1. Resonance
  2. Only nuclear exchange
  3. Only gravity
  4. No electronic interaction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Resonance

Delocalisation makes the nitrogen lone pair less available for protonation than in many aliphatic amines.

6. A quaternary ammonium ion has nitrogen bonded to:

  1. Four carbon-containing groups
  2. Three groups and a free lone pair
  3. One group only
  4. No groups
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Four carbon-containing groups

Four substituents give a positively charged nitrogen without the ordinary amine lone pair.

7. A primary aromatic amine forms a diazonium salt by suitable:

  1. Diazotisation
  2. Only hydrogenation
  3. Only esterification
  4. Only dehydration of an alcohol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Diazotisation

Reaction with nitrous acid generated under cold acidic conditions gives the aromatic diazonium salt.

8. Azo coupling characteristically produces a linkage:

  1. –N=N–
  2. –O–O–
  3. –C≡C– only
  4. –S–S– only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. –N=N–

The azo group joins suitable aromatic systems and often gives strongly coloured compounds.

9. An amide differs from an amine because its nitrogen is associated with a:

  1. Carbonyl group
  2. Noble gas atom only
  3. Metal nucleus only
  4. Double hydrogen bond only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Carbonyl group

An amide contains a C(=O)–N linkage and has different resonance and basicity from an ordinary amine.

10. In aqueous amine basicity comparisons, one must consider:

  1. Solvation, induction and steric effects
  2. Only number of carbon atoms
  3. Only nitrogen isotope
  4. Only colour
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Solvation, induction and steric effects

The protonated and unprotonated species are differently stabilised by these factors, so a simple universal counting rule is insufficient.

11. A primary amine contains nitrogen bonded to:

  1. Two carbon groups and one hydrogen
  2. Three carbon groups
  3. Four carbon groups with positive charge
  4. One carbon group and two hydrogens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. One carbon group and two hydrogens

The usual primary amine formula is RNH₂.

12. A secondary amine usually has formula:

  1. R₃N
  2. R₄N⁺
  3. R₂NH
  4. RNH₂
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. R₂NH

Two organic groups and one hydrogen are attached to nitrogen.

13. A tertiary amine usually has formula:

  1. RCONH₂
  2. R₃N
  3. RNH₂
  4. R₂NH
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. R₃N

Three organic groups attach to nitrogen with no N–H bond.

14. Amines act as Lewis bases because nitrogen has an available:

  1. Lone pair
  2. Empty nuclear shell
  3. Neutron pair
  4. Only positive charge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Lone pair

The lone pair can be donated to a proton or Lewis acid.

15. Aniline is less basic than many aliphatic amines because its nitrogen lone pair is:

  1. Entirely absent
  2. A proton
  3. In a metal d shell
  4. Delocalised into the aromatic ring
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Delocalised into the aromatic ring

Resonance reduces availability of the lone pair for proton binding.

16. Reduction of a nitrobenzene nitro group can form:

  1. Phenol necessarily
  2. Benzaldehyde necessarily
  3. Aniline
  4. Benzoic acid
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Aniline

The nitro group is reduced to an amino group.

17. Reduction of a nitrile R–C≡N commonly forms:

  1. R–Cl
  2. R–CH₂NH₂
  3. R–OH
  4. R–COOH without conditions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. R–CH₂NH₂

Hydrogenation or suitable hydride reduction converts the nitrile carbon to a methylene attached to nitrogen.

18. Hofmann bromamide degradation of RCONH₂ yields an amine with:

  1. One fewer carbon atom
  2. One more carbon atom
  3. The same carbon count always
  4. No nitrogen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. One fewer carbon atom

The carbonyl carbon is lost during conversion of the amide to RNH₂.

19. The carbylamine test is characteristic of:

  1. Only secondary amines
  2. Only tertiary amines
  3. All alcohols
  4. Primary amines
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Primary amines

Primary amines can form isocyanides with chloroform and alcoholic base.

20. The organic product of the carbylamine test is an:

  1. Ester
  2. Alkene
  3. Isocyanide
  4. Aldehyde
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Isocyanide

The R–NC connectivity defines the isocyanide product.

21. A primary aromatic amine is commonly diazotised using nitrous acid at:

  1. Any temperature with no stability limits
  2. About 273–278 K
  3. About 500–600 K
  4. About 1000 K
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 273–278 K

Cold conditions help form and preserve the aromatic diazonium salt.

22. The diazonium group is commonly represented as:

  1. Ar–N₂⁺
  2. Ar–NH₃⁻
  3. Ar–NO₃
  4. Ar–CN⁺ only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Ar–N₂⁺

Aromatic diazonium salts contain the aryl-bound positively charged N₂ group.

23. Sandmeyer reactions can replace the diazonium group using suitable:

  1. Only sodium metal
  2. Only calcium carbonate
  3. Only ethanol vapour
  4. Copper(I) salts
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Copper(I) salts

Copper(I) reagents facilitate replacement by groups such as chloride, bromide or cyanide.

24. Azo coupling joins aromatic units through a:

  1. –C≡C– linkage
  2. Metal-metal bond
  3. –N=N– linkage
  4. –O–O– linkage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. –N=N– linkage

The azo linkage forms when a diazonium electrophile couples with a suitable aromatic partner.

25. Many azo compounds are coloured because they possess an extended:

  1. Zero electron density
  2. Conjugated electron system
  3. Nuclear neutron chain
  4. Only saturated single bonds
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Conjugated electron system

Conjugation can shift electronic absorption into visible wavelengths.

26. Gabriel synthesis is useful for preparing many:

  1. Primary aliphatic amines
  2. Tertiary aromatic amines exclusively
  3. Amides with no nitrogen
  4. Alcohols exclusively
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Primary aliphatic amines

Alkylation and release from phthalimide can provide primary amines without repeated alkylation.

27. Exhaustive alkylation of an amine may ultimately produce a:

  1. Noble gas
  2. Carboxylic acid automatically
  3. Pure alkene only
  4. Quaternary ammonium salt
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Quaternary ammonium salt

Repeated nitrogen alkylation can yield four carbon substituents and positive charge.

28. A quaternary ammonium ion generally has nitrogen bonded to:

  1. Two groups and one hydrogen
  2. No atoms
  3. Four organic groups
  4. One group and two hydrogens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Four organic groups

Four covalent substituent bonds give the familiar R₄N⁺ structure.

29. In an amide, the nitrogen lone pair is strongly influenced by conjugation with:

  1. A halogen nucleus
  2. The carbonyl group
  3. Only a distant alkane
  4. Only water nuclei
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The carbonyl group

Delocalisation into the carbonyl reduces nitrogen basicity and affects bond structure.

30. A primary amine reacting with a suitable acyl chloride can form an:

  1. Amide
  2. Ether
  3. Alkane
  4. Nitrile directly in every case
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Amide

Acyl substitution creates a carbonyl-nitrogen bond.
