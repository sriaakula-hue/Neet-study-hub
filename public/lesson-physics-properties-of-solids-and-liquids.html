Properties of Solids and Liquids

Properties of Solids and Liquids

Key concepts

  • Stress is force per area and strain is a relative deformation. Within the linear elastic range, stress is proportional to strain.
  • Hydrostatic gauge pressure is ρgh for a uniform-density fluid. Absolute pressure includes the pressure at the surface.
  • Heat for a temperature change without a phase transition is Q = mcΔT. Latent heat uses Q = mL.

Worked example

For water with ρ = 1,000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s² and h = 2 m, hydrostatic gauge pressure is 20,000 Pa.

Watch out

Gauge pressure differs from absolute pressure. During an ideal phase change, heat transfer need not change temperature.

Concept walkthrough and worked checkpoints

Use these seven checkpoints to connect the core ideas, practise applications and identify assumptions. Try each before opening its solution.

1. A material has stress 2 × 10⁶ Pa and strain 0.001 in the linear range. Young modulus is:

2 × 10⁹ Pa

Young modulus is stress/strain = 2 × 10⁶/10⁻³ = 2 × 10⁹ Pa.

2. Pascal’s principle concerns pressure applied to a confined fluid being transmitted:

Undiminished throughout the ideal fluid

A pressure increment applied to a confined fluid is transmitted throughout it and to its boundaries.

3. Water mass 2 kg, c = 4,200 J/(kg K), heated by 5 K requires:

42,000 J

Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4,200 × 5 = 42,000 J with no phase change.

4. The SI unit of surface tension is:

N/m

Surface tension is force per unit length, equivalently energy per unit area.

5. For ideal steady incompressible flow, A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ expresses:

Continuity

The volume-flow rate is conserved along a flow tube for an incompressible fluid.

6. During ideal melting at fixed pressure, absorbed heat mainly:

Changes phase without increasing temperature

Latent heat changes the phase while the coexistence temperature remains fixed under the stated conditions.

7. Stokes drag on a small sphere in creeping viscous flow is proportional to:

Speed

F = 6πηrv is linear in v when the low-Reynolds-number assumptions hold.

Practice questions and explanations

Try each question before opening its answer.

1. A 100 N force acts over 0.01 m². Stress is:

  1. 1 Pa
  2. 100 Pa
  3. 1,000 Pa
  4. 10,000 Pa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 10,000 Pa

Stress = F/A = 100/0.01 = 10,000 Pa.

2. Strain is:

  1. Measured in newtons
  2. Dimensionless
  3. Measured in joules
  4. Measured in pascals
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Dimensionless

Strain is a ratio of like dimensions.

3. For ρ = 1,000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s² and h = 2 m, gauge pressure is:

  1. 200 Pa
  2. 2,000 Pa
  3. 20,000 Pa
  4. 200,000 Pa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20,000 Pa

ρgh = 1,000 × 10 × 2 = 20,000 Pa.

4. A material has stress 2 × 10⁶ Pa and strain 0.001 in the linear range. Young modulus is:

  1. 2 × 10⁻⁹ Pa
  2. 2 × 10⁹ Pa
  3. 2 × 10³ Pa
  4. 2 × 10⁶ Pa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 2 × 10⁹ Pa

Young modulus is stress/strain = 2 × 10⁶/10⁻³ = 2 × 10⁹ Pa.

5. Pascal’s principle concerns pressure applied to a confined fluid being transmitted:

  1. Undiminished throughout the ideal fluid
  2. Only upward
  3. Only downward
  4. As zero pressure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Undiminished throughout the ideal fluid

A pressure increment applied to a confined fluid is transmitted throughout it and to its boundaries.

8. For ideal steady incompressible flow, A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ expresses:

  1. Continuity
  2. Radioactive decay
  3. Ohm’s law
  4. Coulomb’s law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Continuity

The volume-flow rate is conserved along a flow tube for an incompressible fluid.

9. During ideal melting at fixed pressure, absorbed heat mainly:

  1. Changes phase without increasing temperature
  2. Always raises temperature
  3. Creates new mass
  4. Removes every intermolecular force
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Changes phase without increasing temperature

Latent heat changes the phase while the coexistence temperature remains fixed under the stated conditions.

10. Stokes drag on a small sphere in creeping viscous flow is proportional to:

  1. Speed
  2. Speed squared always
  3. Inverse speed
  4. Temperature only
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Speed

F = 6πηrv is linear in v when the low-Reynolds-number assumptions hold.

  2. 10000 Pa
  3. 30000 Pa
  4. 40000 Pa
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 20000 Pa

Hydrostatic gauge pressure is ρgh; atmospheric pressure is excluded. Answer: 20000 Pa.

12. A body displaces 0.002 m³ of water. Use ρ=1000 kg/m³ and g=10 m/s². Buoyant force is:

  2. 30 N
  3. 40 N
  4. 20 N
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 20 N

Archimedes’ principle gives buoyancy ρ_fluidV_displacedg. Answer: 20 N.

14. A 2 m wire stretches by 2 mm. Longitudinal strain is:

  1. 0.002
  2. 0.001
  3. 0.0005
  4. 0.0015
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.001

Strain=extension/original length; convert mm to metres. Strain is dimensionless. Answer: 0.001.

15. Steady incompressible flow has speed 2 m/s in area 20 cm². In a section of area 10 cm², speed is:

  2. 2 m/s
  3. 6 m/s

Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4 m/s

Continuity gives A₁v₁=A₂v₂, so v₂=(A₁/A₂)v₁. Answer: 4 m/s.
